Toolkit — memorise cold
Degrees of unsaturation = (2C + 2 + N − X − H) / 2
What a degree iseach ring or C=C = 1; each C≡C = 2. e.g. C₄H₈ → (8+2−8)/2 = 1 → one C=C or one ring.
Naming — orderlongest chain with the main group = stem → number to give the group the lowest locant → substituents as prefixes in alphabetical order → suffix = principal group.
Nucleophileelectron-pair donor (lone pair / π); attacks an electron-poor δ+ carbon. OH⁻, CN⁻, NH₃.
Electrophileelectron-pair acceptor; attacks an electron-rich site (C=C, ring). Br⁺, NO₂⁺, H⁺.
Fissionhomolytic → two radicals (UV, non-polar bonds); heterolytic → two ions (polar bonds / π sites).

C · Evaluative drill

Question 6
A hydrocarbon has the molecular formula C₄H₈.
(a) Draw the displayed formula of every possible structure, and state the type of isomerism relating them. Identify which show cis-trans isomerism and justify with the exact bonding requirement. [6]
(b) For the isomers that react rapidly with bromine, name the reaction type and mechanism, and explain from the bonding why they react while the ring isomer behaves like an alkane. [4]
(c) Predict, with reasoning from intermolecular forces, which isomer has the highest boiling point. [2]
(d) A single molecular formula gives compounds differing in both reactivity and physical properties. State what one structural feature is responsible in each case. [2]
Answer
(a) 1 degree of unsaturation → one C=C or one ring. Positional: but-1-ene ↔ but-2-ene. Chain: but-1-ene ↔ 2-methylpropene. Functional-group / ring: alkenes ↔ cycloalkanes. Cis-trans: only but-2-ene — each doubly-bonded carbon carries an H and a CH₃ (two different groups) and the π bond locks rotation; 2-methylpropene fails (one carbon has two identical CH₃).
(b) The alkenes react by electrophilic addition: the C=C π bond is a region of high electron density that attracts the electrophile (Br₂ is polarised as it approaches). Cyclobutane has only σ bonds, non-polar, no π density, so it behaves like an alkane (radical substitution under UV only).
(c) All C₄, non-polar, only id–id forces, so bp's are close — but the straight-chain (but-1-ene / but-2-ene) has the largest surface contact → strongest id–id → highest bp; 2-methylpropene (more spherical) is lowest.
(d) Reactivity is set by the π bond (present → electrophilic addition; absent → alkane-like); physical properties by shape / surface area (id–id). One formula spans several molecules because the arrangement — where the unsaturation sits, how the chain branches — decides behaviour, not the formula.
Question 7
Consider the following three reactions.
(i) ethene + bromine → 1,2-dibromoethane
(ii) bromoethane + hydroxide ion → ethanol + bromide ion
(iii) ethane + chlorine (UV) → chloroethane + hydrogen chloride
(a) For each, classify the reaction type and mechanism, and state whether the attacking species is a nucleophile, electrophile or radical — justifying from the electronic nature of the organic molecule. [6]
(b) Explain why (i) and (ii) proceed by heterolytic pathways but (iii) must proceed homolytically. [3]
(c) State the single general principle linking a molecule's structure to the mechanism it is forced to follow. [1]
Answer
(a) (i) addition · electrophilic — the C=C π density is electron-rich, so Br₂ becomes the electrophile (induced dipole) and is attacked. (ii) substitution · nucleophilic — C–Br is polar so that carbon is δ+; OH⁻ is the nucleophile (lone-pair donor) attacking the δ+ carbon, Br⁻ leaves. (iii) substitution · free-radical — ethane is non-polar with no δ+ site and no π density, so the attacker is a radical (Cl•).
(b) (i) and (ii) have an electron-rich site (π) or a polar bond (δ+ C) for ions to target, so heterolytic fission gives ions that react. (iii) has neither, so no ion can attack; the only route in is UV homolysis of Cl₂ to radicals.
(c) The mechanism is dictated by structure: a π system invites electrophiles, a polar σ bond (δ+ C) invites nucleophiles, and a non-polar unreactive molecule can only be attacked by radicals.
Question 8
A compound X has molecular formula C₃H₆O₃ and is optically active.
(a) Determine the degree of unsaturation and use it to narrow the functional groups X could contain. [2]
(b) X reacts with sodium carbonate to release a gas. Deduce that functional group and the class of X, and account for the remaining oxygen. [2]
(c) Deduce the full structure of X, name it, and explain exactly what makes it chiral. [3]
(d) Describe X's enantiomer and explain why the two are genuinely different compounds despite identical connectivity — give one property that would distinguish them and one that would not. [3]
Answer
(a) Degrees of unsaturation = (2×3 + 2 − 6)/2 = 1 → one C=O or one ring. With three O atoms, that points to a C=O plus two O–H / C–O environments.
(b) Effervescence with Na₂CO₃ (CO₂) is diagnostic for –COOH → X is a carboxylic acid. The –COOH uses one C and two O; the third O must be a separate –OH group.
(c) X = 2-hydroxypropanoic acid (lactic acid), CH₃CH(OH)COOH (C₃H₆O₃ ✓). The central carbon is chiral — it bears four different groups: CH₃, OH, H and COOH.
(d) The enantiomer is its non-superimposable mirror image (opposite configuration at the chiral carbon). They are genuinely different because a chiral centre has no plane of symmetry, so no rotation makes them coincide. Distinguishes them: they rotate plane-polarised light equally but in opposite directions (and react at different rates with another chiral molecule). Does not distinguish: ordinary physical properties (mp, bp, density) and reactions with achiral reagents.
