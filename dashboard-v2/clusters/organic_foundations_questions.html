Degree of unsaturation (memorise this)
(2C + 2 + N − X − H) / 2
C = carbons, N = nitrogens, X = halogens (Cl/Br/I), H = hydrogens. Each ring or C=C = 1; each C≡C = 2.   e.g. C₄H₈ → (8 + 2 − 8)/2 = 1 → one C=C or one ring.

C · Evaluative drill

Each braids many concepts and ends in a judgement. Say your reasoning out loud, commit, then reveal.

Q1 — one formula, many molecules. A hydrocarbon has molecular formula C₄H₈.
(a) Deduce every possible structure and organise them by the type of isomerism relating them; identify which show cis-trans isomerism and justify with the exact bonding requirement.
(b) For the ones that react rapidly with bromine, name the reaction type and mechanism, and explain from the bonding why they react while the ring isomer behaves like an alkane.
(c) Predict, with reasoning from intermolecular forces, which isomer has the highest boiling point.
(d) Conclude: how can one molecular formula give compounds differing in both reactivity and physical properties — what one structural feature does the work in each case?
Answer
but-1-ene
but-2-ene (cis-trans)
2-methylpropene
cyclobutane
methylcyclopropane
(a) 1 degree of unsaturation → one C=C or one ring. Positional: but-1-ene ↔ but-2-ene. Chain: but-1-ene ↔ 2-methylpropene. Functional-group / ring: alkenes ↔ cycloalkanes. Cis-trans: only but-2-ene — each C=C carbon carries an H and a CH₃ (two different groups) and the π bond locks rotation; 2-methylpropene fails (one carbon has two identical CH₃).
(b) The alkenes react with bromine by electrophilic addition: the π bond is a region of high electron density that attracts the electrophile (Br₂ polarised as it approaches). Cyclobutane has only σ bonds, non-polar, no π density, so it behaves like an alkane (radical substitution under UV only).
(c) All are C₄, non-polar, only id–id, so bp's are close — but the straight-chain (but-1-ene/but-2-ene) has the largest surface contact → strongest id–id → highest bp; 2-methylpropene (more spherical) is lowest.
(d) Reactivity is set by the π bond (present → electrophilic addition; absent → alkane-like); physical properties by shape/surface area governing id–id. One formula spans several molecules because the arrangement — where the unsaturation sits, how the chain branches — is what determines behaviour, not the formula.
Q2 — structure decides the pathway. Three reactions:
(i) ethene + bromine → 1,2-dibromoethane
(ii) bromoethane + hydroxide ion → ethanol + bromide ion
(iii) ethane + chlorine (UV) → chloroethane + HCl
(a) For each, classify the reaction type and mechanism, and state whether the attacking species is a nucleophile, electrophile, or radical — justifying from the electronic nature of the organic molecule.
(b) Explain why (i) and (ii) go heterolytic (ionic) but (iii) must go homolytic (radical).
(c) Conclude with the single general principle linking a molecule's structure to the mechanism it is forced to follow.
Answer
(a) (i) Addition · electrophilic — the C=C π density is electron-rich; Br₂ becomes the electrophile (induced dipole) and is attacked. (ii) Substitution · nucleophilic — C–Br is polar so that C is δ+; OH⁻ is the nucleophile (lone-pair donor) attacking the δ+ carbon, Br⁻ leaves. (iii) Substitution · free-radical — ethane is non-polar with no δ+ site and no π density, so the attacker is a radical (Cl•).
(b) (i)/(ii) have an electron-rich site (π) or a polar bond (δ+ C) for ions to target → heterolytic fission gives ions that react. (iii) has neither, so no ion can attack; the only way in is UV homolysis of Cl₂ to radicals.
(c) The mechanism is dictated by structure — a π system invites electrophiles, a polar σ bond (δ+ C) invites nucleophiles, a non-polar unreactive molecule can only be attacked by radicals. Find the electronic feature and the mechanism follows.
Q3 — deduce and judge. Compound X has molecular formula C₃H₆O₃ and is optically active.
(a) Determine the degree of unsaturation and narrow the functional groups X could contain.
(b) X reacts with sodium carbonate to release a gas. Deduce that functional group and the class of X, and account for the remaining oxygen.
(c) Given X is optically active, deduce its full structure, name it, and explain exactly what makes it chiral.
(d) Evaluate: describe X's enantiomer and explain why the two are genuinely different compounds despite identical connectivity — give one property that would distinguish them and one that would not.
Answer
2-hydroxypropanoic acid (lactic acid)
(a) Degree of unsaturation = (2×3 + 2 − 6)/2 = 1 → one C=O or one ring. With three O atoms, that points to a C=O plus two O–H / C–O environments.
(b) Effervescence with Na₂CO₃ (CO₂) is diagnostic for –COOH → X is a carboxylic acid. The –COOH uses one C and two O; the third O must be a separate –OH group.
(c) X = 2-hydroxypropanoic acid (lactic acid), CH₃CH(OH)COOH (C₃H₆O₃ ✓). The central carbon is chiral — it bears four different groups: CH₃, OH, H, COOH.
(d) The enantiomer is its non-superimposable mirror image (opposite configuration at the chiral carbon). They're genuinely different because a chiral centre has no plane of symmetry, so no rotation makes them coincide. Distinguishes them: they rotate plane-polarised light equally but in opposite directions (and react at different rates with another chiral molecule). Does NOT distinguish: ordinary physical properties (mp, bp, density) and reactions with achiral reagents are identical.
