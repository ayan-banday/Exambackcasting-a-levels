(b) Explain your answer, saying for each of the three named events whether it is what the diagram shows and whether it leads to antigenic shift. [3]

Confidence: high / medium / guessed
Answer - part (b)

Event 1, gene mutation, does not lead to antigenic shift. Accumulated point mutations within a single strain are antigenic DRIFT, and the diagram shows two strains entering one cell rather than one strain accumulating changes.

Event 2, recombination, is not what the diagram shows. Recombination is the exchange of genetic information between two genomes so that a SINGLE genome ends up carrying sequence from both. The diagram shows whole intact RNA segments being repackaged into new virions, which is not recombination.

Event 3, reassortment, is antigenic shift, and the description is the reason. Reassortment requires that both strains infect the same host cell, because the segments have to be present in one cell at packaging.

Gap note, B4-20. You got stuck here in Cluster 2, "why can't recombination create... so only the reassortment... That is exactly the distinction: reassortment swaps whole SEGMENTS between two strains and needs a segmented genome; recombination ends with ONE genome carrying both contributions. Only reassortment gives shift. Point mutation gives drift.

Two consequences you reasoned out unaided afterwards, both examinable and both worth keeping: H2N2 cannot reassort with a strain that was not circulating at the same time, and influenza A cannot reassort with influenza C because the segment counts differ, eight against seven.

(b) Explain why NAD cannot be regenerated from reduced NAD in mitochondria in the absence of oxygen. [3]

Confidence: high / medium / guessed
Answer - part (b)

Oxygen is the final (terminal) electron acceptor at the end of the electron transport chain, where it combines with electrons and protons to form water.

Without oxygen the electrons cannot be removed from the last carrier in the chain, so all the carriers remain reduced and the chain stops working.

Reduced NAD can therefore not be oxidised, because there is nowhere for it to pass its electrons to, so NAD is not regenerated. The link reaction and Krebs cycle also stop, because they need a supply of oxidised NAD.

You built this one unaided in the sitting, including the clause most candidates miss, the carriers cannot accept more electrons. Do not re-learn it; just note that the chain belongs to oxidative phosphorylation, not the Krebs cycle, which you corrected mid-sentence at 5:00.
