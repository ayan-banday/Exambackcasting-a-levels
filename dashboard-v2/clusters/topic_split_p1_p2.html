The three things this table says.
1. Nothing is skippable. The biggest topic in the whole qualification is Integration at 23 of 200 marks. The top six together are only 106. There is no topic you can trade away.
2. Paper 1 is flatter than Paper 2. In P1 six topics sit within 12–16 marks of each other. In P2 the Stats topics are individually larger because they only compete inside a 60-mark section.
3. Your five studied topics are 57% of P1 but only 22% of P2. The gap is entirely Section B — you have done none of the 60 statistics marks.

METHOD, AND WHAT NOT TO TRUST

  1. Two independent datasets. 593 questions from 53 prelim papers across 19 JCs, and 119 questions from 12 A-Level papers. Counted separately, reported separately.
  2. The check that says the count is faithful. All 53 prelim papers reconcile to 90–110 marks, and Pure : Stats falls out at 140 : 60 — exactly the syllabus split. Nothing was tuned to produce that.
  3. Marks, not question counts. A topic's share is its share of marks, which is what decides the grade.
  4. The A-Level source is a school compilation labelled “Modified”, so some stems are reworded. Six questions were dropped where the PDF text ran them together and I could not split them cleanly.
  5. Pre-2017 papers are excluded. They are 9740 and test different content.
  6. Where the two columns disagree by more than about 5 points, believe the prelims — four times the sample. Section B is the exception; both samples are large there.
  7. One judgement call. Some Pure questions straddle two topics (a parametric curve you then integrate). Each is counted once, under whichever topic does the most work. That blurs the Graphs / Integration / Differentiation boundary and is the main reason those three shuffle between columns.
