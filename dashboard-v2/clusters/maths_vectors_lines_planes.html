Toolkit — lines & planes
Equations

Line: \(\mathbf r=\mathbf a+\lambda\mathbf d\) (point \(\mathbf a\), direction \(\mathbf d\); from two points \(\mathbf d=\mathbf b-\mathbf a\)). Cartesian: \(\frac{x-a_1}{d_1}=\frac{y-a_2}{d_2}=\frac{z-a_3}{d_3}\).

Plane: \(\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf n=\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf n\) (normal \(\mathbf n\)). Cartesian \(n_1x+n_2y+n_3z=d\). From 3 points: \(\mathbf n=(\mathbf b-\mathbf a)\times(\mathbf c-\mathbf a)\).

Intersections

Line ∩ plane: substitute the line's \((x,y,z)\) into the plane, solve for \(\lambda\), back-substitute. Line ∩ line: equate components; consistent → point; parallel directions → parallel/same; otherwise → skew. Plane ∩ plane: the line of intersection has direction \(\mathbf n_1\times\mathbf n_2\); find one common point by fixing a variable.

Angles (take the acute one — use \(|\cdot|\))

Line–line: \(\cos\theta=\dfrac{|\mathbf d_1\cdot\mathbf d_2|}{|\mathbf d_1||\mathbf d_2|}\). Line–plane: \(\sin\theta=\dfrac{|\mathbf d\cdot\mathbf n|}{|\mathbf d||\mathbf n|}\) (sin, because you measure to the plane not its normal). Plane–plane: \(\cos\theta=\dfrac{|\mathbf n_1\cdot\mathbf n_2|}{|\mathbf n_1||\mathbf n_2|}\).

Distances & foot of perpendicular

Point to plane: \(\dfrac{|\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf n-d|}{|\mathbf n|}\). Point \(P\) to line: \(\dfrac{|\vec{AP}\times\mathbf d|}{|\mathbf d|}\). Between skew lines: \(\dfrac{|(\mathbf a_2-\mathbf a_1)\cdot(\mathbf d_1\times\mathbf d_2)|}{|\mathbf d_1\times\mathbf d_2|}\). Foot \(F\): for a plane, \(F=P+t\mathbf n\) and solve the plane equation for \(t\); for a line, \(F=\mathbf a+\lambda\mathbf d\) with \(\vec{PF}\cdot\mathbf d=0\). Reflection: \(P'=2F-P\).

[7]Question 1
The line \(l\) has equation \(\mathbf r=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\2\end{pmatrix}+\lambda\begin{pmatrix}2\\-1\\2\end{pmatrix}\) and the plane \(p\) has equation \(2x+y-2z=3\).
(a) Find the position vector of the point where \(l\) meets \(p\). [4]
(b) Find the acute angle between \(l\) and \(p\). [3]
Reveal

(a) Sub \((1{+}2\lambda,\,-\lambda,\,2{+}2\lambda)\) into \(p\): \(2(1{+}2\lambda)+(-\lambda)-2(2{+}2\lambda)=3\) (1) \(\Rightarrow -\lambda-2=3\Rightarrow\lambda=-5\) (1). Point \(=(1{-}10,\,5,\,2{-}10)=\begin{pmatrix}-9\\5\\-8\end{pmatrix}\) (2).

(b) \(\mathbf d\cdot\mathbf n=(2)(2)+(-1)(1)+(2)(-2)=-1\), \(|\mathbf d|=3,|\mathbf n|=3\) (1). \(\sin\theta=\dfrac{|-1|}{9}\) (1) \(\Rightarrow\theta=6.4^\circ\) (1).

[8]Question 2
Lines \(l_1:\ \mathbf r=\begin{pmatrix}1\\2\\1\end{pmatrix}+\lambda\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}\) and \(l_2:\ \mathbf r=\begin{pmatrix}2\\3\\4\end{pmatrix}+\mu\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\\1\end{pmatrix}\).
(a) Show that \(l_1\) and \(l_2\) do not intersect and are not parallel. [4]
(b) Find the exact shortest distance between them. [4]
Reveal

(a) Directions \(\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}\), \(\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\\1\end{pmatrix}\) not proportional → not parallel (1). Equate: \(1{+}\lambda=2,\ 2=3{+}\mu,\ 1{+}\lambda=4{+}\mu\). From first two \(\lambda=1,\mu=-1\) (1); check third: \(1{+}1=2\) but \(4{+}(-1)=3\), \(2\neq3\) (1) → inconsistent, so they do not meet → skew (1).

(b) \(\mathbf d_1\times\mathbf d_2=\begin{pmatrix}-1\\-1\\1\end{pmatrix}\), \(|\cdot|=\sqrt3\) (1). \(\mathbf a_2-\mathbf a_1=\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\3\end{pmatrix}\) (1). Distance \(=\dfrac{|{-}1{-}1{+}3|}{\sqrt3}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{3}\) (2).

[9]Question 3
The points \(A(1,0,0)\), \(B(0,2,0)\), \(C(0,0,3)\) lie on a plane \(p\), and \(O\) is the origin.
(a) Find the Cartesian equation of \(p\). [3]
(b) Find the perpendicular distance from \(O\) to \(p\). [2]
(c) Find the position vector of the foot of the perpendicular from \(O\) to \(p\). [4]
Reveal

(a) \(\mathbf n=\vec{AB}\times\vec{AC}=\begin{pmatrix}-1\\2\\0\end{pmatrix}\times\begin{pmatrix}-1\\0\\3\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}6\\3\\2\end{pmatrix}\) (2). Through \(A\): \(6(1)=6\), so \(6x+3y+2z=6\) (1).

(b) \(\dfrac{|6(0)+3(0)+2(0)-6|}{\sqrt{36+9+4}}=\dfrac{6}{7}\) (2).

(c) \(F=O+t\mathbf n=(6t,3t,2t)\); sub into \(p\): \(49t=6\Rightarrow t=\tfrac{6}{49}\) (2). \(F=\begin{pmatrix}36/49\\18/49\\12/49\end{pmatrix}\) (2).

[7]Question 4
Planes \(p_1:\ x+y+z=1\) and \(p_2:\ x-2y+z=3\).
(a) Find a vector equation of their line of intersection. [4]
(b) Find the acute angle between the two planes, and comment. [3]
Reveal

(a) Direction \(=\mathbf n_1\times\mathbf n_2=\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\1\end{pmatrix}\times\begin{pmatrix}1\\-2\\1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}3\\0\\-3\end{pmatrix}\parallel\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\-1\end{pmatrix}\) (2). Point (set \(z=0\)): \(x+y=1,\ x-2y=3\Rightarrow y=-\tfrac23,x=\tfrac53\) (1). \(\mathbf r=\begin{pmatrix}5/3\\-2/3\\0\end{pmatrix}+\lambda\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\-1\end{pmatrix}\) (1).

(b) \(\mathbf n_1\cdot\mathbf n_2=1-2+1=0\) (1) \(\Rightarrow\cos\theta=0,\ \theta=90^\circ\) (1). The planes are perpendicular (1).

[6]Question 5
Find the position vector of the reflection of the point \(P(3,1,2)\) in the plane \(x+2y+2z=1\).
Reveal

Foot \(F=P+t\mathbf n=(3{+}t,\,1{+}2t,\,2{+}2t)\), \(\mathbf n=\begin{pmatrix}1\\2\\2\end{pmatrix}\); sub into plane: \((3{+}t)+2(1{+}2t)+2(2{+}2t)=1\) (1) \(\Rightarrow 9t+9=1\Rightarrow t=-\tfrac89\) (1). So \(\vec{PF}=t\mathbf n=-\tfrac89\begin{pmatrix}1\\2\\2\end{pmatrix}\) (1). Reflection \(P'=P+2\vec{PF}=P+2t\mathbf n=\begin{pmatrix}3-16/9\\1-32/9\\2-32/9\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}11/9\\-23/9\\-14/9\end{pmatrix}\) (3).

[7]Question 6
The line \(l\) has equation \(\mathbf r=\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\1\end{pmatrix}+\lambda\begin{pmatrix}2\\1\\-2\end{pmatrix}\) and \(P\) is the point \((4,0,3)\).
(a) Find the position vector of the foot of the perpendicular \(F\) from \(P\) to \(l\). [4]
(b) Hence find the exact distance from \(P\) to \(l\). [3]
Reveal

(a) \(F=(1{+}2\lambda,\,1{+}\lambda,\,1{-}2\lambda)\), \(\vec{PF}=(-3{+}2\lambda,\,1{+}\lambda,\,-2{-}2\lambda)\) (1). \(\vec{PF}\cdot\mathbf d=0:\ 2(-3{+}2\lambda)+(1{+}\lambda)-2(-2{-}2\lambda)=9\lambda-1=0\) (2) \(\Rightarrow\lambda=\tfrac19\). \(F=\begin{pmatrix}11/9\\10/9\\7/9\end{pmatrix}\) (1).

(b) \(\vec{PF}=\tfrac19\begin{pmatrix}-25\\10\\-20\end{pmatrix}\), \(|\vec{PF}|=\tfrac19\sqrt{625+100+400}=\tfrac19\sqrt{1125}=\dfrac{15\sqrt5}{9}=\dfrac{5\sqrt5}{3}\) (3).
