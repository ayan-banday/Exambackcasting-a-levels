reverse-chain constant · Priority 1Question 1
Find each of the following.
(a) \(\displaystyle\int x^2\sqrt{x^3+1}\,dx\) [3]
(b) \(\displaystyle\int \frac{x}{\sqrt{4-x^2}}\,dx\) [3]
(c) \(\displaystyle\int_0^1 x\,e^{2x^2}\,dx\) [3]
Reveal

(a) \(u=x^3+1,\ du=3x^2dx\Rightarrow x^2dx=\tfrac13du\). \(\int\tfrac13 u^{1/2}du=\tfrac13\cdot\tfrac23u^{3/2}=\dfrac{2}{9}(x^3+1)^{3/2}+C\). M1A1A1.

(b) \(u=4-x^2,\ du=-2x\,dx\Rightarrow x\,dx=-\tfrac12du\). \(\int-\tfrac12u^{-1/2}du=-u^{1/2}=-\sqrt{4-x^2}+C\).

(c) \(u=2x^2,\ du=4x\,dx\Rightarrow x\,dx=\tfrac14du\). \(\tfrac14\big[e^{2x^2}\big]_0^1=\dfrac{1}{4}(e^2-1)\).

Rule. For \(\int x\cdot f(x^2)\,dx\) (or \(x^2\cdot f(x^3)\)): set \(u=\) the inside, differentiate to get \(du=(\text{deriv})\,dx\), then rearrange for the exact piece you have. The constant is whatever makes \(x\,dx\) equal — here \(-\tfrac12\), \(\tfrac13\), \(\tfrac14\). You are solving for the constant, never "balancing" a phantom \(-2x\). If you find yourself writing \(-2\) out front, you've multiplied when you should have divided.
cyclic vs double IBPQuestion 2
(a) Find \(\displaystyle\int (\ln x)^2\,dx\). [4]
(b) Find \(\displaystyle\int e^{2x}\cos x\,dx\). [4]
Reveal

(a) Repeated IBP. \(u=(\ln x)^2,dv=dx\Rightarrow x(\ln x)^2-\int x\cdot\tfrac{2\ln x}{x}dx=x(\ln x)^2-2\int\ln x\,dx=x(\ln x)^2-2(x\ln x-x)=x(\ln x)^2-2x\ln x+2x+C\). M1M1A1A1.

(b) Cyclic IBP: \(I=\int e^{2x}\cos x\,dx=\dfrac{e^{2x}(2\cos x+\sin x)}{5}+C\) (parts twice, the integral reappears, solve for \(I\)). M1M1M1A1.

Rule. Cyclic IBP only when a factor regenerates — i.e. \(e^{ax}\) with \(\sin bx\)/\(\cos bx\). You differentiate forever and the same shape returns, so you loop twice and solve for \(I\). If a factor decays — \((\ln x)^n\) drops a power each time, \(x^n\) drops toward a constant — it's plain repeated IBP: keep going until the awkward factor is gone. Log/polynomial = repeat; exponential×trig = cyclic.
partial-fraction DE + limiting valueQuestion 3
In a reaction the concentration \(x\) (mol dm⁻³) of a product at time \(t\) minutes satisfies \(\dfrac{dx}{dt}=k(4-x)(1-x)\), \(k>0\). When \(t=0,\ x=0\); when \(t=2,\ x=0.5\).
(a) Show that \(t=\dfrac{1}{6k}\cdots\) — solve the equation, expressing \(t\) in terms of \(x\). [6]
(b) Find the exact value of \(k\). [2]
(c) State the maximum concentration the product can reach, and find the time for \(x\) to reach \(0.9\) mol dm⁻³. [4]
Reveal

(a) \(\dfrac{1}{(4-x)(1-x)}=\dfrac13\!\left(\dfrac{1}{1-x}-\dfrac{1}{4-x}\right)\) (so \(A=-\tfrac13,B=\tfrac13\) — fractions, not whole numbers). Integrate: \(\tfrac13\ln\dfrac{4-x}{1-x}=kt+c\). At \(t=0,x=0:\ c=\tfrac13\ln4\). So \(kt=\tfrac13\ln\dfrac{4-x}{1-x}-\tfrac13\ln4=\tfrac13\ln\dfrac{4-x}{4(1-x)}\Rightarrow t=\dfrac{1}{3k}\ln\dfrac{4-x}{4(1-x)}\). M1M1A1M1M1A1.

(b) \(t=2,x=0.5:\ 2=\dfrac{1}{3k}\ln\dfrac{3.5}{2}=\dfrac{1}{3k}\ln\tfrac74\Rightarrow k=\dfrac{1}{6}\ln\tfrac74\). M1A1.

(c) As \(t\to\infty\) the reaction stops when the smaller factor hits zero: \(x\to1\), so max \(=\mathbf{1}\) mol dm⁻³ (NOT 4). For \(x=0.9:\ t=\dfrac{1}{3k}\ln\dfrac{3.1}{0.4}=\dfrac{\ln(7.75)}{3k}\approx7.3\) min. M1A1M1A1.

Two rules. (1) Partial fractions here give \(\pm\tfrac13\) — if you get a whole number for A or B, you slipped a sign (it's \(-Ax+Bx\)). (2) A logistic DE \(\dfrac{dx}{dt}=k(a-x)(b-x)\) saturates at the SMALLER of \(a,b\) (the first factor to reach zero), not at infinity. And read the units — "in thousands" means \(x=1\) at 1000 people.
Maclaurin from derivatives + limitQuestion 4
A curve has equation \(y=\ln(1+\sin 2x)\).
(a) Show that \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}=-\dfrac{4}{1+\sin 2x}\), and find the Maclaurin series for \(y\) up to the term in \(x^3\). [6]
(b) Hence find \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\ln(1+\sin 2x)-2x}{x^2}\). [3]
Reveal

(a) \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{2\cos2x}{1+\sin2x}\). \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}=\dfrac{-4\sin2x(1+\sin2x)-4\cos^2 2x}{(1+\sin2x)^2}=\dfrac{-4\sin2x-4}{(1+\sin2x)^2}=\dfrac{-4(1+\sin2x)}{(1+\sin2x)^2}=-\dfrac{4}{1+\sin2x}\). Values: \(y(0)=0,y'(0)=2,y''(0)=-4,y'''(0)=8\Rightarrow y=2x-2x^2+\tfrac43x^3-\cdots\). M1A1M1A1A1A1.

(b) \(\dfrac{(2x-2x^2+\cdots)-2x}{x^2}=\dfrac{-2x^2+\cdots}{x^2}\to-2\). M1M1A1.

Rule. The whole point of "show \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}=\dots\)": after the quotient rule the numerator is \(-\sin(\cdot)-\sin^2(\cdot)-\cos^2(\cdot)=-\sin(\cdot)-1=-(1+\sin(\cdot))\), which cancels one power of the denominator. Always look for that cancellation. Then Maclaurin \(=\sum\dfrac{y^{(n)}(0)}{n!}x^n\); for a "hence" limit, substitute the series and everything above the needed power \(\to0\).
projection · area · foot of perpendicularQuestion 5
Points \(A\), \(B\) have position vectors \(\mathbf a=\begin{pmatrix}3\\4\\0\end{pmatrix}\), \(\mathbf b=\begin{pmatrix}1\\2\\2\end{pmatrix}\). Point \(C\) has position vector \(\begin{pmatrix}5\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}\).
(a) Find the scalar projection and the vector projection of \(\vec{OB}\) onto \(\vec{OA}\). [3]
(b) Find the exact area of triangle \(OAB\). [3]
(c) Find the foot of the perpendicular \(F\) from \(C\) to the line \(OA\), and the exact perpendicular distance from \(C\) to \(OA\). [4]
Reveal

(a) \(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b=3+8+0=11,\ |\mathbf a|=5\). Scalar projection \(=\dfrac{\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b}{|\mathbf a|}=\dfrac{11}{5}\). Vector projection \(=\dfrac{\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b}{|\mathbf a|^2}\mathbf a=\dfrac{11}{25}\begin{pmatrix}3\\4\\0\end{pmatrix}\). M1A1A1.

(b) \(\mathbf a\times\mathbf b=\begin{pmatrix}8\\-6\\2\end{pmatrix}\), \(|\cdot|=\sqrt{104}=2\sqrt{26}\); area \(=\tfrac12\cdot2\sqrt{26}=\sqrt{26}\). M1M1A1.

(c) \(F=\left(\dfrac{\vec{OC}\cdot\mathbf a}{|\mathbf a|^2}\right)\mathbf a=\dfrac{15}{25}\begin{pmatrix}3\\4\\0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1.8\\2.4\\0\end{pmatrix}\). Distance \(=|\vec{OC}-\vec{OF}|=\left|\begin{pmatrix}3.2\\-2.4\\1\end{pmatrix}\right|=\sqrt{17}\). M1A1M1A1.

The four you blanked on. Scalar projection of \(\mathbf b\) on \(\mathbf a=\dfrac{\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b}{|\mathbf a|}\) (a number). Vector projection \(=\) that \(\times\ \hat{\mathbf a}=\dfrac{\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b}{|\mathbf a|^2}\mathbf a\). Area of triangle \(=\tfrac12|\mathbf a\times\mathbf b|\) (parallelogram = no \(\tfrac12\)). Foot of perpendicular from a point to a line through \(O\) = the vector projection of the point's position vector onto the line's direction; perpendicular distance = \(|\text{point}-\text{foot}|\) (or \(\tfrac{|\vec{OC}\times\mathbf a|}{|\mathbf a|}\)).
related rates · single-variableQuestion 6
An inverted right circular cone has base radius \(3\) cm and height \(12\) cm. Water drips in at \(2\ \text{cm}^3\,\text{s}^{-1}\). At the instant the depth is \(8\) cm:
(a) find the rate at which the depth is increasing; [5]
(b) find the rate at which the curved (slant) surface area of the water is increasing. [3]
Reveal

(a) Similar triangles: \(\dfrac{r}{h}=\dfrac{3}{12}\Rightarrow r=\dfrac h4\). \(V=\tfrac13\pi r^2h=\dfrac{\pi h^3}{48}\Rightarrow\dfrac{dV}{dh}=\dfrac{\pi h^2}{16}=4\pi\) at \(h=8\). \(\dfrac{dh}{dt}=\dfrac{dV/dt}{dV/dh}=\dfrac{2}{4\pi}=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\ \text{cm s}^{-1}\). M1M1M1M1A1.

(b) \(l=\sqrt{r^2+h^2}=\dfrac{h\sqrt{17}}{4}\); \(A=\pi r l=\dfrac{\pi\sqrt{17}\,h^2}{16}\Rightarrow\dfrac{dA}{dh}=\dfrac{\pi\sqrt{17}\,h}{8}=\pi\sqrt{17}\) at \(h=8\). \(\dfrac{dA}{dt}=\pi\sqrt{17}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2\pi}=\dfrac{\sqrt{17}}{2}\ \text{cm}^2\text{s}^{-1}\). M1A1A1.

Rule. In related rates, get everything into the ONE variable whose rate you know (here \(h\), via \(r=h/4\)). For the slant surface \(A=\pi r l\), do NOT introduce a new \(dl/dt\) — write \(r\) and \(l\) in terms of \(h\) first, then \(\dfrac{dA}{dt}=\dfrac{dA}{dh}\cdot\dfrac{dh}{dt}\). Curved surface of a cone \(=\pi r l\), \(l=\) slant \(=\sqrt{r^2+h^2}\).
