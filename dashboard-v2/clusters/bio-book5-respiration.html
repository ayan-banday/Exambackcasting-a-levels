Name and outline two respiratory processes that depend on the proteins found in the mitochondrial matrix. [6] [INFERRED: no official key for the 9477 specimen; the outlines below are built from LO 3(g)'s own wording and cross-checked against HCI Prelim 2021 P2 Q8b to Q8c and NYJC Prelim 2025 P3 Q2a]

1. Name of process 1: the link reaction, the oxidative decarboxylation of pyruvate.

2. Outline: pyruvate is actively transported from the cytoplasm into the matrix.

3. It is decarboxylated, removing one carbon as carbon dioxide, and dehydrogenated by a dehydrogenase, so NAD is reduced to reduced NAD.

(b) During cellular respiration, NAD is reduced. Describe the role of NAD in cellular respiration. [3]

Describe the role of NAD in cellular respiration. [3] [INFERRED: no official key for the 9477 specimen; cross-checked against RVHS Prelim 2023 P2 Q8a and Topical 2021 STQ Q4e, whose keys give the same points]

1. NAD is a coenzyme that acts as a hydrogen (electron) carrier.

2. It accepts the hydrogen atoms removed from respiratory substrates by dehydrogenase enzymes during glycolysis, the link reaction and the Krebs cycle, becoming reduced NAD.

3. It then carries these hydrogens and electrons to the inner mitochondrial membrane, where it is oxidised and delivers the electrons to the first carrier of the electron transport chain, so that ATP can be made by oxidative phosphorylation.

4. Because it is regenerated when it is oxidised, the same NAD molecules can be used repeatedly, allowing the dehydrogenation reactions to continue.

5. Two prelim schemes on the same question add "3 ATP molecules are produced per molecule of reduced NAD" or "each NADH gives rise to 2.5 ATP". Do not write either. LO 3(h) excludes total ATP yield calculation in writing, and the two schemes disagree with each other, which is the clearest possible sign that the number is not what is being marked.

(c) NAD and NADP are coenzymes that serve similar and yet different roles in respiration and photosynthesis respectively. Compare the roles of NAD and NADP. [3]

Confidence: high / medium / guessed
Answer - part (c)

Compare the roles of NAD and NADP. [3]

1. Similarity: both can become reduced by accepting protons and electrons.

2. Similarity: both allow electron transfer to proceed in the electron transport chain.

3. Difference, role in accepting electrons and protons: NADP is the final acceptor of electrons and protons of non-cyclic photophosphorylation, whereas NAD accepts electrons and protons from the reduction / dehydrogenation of substrates in glycolysis, the link reaction and the Krebs cycle.

(b) Explain why NAD cannot be regenerated from reduced NAD in mitochondria in the absence of oxygen. [3]

Confidence: high / medium / guessed
Answer - part (b)

Explain why NAD cannot be regenerated from reduced NAD in mitochondria in the absence of oxygen. [3] [INFERRED: no official key for the 9477 specimen; cross-checked against NJC Prelim 2022 P2 Q7c, which sets the identical question]

1. Oxygen is the final (terminal) electron acceptor at the end of the electron transport chain, where it combines with electrons and protons to form water.

2. Without oxygen the electrons cannot be removed from the last carrier in the chain, so all the carriers remain reduced and the chain stops working.

3. Reduced NAD can therefore not be oxidised, because there is nowhere for it to pass its electrons to, so NAD is not regenerated.

4. As a consequence no proton gradient is maintained, and the link reaction and Krebs cycle also stop because they need a supply of oxidised NAD.

(c) Figure 7.1 is a diagram of ATP synthase in the inner mitochondrial membrane. Explain how the proton gradient that drives ATP synthesis is produced. [3]

Confidence: high / medium / guessed
Answer - part (c)

Explain how the proton gradient that drives ATP synthesis is produced. [3] [INFERRED: no official key for the 9477 specimen; cross-checked against TJC Prelim 2023 P2 Q6b, whose key gives the same three steps]

1. Reduced NAD and reduced FAD are oxidised at the inner mitochondrial membrane and release electrons to the electron transport chain.

2. The electrons are passed along a series of carriers, each at a lower energy level, so energy is released at each transfer.

3. This energy is used by the carriers to actively pump protons (H+) from the matrix across the inner membrane into the intermembrane space.

4. Because the inner membrane is impermeable to protons, they accumulate there, so the intermembrane space develops a higher H+ concentration and lower pH than the matrix, which is an electrochemical (proton) gradient across the membrane.

(d) With reference to Figure 7.1, outline how the proton gradient drives ATP synthesis. [3]

Confidence: high / medium / guessed
Answer - part (d)

With reference to Figure 7.1, outline how the proton gradient drives ATP synthesis. [3] [INFERRED: no official key for the 9477 specimen]

1. Protons diffuse down the electrochemical gradient from the intermembrane space back into the matrix, and because the rest of the membrane is impermeable to them they can only pass through the channel in the ATP synthase. This is chemiosmosis.

2. The flow of protons through the channel causes the central stalk of the ATP synthase to rotate.

3. The rotation changes the shape of the catalytic sites in the head of the enzyme that projects into the matrix, and this conformational change catalyses the joining of ADP and inorganic phosphate to form ATP. This is oxidative phosphorylation.

4. Note the ceiling this sets. Answer

Describe the features of the processes of aerobic respiration that allow energy from a glucose molecule to be harnessed. [15]

Any fourteen from the following. Starred points are compulsory for the award of full marks.

1. Aerobic respiration is the process in which glucose is completely oxidised, with a yield of a total of 36 / 38 ATP. Do not write this total under 9477. LO 3(h) excludes total ATP yield calculation in writing, and this scheme is a 9744-era prelim. Say the great majority of the ATP instead.

2. Enzyme-catalysed reactions (starred), with glucose / pyruvate / citrate as the substrates.

3. Decarboxylases catalyse removal of carbon dioxide from pyruvate during the link reaction and from intermediates during the Krebs cycle.

4. Dehydrogenases catalyse removal of hydrogen atoms from triose phosphate during glycolysis and from intermediates during the Krebs cycle. Reference to a substrate being "broken down" or "converted" instead of being decarboxylated or oxidised is rejected.

5. Redox reactions (starred), where substrates such as PGAL / pyruvate / citrate are oxidised by removal of hydrogen atoms.

6. Coenzymes such as NAD / FAD are reduced by accepting hydrogen atoms from substrates. Stating that substrates such as glucose / PGAL / pyruvate are reduced is rejected: substrates are oxidised and coenzymes are reduced.

7. Substrate-level phosphorylation (starred), the endergonic phosphorylation of ADP.

8. Being coupled to exergonic dephosphorylation of an organic substrate. "Formation of ATP from ADP" without reference to the substrate as the source of phosphate is ignored.

9. Oxidative phosphorylation (starred): reduced coenzymes / NADH and FADH2 transfer electrons down the electron transport chain.

10. Electron transport occurs across the inner mitochondrial membrane.

11. The final electron acceptor, oxygen, is reduced to water.

12. Energy released from the electrons flowing through the ETC is used to power the pumping of H+ ions across the inner mitochondrial membrane, to establish a proton gradient.

13. There is diffusion of hydrogen ions through the ATP synthase complex.

14. There is phosphorylation of ADP with Pi to ATP. "Formation of ATP from ADP" without reference to inorganic phosphate is ignored.

15. The scheme also awards 3 ATP per NADH and 2 ATP per FADH2. Do not write this either, for the same reason as point 1.

16. Glycolysis occurs in the cytosol / cytoplasm, with phosphorylation of glucose, and one molecule of glucose being converted to 2 molecules of pyruvate, with the generation of 2 net ATP and 2 NADH. Merging glucose activation with the oxidation of glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate is rejected: these are separate steps.

17. The link reaction and Krebs cycle occur in the mitochondrial matrix in the presence of oxygen.

18. One molecule of pyruvate is oxidatively decarboxylated into acetyl CoA and 1 NADH during the link reaction.

19. One molecule of acetyl CoA combines with a 4C molecule / oxaloacetate to form a 6C intermediate / citrate during the Krebs cycle, and there is regeneration of oxaloacetate.

20. There is oxidative decarboxylation to release 2 carbons as CO2, substrate-level phosphorylation to generate 1 ATP per acetyl CoA, and dehydrogenation to produce 3 NADH and 1 FADH2 per acetyl CoA. A tally of products without consideration of stoichiometry, that is with respect to per glucose or per acetyl CoA, is rejected.

A conical flask containing yeast, sucrose, and water and/or ethanol stands in a water bath at about 37 °C. A rubber bung in the neck carries rubber tubing which leads to the open lower end of an inverted, water-filled burette clamped on a retort stand; gas collecting in the burette displaces the water.

The procedure is: measure 100 cm3 of distilled water into a conical flask; weigh 1.0 g of yeast and 2.0 g of sucrose, add them to the flask and shake to mix; incubate the flask in the water bath for 5 minutes; fill the burette with water, invert and clamp it; attach the bung with tubing to the flask and insert the tubing into the open end of the burette; note the initial volume of water in the burette; measure the volume of carbon dioxide given off for 8 minutes by the amount of water displaced; then repeat all steps using 100 cm3 of 5.0% ethanol, and again using 100 cm3 of 10.0% ethanol.

Record your results in an appropriate table. [3]

Marks are awarded for the following.

1. H, headings with units.

2. P, precision of data, that is the volume of CO2 given to 1 or 2 decimal places, consistently.

3. T, trend of data, that is a decrease in CO2 with increase in ethanol concentration.

4. Note that the three marks here are the same three criteria the specimen awards five marks for in Question 1(b). Headings with units, consistent precision, and a sensible order for the independent variable are the whole of results-table marking.

(b) Yeast cells contain enzymes that catalyse respiratory reactions. A sample of yeast cells is added to a piece of Visking tubing placed inside a beaker containing 100 cm3 of distilled water. The Visking tubing is selectively permeable, so any carbon dioxide produced by the yeast diffuses through into the surrounding water. To test for the release of carbon dioxide, 1.0 cm3 of the water surrounding the Visking tubing is added to two drops of an indicator, B. As the concentration of carbon dioxide increases, B changes from blue to green to yellow. Explain why the indicator changes from blue to yellow. [1]

Confidence: high / medium / guessed
Answer - part (b)

Explain why the indicator changes from blue to yellow. [1]

1. Carbon dioxide dissolves to form (carbonic) acid. Accept: the solution becomes acidic.

(c) This investigation used colour to indicate the concentration of carbon dioxide in the sample. Suggest three improvements to this investigation that would increase the accuracy of the results. [3]

Confidence: high / medium / guessed
Answer - part (c)

Suggest three improvements that would increase the accuracy of the results. [3]

Any three of the following.

1. Use a colour chart or colorimeter for a more objective way of determining colour.

2. Use smaller intervals of time, for example 30 s, instead of 1 minute.

3. Conduct replicates to check for anomalous results, or conduct replicates and find the mean. Reject: "repeat experiment to check reproducibility of results".

4. Use a syringe to measure a fixed volume of the indicator or of the surrounding water, instead of using a dropper.

5. Use a thermostatically controlled water bath to maintain constant temperature.

6. Any other valid point, for example use a pH meter or a carbon dioxide probe.

(d) Respiration rates can also be measured using the redox indicator TTC. During respiration, hydrogen ions are removed from reducing sugars to reduce hydrogen carriers such as NAD and FAD; a redox indicator can be used as a hydrogen carrier in experimental conditions instead of NAD or FAD; and the colour change of the redox indicator can be measured using a colorimeter. Using a 10 cm3 syringe, 5 cm3 of yeast cell suspension was added into a conical flask, followed by 10 cm3 of sucrose solution and 1 cm3 of TTC. Explain why the colour change of TTC can only be observed after 10 minutes and not immediately upon addition. [2]

Confidence: high / medium / guessed
Answer - part (d)

Explain why the colour change of TTC can only be observed after 10 minutes and not immediately upon addition. [2]

1. Time is needed for the yeast enzymes to hydrolyse sucrose into reducing sugars.

2. To be used as respiratory substrates, where hydrogen ions are removed and passed to TTC to cause the colour change.

Examiner comment: this part was not well done, with only a small proportion scoring full credit; students who failed to do so often lost credit for failing to recognise the need for yeast enzymes to hydrolyse sucrose, which is a non-reducing sugar, into glucose and fructose, which are reducing sugars, before they can be used as respiratory substrates. In addition, some students failed to explain how TTC can be reduced by the hydrogen ions removed from reducing sugars during respiration.
