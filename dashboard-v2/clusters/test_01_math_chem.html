[9]Question 1 — Calculus
A curve is defined by \(y = \dfrac{x^2}{x-1}\), \(x \neq 1\).
(a) Find \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) and hence the coordinates and nature of the stationary points. [5]
(b) The region bounded by the curve \(y = \dfrac{x^2}{x-1}\)... instead, find \(\displaystyle\int \dfrac{x^2}{x-1}\,dx\) by first writing the integrand in the form \(x + 1 + \dfrac{1}{x-1}\). [4]
Reveal solution

(a) Quotient rule: \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{2x(x-1)-x^2}{(x-1)^2}=\dfrac{x^2-2x}{(x-1)^2}=\dfrac{x(x-2)}{(x-1)^2}\). Set \(=0\Rightarrow x=0\) or \(x=2\). Points: \((0,0)\) and \((2,4)\). Sign test: at \(x=0\) it is a **maximum**, at \(x=2\) a **minimum** (the curve has a min above the max here — classic for this reciprocal form). M1 quotient rule A1 derivative A1 x-values A1A1 points + nature.

(b) Long division: \(\dfrac{x^2}{x-1}=x+1+\dfrac{1}{x-1}\). \(\displaystyle\int = \dfrac{x^2}{2}+x+\ln|x-1|+C\). M1 division A2 integral A1 +C.

[9]Question 2 — Vectors
Relative to origin \(O\), points \(A\), \(B\), \(C\) have position vectors \(\mathbf{a}=\begin{pmatrix}2\\1\\-1\end{pmatrix}\), \(\mathbf{b}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\3\\1\end{pmatrix}\), \(\mathbf{c}=\begin{pmatrix}4\\-1\\k\end{pmatrix}\).
(a) Find the acute angle \(AOB\). [3]
(b) Point \(P\) divides \(AB\) in the ratio \(1:2\). Find the position vector of \(P\). [2]
(c) Find the value of \(k\) for which \(A\), \(B\), \(C\) are collinear, or state with reasoning that no such \(k\) exists. [4]
Reveal solution

(a) \(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}=0+3-1=2\); \(|\mathbf{a}|=\sqrt6,\ |\mathbf{b}|=\sqrt{10}\). \(\cos\theta=\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{60}}\Rightarrow\theta=75.0^\circ\). M1A1A1.

(b) Ratio theorem \(P=\dfrac{2\mathbf{a}+1\mathbf{b}}{3}=\dfrac{1}{3}\begin{pmatrix}4\\5\\-1\end{pmatrix}\). M1A1.

(c) \(\vec{AB}=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}=\begin{pmatrix}-2\\2\\2\end{pmatrix}\), \(\vec{AC}=\begin{pmatrix}2\\-2\\k+1\end{pmatrix}\). Collinear needs \(\vec{AC}=\lambda\vec{AB}\): from x, \(\lambda=-1\); check y: \(-2=-1(2)\) ✓; z: \(k+1=-1(2)=-2\Rightarrow k=-3\). M1 displacement M1 parallel condition A1 consistent A1 \(k=-3\).

A 2.00 g sample of impure calcium carbonate is dissolved in 50.0 cm³ of 1.00 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid. The excess acid required 21.5 cm³ of 0.500 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide for neutralisation.
(a) Write equations for both reactions. [2]
(b) Calculate the percentage by mass of \(\ce{CaCO3}\) in the sample. [6]

(b) Total HCl \(=0.0500\times1.00=0.0500\) mol. Excess HCl \(=\)NaOH\(=0.0215\times0.500=0.01075\) mol. HCl reacting with \(\ce{CaCO3}=0.0500-0.01075=0.03925\) mol. \(\ce{CaCO3}=0.03925/2=0.019625\) mol \(\times100.1=1.965\) g. % \(=1.965/2.00\times100=\mathbf{98.2\%}\). M1 total M1 excess M1 reacted M1 /2 M1 mass A1 %.

[7]Question 4 — Redox
Acidified potassium manganate(VII) oxidises iron(II) ions. In a titration, 25.0 cm³ of an iron(II) sulfate solution required 24.0 cm³ of 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ \(\ce{KMnO4}\).
(a) Give the oxidation number of Mn in \(\ce{MnO4-}\) and in \(\ce{Mn^2+}\), and construct the overall ionic equation. [3]
(b) Calculate the concentration of \(\ce{Fe^2+}\) in mol dm⁻³. [4]
Reveal solution

(a) Mn: \(+7\) in \(\ce{MnO4-}\), \(+2\) in \(\ce{Mn^2+}\). \(\ce{MnO4- + 8H+ + 5Fe^2+ -> Mn^2+ + 4H2O + 5Fe^3+}\). A1 ON A1A1 equation.

(b) \(n(\ce{MnO4-})=0.0240\times0.0200=4.80\times10^{-4}\) mol. \(n(\ce{Fe^2+})=5\times=2.40\times10^{-3}\) mol. \([\ce{Fe^2+}]=2.40\times10^{-3}/0.0250=\mathbf{0.0960\ mol\,dm^{-3}}\). M1M1M1A1.

[8]Question 5 — Bonding & structure
(a) Predict and explain the shape and bond angle of \(\ce{PF3}\) and of \(\ce{PF5}\). [4]
(b) The boiling points are: \(\ce{PH3}\) −88 °C, \(\ce{NH3}\) −33 °C, \(\ce{AsH3}\) −62 °C. Explain the trend, accounting for the anomaly. [4]
Reveal solution

(a) \(\ce{PF3}\): 3 bp + 1 lp → **trigonal pyramidal**, \(\approx 107^\circ\) (lp repels more). \(\ce{PF5}\): 5 bp, 0 lp → **trigonal bipyramidal**, 90° and 120°. A1A1A1A1.

(b) \(\ce{NH3}\) is anomalously high because it **hydrogen bonds** (H–N, N has a lone pair). \(\ce{PH3}\) < \(\ce{AsH3}\) because both have only id–id/pd–pd, and id–id increases with more electrons (As > P). So order: \(\ce{PH3} < \ce{AsH3} < \ce{NH3}\). A1 H-bond named A1 condition A1 id–id ↑ electrons A1 trend.

[8]Question 6 — Organic Foundations (evaluative)
Compound X, \(\ce{C4H8}\), decolourises bromine water rapidly; its isomer Y, also \(\ce{C4H8}\), does not.
(a) Deduce structures for X and Y and classify the isomerism relating them. [3]
(b) Name the type and mechanism of X's reaction with bromine, and explain from the bonding why X reacts but Y does not. [3]
(c) One alkene isomer of \(\ce{C4H8}\) exists as cis and trans forms; another does not. Explain, and conclude what structural feature is required for cis-trans isomerism. [2]
Reveal solution

(a) X = an alkene (e.g. but-1-ene / but-2-ene, C=C); Y = **cyclobutane** or methylcyclopropane (ring). X ↔ Y are **functional-group / ring–chain isomers** (same formula, one has C=C, the other a ring). A1A1A1.

(b) **Electrophilic addition.** The C=C π bond is electron-rich; it induces a dipole in \(\ce{Br2}\) and attacks the δ+ Br (electrophile). Y (a saturated ring) has no π density → no site for the electrophile → does not react (only radical substitution under UV). A1A1A1.

(c) but-2-ene shows cis-trans (each C=C carbon has H + CH₃, two different groups; π locks rotation); 2-methylpropene does not (one carbon has two identical CH₃). **Conclusion:** cis-trans needs a C=C AND two different groups on each doubly-bonded carbon. A1A1.

[6]Question 7 — Organic mechanism (evaluative)
Bromoethane and ethene both react, but by opposite mechanisms.
(a) For bromoethane + aqueous \(\ce{OH-}\): classify type and mechanism, name the attacking species, and explain via bond polarity which carbon is attacked. [3]
(b) Conclude with the general principle that decides whether a molecule is attacked by a nucleophile, an electrophile, or a radical — illustrating each with one of: bromoethane, ethene, ethane. [3]
Reveal solution

(a) **Nucleophilic substitution.** \(\ce{OH-}\) is the nucleophile (lone-pair donor). C–Br is polar → that C is δ+; \(\ce{OH-}\) attacks the δ+ carbon, Br⁻ leaves. A1A1A1.

(b) **Conclusion:** a polar σ bond (δ+ C, e.g. bromoethane) invites a **nucleophile**; an electron-rich π system (ethene) invites an **electrophile**; a non-polar molecule with neither (ethane) can only be attacked by a **radical** (UV homolysis). Structure dictates the mechanism. A1A1A1.
