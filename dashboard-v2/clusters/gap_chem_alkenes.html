Question 1 (Adapted from materials)

Question
Cyclohexene reacts with bromine dissolved in tetrachloromethane.
(a) State the reagents and conditions, and explain why the condition on light is needed. [2]
(b) Draw the full mechanism (curly arrows, partial charges, intermediate, slow/fast steps) and name it. [4]
(c) Only trans-1,2-dibromocyclohexane forms, not a cis/trans mixture. Explain what this reveals about the intermediate and the second step. [2]
Solution
(a) Reagents / conditions / why dark
B1 B1
\(\ce{Br2}\) in \(\ce{CCl4}\), room temperature, absence of UV light. UV would homolyse \(\ce{Br2}\) into \(\ce{Br}\) radicals and trigger free-radical substitution of the alkyl \(\ce{C-H}\) bonds — a competing reaction; the dark keeps it to clean electrophilic addition.
(b) Mechanism = electrophilic addition
\(\ce{Br2}\) is non-polar, so the π cloud induces the dipole first. M1 induced dipole + 2 arrows (π→Br, Br–Br→Br), M1 rds labelled, A1 carbocation shown, A1 fast step arrow Br⁻→C
Electrophilic addition. Slow (rds): the π bond AND the \(\ce{Br-Br}\) bond break. Fast: lone pair on \(\ce{Br^-}\) attacks the carbon (not the +).
(c) What trans-only implies
M1 A1
A single stereochemistry means the two \(\ce{Br}\) added to opposite faces. This implies the intermediate is a cyclic bromonium ion (not an open planar carbocation) that blocks one face, so \(\ce{Br^-}\) must attack from the opposite side → trans only.
Mark-droppers (mechanism drawing): partial charges on the non-polar \(\ce{Br2}\); two arrows in the slow step; the fast-step arrow to the carbon, not the "+"; label "slow/rds".

Question 2 (Adapted from materials)

Question
But-2-ene exists as cis and trans isomers.
(a) State which isomer has the higher boiling point and which the higher melting point, with full reasoning. [3]
(b) The boiling points of cis- and trans-but-2-ene differ by only ~3 °C. Explain why the difference is so small. [2]
(c) Give the organic product(s) when but-2-ene is heated with acidified \(\ce{KMnO4}\). [1]
(d) Does 2-methylbut-2-ene show cis-trans isomerism? Explain. [2]
Solution
(a) bp and mp
M1 bp, M1 mp, A1
cis has the higher bp: the two \(\ce{CH3}\) groups on the same side give a small net dipole → weak permanent dipole–permanent dipole (pd–pd) on top of dispersion; trans is symmetrical, dipoles cancel → dispersion only. trans has the higher mp: the trans isomer is more symmetrical / straighter → packs better in the solid lattice → dispersion acts more effectively → more energy to melt.
(b) Why the bp gap is small
M1 A1
The \(\ce{CH3}\) group is only very weakly polar, so even the cis isomer has only a very small net dipole → the pd–pd contribution is tiny → the two isomers differ little. (With \(\ce{C-Cl}\) bonds the difference would be large.)
(c) Oxidative cleavage
Each \(\ce{=CHCH3}\) carbon has 1 H → carboxylic acid. A1
Two molecules of ethanoic acid, \(\ce{CH3COOH}\).
(d) 2-methylbut-2-ene?
M1 A1
No. One \(\ce{C=C}\) carbon carries two identical \(\ce{CH3}\) groups, so the "two different groups on each \(\ce{C=C}\) carbon" criterion fails → no cis-trans isomerism.
The two answers point opposite ways: cis = higher bp (polarity), trans = higher mp (packing). And the size of the bp gap tracks how polar the substituents are.

Question 3 (from HCI MCQ key)

Question
Hydroboration is an anti-Markovnikov electrophilic addition: overall \(\ce{H}\) and \(\ce{OH}\) add across \(\ce{C=C}\) with \(\ce{H}\) going to the more substituted carbon.
2-methylbut-1-ene, \(\ce{CH2=C(CH3)CH2CH3}\), is converted to an alcohol by (i) steam + \(\ce{H3PO4}\); (ii) hydroboration.
(a) Give the major alcohol from each route and name it. [2]
(b) Explain, using carbocation stability, why route (i) gives its product. [3]
Solution
(a) The two alcohols
A1 A1
(i) Hydration (Markovnikov) → 2-methylbutan-2-ol, \(\ce{CH3CH2C(OH)(CH3)CH3}\) — OH on the more substituted C2. (ii) Hydroboration (anti-Markovnikov) → 2-methylbutan-1-ol, \(\ce{CH3CH2CH(CH3)CH2OH}\) — OH on the terminal C1.
(b) Why hydration gives the 2-ol
M1 M1 A1
\(\ce{H+}\) adds to the terminal \(\ce{=CH2}\) so the \(+\) sits on C2, giving a 3° carbocation.
The presence of more electron-donating alkyl groups stabilises the carbocation by reducing its positive charge, so the 3° carbocation forms in preference to the 1°; the nucleophile (water) then joins C2 → the 2-ol as major product.
Same alkene, opposite regiochemistry: the carbocation route (hydration) puts OH on the more-substituted carbon; hydroboration puts it on the terminal carbon.

Question 4 (from HCI MCQ key)

Question
In a Diels-Alder reaction a conjugated diene and an alkene (dienophile) combine: the two ends of the diene each form a new \(\ce{C-C}\) bond to the two ends of the dienophile, closing a six-membered ring with one \(\ce{C=C}\) remaining.
2,3-dimethylbuta-1,3-diene, \(\ce{CH2=C(CH3)-C(CH3)=CH2}\), reacts with ethene.
(a) Deduce the molecular formula of the product. [1]
(b) Draw the product and mark the remaining \(\ce{C=C}\); name it. [3]
Solution
(a) Formula
Addition — atoms conserved. A1
\(\ce{C6H10 + C2H4 -> C8H14}\).
(b) Product
Diene C1 and C4 (the two \(\ce{=CH2}\) ends) bond to ethene's carbons → six-membered ring; the central \(\ce{C2=C3}\) (both bearing \(\ce{CH3}\)) is the surviving \(\ce{C=C}\). M1 ring, M1 connectivity, A1 name + C=C
Product = 1,2-dimethylcyclohex-1-ene — a cyclohexene ring with a \(\ce{CH3}\) on each of the two double-bond carbons. \(\ce{C8H14}\) ✓.
Pattern recognition: not a taught mechanism — you map the described bond-forming pattern onto new reactants and conserve atoms. The two methyls sit on the carbons that were the diene's middle (which become the ring's \(\ce{C=C}\)).

Question 5 (Adapted from materials)

Question
Starting from propene, state the reagents and conditions to prepare each of the following. [6]
(a) propane-1,2-diol   (b) 1,2-dibromopropane   (c) propan-2-ol   (d) propan-1-ol   (e) propane
Solution — reagents + conditions
(a) propane-1,2-diol: \(\ce{KMnO4}\), dilute \(\ce{NaOH}\) (alkaline), cold. (mild oxidation → diol)
(b) 1,2-dibromopropane: \(\ce{Br2}\) in \(\ce{CCl4}\), room temperature, absence of UV light.
(c) propan-2-ol: steam \(\ce{H2O(g)}\), \(\ce{H3PO4}\) catalyst, high temperature and pressure. (Markovnikov → OH on C2)
(d) propan-1-ol: hydroboration (anti-Markovnikov → OH on the terminal C1).
(e) propane: \(\ce{H2}\), Ni catalyst.
The pair that catches people: (c) vs (d) — both make an alcohol from propene, but hydration gives the 2-ol (Markovnikov, via the more stable carbocation) and hydroboration gives the 1-ol (anti-Markovnikov). Conditions must be stated in full — "cold/alkaline" vs "hot/acidified" etc. flip the product elsewhere.
