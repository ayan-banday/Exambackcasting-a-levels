[10]Question 1
A student is given a green crystalline solid believed to be hydrated ammonium iron(II) sulfate, \(\ce{(NH4)2Fe(SO4)2\cdot xH2O}\). She dissolves \(9.64\text{ g}\) of it in dilute sulfuric acid and makes the solution up to \(250\ \text{cm}^3\). She titrates \(25.0\ \text{cm}^3\) portions against \(0.0200\ \text{mol dm}^{-3}\) potassium manganate(VII), and the mean titre is \(24.6\ \text{cm}^3\).
(a) Determine \(x\), showing all your reasoning. [7]
(b) The end-point is judged by the first permanent pink colour, with no added indicator. Explain why no indicator is needed, and state one reason the value of \(x\) obtained might be lower than the true value. [3]
Reveal solution

(a) Half/overall: \(\ce{MnO4- + 8H+ + 5Fe^2+ -> Mn^2+ + 4H2O + 5Fe^3+}\). \(n(\ce{MnO4-})=0.0246\times0.0200=4.92\times10^{-4}\) mol per titre. \(n(\ce{Fe^2+})\) in 25.0 cm³ \(=5\times=2.46\times10^{-3}\) mol; in 250 cm³ \(=2.46\times10^{-2}\) mol = mol of salt. \(M=\dfrac{9.64}{0.0246}=391.9\) g mol⁻¹. Anhydrous \(\ce{(NH4)2Fe(SO4)2}=284.1\); water \(=391.9-284.1=107.8\); \(x=107.8/18\approx\mathbf{6}\). M1 eqn M1 n(MnO4) M1 ×5 M1 ×10 M1 M M1 water A1 x=6.

(b) \(\ce{MnO4-}\) is self-indicating — it is decolourised while \(\ce{Fe^2+}\) remains, and the first excess drop gives a permanent pink. A lower \(x\) would follow if some \(\ce{Fe^2+}\) had been air-oxidised to \(\ce{Fe^3+}\) before titration (fewer mol titrated → larger apparent \(M_r\)… reason accordingly): actually air-oxidation lowers \(n(\ce{Fe^2+})\), raising the apparent \(M\) and \(x\); a valid "lower x" reason is impure salt containing extra anhydrous mass. A1 self-indicating A1A1 reasoned source of error.

[13]Question 3
Hydrocarbon \(P\) has molecular formula \(\ce{C5H10}\). \(P\) exists as a pair of cis–trans isomers and reacts rapidly with bromine in the dark to give a single organic product \(Q\), which is optically active.
P (C₅H₁₀) ——Br₂, dark——▶ Q (C₅H₁₀Br₂), optically active
(a) Deduce the structural formulae of \(P\) and \(Q\), and name \(P\). [3]
(b) Explain how \(Q\) can be optically active even though \(P\) is not chiral. Identify the chiral centre(s). [3]
(c) Describe the mechanism of the reaction \(P\to Q\), explaining each step and why the first step is rate-determining. [5]
(d) State why the reaction is carried out in the dark. [2]
Reveal solution

(a) Cis–trans + one Br₂-addition product that is chiral → \(P=\) pent-2-ene, \(\ce{CH3CH=CHCH2CH3}\). \(Q=\) 2,3-dibromopentane, \(\ce{CH3CHBrCHBrCH2CH3}\). A1A1A1.

(b) In \(Q\) both C-2 and C-3 are bonded to four different groups (C-2: \(\ce{CH3, Br, H, CHBrC2H5}\); C-3: \(\ce{C2H5, Br, H, CHBrCH3}\)) → two chiral centres, so \(Q\) is optically active. \(P\) is planar at the C=C with no chiral carbon. A1A1A1.

(c) Electrophilic addition. Step 1 (slow, RDS): the electron-rich \(\ce{C=C}\) induces a dipole in \(\ce{Br2}\); the π electrons attack the δ+ Br, the Br–Br bond breaks heterolytically, forming a carbocation and \(\ce{Br-}\). Slow because it makes the unstable carbocation (high \(E_a\)). Step 2 (fast): \(\ce{Br-}\) attacks the carbocation with its lone pair, forming the second C–Br bond. M1M1A1M1A1.

(d) In light, UV would cause homolytic fission of \(\ce{Br2}\) to radicals, so free-radical substitution would compete as a side reaction and lower the yield of the single addition product. A1A1.

[12]Question 4
Methane reacts with chlorine only in the presence of ultraviolet light; ethene reacts readily with chlorine in the dark.
(a) Explain this difference fully, referring to the bonding in each molecule and the type of mechanism each undergoes. [5]
(b) Even with methane in large excess, the reaction of methane with chlorine gives a mixture containing \(\ce{CH3Cl}\), \(\ce{CH2Cl2}\), \(\ce{CHCl3}\) and \(\ce{CCl4}\). Explain, using the mechanism, why a mixture forms. [4]
(c) Evaluate which of the two reaction types gives the cleaner conversion to a single product, and justify. [3]
Reveal solution

(a) Methane is non-polar and saturated with no δ+ site and no π density — no ion can attack, so it reacts only by free-radical substitution, which needs UV to homolyse \(\ce{Cl2}\) into radicals. Ethene has an electron-rich \(\ce{C=C}\) π bond, which attracts an electrophile and undergoes electrophilic addition readily at room temperature in the dark. A1A1A1A1A1.

(b) Propagation regenerates a \(\ce{Cl.}\) radical, so the chain continues; a \(\ce{Cl.}\) can abstract an H from \(\ce{CH3Cl}\) (and \(\ce{CH2Cl2}\)…) just as from \(\ce{CH4}\), giving \(\ce{.CH2Cl}\) which reacts on to \(\ce{CH2Cl2}\), etc. Successive substitutions are not controllable, so a mixture forms even with excess methane. M1M1A1A1.

(c) The electrophilic addition (ethene) is cleaner: it gives essentially a single product because there is one π bond and no chain to over-react; the radical chain substitution inherently over-substitutes to a mixture. M1A1A1.

[8]Question 6
Nitrogen forms the radical molecule \(\ce{NO2}\), its dimer \(\ce{N2O4}\), and the nitrite ion \(\ce{NO2-}\).
(a) Suggest, with reasoning, how the \(\ce{O-N-O}\) bond angle in \(\ce{NO2}\) compares with that in the nitrite ion \(\ce{NO2-}\). [3]
(b) \(\ce{NO2}\) dimerises readily to \(\ce{N2O4}\) (which contains an \(\ce{N-N}\) single bond), whereas \(\ce{NO2-}\) shows no tendency to dimerise. Account for this difference. [2]
(c) Predict, with justification, which of solid \(\ce{N2O4}\) and solid sodium nitrite \(\ce{NaNO2}\) has the higher melting point, and which conducts electricity when molten. [3]
Reveal solution

(a) On the central N, \(\ce{NO2}\) has a single unpaired electron whereas \(\ce{NO2-}\) has a full lone pair. A lone pair repels the bonding pairs more strongly than a single electron does, so it closes the angle further. Therefore \(\ce{NO2-}\) has the smaller angle (≈115°) and \(\ce{NO2}\) the larger (≈134°). M1 identify unpaired e⁻ vs lone pair M1 lone pair repels more A1 which is larger.

(b) \(\ce{NO2}\) has an unpaired electron; two molecules pair their unpaired electrons to form the \(\ce{N-N}\) bond (removing the reactive radical). \(\ce{NO2-}\) is a closed-shell ion with no unpaired electron, so it has nothing to pair → no dimerisation. A1A1.

(c) \(\ce{NaNO2}\) is giant ionic and \(\ce{N2O4}\) is simple molecular. Melting \(\ce{NaNO2}\) breaks strong electrostatic attractions between ions, vs only weak intermolecular forces in \(\ce{N2O4}\) → \(\ce{NaNO2}\) has the far higher mp. Molten \(\ce{NaNO2}\) conducts (mobile ions); molten \(\ce{N2O4}\) does not (neutral molecules, no mobile charge). M1 structure types A1 mp reasoned A1 conductivity.

[11]Question 7
Under ultraviolet light, propene \(\ce{CH2=CHCH3}\) undergoes substitution of a hydrogen on the \(\ce{CH3}\) group by bromine (the "allylic" position, next to the \(\ce{C=C}\)), rather than addition across the double bond. Bond energies: a normal alkane \(\ce{C-H}\) is \(+410\) kJ mol⁻¹; the allylic \(\ce{C-H}\) in propene is \(+360\) kJ mol⁻¹.
(a) The radical formed by removing the allylic hydrogen is unusually stable. Suggest, in terms of electron delocalisation, why. [2]
(b) Hence account for the fact that, under UV light, bromine attacks the \(\ce{CH3}\) group of propene far more readily than it attacks a \(\ce{C-H}\) bond in ethane. [3]
(c) In the dark at room temperature, propene instead reacts rapidly with bromine by an entirely different mechanism. Name this mechanism, and explain what feature of propene makes it available while ethane has no such pathway. [3]
(d) When ethene reacts completely with bromine by that room-temperature mechanism, the mass spectrum of the product shows molecular ion peaks in the ratio \(1:2:1\). Explain. (\(\ce{Br}\) exists as \(^{79}\ce{Br}\) and \(^{81}\ce{Br}\) in roughly equal abundance.) [3]
Reveal solution

(a) The unpaired electron sits on a carbon adjacent to the \(\ce{C=C}\); it is delocalised over the π system (spread across more than one carbon), which lowers its energy and stabilises the radical. M1A1.

(b) Two effects reinforce: the allylic \(\ce{C-H}\) is weaker (360 vs 410 kJ mol⁻¹) so less energy is needed to break it, and the resulting radical is stabilised by delocalisation. Both lower the activation energy of the hydrogen-abstraction step, so propene's \(\ce{CH3}\) is attacked far more readily than the strong, unstabilised \(\ce{C-H}\) of ethane. M1 weaker bond M1 stable radical A1 lower Eₐ / faster.

(c) Electrophilic addition. Propene has an electron-rich \(\ce{C=C}\) π bond, which polarises \(\ce{Br2}\) and is attacked by the resulting δ+ Br (the electrophile). Ethane has only non-polar σ bonds and no π density, so there is no site for an electrophile → no addition pathway. A1 name A1A1 π vs no π.

(d) The addition product 1,2-dibromoethane contains two Br atoms. With \(^{79}\ce{Br}\) and \(^{81}\ce{Br}\) each ≈50%, the isotopic combinations \(^{79}{+}^{79}\), \((^{79}{+}^{81}\text{ or }^{81}{+}^{79})\), \(^{81}{+}^{81}\) occur in probability \(1:2:1\), giving molecular-ion peaks at \(M,\ M{+}2,\ M{+}4\) in that ratio. M1 two Br M1 combinations A1 1:2:1.
