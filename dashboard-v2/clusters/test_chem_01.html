[8]Question 1 — Stoichiometry (back-titration)
A 2.00 g sample of impure \(\ce{CaCO3}\) is dissolved in 50.0 cm³ of 1.00 mol dm⁻³ \(\ce{HCl}\). The excess acid needs 21.5 cm³ of 0.500 mol dm⁻³ \(\ce{NaOH}\) to neutralise.
(a) Equations for both reactions. [2]
(b) Percentage by mass of \(\ce{CaCO3}\). [6]
Reveal solution

(a) \(\ce{CaCO3 + 2HCl -> CaCl2 + H2O + CO2}\); \(\ce{HCl + NaOH -> NaCl + H2O}\). A1A1.

(b) Total HCl \(=0.0500\); excess \(=\)NaOH\(=0.01075\); reacted with carbonate \(=0.03925\); \(n(\ce{CaCO3})=0.019625\); mass \(=1.965\) g; % \(=\mathbf{98.2\%}\). M1M1M1M1M1A1.

[7]Question 2 — Redox titration
25.0 cm³ of \(\ce{FeSO4}\) solution needs 24.0 cm³ of 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ \(\ce{KMnO4}\) (acidified).
(a) Oxidation numbers of Mn in \(\ce{MnO4-}\) and \(\ce{Mn^2+}\); overall ionic equation. [3]
(b) \([\ce{Fe^2+}]\) in mol dm⁻³. [4]
Reveal solution

(a) \(+7\) and \(+2\); \(\ce{MnO4- + 8H+ + 5Fe^2+ -> Mn^2+ + 4H2O + 5Fe^3+}\). A1A1A1.

(b) \(n(\ce{MnO4-})=4.80\times10^{-4}\); \(n(\ce{Fe^2+})=5\times=2.40\times10^{-3}\); \([\ce{Fe^2+}]=\mathbf{0.0960}\) mol dm⁻³. M1M1M1A1.

[8]Question 3 — Bonding & structure
(a) Predict and explain the shape and bond angle of \(\ce{PF3}\) and of \(\ce{PF5}\). [4]
(b) Boiling points: \(\ce{PH3}\) −88 °C, \(\ce{NH3}\) −33 °C, \(\ce{AsH3}\) −62 °C. Explain the trend and the anomaly. [4]
Reveal solution

(a) \(\ce{PF3}\): 3 bp + 1 lp → **trigonal pyramidal ≈107°**. \(\ce{PF5}\): 5 bp → **trigonal bipyramidal, 90° & 120°**. A1A1A1A1.

(b) \(\ce{NH3}\) anomalously high — **hydrogen bonding** (H–N + lone pair). \(\ce{PH3}<\ce{AsH3}\): only id–id/pd–pd, id–id ↑ with electrons. Order \(\ce{PH3}<\ce{AsH3}<\ce{NH3}\). A1A1A1A1.

[9]Question 4 — Organic Foundations (evaluative)
Compound X, \(\ce{C4H8}\), decolourises bromine water rapidly; its isomer Y, also \(\ce{C4H8}\), does not.
(a) Deduce structures for X and Y and classify the isomerism. [3]
(b) Type and mechanism of X + bromine; explain from bonding why X reacts but Y does not. [3]
(c) One \(\ce{C4H8}\) alkene shows cis-trans, another does not — explain, and conclude the requirement. [3]
Reveal solution

(a) X = alkene (but-1-ene/but-2-ene, C=C); Y = cyclobutane (ring). Ring–chain / functional-group isomers. A1A1A1.

(b) **Electrophilic addition**: C=C π density attacks the induced-dipole δ+ Br. Y (saturated ring) has no π → no electrophile site → no reaction (only UV radical substitution). A1A1A1.

(c) but-2-ene: each C=C carbon has H + CH₃ (different) + π locks rotation → cis/trans. 2-methylpropene: one carbon has two CH₃ (identical) → none. **Conclusion:** C=C + two different groups on each doubly-bonded carbon. A1A1A1.

[8]Question 5 — Organic mechanism (evaluative)
(a) Bromoethane + aqueous \(\ce{OH-}\): classify type + mechanism, name the attacking species, explain via bond polarity which carbon is attacked and what leaves. [4]
(b) Conclude the general principle deciding whether a molecule is attacked by a nucleophile, electrophile, or radical — illustrate each with bromoethane, ethene, ethane. [4]
Reveal solution

(a) **Nucleophilic substitution.** \(\ce{OH-}\) = nucleophile (lone-pair donor). C–Br polar → C is δ+; \(\ce{OH-}\) attacks that δ+ carbon; Br⁻ leaves. A1A1A1A1.

(b) **Conclusion:** polar σ bond / δ+ C (bromoethane) → **nucleophile**; electron-rich π (ethene) → **electrophile**; non-polar, no site (ethane) → **radical** (UV). Structure decides mechanism. A1A1A1A1.
