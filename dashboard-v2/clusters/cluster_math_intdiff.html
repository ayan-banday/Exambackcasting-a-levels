Question 1 [10]
HCI Tutorial 5A Q1
Differentiate each of the following with respect to \(x\), simplifying your answers. (a) \(\displaystyle \frac{4x^3+2x^2+1}{x}\) (b) \(\operatorname{cosec}^3\sqrt{x}\) (c) \(\ln\sqrt{\dfrac{x-a}{x+a}}\,\), where \(0 (e) \(e^{\sin^2 x}\big(e^{-x^2}+1\big)\)
Before you reveal — say it out loud:  Confidence:  high  /  medium  /  guessed
Solution — verbatim HCI
(a) split the single-term denominator first
When the denominator is a single power of \(x\), divide through term by term rather than using the quotient rule. \[ \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}\!\left[\frac{4x^3+2x^2+1}{x}\right]=\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}\big[4x^2+2x+x^{-1}\big]=8x+2-\frac{1}{x^2}. \] (1)
(b) chain rule through three layers
Outer power \((\cdot)^3\), then \(\operatorname{cosec}\sqrt x\) with \(\tfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm du}\operatorname{cosec}u=-\operatorname{cosec}u\cot u\), then \(\sqrt x\) with derivative \(\tfrac{1}{2\sqrt x}\). \[ \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}\big[(\operatorname{cosec}\sqrt x)^3\big]=3(\operatorname{cosec}^2\sqrt x)\big(-\operatorname{cosec}\sqrt x\cot\sqrt x\big)\!\left(\frac{1}{2\sqrt x}\right)=-\frac{3}{2\sqrt x}\operatorname{cosec}^3\sqrt x\,\cot\sqrt x. \] (1)(1)
(c) use log laws to break the expression up first
\(\ln\sqrt{\tfrac{x-a}{x+a}}=\tfrac12\big(\ln(x-a)-\ln(x+a)\big)\); differentiate each log term separately. \[ \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}\!\left(\ln\sqrt{\tfrac{x-a}{x+a}}\right)=\tfrac12\!\left(\frac{1}{x-a}-\frac{1}{x+a}\right)=\tfrac12\cdot\frac{(x+a)-(x-a)}{(x-a)(x+a)}=\frac{a}{x^2-a^2}. \] (1)(1)
(e) product rule, each factor by the chain rule
Product of \(u=e^{\sin^2 x}\) (so \(u'=e^{\sin^2 x}\cdot 2\sin x\cos x\)) and \(w=e^{-x^2}+1\) (so \(w'=-2x\,e^{-x^2}\)); then use \(\sin 2x=2\sin x\cos x\). \[ \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}\Big[e^{\sin^2 x}\big(e^{-x^2}+1\big)\Big]=e^{\sin^2 x}(2\sin x\cos x)\big(e^{-x^2}+1\big)+e^{\sin^2 x}\big(-2x\,e^{-x^2}\big) \] (1)(1) \[ =e^{\sin^2 x}\Big[e^{-x^2}(\sin 2x-2x)+\sin 2x\Big]. \] (1)
Covers: standard differentiation — simplify-before-quotient, multi-layer chain rule, laws of logarithms, product + chain.

Question 2 [8]
HCI Tutorial 5A Q2
Find and simplify the derivative with respect to \(x\) of each of the following functions. (a) \(\tan^{-1}\!\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\) (b) \(\sin^{-1}\sqrt{1-x^2}\) (c) \(\cos^{-1}\!\big(e^{-x^2}\big)\)
Before you reveal — say it out loud:  Confidence:  high  /  medium  /  guessed
Solution — verbatim HCI
(a)
MF26: \(\tfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm du}\tan^{-1}u=\tfrac{1}{1+u^2}\) with \(u=x^{-1/2}\), \(u'=-\tfrac12 x^{-3/2}\); remember to differentiate the inside. \[ \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}\tan^{-1}\!\frac{1}{\sqrt x}=\frac{1}{1+\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt x}\right)^2}\times\left(-\tfrac12 x^{-3/2}\right)=\frac{x}{x+1}\times\left(-\frac{1}{2x\sqrt x}\right)=-\frac{1}{2\sqrt x\,(x+1)}. \] (1)(1)
(b)
MF26: \(\tfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm du}\sin^{-1}u=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-u^2}}\) with \(u=\sqrt{1-x^2}\); note \(\sqrt{x^2}=|x|\), not \(x\). \[ \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}\sin^{-1}\sqrt{1-x^2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-(1-x^2)}}\times\frac{1}{2\sqrt{1-x^2}}\times(-2x)=-\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2}\,\sqrt{1-x^2}}=-\frac{x}{|x|\sqrt{1-x^2}}. \] (1)(1)
(c)
MF26: \(\tfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm du}\cos^{-1}u=-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-u^2}}\) with \(u=e^{-x^2}\), \(u'=-2x\,e^{-x^2}\); then rationalise with \((a^m)^n=a^{mn}\). \[ \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}\cos^{-1}\!\big(e^{-x^2}\big)=\frac{-1}{\sqrt{1-\left(e^{-x^2}\right)^2}}\times e^{-x^2}\times(-2x)=\frac{2x\,e^{-x^2}}{\sqrt{1-e^{-2x^2}}}\times\frac{e^{x^2}}{e^{x^2}}=\frac{2x}{\sqrt{e^{2x^2}-1}}. \] (1)(1)
Covers: differentiation of inverse trigonometric functions (MF26 standard forms + chain rule).

Question 3 [8]
A-Level 2010 P1 Q4
A curve has equation \(x^2-y^2+2xy+4=0\). (i) Find \(\dfrac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}\) in terms of \(x\) and \(y\). [4] (ii) Find the coordinates of each point on the curve at which the tangent is parallel to the \(x\)-axis. [4]
Before you reveal — say it out loud:  Confidence:  high  /  medium  /  guessed
Solution — verbatim HCI
(i) differentiate every term implicitly w.r.t. \(x\)
Treat \(y\) as a function of \(x\): \(\tfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}(y^2)=2y\,\tfrac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}\), and \(2xy\) needs the product rule \(=2\big(y+x\tfrac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}\big)\). \[ 2x-2y\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}+2x\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}+2y=0 \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}=\frac{x+y}{y-x}. \] (1)(1)(1)(1)
(ii) tangent parallel to \(x\)-axis means \(\dfrac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}=0\)
A zero gradient needs the numerator \(x+y=0\), i.e. \(y=-x\); substitute back into the curve. \[ \frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}=0\Rightarrow x+y=0\Rightarrow y=-x;\qquad x^2-x^2-2x^2+4=0\Rightarrow x^2=2\Rightarrow x=\pm\sqrt2. \] (1)(1) \[ \text{Points: }\ \big(\sqrt2,\,-\sqrt2\big)\ \text{and}\ \big(-\sqrt2,\,\sqrt2\big). \] (1)(1)
Covers: implicit differentiation; locating points with a horizontal tangent.

Question 4 [7]
A-Level N2011 P1 Q3 · HCI Tutorial 5B Q3
The parametric equations of a curve are \(x=t^2\) and \(y=\dfrac{2}{t}\). The point \(P\) on the curve has parameter \(p\), so \(P\) is \(\big(p^2,\tfrac{2}{p}\big)\). (i) Find the equation of the tangent to the curve at \(P\), simplifying your answer. [2] (ii) This tangent meets the \(x\)-axis at \(Q\) and the \(y\)-axis at \(R\). Find the coordinates of \(Q\) and of \(R\). [2] (iii) The point \(F\) is the midpoint of \(QR\). Find a cartesian equation of the curve traced by \(F\) as \(p\) varies. [3]
Before you reveal — say it out loud:  Confidence:  high  /  medium  /  guessed
Solution — verbatim HCI
(i) parametric gradient, then point-gradient form
\(\dfrac{\mathrm dx}{\mathrm dt}=2t\), \(\dfrac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dt}=-\dfrac{2}{t^2}\); \(\dfrac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}=\dfrac{\mathrm dy/\mathrm dt}{\mathrm dx/\mathrm dt}\). Use \(t=p\) at \(P\). \[ \frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}=\frac{-2/t^2}{2t}=-\frac{1}{t^3};\qquad y-\frac{2}{p}=-\frac{1}{p^3}\big(x-p^2\big)\;\Rightarrow\; y=-\frac{x}{p^3}+\frac{3}{p}. \] (1)(1)
(ii) set each variable to zero in turn
At \(R\) the tangent meets the \(y\)-axis, so \(x=0\); at \(Q\) it meets the \(x\)-axis, so \(y=0\). \[ x=0\Rightarrow y=\tfrac{3}{p}\Rightarrow R\big(0,\tfrac{3}{p}\big);\qquad y=0\Rightarrow \tfrac{x}{p^3}=\tfrac{3}{p}\Rightarrow x=3p^2\Rightarrow Q\big(3p^2,0\big). \] (1)(1)
(iii) midpoint, then eliminate the parameter \(p\)
Midpoint \(F=\big(\tfrac{3p^2+0}{2},\tfrac{0+3/p}{2}\big)=\big(\tfrac{3p^2}{2},\tfrac{3}{2p}\big)\); call these \(x\) and \(y\), then eliminate \(p\). \[ x=\frac{3p^2}{2}\ \ (1),\qquad y=\frac{3}{2p}\ \ (2)\Rightarrow p=\frac{3}{2y}\ \ (3). \] (1) \[ \text{Sub (3) into (1): } x=\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{3}{2y}\right)^2=\frac{27}{8y^2}\;\Rightarrow\; y^2=\frac{27}{8x}. \] (1)(1)
Covers: parametric differentiation; tangent to a parametric curve; locus by eliminating the parameter.

Question 5 [8]
A-Level 2019 P1 Q6
A curve \(C\) has equation \(y=xe^{-x}\). (i) Find the equations of the tangents to \(C\) at the points where \(x=1\) and where \(x=-1\). [6] (ii) Find the acute angle between these two tangents. [2]
Before you reveal — say it out loud:  Confidence:  high  /  medium  /  guessed
Solution — verbatim HCI
(i) product rule for the gradient function
\(y=xe^{-x}\) is a product: \(\dfrac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}=e^{-x}+x\big(-e^{-x}\big)=(1-x)e^{-x}\). Evaluate the point and the gradient at each \(x\). \[ \frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}=(1-x)e^{-x}. \] (1) \[ x=1:\ y=\tfrac1e,\ \tfrac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}=0\ \Rightarrow\ \text{tangent } y=\tfrac1e. \] (1)(1) \[ x=-1:\ y=-e,\ \tfrac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}=2e\ \Rightarrow\ y+e=2e(x+1)\ \Rightarrow\ y=2ex+e. \] (1)(1)(1)
(ii) angle from the gradient
One tangent is horizontal (gradient \(0\)); the other has gradient \(2e\), so the acute angle it makes with the horizontal is \(\tan^{-1}(2e)\). \[ \theta=\tan^{-1}(2e)=79.6^\circ\ \ (\text{1 d.p.}). \] (1)(1)
Covers: tangents to an exponential curve; product rule; angle between two lines.

Question 6 [5]
SAJC 2009 Promo Q4 · HCI Supplementary Q10
A right circular cone has base radius \(5\) cm and height \(15\) cm, and is initially full of water. Water leaks from the vertex of the cone at a rate of \(10\) cm\(^3\)s\(^{-1}\). Find the exact rate of change of the depth of water at the instant when the depth is \(12\) cm. [The volume of a cone is \(\tfrac13\times(\text{base area})\times(\text{height})\).]
Before you reveal — say it out loud:  Confidence:  high  /  medium  /  guessed
Solution — verbatim HCI
Name the variables and relate radius to depth
Let \(h\) cm be the depth of water and \(R\) cm the radius of the water surface at that depth. By similar triangles the empty cone above the water has height \(15-h\). \[ \frac{15-h}{15}=\frac{R}{5}\;\Rightarrow\;R=\frac{15-h}{3}. \] (1)
Volume of water, then differentiate w.r.t. \(h\)
\(V=\) (full cone) \(-\) (empty cone above the water), each by \(\tfrac13\pi r^2 h\). \[ V=\tfrac13\pi(5)^2(15)-\tfrac{1}{27}\pi(15-h)^3\;\Rightarrow\;\frac{\mathrm dV}{\mathrm dh}=\frac{\pi(15-h)^2}{9}. \] (1)
Chain the rates and substitute \(h=12\)
Water is leaking, so \(\dfrac{\mathrm dV}{\mathrm dt}=-10\). Use \(\dfrac{\mathrm dV}{\mathrm dt}=\dfrac{\mathrm dV}{\mathrm dh}\cdot\dfrac{\mathrm dh}{\mathrm dt}\). \[ -10=\frac{\pi(15-h)^2}{9}\,\frac{\mathrm dh}{\mathrm dt};\qquad h=12:\ -10=\pi\frac{\mathrm dh}{\mathrm dt}\;\Rightarrow\;\frac{\mathrm dh}{\mathrm dt}=-\frac{10}{\pi}\ \text{cm s}^{-1}. \] (1)(1)
Covers: connected rates of change; similar triangles; \(\tfrac{\mathrm dV}{\mathrm dt}=\tfrac{\mathrm dV}{\mathrm dh}\tfrac{\mathrm dh}{\mathrm dt}\).

A-Level 2019 P2 Q3
A solid cylinder has radius \(r\) cm, height \(h\) cm, and a fixed total surface area of \(900\) cm\(^2\). Find the exact value of the maximum possible volume of the cylinder, and find also the ratio \(r:h\) that gives this maximum volume.
Before you reveal — say it out loud:  Confidence:  high  /  medium  /  guessed
Solution — verbatim HCI
Use the surface-area constraint to make \(V\) a function of \(r\) alone
Total surface area \(=2\pi r^2+2\pi rh=900\); solve for \(h\), then substitute into \(V=\pi r^2 h\). \[ r^2+rh=\frac{450}{\pi}\Rightarrow h=\frac{450}{\pi r}-r;\qquad V=\pi r^2 h=450r-\pi r^3. \] (1)(1)
Stationary value of \(V\), confirmed a maximum
Set \(\dfrac{\mathrm dV}{\mathrm dr}=0\); check the sign of \(\dfrac{\mathrm d^2V}{\mathrm dr^2}\). \[ \frac{\mathrm dV}{\mathrm dr}=450-3\pi r^2=0\Rightarrow r=\sqrt{\tfrac{150}{\pi}};\qquad \frac{\mathrm d^2V}{\mathrm dr^2}=-6\pi r<0\ (\text{maximum}). \] (1)(1)
Maximum volume and the ratio \(r:h\)
Substitute \(r=\sqrt{150/\pi}\) back; then \(h=\dfrac{300}{\pi r}\) gives \(\dfrac{r}{h}=\dfrac{\pi r^2}{300}=\dfrac{150}{300}\). \[ V_{\max}=450\sqrt{\tfrac{150}{\pi}}-150\sqrt{\tfrac{150}{\pi}}=300\sqrt{\tfrac{150}{\pi}}\ \text{cm}^3;\qquad r:h=1:2. \] (1)(1)
Covers: maxima & minima (optimisation); stationary point; second-derivative test.

(1)

A-Level 2020 P1 Q6 · do not use a calculator
It is given that \(f(x)=2-\sin 4x\). (i) Find \(\displaystyle\int f(x)\,\mathrm dx\). [1] (ii) Find the exact value, in terms of \(\pi\), of \(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/2} x\,f(x)\,\mathrm dx\). [4] (iii) Find the exact value, in terms of \(\pi\), of \(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\big(f(x)\big)^2\,\mathrm dx\). [4]
Before you reveal — say it out loud:  Confidence:  high  /  medium  /  guessed
Solution — verbatim HCI
(i) integrate directly
\[ \int 2-\sin 4x\,\mathrm dx=2x+\tfrac14\cos 4x+C. \] (1)
(ii) integration by parts
Take \(u=x\) (so \(u'=1\)) and \(v'=2-\sin 4x\) (so \(v=2x+\tfrac14\cos 4x\), from part (i)). \[ \int_{0}^{\pi/2}\! x\,f(x)\,\mathrm dx=\Big[x\big(2x+\tfrac14\cos 4x\big)\Big]_{0}^{\pi/2}-\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\!\big(2x+\tfrac14\cos 4x\big)\,\mathrm dx \] (1)(1) \[ =\frac{\pi}{2}\Big(\pi+\tfrac14\Big)-\Big[x^2+\tfrac{1}{16}\sin 4x\Big]_{0}^{\pi/2}=\frac{\pi^2}{2}+\frac{\pi}{8}-\frac{\pi^2}{4}=\frac{\pi^2}{4}+\frac{\pi}{8}. \] (1)(1)
(iii) expand, then use the double-angle identity on \(\sin^2 4x\)
\((2-\sin 4x)^2=4-4\sin 4x+\sin^2 4x\); replace \(\sin^2 4x=\tfrac{1-\cos 8x}{2}\) so it can be integrated. \[ \int_{0}^{\pi/2}\!(2-\sin 4x)^2\,\mathrm dx=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\!\Big[4-4\sin 4x+\tfrac{1-\cos 8x}{2}\Big]\mathrm dx \] (1)(1) \[ =\Big[4x+\cos 4x+\tfrac12 x-\tfrac{1}{16}\sin 8x\Big]_{0}^{\pi/2}=2\pi+1+\tfrac{\pi}{4}-1=\frac{9\pi}{4}. \] (1)(1)
Covers: trigonometric integration (double-angle identity); integration by parts; \(\int(f)^2\).

Question 14 [5]
A-Level 2019 P1 Q2
The curve \(C\) has equation \(y=x^3+x-1\). (i) \(C\) crosses the \(x\)-axis at the point \((a,0)\). Find the value of \(a\) correct to 3 decimal places. [1] (ii) You are given \(b>a\). The region \(P\) is bounded by \(C\), the \(x\)-axis and the lines \(x=-1\) and \(x=0\). The region \(Q\) is bounded by \(C\), the line \(x=b\), and the part of the \(x\)-axis between \(x=a\) and \(x=b\). Given that the area of \(Q\) is \(2\) times the area of \(P\), find the value of \(b\) correct to 3 decimal places. [4]

Solution — verbatim HCI
(i) root from the GC
\[ a=0.6823278\ldots=0.682\ (\text{3 d.p.}). \] (1)
(ii) area of \(P\) — the curve is below the axis there, so negate
On \(-1\le x\le0\) the curve lies below the \(x\)-axis, so the area is \(-\int\). \[ \text{Area of }P=-\int_{-1}^{0}(x^3+x-1)\,\mathrm dx=1.75. \] (1)
Area of \(Q\), then set \(Q=2P\) and solve for \(b\)
On \(a\le x\le b\) the curve is above the axis; integrate from \(a=0.6823278\) to \(b\). \[ \text{Area of }Q=\int_{a}^{b}(x^3+x-1)\,\mathrm dx=\Big[\tfrac{x^4}{4}+\tfrac{x^2}{2}-x\Big]_{0.6823278}^{b}=\tfrac{b^4}{4}+\tfrac{b^2}{2}-b+0.395353. \] (1) \[ Q=2P:\ \tfrac{b^4}{4}+\tfrac{b^2}{2}-b+0.395353=3.5\Rightarrow \tfrac{b^4}{4}+\tfrac{b^2}{2}-b=3.104647. \] (1) \[ \text{By GC, } b=1.892\ (\text{3 d.p.})\quad(b=-1.353\ \text{rejected since } b>a). \] (1)
Covers: area under a curve (regions above and below the axis); solving an area equation numerically.

Question 15 [6]
A-Level J84 P1 Q7 · HCI Tutorial 6B Q2
\(R\) is the region enclosed by the curves \(y=x^2\) and \(y=2-x^2\). (i) Calculate the area of \(R\). [3] (ii) Calculate the volume of the solid generated when \(R\) is rotated through \(\pi\) radians about the \(y\)-axis. [3]
Before you reveal — say it out loud:  Confidence:  high  /  medium  /  guessed
Solution — verbatim HCI
(i) intersection, then top minus bottom
Curves meet where \(x^2=2-x^2\Rightarrow x=\pm1\). On \(-1\le x\le1\) the upper curve is \(y=2-x^2\). \[ \text{Area}=\int_{-1}^{1}\big[(2-x^2)-x^2\big]\,\mathrm dx=\Big[2x-\tfrac{2x^3}{3}\Big]_{-1}^{1}=\frac{8}{3}\ \text{units}^2. \] (1)(1)(1)
(ii) rotate about the \(y\)-axis — use the symmetry short-cut and make \(x^2\) the subject
\(R\) is symmetric about the \(y\)-axis, so rotating it through \(\pi\) equals rotating one side through \(2\pi\): \(V=\pi\int x^2\,\mathrm dy\). Lower curve \(y=x^2\Rightarrow x^2=y\) for \(0\le y\le1\); upper curve \(y=2-x^2\Rightarrow x^2=2-y\) for \(1\le y\le2\). \[ V=\pi\!\left[\int_{0}^{1} y\,\mathrm dy+\int_{1}^{2}(2-y)\,\mathrm dy\right]=\pi\Big[\tfrac{y^2}{2}\Big]_{0}^{1}+\pi\Big[2y-\tfrac{y^2}{2}\Big]_{1}^{2}=\pi\ \text{units}^3. \] (1)(1)(1)
Covers: area between two curves; volume of revolution about the \(y\)-axis (make \(x^2\) the subject); symmetry short-cut.
