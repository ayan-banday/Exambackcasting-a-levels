Question 1 [6 marks]

(a) Wild-type freshwater snails, Physa heterostropha, have pigmented shells. Two pure-breeding albino snails were crossed and their F1 was pigmented.

State what this result rules out about the two albino parents, and name the type of gene interaction it points to. [2]

Confidence: high / medium / guessed
Answer - part (a)
How it is markedAny two.

Two pure-breeding albino parents are each homozygous, and their F1 is pigmented, so the two parents cannot be homozygous recessive for the same gene; if they were, every offspring would be albino as well.

The two albino lines must therefore be homozygous recessive at two different gene loci, and the pigmented F1 carries at least one dominant allele of each.

This is a gene interaction, that is, epistasis, in which one characteristic is controlled by two genes.

Gap note. This part is here because you defined gene interaction as suppression at 0:52:27. Suppression is epistasis, which is the special case. Gene interaction is the broader idea: two genes contributing to ONE character, as against a dihybrid where each gene controls its own character. That framing is what makes epistasis make sense.

(b) In Labradors, the TYRP1 gene has alleles B, coding for an enzyme that produces melanin giving black fur, and b, coding for an enzyme that produces a brown melanin giving chocolate fur. A second gene MC1R has alleles E, which allows the alleles of TYRP1 to be expressed, and e, which prevents them from being expressed. A dog with no melanin is yellow.

Explain, in terms of enzymes, why a dog of genotype B B e e is yellow. [1]

Confidence: high / medium / guessed
Answer - part (b)

A homozygous ee dog makes no functional product at the MC1R step, so the TYRP1 enzyme is not expressed, no melanin of either kind is deposited, and the dog is yellow whatever its TYRP1 genotype is.

A scheme in the bank prints penalise once if no reference to enzyme. You did name the enzyme unprompted in the Book 6 sitting, this part is here to keep that habit, not to correct it.

(c) Crossing two heterozygotes for both genes typically gives 9 black : 3 brown : 4 yellow.

Explain how the phenotypic ratio will differ if genes B/b and E/e are located on the same homologous pair of chromosomes, tightly linked, with the dominant alleles of both genes on one chromosome and the recessive alleles of both on its homologue. [3]

Confidence: high / medium / guessed
Answer - part (c)

3 black : 1 yellow.

Tightly linked, hence no crossing over, or no chiasma is formed between gene B/b and gene E/e.

The two genes are inherited as one unit.

Read the two ratios together, because that is why they are set side by side. Unlinked, the double heterozygote makes four gamete types and the classes merge into 9 : 3 : 4. Completely linked with both dominants on one chromosome, it makes only the two parental gametes BE and be, so it behaves like a single gene: 3 B\_E\_ black : 1 bbee yellow, with no brown class at all, because the genotype that would produce brown, bbE\_, is never formed. The class disappears rather than shrinking.

Write the merged class with its components in brackets, which is the school's own form and the notation you said you had never seen: 9 B\_E\_ : 3 bbE\_ : 4 (3 B\_ee + 1 bbee).

Question 2 [6 marks]

Following self-pollination, an isolated maize plant produced a total of 380 grains with four different phenotypes. The expected phenotypic ratio was 9 purple, smooth : 3 purple, shrunken : 3 yellow, smooth : 1 yellow, shrunken. The observed numbers were purple smooth 216, purple shrunken 78, yellow smooth 55, yellow shrunken 31.

A chi-squared test was carried out. The values of (O − E)² / E for the two purple classes have already been worked out as 0.024 and 0.639.

(a) Calculate the expected number (E) for each of the four phenotypes. [1]

Confidence: high / medium / guessed
Answer - part (a)

The total of 380 is divided in the ratio 9 : 3 : 3 : 1, that is, into 16 parts of 23.75 each.

Purple, smooth = 380 × 9/16 = 213.75. Purple, shrunken = 380 × 3/16 = 71.25. Yellow, smooth = 380 × 3/16 = 71.25. Yellow, shrunken = 380 × 1/16 = 23.75.

Work the fraction out, do not estimate it. A candidate who writes "about 216" for the first class corrupts every downstream cell. This is the step you could not resolve aloud at 1:21:25.

(b) Calculate the two missing values of (O − E)² / E, for yellow smooth grains and yellow shrunken grains. [1]

Confidence: high / medium / guessed
Answer - part (b)

Yellow, smooth: (55 − 71.25)² ÷ 71.25 = 264.06 ÷ 71.25 = 3.71.

Yellow, shrunken: (31 − 23.75)² ÷ 23.75 = 52.56 ÷ 23.75 = 2.21.

(c) Calculate the chi-squared value. [1]

Confidence: high / medium / guessed
Answer - part (c)

χ² = 0.024 + 0.639 + 3.71 + 2.21 = 6.58, accept 6.58 to 6.59.

(d) The critical values of chi-squared at a probability of 0.05 are 3.84 for 1 degree of freedom, 5.99 for 2, 7.81 for 3 and 9.49 for 4. The number of degrees of freedom is given by v = c − 1, where v is the degrees of freedom and c is the number of classes.

Use your calculated chi-squared value to conclude whether the observed number of each phenotype is significantly different to the expected number. Explain your conclusion. [2]

Confidence: high / medium / guessed
Answer - part (d)

There are four phenotypic classes, so c = 4 and v = c − 1 = 3. The critical value is quoted with its degrees of freedom attached: χ²crit, v = 3, α = 0.05 = 7.81.

The calculated χ² of 6.58 is smaller than the critical value of 7.81, so the probability that chance alone is the reason for the difference between observed and expected results is more than 5%. The deviation is not significant, hence do not reject H0.

The observed results do fit the expected 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 ratio, so the two genes are inherited independently and are not linked.

Two disciplines the schemes enforce. Never write "accept H0", every scheme in the bank words the negative branch do not reject H0. And "due to chance" on its own is rejected, so the full probability sentence is compulsory.

The collision behind it: your notes write df = n − 1 with n meaning classes, while the syllabus and the specimen print v = c − 1 with c = classes and n = sample size. Question 5 [8 marks]

(a) The diagram of phagocytosis of a pathogen by a neutrophil labels four features: P, the surface molecules on the pathogen recognised by the neutrophil; Q, the arm-like membrane extensions engulfing the pathogen; R, the membrane-bound organelle containing hydrolytic enzymes that fuses with the engulfed vesicle; and S, the vesicle containing the engulfed pathogen.

Which row shows the correct identification of the structures and function? [1]

AP antibiotic, Q fimbriae, R lysozyme, S lysosome; innate immunity.
BP antibiotic, Q pseudopodia, R Golgi vesicles, S phagosome; cell-mediated immunity.
CP antigen, Q fimbriae, R Golgi vesicles, S lysozyme; cell-mediated immunity.
DP antigen, Q pseudopodia, R lysosome, S phagosome; innate immunity.
Confidence: high / medium / guessed
Answer - part (a)

The answer is D.

P is an antigen, not an antibiotic, an antigen is any substance recognised by the immune system, while an antibiotic is a substance produced by some microorganisms that kills or inhibits others. That rules out A and B.

Q is a pseudopodium, an extension of the cell surface membrane. Neutrophils are animal cells with no cell wall, and fimbriae are bacterial adherence structures. That rules out A and C.

R is the lysosome, the membrane-bound organelle containing hydrolytic enzymes. Golgi vesicles are secretory, and lysozyme is an enzyme, not an organelle, so it cannot label a membrane-bound structure. That rules out B and C.

S is the phagosome, the vesicle containing the engulfed pathogen. After fusion with the lysosome it becomes the phagolysosome.

The line of defence is innate immunity, because phagocytosis by a neutrophil is second line and non-specific.

Explain how somatic recombination during B cell development results in the formation of millions of different antibody molecules. [3]

Confidence: high / medium / guessed

(a) The immune response involves different cells and molecules. To ensure a coordinated response, cells and molecules communicate with one another regularly.

Explain how cell signalling facilitates this communication. [3]

Confidence: high / medium / guessed
Answer - part (a)
How it is markedAny three.

Ligands are small molecules that are transported via the bloodstream to various organs and cells. Examples specific to the immune system are cytokines and interferons.

Cell surface receptors on immune cells have ligand-binding sites whose shapes are complementary to the shape of the ligand.

Upon ligand binding, relay proteins in the cell transduce the signal to effector proteins, by a phosphorylation cascade or second messengers.

Different types of cell give different cellular responses to the same ligand, because each cell contains different proteins.

Gap note. You asked at 0:10:20 whether interferon is examinable. It is, LO (a) names interferons as the antiviral cytokine. The chain: a virus-infected cell secretes interferon → it diffuses to neighbouring uninfected cells → binds their receptors → induces an antiviral state, so those cells express genes for antiviral proteins that inhibit viral protein synthesis. The neighbours are protected before they are infected, which is the point of it. In innate responses cytokines are produced by macrophages and natural killer cells.

Question 8 [4 marks]

(a) The basic reproduction number, R₀, of a disease depends on a number of factors. Which conditions are expected to lead to an increase in the R₀ number of viral dengue disease? [1]

Amigration of people from where the disease is frequent to an area that the disease does not occur
Bincreased travel between countries in which viral dengue disease is well established
Cincreased rainfall in an area where the disease is always present but occurs at a low frequency
Dan increase in the incidence of the disease in a population where outbreaks of the disease are frequent
Confidence: high / medium / guessed
Answer - part (a)

The answer is C.

R₀ is the mean number of secondary cases produced by one infected individual introduced into a wholly susceptible population, so it rises when transmission becomes easier. Dengue is transmitted by the Aedes mosquito vector, and increased rainfall leaves more standing water for the mosquitoes to breed in, so vector numbers rise and each infected person infects more people.

A is wrong, the new area has no established vector population, so infected arrivals produce few or no secondary cases.

B is wrong, in countries where the disease is already well established the population has already been exposed, so extra travel between them does not raise R₀.

D is wrong, in a population with frequent outbreaks a large proportion is already immune, which lowers the number of susceptible contacts and so lowers the effective reproduction number. Incidence is not the same quantity as transmissibility.

(b) Explain the meaning of the basic reproduction number, R₀, as an indicator of the transmissibility of an infectious agent. [1]

Confidence: high / medium / guessed
Answer - part (b)

R₀ is the mean number of secondary cases produced by one infected individual introduced into a wholly susceptible population.

The higher the R₀, the more transmissible or contagious the infectious agent, because each primary case generates more new cases.

Gap note. From 1:02:25 you circled this three times and landed on fragments each time. Three parts of it carry the meaning: it is a mean, not a fixed count; it counts secondary cases from one primary case; and it assumes everybody else is susceptible, which is what makes R₀ a property of the pathogen rather than of the outbreak.

The three regimes, which you did have: R₀ > 1 case numbers grow and the outbreak spreads; R₀ = 1 numbers stay steady and the disease is endemic; R₀ < 1 case numbers fall and the outbreak dies out. You asked "what's endemic?" at 1:03:40, that is it, constantly present at a stable level.

And the four terms scaled by extent, which your notes do not teach: outbreak, a sudden rise above what is expected in a limited area; epidemic, a rapid rise across a region or country; pandemic, an epidemic across multiple countries or continents, with influenza as the syllabus's own named example.

(c) The proportion of a population that must be immune before the transmission cycle of a disease is broken is given by 1 − 1/R₀. Measles has an R₀ of 15.0 and smallpox an R₀ of 5.0.

Calculate the percentage of the population that must be immune to break the transmission cycle of measles and of smallpox. Show your working. [2]

Confidence: high / medium / guessed
Answer - part (c)

Measles: 1 − 1/15.0 = 1 − 0.0667 = 0.933, so 93.3% (accept 93%).

Smallpox: 1 − 1/5.0 = 1 − 0.200 = 0.800, so 80.0% (accept 80%).

Gap note. At 1:04:08 you met 1 − 1/R₀ for the first time and said so. The syllabus lists "the use of R₀ value" under MATHEMATICAL REQUIREMENTS, beside Hardy-Weinberg and the statistical tests, so this is an instruction to compute with it, not only to explain it.

Three more worth having ready because they make the relationship intuitive: at R₀ = 2, half the population must be immune; at R₀ = 4, three quarters; at R₀ = 10, ninety per cent. And the consequence: smallpox needed about 80% and was eradicated; measles needs about 93%, which is a far harder target to reach and hold, which is why it has not been.
