A curve has equation \(\sin^{-1}y+xe^{y}=3x\). Find \(\dfrac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}\) in terms of \(x\) and \(y\). [3]
CJC 2009 Promo Q10 · HCI Differentiation Supplementary Exercise Q1(b)
Confidence: high / medium / guessed
differentiate term by term, keeping the chain factor on every \(y\)
Differentiate every term with respect to \(x\). From MF26, \(\dfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}\left(\sin^{-1}y\right)=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-y^{2}}}\dfrac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}\), and \(xe^{y}\) needs the product rule with \(\dfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}\left(e^{y}\right)=e^{y}\dfrac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}\). \[ \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-y^{2}}}\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}+xe^{y}\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}+e^{y}=3 \] M1 \[ \frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}+\sqrt{1-y^{2}}\,xe^{y}\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}=\left(3-e^{y}\right)\sqrt{1-y^{2}} \] M1 \[ \boxed{\ \frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}=\frac{\left(3-e^{y}\right)\sqrt{1-y^{2}}}{1+xe^{y}\sqrt{1-y^{2}}}\ } \] A1
Two separate slips live in this one line. Forgetting the \(\dfrac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}\) factor after differentiating \(\sin^{-1}y\) or \(e^{y}\) kills the M1; treating \(xe^{y}\) as a single chain-rule object instead of a product kills the other. Every time the variable being differentiated is \(y\), a \(\dfrac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}\) must come out with it.

(a) work in the parameter, then convert to coordinates
Meeting the \(x\)-axis means \(y=0\); the maximum of \(y\) is where \(y\) is largest, and \(y=1-\cos t\) is largest when \(\cos t\) is smallest. \[ y=0\ \Longrightarrow\ 1-\cos t=0\ \Longrightarrow\ \cos t=1\ \Longrightarrow\ t=0\ \text{ or }\ t=2\pi \] M1 \[ t=0:\ x=0-\cos 0=-1;\qquad t=2\pi:\ x=2\pi-\cos 2\pi=2\pi-1 \] \[ \text{\(x\)-axis crossings: }(-1,\,0)\ \text{ and }\ (2\pi-1,\,0) \] A1 \[ y\text{ maximum}\ \Longrightarrow\ \cos t=-1\ \Longrightarrow\ t=\pi\ \Longrightarrow\ y=2,\quad x=\pi-(-1)=\pi+1 \] \[ \text{Maximum point: }(\pi+1,\,2) \] A1
One arch, symmetric about \(t=\pi\), rising from \((-1,0)\) to \((\pi+1,2)\) and back down to \((2\pi-1,0)\).
A1 for a correct single arch with both endpoints and the maximum labelled.

[It is given that the curved surface area and volume of a right circular cone with radius \(r\), slant height \(l\) and height \(h\) are \(\pi rl\) and \(\tfrac13\pi r^{2}h\) respectively.]

The Sunny Island Art Museum is organising a Clay Sculpting Workshop where participants make a closed right circular cone with radius \(r\) cm, slant height \(l\) cm, height \(h\) cm and a semi-vertical angle of \(\tfrac{\pi}{6}\) radians.

[5]

The semi-vertical angle fixes the shape: \(\tan\tfrac{\pi}{6}=\dfrac{r}{h}\) and \(\sin\tfrac{\pi}{6}=\dfrac{r}{l}\). \[ \frac{1}{\sqrt3}=\frac{r}{h}\ \Longrightarrow\ r=\frac{h}{\sqrt3};\qquad \frac12=\frac{r}{l}\ \Longrightarrow\ l=2r=\frac{2h}{\sqrt3} \] B1 \[ V=\tfrac13\pi r^{2}h=\tfrac13\pi\left(\frac{h}{\sqrt3}\right)^{2}h=\frac{1}{9}\pi h^{3}\ \Longrightarrow\ \frac{\mathrm dV}{\mathrm dh}=\tfrac13\pi h^{2} \] A1 \[ A=\pi r^{2}+\pi rl=\pi\left(\frac{h}{\sqrt3}\right)^{2}+\pi\left(\frac{h}{\sqrt3}\right)\left(\frac{2h}{\sqrt3}\right)=\frac{\pi h^{2}}{3}+\frac{2\pi h^{2}}{3}=\pi h^{2}\ \Longrightarrow\ \frac{\mathrm dA}{\mathrm dh}=2\pi h \] A1 "Closed" cone, so the total surface area includes the circular base as well as the curved surface. \(\alpha=\dfrac{\mathrm dA}{\mathrm dt}\) and \(\beta=\dfrac{\mathrm dV}{\mathrm dt}\), and \(\alpha:\beta=3:7\) means \(7\alpha=3\beta\). \[ 7\frac{\mathrm dA}{\mathrm dt}=3\frac{\mathrm dV}{\mathrm dt}\ \Longrightarrow\ 7\frac{\mathrm dA}{\mathrm dh}\frac{\mathrm dh}{\mathrm dt}=3\frac{\mathrm dV}{\mathrm dh}\frac{\mathrm dh}{\mathrm dt} \] M1 \[ 7(2\pi h)=3\left(\tfrac13\pi h^{2}\right)\ \Longrightarrow\ 14\pi h=\pi h^{2}\ \Longrightarrow\ h=14\ \ (\text{since }h>0)\quad\text{(shown)} \] A1

4   Integration techniques and a volume of revolutionTotal: 15
(a) Find \(\displaystyle\int\frac{x^{3}+x^{2}-x+5}{x^{2}+x-2}\,\mathrm dx\). [4]
HCI Integration Tutorial 6A Q3(b)
Confidence: high / medium / guessed
(a)
Degree of numerator \(=3\), degree of denominator \(=2\). The fraction is improper, so long division comes first — there is no partial-fraction split until it is done. \[ \frac{x^{3}+x^{2}-x+5}{x^{2}+x-2}=x+\frac{x+5}{x^{2}+x-2}=x+\frac{x+5}{(x+2)(x-1)} \] M1 \[ \frac{x+5}{(x+2)(x-1)}=\frac{A}{x+2}+\frac{B}{x-1}\ \Longrightarrow\ A(x-1)+B(x+2)=x+5 \] \[ x=1:\ 3B=6\Rightarrow B=2;\qquad x=-2:\ -3A=3\Rightarrow A=-1 \] A1 \[ \int\frac{x^{3}+x^{2}-x+5}{x^{2}+x-2}\,\mathrm dx=\int x-\frac{1}{x+2}+\frac{2}{x-1}\,\mathrm dx \] M1 \[ =\boxed{\ \tfrac12x^{2}-\ln|x+2|+2\ln|x-1|+C\ } \] A1
Fourth appearance of this trap. Check the two degrees before you do anything else with an algebraic fraction — splitting, integrating or expanding. Numerator degree \(\ge\) denominator degree means divide first, every time. Note also that both logarithms keep modulus signs, because \(x+2\) and \(x-1\) each change sign.

5   A region bounded by two curvesTotal: 6

The region \(R\) is bounded by the curves \(y=\sqrt{x}\), \(y=\sqrt{2-x}\) and the \(x\)-axis.

(a) Find the exact value of the area of \(R\). [3]
CJC 2007 C2 Block Test 1 Q9(i) · HCI Integration Supplementary (Applications) Q4
Confidence: high / medium / guessed
(a) find where the curves cross, then split the region at that value of \(x\)
\[ \sqrt x=\sqrt{2-x}\ \Longrightarrow\ x=2-x\ \Longrightarrow\ x=1 \] B1 To the left of \(x=1\) the upper boundary is \(y=\sqrt x\); to the right of it the upper boundary is \(y=\sqrt{2-x}\), which reaches the \(x\)-axis at \(x=2\). The two pieces are mirror images in \(x=1\), so the second integral equals the first. \[ \text{Area}=\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt x\,\mathrm dx+\int_{1}^{2}\sqrt{2-x}\,\mathrm dx=2\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt x\,\mathrm dx \] M1 \[ =2\left[\frac{x^{3/2}}{\tfrac32}\right]_{0}^{1}=2\left(\tfrac23\right)=\boxed{\ \tfrac43\ \text{units}^{2}\ } \] A1
(b) Find the exact value of the volume of the solid formed when \(R\) is rotated through \(2\pi\) radians about the \(y\)-axis. [3]
CJC 2007 C2 Block Test 1 Q9(iii) · HCI Integration Supplementary (Applications) Q4
Confidence: high / medium / guessed
(b) rotating about \(y\) means \(\pi\displaystyle\int x^{2}\,\mathrm dy\), so make \(x\) the subject of both curves
\(y=\sqrt x\Rightarrow x=y^{2}\) (the left boundary) and \(y=\sqrt{2-x}\Rightarrow x=2-y^{2}\) (the right boundary). The two curves meet at \(x=1,\ y=1\), and the region runs from \(y=0\) up to \(y=1\). The solid is the outer rotation minus the inner rotation. \[ V=\pi\int_{0}^{1}\left(2-y^{2}\right)^{2}\mathrm dy-\pi\int_{0}^{1}\left(y^{2}\right)^{2}\mathrm dy \] M1 \[ =\pi\int_{0}^{1}\left(4-4y^{2}+y^{4}\right)-y^{4}\,\mathrm dy=\pi\int_{0}^{1}4-4y^{2}\,\mathrm dy \] A1 \[ =4\pi\left[y-\frac{y^{3}}{3}\right]_{0}^{1}=4\pi\left(1-\tfrac13\right)=\boxed{\ \tfrac{8\pi}{3}\ \text{units}^{3}\ } \] A1
The \(y^{4}\) terms cancel — that is the whole point of the question. Anyone who rotates the two pieces separately and adds, instead of subtracting inner from outer, ends up integrating \(y^4\) twice and gets neither mark. For comparison, rotating the same \(R\) about the \(x\)-axis gives \(\pi\), a completely different number.
