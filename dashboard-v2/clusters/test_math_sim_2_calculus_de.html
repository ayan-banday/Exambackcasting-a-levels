HCI 2023 Prelim Paper 1 Q3
A curve \(C\) has equation \(x^2y+y^4=5\).
(a) Find \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) in terms of \(x\) and \(y\). [2]
Before you reveal — say it out loud: confidence: high / medium / guessed
Differentiate implicitly with respect to \(x\), using the product rule on \(x^2y\):
\(2xy+x^2\dfrac{dy}{dx}+4y^3\dfrac{dy}{dx}=0\)
\(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\left(x^2+4y^3\right)=-2xy\)
\(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=-\dfrac{2xy}{x^2+4y^3}\)
(b) Find the exact distance between the two tangents to \(C\) that are parallel to the \(x\)-axis. [3]
Before you reveal — say it out loud: confidence: high / medium / guessed
Tangents parallel to the \(x\)-axis need \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=0\), i.e. \(-2xy=0\), so \(x=0\) or \(y=0\).

If \(y=0\): the curve gives \(0+0=5\), impossible. Reject.
If \(x=0\): \(y^4=5\Rightarrow y=\pm 5^{1/4}\).

So the two horizontal tangents are \(y=5^{1/4}\) and \(y=-5^{1/4}\).
Distance \(=2\sqrt[4]{5}\) (\(\approx 2.99\)).
Rejecting \(y=0\) is a real mark — setting a numerator to zero always produces candidates that the curve itself may not admit.

HCI 2025 Prelim Paper 1 Q9
(a) Find \(\displaystyle\int(2x-1)\cos x\,\mathrm dx\). [3]
Before you reveal — say it out loud: confidence: high / medium / guessed
Integration by parts with \(u=2x-1\), \(\dfrac{dv}{dx}=\cos x\):
\(\displaystyle\int(2x-1)\cos x\,\mathrm dx=(2x-1)\sin x-\int 2\sin x\,\mathrm dx\)
\(=(2x-1)\sin x+2\cos x+C\)
Check by differentiating: \(2\sin x+(2x-1)\cos x-2\sin x=(2x-1)\cos x\) ✓.
(b) Hence find the exact value of \(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\big|2x-1\big|\cos x\,\mathrm dx\), giving your answer in the form \(A-4\cos B\) where \(A\) and \(B\) are exact constants. [4]
Before you reveal — say it out loud: confidence: high / medium / guessed
The modulus changes sign at \(2x-1=0\), i.e. \(x=\tfrac12\), which lies inside \(\left[0,\tfrac\pi2\right]\). Split there.

On \(\left[0,\tfrac12\right]\), \(2x-1\le0\) so \(|2x-1|=-(2x-1)\).
On \(\left[\tfrac12,\tfrac\pi2\right]\), \(2x-1\ge0\) so \(|2x-1|=2x-1\).

Let \(F(x)=(2x-1)\sin x+2\cos x\).
\(F(0)=-0+2=2\); \(F\!\left(\tfrac12\right)=0+2\cos\tfrac12=2\cos\tfrac12\); \(F\!\left(\tfrac\pi2\right)=(\pi-1)(1)+0=\pi-1\).

\(\displaystyle\int_0^{1/2}-(2x-1)\cos x\,\mathrm dx=-\left[F\!\left(\tfrac12\right)-F(0)\right]=-\left(2\cos\tfrac12-2\right)=2-2\cos\tfrac12\)
\(\displaystyle\int_{1/2}^{\pi/2}(2x-1)\cos x\,\mathrm dx=F\!\left(\tfrac\pi2\right)-F\!\left(\tfrac12\right)=(\pi-1)-2\cos\tfrac12\)

Total \(=2-2\cos\tfrac12+\pi-1-2\cos\tfrac12=\boxed{\pi+1-4\cos\tfrac12}\)
So \(A=\pi+1\), \(B=\tfrac12\). Verified symbolically.
The whole question is the split. Integrating straight through gives \(\pi-1\) and scores almost nothing.

HCI 2025 Prelim Paper 1 Q10
(a) Express \(\dfrac{1}{Q(500-Q)}\) in partial fractions. [2]
Before you reveal — say it out loud: confidence: high / medium / guessed
\(\dfrac{1}{Q(500-Q)}=\dfrac{A}{Q}+\dfrac{B}{500-Q}\Rightarrow 1=A(500-Q)+BQ\).
\(Q=0\): \(1=500A\Rightarrow A=\dfrac{1}{500}\).   \(Q=500\): \(1=500B\Rightarrow B=\dfrac{1}{500}\).
\(\dfrac{1}{Q(500-Q)}=\dfrac{1}{500}\left(\dfrac1Q+\dfrac{1}{500-Q}\right)\)
(b) At time \(t\) years the bird population on an island is \(Q\). The birth rate is proportional to \(Q\) and the death rate is proportional to \(Q^2\). There were 1000 birds initially, and the population stays unchanged when there are 500 birds. Show that \(\dfrac{\mathrm dQ}{\mathrm dt}=kQ(500-Q)\) for a positive constant \(k\). [2]
Before you reveal — say it out loud: confidence: high / medium / guessed
Increase rate \(=aQ\) and decrease rate \(=bQ^2\) for positive constants \(a,b\).
\(\dfrac{\mathrm dQ}{\mathrm dt}=aQ-bQ^2\).

The population is unchanged at \(Q=500\), so \(\dfrac{\mathrm dQ}{\mathrm dt}=0\) there:
\(500a-250000b=0\Rightarrow a=500b\).

Substituting: \(\dfrac{\mathrm dQ}{\mathrm dt}=500bQ-bQ^2=bQ(500-Q)\).
So \(\dfrac{\mathrm dQ}{\mathrm dt}=kQ(500-Q)\) with \(k=b>0\). Shown.
(c) Hence solve the differential equation, expressing \(Q\) in terms of \(k\) and \(t\). [5]
Before you reveal — say it out loud: confidence: high / medium / guessed
Separate: \(\displaystyle\int\frac{1}{Q(500-Q)}\,\mathrm dQ=\int k\,\mathrm dt\).
Using (a): \(\dfrac{1}{500}\displaystyle\int\left(\frac1Q+\frac{1}{500-Q}\right)\mathrm dQ=kt+C\)
\(\dfrac{1}{500}\Big[\ln|Q|-\ln|500-Q|\Big]=kt+C\)
\(\ln\left|\dfrac{Q}{500-Q}\right|=500kt+C'\)
\(\dfrac{Q}{500-Q}=Ae^{500kt}\).

Initial condition \(Q=1000\) at \(t=0\): \(\dfrac{1000}{500-1000}=\dfrac{1000}{-500}=-2=A\).
So \(\dfrac{Q}{500-Q}=-2e^{500kt}\Rightarrow Q=-2e^{500kt}(500-Q)\Rightarrow Q\left(1-2e^{500kt}\right)=-1000e^{500kt}\)
\(Q=\dfrac{1000e^{500kt}}{2e^{500kt}-1}\)

Checks: at \(t=0\), \(Q=\dfrac{1000}{1}=1000\) ✓. As \(t\to\infty\), \(Q\to\dfrac{1000}{2}=500\) ✓ — the population falls from 1000 towards the equilibrium 500, which is what starting above equilibrium should do. Both verified symbolically.
The constant \(A\) came out negative because \(Q>500\); dropping the modulus without thinking is the standard way to lose this.
