HCI 2022 Prelim Paper 1 Q1
Do not use a calculator in this question.
(a) Solve algebraically the inequality \(\dfrac{x-1}{x-2}\ \ge\ \dfrac{x-2}{x-1}\). [3]
Before you reveal — say it out loud: confidence: high / medium / guessed
\(\dfrac{x-1}{x-2}-\dfrac{x-2}{x-1}\ge0\Rightarrow\dfrac{(x-1)^2-(x-2)^2}{(x-2)(x-1)}\ge0\).
Difference of squares: \((x-1)^2-(x-2)^2=\big[(x-1)-(x-2)\big]\big[(x-1)+(x-2)\big]=2x-3\).
So \(\dfrac{2x-3}{(x-2)(x-1)}\ge0\); critical values \(1,\tfrac32,2\); sign diagram \(-\,+\,-\,+\).
\(12\).
(b) Given \(\mathrm{f}(x)=\dfrac{x-1}{x-2}\), state the value of \(a\) such that \(\mathrm{f}(x-a)=\dfrac{2x-3}{2x-5}\). [1]
Before you reveal — say it out loud: confidence: high / medium / guessed
\(\mathrm{f}(x)=1+\dfrac{1}{x-2}\), so \(\mathrm{f}(x-a)=1+\dfrac{1}{x-a-2}\). Also \(\dfrac{2x-3}{2x-5}=1+\dfrac{2}{2x-5}=1+\dfrac{1}{x-\frac52}\).
Matching: \(a+2=\tfrac52\), so \(a=\tfrac12\).
(c) Hence solve \(\dfrac{2x-3}{2x-5}\ \ge\ \dfrac{2x-5}{2x-3}\). [2]
Before you reveal — say it out loud: confidence: high / medium / guessed
Replace \(x\) by \(x-\tfrac12\) in the answer to (a):
\(12\Rightarrow x>\tfrac52\).
\(\tfrac32\tfrac52\).

HCI 2023 Prelim Paper 1 Q5
A curve \(G\) with equation \(y=\mathrm{f}(3x-2)+a\), where \(a\) is a positive real constant, is transformed onto the curve \(y=\mathrm{f}(x)\) by a sequence of transformations.
(a) Describe fully, in terms of \(a\), the sequence of transformations. [3]
Before you reveal — say it out loud: confidence: high / medium / guessed
Going from \(G\) back to \(\mathrm{f}\), every step reverses and the order flips.
1. Translate \(a\) units in the negative \(y\)-direction → \(y=\mathrm{f}(3x-2)\).
2. Stretch parallel to the \(x\)-axis with scale factor 3 → \(y=\mathrm{f}(x-2)\).
3. Translate 2 units in the negative \(x\)-direction → \(y=\mathrm{f}(x)\).

Trace a point to be sure. Take \(\mathrm{f}(x)=x^2\), so \(G:\ y=(3x-2)^2+a\) passes through \(\left(\tfrac23,a\right)\).
Step 1 → \(\left(\tfrac23,0\right)\).   Step 2 (stretch \(\times3\)) → \((2,0)\) on \(y=(x-2)^2\).   Step 3 (2 units left) → \((0,0)\) on \(y=x^2\) ✓.
(b) A point on \(G\) with coordinates \((b,0)\) is mapped onto the point \((c,d)\) on the curve \(y=\dfrac{1}{\mathrm{f}(x)}\). Find \(c\) and \(d\) in terms of \(a\) and \(b\). [4]
Before you reveal — say it out loud: confidence: high / medium / guessed
\((b,0)\) on \(G\) means \(\mathrm{f}(3b-2)+a=0\), so \(\mathrm{f}(3b-2)=-a\).
Write \(X=3b-2\); then \(\mathrm{f}(X)=-a\), so the point \((X,-a)\) lies on \(y=\mathrm{f}(x)\).
On \(y=\dfrac{1}{\mathrm{f}(x)}\) the \(x\)-coordinate is unchanged and the \(y\)-coordinate is the reciprocal:
\(c=3b-2\) and \(d=-\dfrac1a\).
\(a>0\) is given, so \(d\) is defined and negative.

HCI 2023 Prelim Paper 1 Q9
The function \(\mathrm{f}\) is defined by \(\mathrm{f}:x\mapsto\ln(x+1)^2+2\), \(x\in\mathbb{R}\), \(x\ge0\).
(a) Show that \(\mathrm{f}\) has an inverse. [2]
Before you reveal — say it out loud: confidence: high / medium / guessed
For \(x\ge0\), \(x+1\ge1>0\), so \((x+1)^2\) is strictly increasing on the domain, and \(\ln\) is strictly increasing. A composition of strictly increasing functions is strictly increasing, so \(\mathrm{f}\) is strictly increasing.
Hence every horizontal line \(y=k\) meets \(y=\mathrm{f}(x)\) at most once, so \(\mathrm{f}\) is one-one and \(\mathrm{f}^{-1}\) exists.
(b) Find \(\mathrm{f}^{-1}(x)\) and state the domain of \(\mathrm{f}^{-1}\). [3]
Before you reveal — say it out loud: confidence: high / medium / guessed
\(y=\ln(x+1)^2+2=2\ln(x+1)+2\) (valid since \(x+1>0\)).
\(\Rightarrow \ln(x+1)=\dfrac{y-2}{2}\Rightarrow x+1=e^{(y-2)/2}\Rightarrow x=e^{(y-2)/2}-1\).
\(\mathrm{f}^{-1}(x)=e^{(x-2)/2}-1\).
\(R_\mathrm{f}\): at \(x=0\), \(\mathrm{f}=\ln1+2=2\); as \(x\to\infty\), \(\mathrm{f}\to\infty\). Increasing, so \(R_\mathrm{f}=[2,\infty)\).
\(D_{\mathrm{f}^{-1}}=[2,\infty)\).
(c) The function \(\mathrm{g}\) is defined by \(\mathrm{g}:x\mapsto\dfrac{1-x}{x+1}\), \(x\in\mathbb{R}\), \(x\ne-1\). Given that \(\mathrm{g}^{-1}\) exists, show that \(\mathrm{g}^{-1}\mathrm{f}^{-1}\) exists, and find the range of \(\mathrm{g}^{-1}\mathrm{f}^{-1}\). [3]
Before you reveal — say it out loud: confidence: high / medium / guessed
\(\mathrm{g}^{-1}\mathrm{f}^{-1}\) exists \(\iff R_{\mathrm{f}^{-1}}\subseteq D_{\mathrm{g}^{-1}}\), and \(D_{\mathrm{g}^{-1}}=R_\mathrm{g}\).
\(\mathrm{g}(x)=\dfrac{1-x}{x+1}=\dfrac{-(x+1)+2}{x+1}=-1+\dfrac{2}{x+1}\), so \(R_\mathrm{g}=\mathbb{R}\setminus\{-1\}\).
\(R_{\mathrm{f}^{-1}}=D_\mathrm{f}=[0,\infty)\).
Since \(-1\notin[0,\infty)\), \(R_{\mathrm{f}^{-1}}\subseteq D_{\mathrm{g}^{-1}}\), so \(\mathrm{g}^{-1}\mathrm{f}^{-1}\) exists.

Range: push \(D_{\mathrm{f}^{-1}}=[2,\infty)\) through. \(\mathrm{f}^{-1}\) maps it to \([0,\infty)\). Then \(\mathrm{g}^{-1}\): note \(\mathrm{g}\) is self-inverse here, since \(\mathrm{g}(\mathrm{g}(x))=x\). So \(\mathrm{g}^{-1}=\mathrm{g}\), and \(\mathrm{g}\) maps \([0,\infty)\) to \((-1,1]\) (\(\mathrm{g}(0)=1\), and \(\mathrm{g}\to-1^+\) as \(x\to\infty\)).
\(R_{\mathrm{g}^{-1}\mathrm{f}^{-1}}=(-1,1]\).
(d) Find the exact value of \(x\) such that \(\mathrm{g}^{-1}\mathrm{f}^{-1}(x)=\dfrac12\). [3]
Before you reveal — say it out loud: confidence: high / medium / guessed
Since \(\mathrm{g}^{-1}=\mathrm{g}\), we need \(\mathrm{g}\big(\mathrm{f}^{-1}(x)\big)=\tfrac12\).
Let \(u=\mathrm{f}^{-1}(x)\). Then \(\dfrac{1-u}{u+1}=\dfrac12\Rightarrow 2-2u=u+1\Rightarrow u=\dfrac13\).
So \(\mathrm{f}^{-1}(x)=\dfrac13\Rightarrow x=\mathrm{f}\!\left(\dfrac13\right)=\ln\!\left(\dfrac43\right)^2+2=2\ln\dfrac43+2\).
\(x=2\ln\dfrac43+2\).
Check it lies in \(D_{\mathrm{f}^{-1}}=[2,\infty)\): \(2\ln\tfrac43\approx0.575>0\) ✓.

HCI 2022 Prelim Paper 1 Q8 · fully inside the 2026 cartesian-only scope
Do not use a graphing calculator for this question.
(a) The complex number \(a+i\) is a root of \(z^2-2\sqrt2\,z+b=0\), where \(a\) and \(b\) are positive real constants. Find the exact values of \(a\) and \(b\). [3]
Before you reveal — say it out loud: confidence: high / medium / guessed
The coefficients \(1,\ -2\sqrt2,\ b\) are all real, so the conjugate \(a-i\) is also a root.
Sum of roots \(=(a+i)+(a-i)=2a=2\sqrt2\Rightarrow \boxed{a=\sqrt2}\).
Product of roots \(=(a+i)(a-i)=a^2+1=2+1=3=b\Rightarrow \boxed{b=3}\).
Both positive, as required.
(b) It is given that \(\mathrm{f}(z)=z^6-3z^4+11z^2-9\). Show that \(\mathrm{f}(z)=\mathrm{f}(-z)\). [1]
Before you reveal — say it out loud: confidence: high / medium / guessed
\(\mathrm{f}(-z)=(-z)^6-3(-z)^4+11(-z)^2-9=z^6-3z^4+11z^2-9=\mathrm{f}(z)\), since every power is even. Shown.
(c) It is given that \(a+i\) is a root of \(\mathrm{f}(z)=0\). Using the results in (a) and (b), show that \(\mathrm{f}(z)\) can be factorised into 3 quadratic factors with real coefficients, in exact form. [4]
Before you reveal — say it out loud: confidence: high / medium / guessed
From (a), \(\sqrt2+i\) is a root. Coefficients are real, so \(\sqrt2-i\) is a root.
From (b), \(\mathrm{f}(-z)=\mathrm{f}(z)\), so \(-\sqrt2-i\) and \(-\sqrt2+i\) are roots too.

The pair \(\sqrt2\pm i\) gives the real quadratic \(z^2-2\sqrt2 z+3\) (from part (a)).
The pair \(-\sqrt2\pm i\) gives \(z^2+2\sqrt2 z+3\).
Their product: \((z^2+3)^2-(2\sqrt2 z)^2=z^4+6z^2+9-8z^2=z^4-2z^2+9\).

Divide: \(\dfrac{z^6-3z^4+11z^2-9}{z^4-2z^2+9}=z^2-1\).
\(\mathrm{f}(z)=\left(z^2-2\sqrt2 z+3\right)\left(z^2+2\sqrt2 z+3\right)\left(z^2-1\right)\), all three with real coefficients.
Verified by expansion.

HCI 2023 Prelim Paper 1 Q6
(a) By writing \(\dfrac{1}{(2r+1)(2r+3)}\) in partial fractions, find an expression for \(\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(2r+1)(2r+3)}\) in terms of \(n\). [4]
Before you reveal — say it out loud: confidence: high / medium / guessed
Partial fractions: \(\dfrac{1}{(2r+1)(2r+3)}=\dfrac12\left(\dfrac{1}{2r+1}-\dfrac{1}{2r+3}\right)\).

\(\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{n}=\frac12\left[\left(\frac13-\frac15\right)+\left(\frac15-\frac17\right)+\cdots+\left(\frac{1}{2n+1}-\frac{1}{2n+3}\right)\right]\)
Telescoping leaves the first and last: \(=\dfrac12\left(\dfrac13-\dfrac{1}{2n+3}\right)\).
Simplify: \(=\dfrac12\cdot\dfrac{(2n+3)-3}{3(2n+3)}=\dfrac{2n}{6(2n+3)}=\boxed{\dfrac{n}{3(2n+3)}}\).
Check \(n=1\): formula gives \(\dfrac{1}{15}\); direct \(\dfrac{1}{3\cdot5}=\dfrac{1}{15}\) ✓.
(b) Explain why \(\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\left[-\left(\frac13\right)^{r}+\frac{1}{(2r+1)(2r+3)}\right]\) is a convergent series, and state its sum to infinity. [4]
Before you reveal — say it out loud: confidence: high / medium / guessed
Split into two series.
First: \(\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}-\left(\tfrac13\right)^r\) is geometric with first term \(-\tfrac13\) and ratio \(\tfrac13\). Since \(\left|\tfrac13\right|<1\) it converges, to \(\dfrac{-\frac13}{1-\frac13}=-\dfrac12\).
Second: from (a) the partial sum is \(\dfrac{n}{3(2n+3)}\to\dfrac{1}{6}\) as \(n\to\infty\), so it converges to \(\dfrac16\).

Both parts converge, so the sum of the two series converges, and the limits add:
Sum to infinity \(=-\dfrac12+\dfrac16=-\dfrac13\).
