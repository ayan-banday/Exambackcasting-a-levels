The one habit: for a plane, always get to scalar-product form \(\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf n = \mathbf a\cdot\mathbf n\) first — the normal \(\mathbf n\) is the key to every angle, distance, and intersection that follows.
Planes toolkit — everything hangs off the normal \(\mathbf n\)
Parametric form\(\mathbf r = \mathbf a + \lambda\mathbf d_1 + \mu\mathbf d_2\) (two non-parallel directions in the plane).
Scalar-product form\(\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf n = \mathbf a\cdot\mathbf n\), with \(\mathbf n = \mathbf d_1\times\mathbf d_2\). Cartesian: \(n_1x+n_2y+n_3z=d\).
Line \(\cap\) planeSub the line's \(\mathbf r\) into \(\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf n=d\), solve for \(\lambda\), back-substitute.
Angle line–plane\(\theta = 90^\circ - \cos^{-1}\dfrac{|\mathbf d\cdot\mathbf n|}{|\mathbf d||\mathbf n|}\) (angle to the plane, not the normal).
Angle plane–plane\(\cos\theta = \dfrac{|\mathbf n_1\cdot\mathbf n_2|}{|\mathbf n_1||\mathbf n_2|}\).
Line of intersection (2 planes)Direction \(\mathbf n_1\times\mathbf n_2\); one point by inspection or GC.
Distance point \(A\) to plane\(\big|\overrightarrow{BA}\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}\big|\), \(B\) any point on the plane.
Distance point \(A\) to line\(\big|\overrightarrow{BA}\times\hat{\mathbf d}\big|\), \(B\) any point on the line.
Foot of perp \(A\)→planeLine through \(A\) along \(\mathbf n\); intersect with the plane.
Foot of perp \(A\)→line\(F=\mathbf a+\lambda\mathbf d\) with \(\overrightarrow{AF}\cdot\mathbf d=0\).

Question 1 (HCI Tutorial 7C Q1a)

Example
Find a vector equation of the plane, in parametric form and in scalar-product form, where the plane contains the points \(A\), \(B\), \(C\) with position vectors \(\mathbf i+\mathbf j-2\mathbf k\), \(\mathbf i+\mathbf k\) and \(-2\mathbf i+\mathbf j-3\mathbf k\). [3]
Solution — verbatim HCI
Two directions in the plane
M1
\( \overrightarrow{AB} = \begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\-2\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}0\\-1\\3\end{pmatrix} \), \( \overrightarrow{AC} = \begin{pmatrix}-2\\1\\-3\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\-2\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}-3\\0\\-1\end{pmatrix} \).
Parametric form
A1
\( \mathbf r = \begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\-2\end{pmatrix}+\lambda\begin{pmatrix}0\\-1\\3\end{pmatrix}+\mu\begin{pmatrix}3\\0\\1\end{pmatrix},\ \lambda,\mu\in\mathbb R. \) (Note: director vectors must be non-parallel.)
Normal → scalar-product form
\(\mathbf n = \overrightarrow{AB}\times\overrightarrow{AC}\); then \(\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf n = \mathbf a\cdot\mathbf n\). A1
\( \mathbf n = \begin{pmatrix}0\\-1\\3\end{pmatrix}\times\begin{pmatrix}3\\0\\1\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}-1\\9\\3\end{pmatrix} \); \( \mathbf r\cdot\begin{pmatrix}-1\\9\\3\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\-2\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}-1\\9\\3\end{pmatrix} = 2 \). Hence \( \mathbf r\cdot\begin{pmatrix}-1\\9\\3\end{pmatrix}=2 \).

Practice (HCI Tutorial 7C Q1f)
Find a vector equation of the plane (parametric + scalar-product form) that contains the line \( \mathbf r = \begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\1\end{pmatrix}+\lambda\begin{pmatrix}-1\\2\\-1\end{pmatrix} \) and is perpendicular to the plane \( 2x+3y+4z=5 \). [4]
Answer — verbatim HCI
Two directions parallel to the required plane
Given plane's normal \((2,3,4)\) is parallel to the required plane (perpendicular planes). The line's direction \((-1,2,-1)\) is too. M1
\( \mathbf r = \begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\1\end{pmatrix}+\lambda\begin{pmatrix}-1\\2\\-1\end{pmatrix}+\mu\begin{pmatrix}2\\3\\4\end{pmatrix},\ \lambda,\mu\in\mathbb R \). ((1,1,1) lies on the required plane.)
Normal + scalar-product form
M1 A1 A1
\( \mathbf n = \begin{pmatrix}-1\\2\\-1\end{pmatrix}\times\begin{pmatrix}2\\3\\4\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}11\\2\\-7\end{pmatrix} \); \( \mathbf r\cdot\begin{pmatrix}11\\2\\-7\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\1\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}11\\2\\-7\end{pmatrix} = 6 \). Hence \( \mathbf r\cdot\begin{pmatrix}11\\2\\-7\end{pmatrix}=6 \).
Why harder: "perpendicular to a plane" means that plane's normal lies IN your plane — so it's a valid direction vector, alongside the given line's direction.

Archetype
Build a plane's equation from what defines it.

Get two non-parallel directions in the plane, cross them for \(\mathbf n\), then \(\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf n=\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf n\). "Contains a line", "perpendicular to a plane", "parallel to a vector" all just hand you directions or the normal.

Question 2 (HCI Tutorial 7C Q3a)

Example
Find the acute angle between the line \( l:\ \mathbf r = (\mathbf i+\mathbf j-2\mathbf k)+\lambda(\mathbf i-\mathbf j+\mathbf k) \) and the plane \(\Pi:\ \mathbf r\cdot(\mathbf i+2\mathbf j-\mathbf k)=2\), and the point of intersection of \(l\) and \(\Pi\). [5]
Solution — verbatim HCI
Acute angle (line–plane uses \(90^\circ-\cos^{-1}\))
M1 A1
Angle \( = 90^\circ - \cos^{-1}\dfrac{\left|\begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\\1\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}1\\2\\-1\end{pmatrix}\right|}{\sqrt3\,\sqrt6} = 90^\circ - \cos^{-1}\dfrac{|1-2-1|}{\sqrt3\sqrt6} = 28.1^\circ \) (1 d.p.).
Point of intersection
Sub \(l\): \((1+\lambda,\,1-\lambda,\,-2+\lambda)\) into \(\Pi\). M1 A1
\( \begin{pmatrix}1+\lambda\\1-\lambda\\-2+\lambda\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}1\\2\\-1\end{pmatrix}=2 \Rightarrow 1+\lambda+2-2\lambda+2-\lambda=2 \Rightarrow \lambda=\tfrac32 \).
Coordinates
A1
Point of intersection \( = \left(\tfrac52,\,-\tfrac12,\,-\tfrac12\right) \). (Give in coordinate form as required.)

Question 3 (HCI Tutorial 7C Q3b, Q3c)

Example
(b) Find an equation of the line of intersection of the planes \(\mathbf r\cdot(\mathbf i+2\mathbf j-\mathbf k)=2\) and \(\mathbf r\cdot(2\mathbf i+3\mathbf j+\mathbf k)=1\). [4]
(c) Find the acute angle between the planes \(\mathbf r\cdot(2\mathbf i-2\mathbf j+\mathbf k)=2\) and \(\mathbf r\cdot(8\mathbf i-6\mathbf j+3\mathbf k)=8\). [3]
Solution — verbatim HCI
(b) Direction = \(\mathbf n_1\times\mathbf n_2\)
M1 A1
\( \begin{pmatrix}1\\2\\-1\end{pmatrix}\times\begin{pmatrix}2\\3\\1\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}5\\-3\\-1\end{pmatrix} \). By inspection \((1,0,-1)\) satisfies both planes.
(b) The line
M1 A1
\( \mathbf r = \begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\-1\end{pmatrix}+t\begin{pmatrix}5\\-3\\-1\end{pmatrix},\ t\in\mathbb R \).
(c) Angle between planes = angle between normals
M1 A1 A1
\( \cos^{-1}\dfrac{\left|\begin{pmatrix}2\\-2\\1\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}8\\-6\\3\end{pmatrix}\right|}{\sqrt9\,\sqrt{109}} = \cos^{-1}\dfrac{|16+12+3|}{\sqrt9\sqrt{109}} = 8.2^\circ \) (1 d.p.).

The planes \(p_1\) and \(p_2\) have equations \(\mathbf r\cdot\begin{pmatrix}2\\-2\\1\end{pmatrix}=1\) and \(\mathbf r\cdot\begin{pmatrix}-6\\3\\2\end{pmatrix}=-1\), and meet in the line \(l\).
(i) Find the acute angle between \(p_1\) and \(p_2\). [3]
(ii) Find a vector equation for \(l\). [4]
Answer — verbatim HCI
(i) Angle
M1 A1 A1
\( \cos\theta = \dfrac{\left|\begin{pmatrix}2\\-2\\1\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}-6\\3\\2\end{pmatrix}\right|}{\sqrt9\,\sqrt{49}} = \dfrac{16}{21} \Rightarrow \theta = 40.4^\circ \) (1 d.p.).

Direction \(\mathbf n_1\times\mathbf n_2\); a point by inspection. M1 A1 M1 A1
\( \begin{pmatrix}2\\-2\\1\end{pmatrix}\times\begin{pmatrix}-6\\3\\2\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}-7\\-10\\-6\end{pmatrix} \parallel \begin{pmatrix}7\\10\\6\end{pmatrix} \). By inspection \((1,1,1)\) satisfies both. \( l:\ \mathbf r = \begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\1\end{pmatrix}+\lambda\begin{pmatrix}7\\10\\6\end{pmatrix},\ \lambda\in\mathbb R \).

Question 4 (HCI Tutorial 7C Q4b, c, e, f)

Example — the multi-part workup
\(A=(1,2,3)\); \( l_1:\ \mathbf r=\begin{pmatrix}2\\1\\1\end{pmatrix}+t\begin{pmatrix}0\\-1\\1\end{pmatrix} \) (which lies on \(\Pi\)); \(\Pi:\ \mathbf r\cdot\begin{pmatrix}-1\\2\\2\end{pmatrix}=2\).
(b) Perpendicular distance from \(A\) to \(\Pi\). [2]
(c) Perpendicular distance from \(A\) to \(l_1\). [3]
(f) Position vector of the foot of perpendicular from \(A\) to \(\Pi\). [4]
Solution — verbatim HCI
(b) Distance \(A\)→\(\Pi\): \(\big|\overrightarrow{BA}\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}\big|\)
Take \(B(2,1,1)\) on \(\Pi\); \(\mathbf n=(-1,2,2)\), \(|\mathbf n|=3\). M1 A1
\( AN_1 = \left|\begin{pmatrix}-1\\1\\2\end{pmatrix}\cdot\tfrac13\begin{pmatrix}-1\\2\\2\end{pmatrix}\right| = \tfrac13|1+2+4| = \tfrac73 \) units.
(c) Distance \(A\)→\(l_1\): \(\big|\overrightarrow{BA}\times\hat{\mathbf d}\big|\)
\(B(2,1,1)\) on \(l_1\); \(\mathbf d=(0,-1,1)\), \(|\mathbf d|=\sqrt2\). M1 M1 A1
\( AN_2 = \left|\begin{pmatrix}-1\\1\\2\end{pmatrix}\times\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}0\\-1\\1\end{pmatrix}\right| = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\left|\begin{pmatrix}3\\1\\1\end{pmatrix}\right| = \sqrt{\tfrac{11}{2}} \) units.
(f) Foot of perp \(A\)→\(\Pi\): line through \(A\) along \(\mathbf n\), intersect \(\Pi\)
\(l_3:\ \mathbf r=\begin{pmatrix}1\\2\\3\end{pmatrix}+\lambda\begin{pmatrix}-1\\2\\2\end{pmatrix}\); sub into \(\Pi\). M1 M1 A1
\( (1-\lambda)(-1)+(2+2\lambda)(2)+(3+2\lambda)(2)=2 \Rightarrow -1+\lambda+4+4\lambda+6+4\lambda=2 \Rightarrow \lambda=-\tfrac79 \).
\( \overrightarrow{ON_1} = \begin{pmatrix}1+\tfrac79\\2-\tfrac{14}{9}\\3-\tfrac{14}{9}\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}16/9\\4/9\\13/9\end{pmatrix} \). A1
HCI's own warning: \(AN_2\) (dist to the line) is NOT generally equal to \(AN_1\) (dist to the plane) — they're equal only if \(l_1\) passes through the foot of perpendicular from \(A\) to \(\Pi\).

Question 5 (A Level N2013/II/4 part iii)

Example — A-Level
The planes \(p_1:\ \mathbf r\cdot\begin{pmatrix}2\\-2\\1\end{pmatrix}=1\) and \(p_2:\ \mathbf r\cdot\begin{pmatrix}-6\\3\\2\end{pmatrix}=-1\) meet in the line \(l\), which passes through \((1,1,1)\) (from Q3). The point \(A(4,3,c)\) is equidistant from the planes \(p_1\) and \(p_2\). Calculate the two possible values of \(c\). [6]
Solution — verbatim HCI
Set up both distances from a common point \(B(1,1,1)\) on both planes
\(\overrightarrow{BA}=\begin{pmatrix}3\\2\\c-1\end{pmatrix}\). Equidistant ⟹ dist to \(p_1\) = dist to \(p_2\). M1 M1
Dist to \(p_1 = \left|\begin{pmatrix}3\\2\\c-1\end{pmatrix}\cdot\tfrac13\begin{pmatrix}2\\-2\\1\end{pmatrix}\right| = \tfrac13|6-4+c-1| = \tfrac13|c+1|\).
Dist to \(p_2 = \left|\begin{pmatrix}3\\2\\c-1\end{pmatrix}\cdot\tfrac17\begin{pmatrix}-6\\3\\2\end{pmatrix}\right| = \tfrac17|-18+6+2c-2| = \tfrac27|c-7|\).
Equate, square, solve
M1 M1 A1 A1
\( \tfrac13|c+1| = \tfrac27|c-7| \Rightarrow \tfrac19(c+1)^2 = \tfrac{4}{49}(c-7)^2 \Rightarrow 49(c^2+2c+1)=36(c^2-14c+49) \)
\( \Rightarrow 13c^2+602c-1715=0 \Rightarrow c=\tfrac{35}{13} \text{ or } c=-49 \).
Why \(B(1,1,1)\): it lies on both planes (it's on \(l\)), so it's the shared reference point for both distances. Squaring is needed to clear the modulus — remember it gives two roots.

Question 6 (A Level 2019 H2 P1/Q12)

Challenge — unfamiliar application
A ray of light is sent in direction \(\begin{pmatrix}-2\\-3\\-6\end{pmatrix}\) from \(P(2,2,4)\), passes through a prism entering at \(Q\) and emerging at \(R\), and is picked up at \(S(-5,-6,-7)\). The top of the prism is part of the plane \(x+y+z=1\); the base is part of \(x+y+z=-9\). Ray \(PQ\) is parallel to ray \(RS\).
(i) Find the exact coordinates of \(Q\) and \(R\). [5]
(ii) The acute angle between \(PQ\) and the top normal is \(\theta\); between \(QR\) and that normal is \(\beta\). Find \(\cos\theta\) and \(\cos\beta\). [3]
Solution — verbatim HCI
(i) \(Q\) = line \(PQ\) ∩ top plane
\(PQ:\ \mathbf r=(2,2,4)+\lambda(-2,-3,-6)\) into \(x+y+z=1\). M1 A1
\( 2-2\lambda+2-3\lambda+4-6\lambda=1 \Rightarrow \lambda=\tfrac{7}{11} \Rightarrow Q=\left(\tfrac{8}{11},\tfrac{1}{11},\tfrac{2}{11}\right) \).
(i) \(R\) = line \(RS\) ∩ base plane
\(RS\) has the same direction \((-2,-3,-6)\) (parallel), through \(S(-5,-6,-7)\), into \(x+y+z=-9\). M1 A1 A1
\( \mathbf r=(-5,-6,-7)+\mu(-2,-3,-6) \); \( -5-2\mu-6-3\mu-7-6\mu=-9 \Rightarrow \mu=-\tfrac{9}{11} \Rightarrow R=\left(-\tfrac{37}{11},-\tfrac{39}{11},-\tfrac{23}{11}\right) \).
(ii) \(\cos\theta\), \(\cos\beta\)
\(\theta\): between \(PQ\) direction and the top normal \((1,1,1)\). \(\beta\): between \(\overrightarrow{RQ}\) and \((1,1,1)\). M1 A1 A1
\( \cos\theta = \dfrac{\left|\begin{pmatrix}2\\3\\6\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\1\end{pmatrix}\right|}{\sqrt{49}\,\sqrt3} = \dfrac{11}{7\sqrt3} = \dfrac{11\sqrt3}{21} \).
\( \overrightarrow{RQ} = \tfrac{5}{11}\begin{pmatrix}9\\8\\5\end{pmatrix} \), so \( \cos\beta = \dfrac{\left|\begin{pmatrix}9\\8\\5\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\1\end{pmatrix}\right|}{\sqrt{170}\,\sqrt3} = \dfrac{22}{\sqrt{510}} = \dfrac{11\sqrt{510}}{255} \).
Why it's the hardest: a real-world context (Snell's law follows in (iv)) dressed on pure "line ∩ plane" + "angle to a normal." Strip the story: \(Q,R\) are just line-plane intersections; the angles are line-to-normal. Everything reduces to the toolkit.
