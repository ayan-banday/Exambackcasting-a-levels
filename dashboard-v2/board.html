Never cut, however small the number: Biostats (82% shared — 1.0 marks a year on its own, but the chi-squared is welded onto genetics crosses, so skipping it costs Inheritance marks) · Genetics of Bacteria (59%) · Mutations (51%) · Lipids (49%, and only 0.7 marks a year of its own — half its footprint sits inside membranes and respiration) · Eukaryotic Gene Expression (49%) · DNA Structure & Replication (46%, and it earned no primary marks in the specimen while still being needed inside other answers) · Genome Organisation (42%) · Membrane Transport (41%) · Cell & Nuclear Division (40%) · Proteins (39%). Ten of 26 topics run above 38% shared. Chemistry has nothing like that spread, and it is why the bottom of this table is far more dangerous than the bottom of the Chem one.
Highest return per hour: Biological Evolution — 20.3 marks a year, the largest topic in the subject by a clear margin and 13.2% of Paper 3 · Climate Change — 11.7 marks a year off six short extension sections, the best marks-to-content ratio on the page, and it nearly doubled in the specimen · Infectious Diseases — 13.0 marks a year and present in 16 of the 19 papers counted, so it is the closest thing to a guaranteed appearance.
The 9477 specimen moves marks out of molecular genetics: Book 2 falls 20.2% → 10.3%, the biggest shift on the page. Book 1 rises 17.3% → 24.8%, Energetics 8.3% → 14.4%, Climate Change 5.3% → 9.8%, and Viruses & Bacteria drops 5.9% → 1.5%. Read the size of each move with care: the specimen is one sitting of 195 marks, so a single 12-mark question swings a topic several points. The book-level direction is the trustworthy part, not the per-topic decimals. What is unmistakable is the question shape — specimen P3 Q1 is a 32-mark case study running the link reaction, meiotic recombination, PCR, molecular clocks and the species concept through one stem. Cross-topic fusion inside a single question is now the default.
How to read the columns. Each mark was tagged to the topic its mark scheme actually rewards, with up to two secondary topics where the answer had to reach into another topic to score — that is what the Shared column counts. Paper 3 Section B options were both tagged and each counted at half, since only one gets sat. The P1 column is the coarsest number here: 120 MCQ marks total, so one question moves a topic by 0.8 points. Where the corpus groups things differently from the 26 above, Inheritance was split by content (Mendelian crosses to 6a; linkage, epistasis and chi-squared-on-a-cross to 6b), and the school's separate Water & Macromolecules material folded into the biomolecule topic each question was really testing.
