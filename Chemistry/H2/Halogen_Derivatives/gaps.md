| 1 | **SN2 mechanism — drawing the transition state.** Fumbled how to draw it. | ACTIVE | Nucleophile attacks the **δ+ carbon from the side directly opposite the leaving group** (backside attack); a **5-coordinate transition state** forms with a **partial (dashed) C–Nu bond forming and partial C–X bond breaking**; leaving group departs with **inversion of configuration**. **One step, concerted.** Curly arrow: Nu lone pair → C; C–X bond pair → X. |
| 2 | **Why primary → SN2, tertiary → SN1.** | FRAGILE | Primary: the 1° carbocation is **too unstable** (only one electron-donating alkyl group to disperse the + charge) so SN1 is disfavoured; **low steric hindrance** lets the nucleophile reach the carbon → SN2. Tertiary: 3° carbocation is **stabilised by three alkyl groups** (SN1 favoured); steric bulk blocks SN2. |
| 3 | **SN1 vs SN2 kinetics / rate orders.** Was unsure. | ACTIVE | **SN2: rate = k[RX][Nu⁻], second order, single step.** **SN1: rate = k[RX], first order** — independent of [Nu⁻] — because the **rate-determining step is the slow heterolytic formation of the carbocation.** |
| 4 | **Halogenoalkane + NH₃ → amine.** Worked it but shakily. | FRAGILE | **Excess ethanolic NH₃, heat in a sealed tube.** Step 1 nucleophilic substitution (N lone pair attacks δ+ C, C–Br breaks) → alkylammonium salt; step 2 a **second NH₃ acts as a Brønsted base and removes H⁺** → amine + NH₄Br. **Excess NH₃** minimises further substitution to 2°/3°/quaternary ammonium salt. |
| 5 | **Naming + intramolecular cyclisation.** "4-bromobutan-1-amine wtf" + the 5-membered ring. | ACTIVE | –NH₂ is the principal group (suffix **-amine**, lowest locant); halogen is a **prefix** → **4-bromobutan-1-amine**. Cyclisation: the amino-halogenoalkane undergoes **intramolecular nucleophilic substitution** — its own –NH₂ attacks the δ+ C–Br carbon → a **cyclic amine + HBr** (favourable when the ring is 5- or 6-membered). |
