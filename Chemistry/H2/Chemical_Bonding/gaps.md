## From the diagnostic test (5 Aug, Q3 — NaCl / MgCl₂ / AlCl₃ melting points + Al₂Cl₆)

| # | Gap | State | Correct answer / mark-scheme wording |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Why MgCl₂ melts higher than NaCl.** Content was right; the wording and presentation were the gap. | ACTIVE (wording) | Lattice energy magnitude ∝ **\|q⁺ × q⁻\| / (r⁺ + r⁻)**. Mg²⁺ has a **higher charge (+2 vs +1)** and a **smaller ionic radius** than Na⁺, so MgCl₂ has the **larger-magnitude lattice energy** → **more energy needed to overcome the stronger electrostatic attraction** → higher m.p. **Say "larger magnitude of lattice energy", never "larger lattice energy"** (LE is negative). **Quote the actual charges and the Data-Booklet ionic radii and add them into the formula** to show it explicitly — don't just assert "bigger charge, smaller radius". |
| 2 | **Why AlCl₃ is covalent, not ionic (Fajans / polarising power).** Knew the gist, needs the verbatim chain. | ACTIVE | **Al³⁺ has a very high charge density** (high charge +3, small ionic radius) → **high polarising power** → it **distorts / polarises the electron cloud of the polarisable Cl⁻** → the electron density is drawn between the nuclei → **effective overlap of the electron clouds → covalent bond**. Say "**overlap of electron clouds**", not "overlap of orbitals". |
| 3 | **Describe the bonding holding two AlCl₃ units together in Al₂Cl₆.** Didn't know how to phrase it. | ACTIVE | It is a **dative (coordinate) bond**: a **lone pair on a Cl of one AlCl₃ is donated into the empty (vacant) orbital of the electron-deficient Al of the other AlCl₃**. |
| 4 | **Why molten AlCl₃ does not conduct but molten NaCl does.** | ACTIVE | Molten NaCl has **free, mobile ions that act as mobile charge carriers**, so it conducts. AlCl₃ exists as **discrete, neutral Al₂Cl₆ molecules with no mobile ions**, so it cannot conduct. |
