## The spine: every bond is electrostatic; electronegativity picks the type

All three bond types are electrostatic — attraction between + and − charges. Which type you get is set by the **electronegativity difference (ΔEN)** between the atoms:

| ΔEN | What the electrons do | Bond type |
|---|---|---|
| Large (metal + non-metal) | transferred | ionic |
| Small (two non-metals) | shared | covalent |
| ~zero (metal atoms) | pooled / delocalised | metallic |

It's a **continuum, not three boxes** — most bonds have mixed character (section 2).

---

## 1. The three bond types — verbatim definitions *(memorise)*

- **Ionic bond:** the electrostatic force of attraction between **oppositely charged ions (cations and anions)**.
- **Covalent bond:** the electrostatic force of attraction between the **shared pair of electrons and the two positively charged nuclei**.
- **Metallic bond:** the electrostatic force of attraction between the **lattice of positive metal ions (cations) and the sea of delocalised electrons**.

---

## 2. Electronegativity — the decider

**Electronegativity:** the **ability of an atom to attract the bonding (shared) pair of electrons in a covalent bond towards itself.** *(memorise)*
- Increases across a period (nuclear charge ↑, atomic radius ↓); decreases down a group.
- **Predict bond type:** large ΔEN → ionic; small ΔEN → covalent.
- **Polar covalent bond:** unequal sharing (ΔEN present) → δ+/δ− dipole. (This is what fed cluster 1's polarity.)

**Fajans — mixed character:** a bond is never purely one type.
- **Covalent character in an ionic bond:** the cation polarises (distorts) the anion's electron cloud. Greater when the cation is **small and highly charged** (high polarising power / charge density) and the anion is **large** (high polarisability). E.g. AlCl₃ is covalent despite being metal + non-metal.

---

## 3. Covalent bonding — how it's actually built

- **σ (sigma) bond:** head-on / axial overlap of orbitals → strong.
- **π (pi) bond:** side-on overlap of p orbitals → weaker (poorer overlap).
- **Single = 1σ. Double = 1σ + 1π. Triple = 1σ + 2π.** A π needs a σ first; only one σ is possible between two atoms.
- **Bond order ↑ → shorter, stronger bond.**
- **Bond energy:** the **average energy required to break one mole of a covalent bond in the gaseous state.** *(memorise)*
- **Bond length:** distance between the two nuclei; smaller atoms + higher bond order → shorter, stronger.
- ⚠️ **F–F anomaly:** F–F is *shorter but weaker* than Cl–Cl, because of lone-pair repulsion between the small, close F atoms.

---

## 4. Dot-and-cross diagrams

- Show **valence electrons only**; dots for one atom, crosses for the other. Octet = 8 outer electrons (H = 2).
- **Ionic:** show the transfer; draw the cation with an empty outer shell + its charge; combine in the whole-number ratio that gives neutrality.
- **Covalent:** show the shared pair(s) in the overlap region; show lone pairs.
- **Polyatomic ions:** draw inside square brackets with the charge outside.

**Octet exceptions — know all three:**

| Exception | What | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Electron-deficient | central atom has **< 8** e⁻ | BF₃, BeCl₂, AlCl₃ |
| Expanded octet | central atom has **> 8** e⁻ (Period 3+, uses accessible d orbitals) | PCl₅ (10), SF₆ (12) |
| Radical | **odd** number of electrons | NO, NO₂ |

⚠️ **Period 2 (C, N, O) cannot expand the octet** — only 2s + 2p are available.

**Co-ordinate (dative) bond:** a covalent bond where **both shared electrons come from the same atom** (a lone pair donated into an empty orbital). Shown by an **arrow →** from donor to acceptor. E.g. NH₄⁺ (N lone pair → H⁺), Al₂Cl₆, NH₃BF₃.

## 5. Hybridisation (carbon)

| Hybrid | Mix | Shape | Angle | Example | Bonds at C |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **sp³** | 1s + 3p | tetrahedral | 109.5° | ethane, CH₄ | 4 σ |
| **sp²** | 1s + 2p | trigonal planar | 120° | ethene, benzene | 3 σ + 1 π (unhybridised p) |
| **sp** | 1s + 1p | linear | 180° | ethyne | 2 σ + 2 π |

More **s character → shorter, stronger** bond (sp > sp² > sp³). This is the root of graphite (sp²) vs diamond (sp³) from cluster 1.

---

## 6. Ionic & metallic strength (feeds structure → property)

- **Lattice energy:** the **energy released when one mole of a solid ionic compound is formed from its constituent gaseous ions.** *(memorise)* ∝ (charge of cation × charge of anion) / (sum of ionic radii) → higher charges + smaller radii = stronger.
- **Metallic bond strength** ↑ with: **number of delocalised electrons** contributed per atom, and the **charge density of the cation** (charge / size). More electrons, smaller and higher-charged cation → stronger.

## Quick recall — the spine

1. Every bond is **electrostatic**; the **electronegativity difference** picks ionic / covalent / metallic (a continuum — Fajans gives mixed character).
2. Covalent = σ (head-on, strong) + π (side-on, weak); bond order ↑ → shorter/stronger; watch the **F–F anomaly**.
3. Dot-and-cross: octet + three exceptions (electron-deficient / expanded / radical); **Period 2 can't expand**; dative = both electrons from one atom.
4. Hybridisation sp / sp² / sp³ → shape + % s character → bond strength.
5. Ionic strength = lattice energy (charge / radius); metallic strength = delocalised electrons + cation charge density.
