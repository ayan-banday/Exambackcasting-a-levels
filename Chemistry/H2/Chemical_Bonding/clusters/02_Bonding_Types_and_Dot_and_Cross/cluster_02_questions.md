## Q1
*For ethene, ethyne and hydrogen cyanide:*

*(a) State the hybridisation of each carbon atom. [3]*

*(b) State the total number of σ and π bonds in each molecule. [3]*

*(c) Predict the H–C–H bond angle in ethene and the shape of HCN. [2]*

(a) In ethene both carbons are **sp²**. In ethyne both carbons are **sp**. In HCN the carbon is **sp** (and the nitrogen is sp). [3]

(b) Ethene has **5 σ and 1 π** (four C–H sigma, one C–C sigma, one pi in the C=C). Ethyne has **3 σ and 2 π** (two C–H sigma, one C–C sigma, two pi in the triple bond). HCN has **2 σ and 2 π** (the H–C and C–N sigma, two pi in the C≡N). [3]

(c) The H–C–H bond angle in ethene is **120°** because each carbon is **sp², trigonal planar**. HCN is **linear**, with a bond angle of **180°**. [2]

## Q2
*(a) Explain why the C≡C bond is shorter and stronger than the C–C bond. [2]*

*(b) Bond energies: F–F 158, Cl–Cl 242, Br–Br 193 kJ mol⁻¹. F–F is anomalously weak. Explain. [3]*

*(c) Explain, in terms of bond energy, why N₂ is very unreactive. [1]*

(a) The C≡C bond has a **bond order of three** (one sigma and two pi bonds) compared with one for C–C. There is **more shared electron density between the two nuclei**, giving a **stronger electrostatic attraction between the shared electrons and both positively charged nuclei**. This pulls the nuclei **closer together, so the bond is shorter**, and it also means **more energy is needed to break it, so the bond is stronger**. [2]

(b) Fluorine is a **very small atom**, so the **lone pairs on the two bonded fluorine atoms are held close together**. This causes strong **lone pair–lone pair repulsion**, which **weakens the F–F bond** and makes it **anomalously weak**. [3]

(c) Nitrogen has a very strong **N≡N triple bond with a very high bond energy**, so a **large amount of energy is needed to break it**, which makes nitrogen **unreactive**. [1]

## Q3
*(a) Draw dot-and-cross diagrams for BF₃ and SF₆, and state how each relates to the octet rule. [4]*

*(b) BF₃ reacts with NH₃ to form a single compound. Draw the bond that forms and explain its nature. [3]*

(a) In BF₃, boron has only **six electrons** in its outer shell (three bond pairs), so it is **electron-deficient** (below the octet). In SF₆, sulfur has **twelve electrons** (six bond pairs), so it has an **expanded octet**, which is possible because **sulfur is in Period 3 and can use energetically accessible d orbitals**. [4]

(b) The **lone pair on the nitrogen atom is donated into the empty orbital on boron**, forming a **co-ordinate (dative) bond**. **Both of the shared electrons come from the nitrogen atom**, and once formed the bond is **identical to a normal covalent bond**. [3]

## Q4
*(a) Define electronegativity. [1]*

*(b) Both are chlorides, yet NaCl melts at 801 °C while AlCl₃ sublimes at 178 °C. Explain this large difference in terms of bonding. [4]*

(a) Electronegativity is the **ability of an atom to attract the bonding (shared) pair of electrons in a covalent bond towards itself.** [1]

(b) Sodium chloride is **ionic**, with a **giant ionic lattice** held together by **strong electrostatic forces of attraction between the Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions**, so it has a **high melting point**. In aluminium chloride, the **Al³⁺ ion is small and highly charged**, giving it a **high charge density and strong polarising power**, so it **polarises the electron cloud of the chloride ion (Fajans)**. This gives the bonding **significant covalent character**, so AlCl₃ is **simple molecular**, and **only weak dispersion forces need to be overcome**, giving a **low sublimation temperature**. [4]

## Q5
*Lattice energies / kJ mol⁻¹: NaF −918, NaCl −780, MgO −3791, CaO −3401.*

*(a) Explain why NaF has a more exothermic lattice energy than NaCl. [2]*

*(b) Explain why MgO has a much more exothermic lattice energy than NaF. [2]*

*(c) Explain why MgO has a more exothermic lattice energy than CaO. [1]*

Lattice energy depends on the ionic charges and radii through **lattice energy ∝ (charge of cation × charge of anion) / (sum of the ionic radii, r⁺ + r⁻)**.

(a) The charges are the same (+1 and −1), but the **fluoride ion is smaller than the chloride ion**, so the **sum of the ionic radii is smaller**. By the relationship, a **smaller denominator gives a more exothermic lattice energy**, so NaF is more exothermic than NaCl. [2]

(b) In MgO the ions have **higher charges (+2 and −2, a charge product of 4 compared with 1 for NaF)** and **smaller radii**. By the relationship, the **larger numerator and smaller denominator make MgO much more exothermic** than NaF. [2]

(c) The charges are the same (+2 and −2), but the **Mg²⁺ ion is smaller than the Ca²⁺ ion**, so the **sum of the ionic radii is smaller**, making **MgO more exothermic** than CaO. [1]

## Q6
*(a) Explain, in terms of metallic bonding, why melting point increases Na < Mg < Al. [3]*

*(b) Explain why magnesium conducts electricity better than sodium. [1]*

(a) Going from sodium to magnesium to aluminium, each atom contributes **more delocalised valence electrons (one, then two, then three)** and the cation has a **higher charge and smaller radius, and so a higher charge density**. This gives **stronger metallic bonding**, so **more energy is needed to overcome it** and the **melting point increases**. [3]

(b) Magnesium contributes **two delocalised electrons per atom** compared with one for sodium, so it has **more mobile charge carriers** and is the **better conductor**. [1]

## Q7
*(a) State what is electrostatically attracted to what in an ionic bond, a covalent bond, and a metallic bond. [3]*

*(b) Hence explain why a molten ionic compound conducts electricity but a molten simple molecular substance does not. [2]*

(a) In an ionic bond the attraction is between the **oppositely charged ions (cations and anions)**. In a covalent bond it is between the **shared pair of electrons and the two positively charged nuclei**. In a metallic bond it is between the **lattice of positive metal ions (cations) and the sea of delocalised electrons**. [3]

(b) When an ionic compound is molten, the **ions are free to move**, so they act as **mobile charge carriers** and it **conducts electricity**. A molten simple molecular substance is made of **neutral molecules with no ions and no free electrons**, so there are **no mobile charge carriers** and it **does not conduct**. [2]
