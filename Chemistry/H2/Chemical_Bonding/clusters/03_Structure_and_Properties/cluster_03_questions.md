## Q1
*Iodine, magnesium, sodium chloride and silicon(IV) oxide each have a different structure.*

*(a) State the structure and bonding in each. [4]*

*(b) Arrange them in order of increasing melting point and explain the order. [4]*

(a) Iodine — **simple molecular**. Magnesium — **giant metallic**. Sodium chloride — **giant ionic**. Silicon(IV) oxide — **giant molecular (giant covalent)**. [4]

(b) Increasing melting point: **iodine < magnesium < sodium chloride < silicon(IV) oxide**.
Iodine is simple molecular, so only the **weak intermolecular forces between the molecules are overcome**, which requires **little energy**.
Magnesium is giant metallic, so the **strong attraction between the metal cations and the delocalised electrons** must be overcome.
Sodium chloride is giant ionic, so the **strong electrostatic attractions between the oppositely charged ions** must be overcome, needing **more energy**.
Silicon(IV) oxide is giant covalent, so a **large amount of energy is needed to break the many strong covalent bonds between the atoms**, giving it the highest melting point. [4]

## Q2
*(a) Explain why sodium chloride conducts electricity when molten but not when solid. [2]*

*(b) Explain why graphite conducts electricity but diamond does not. [3]*

*(c) Explain why sodium conducts electricity in the solid state. [2]*

(a) When solid, the **ions are in fixed positions and are not free to move**, so it does not conduct. When molten, the **ions are free to move and act as mobile charge carriers**, so it conducts. [2]

(b) In graphite each carbon is bonded to **only three other carbons**, and the **unused electron on each carbon forms a delocalised π system in which the delocalised electrons are free to move along the layers and act as mobile charge carriers**, so graphite conducts. In diamond each carbon is bonded to **four other carbons and all the valence electrons are held in localised covalent bonds**, so there are **no mobile charge carriers** and it does not conduct. [3]

(c) Sodium has **delocalised electrons that are free to move and act as mobile charge carriers**, so it conducts in the solid state. [2]

## Q3
*Explain, in terms of structure and bonding, why an ionic solid is brittle but a metal is malleable. [5]*

An ionic solid is **brittle** because when a force is applied it **forces ions of like charge next to each other**, and the **repulsion between the like charges causes the lattice to shatter**. A metal is **malleable** because when a force is applied the **layers of ions slide over one another into new positions**; the **sea of delocalised electrons prevents repulsion between the cations as they move past one another**, so the **metallic bonding remains intact** and the metal deforms instead of breaking apart. [5]

## Q4
*(a) Explain why graphite conducts electricity along its layers but not in the direction at right angles to the layers. [3]*

*(b) Explain why graphite is soft and slippery. [2]*

(a) Within each layer the **delocalised electrons are free to move along the layer and act as mobile charge carriers**, so graphite conducts along the layers. The **delocalised electrons are unable to jump from one layer to the next**, so graphite does not conduct in the direction at right angles to the layers. [3]

(b) Graphite is soft and slippery because the **layers are held together only by weak dispersion forces** and can **slide over each other**. [2]

## Q5
*(a) Explain, in terms of structure and bonding, why iodine has a much lower melting point than silicon(IV) oxide. [3]*

*(b) Explain why iodine does not conduct electricity. [2]*

(a) Iodine is **simple molecular**, so only the **weak intermolecular forces between the molecules need to be overcome**, requiring little energy. Silicon(IV) oxide is **giant covalent**, so a **large amount of energy is needed to break the many strong covalent bonds between the atoms**, giving it a much higher melting point. [3]

(b) Iodine does not conduct because it has **no mobile charge carriers** — there are **no ions and no delocalised electrons**. [2]

## Q6
*A solid X has a high melting point, does not conduct when solid but conducts when molten, and dissolves in water.*

*(a) Deduce the structure and bonding in X, giving your reasoning. [4]*

*(b) Explain why X does not conduct electricity when solid. [2]*

(a) X is **giant ionic**. The **high melting point** shows a giant lattice held by strong bonding. The fact that it **does not conduct when solid but conducts when molten** shows it contains **ions that are fixed in the solid but become mobile charge carriers when molten**. **Solubility in water** is typical of an ionic compound. [4]

(b) X does not conduct when solid because the **ions are held in fixed positions and are not free to move**. [2]
