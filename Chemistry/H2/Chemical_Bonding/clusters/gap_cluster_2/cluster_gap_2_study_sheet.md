## PART A — Fixes (full mark-scheme wording)

**1. The F–F anomaly.**
Fluorine is a very small atom, so the lone pairs on the two bonded fluorine atoms are held very close together. This causes strong **lone pair–lone pair repulsion** between the two fluorine atoms, which weakens the bond. So although the F–F bond is shorter than Cl–Cl, it is **anomalously weak** and has a lower bond energy. The trap to remember: a small atom would normally give a stronger bond, but here the lone-pair repulsion overrides that and makes it weaker.

**2. Expanded octet and the period rule.**
An atom can only **expand its octet** (hold more than eight electrons in its outer shell) if it is in **Period 3 or below**, because only then does it have energetically accessible **d orbitals**. So **SF₆ can expand its octet** (sulfur is in Period 3 and holds twelve electrons). **BF₃ does not expand** anything — boron is in **Period 2** and instead has an **incomplete octet** of only six electrons, which is called electron-deficient. These are two different exceptions: BF₃ is electron-deficient because boron is Period 2 and can never expand, while SF₆ is expanded because sulfur is Period 3 and can. Note the correction: boron is in **Period 2**, not Period 3.

**3. Shorter and stronger are two separate statements.**
A triple bond has three shared pairs of electrons between the two nuclei, compared with one shared pair in a single bond. The greater shared electron density gives a **stronger electrostatic attraction between the shared electrons and the two positively charged nuclei**. This pulls the nuclei closer together, so the bond is **shorter**. The same stronger attraction also means that **more energy is required to break the bond**, so the bond is **stronger**. Both come from the same cause (more bonding electron density) but you state them separately: shorter means a smaller internuclear distance, stronger means a higher bond energy.

**4. Why N₂ is unreactive (say it with confidence).**
Nitrogen has a **triple bond, N≡N, with a very high bond energy**. A large amount of energy is needed to break this bond before nitrogen can react, so nitrogen has a **high activation energy** for its reactions and is therefore **very unreactive**. Your answer was right. Just commit to it: strong triple bond, very high bond energy, hard to break, unreactive.

**5. AlCl₃ is simple molecular (the step you left out).**
Your covalent-character reasoning was excellent. The final step you missed: because Al³⁺ so strongly polarises the chloride ions, AlCl₃ is not a giant ionic lattice at all, it is a **simple molecular** substance. That is why it sublimes at such a low temperature, because only the **weak dispersion forces between the molecules** are overcome, not a giant lattice. The full chain in order: Al³⁺ has a high charge density, so it has a strong polarising power, which distorts the electron cloud of the chloride ion, giving the bonding significant covalent character, making AlCl₃ **simple molecular**, so only weak intermolecular forces hold it together, so it sublimes at a low temperature.

**6. Molten conductivity.**
When an ionic compound is molten, the **ions are free to move**, so they act as **mobile charge carriers** and the liquid conducts electricity. A simple molecular substance is made of **neutral molecules**. Its covalent bonds are **within** each molecule, not between molecules, and there are no ions and no free electrons, so there are **no mobile charge carriers** and it does not conduct even when molten. Watch the wording: the covalent bonds are inside each molecule, while the weak forces between molecules are the intermolecular forces.
