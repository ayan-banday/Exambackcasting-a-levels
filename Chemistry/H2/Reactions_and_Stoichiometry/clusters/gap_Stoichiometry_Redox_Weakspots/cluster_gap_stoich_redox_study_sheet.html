Question 1

Calculate the total number of ions present in \( 2.50\ \text{g} \) of anhydrous sodium carbonate, \(\ce{Na2CO3}\). [3]
Solution
Step 1
Moles of \(\ce{Na2CO3}\); \(M_r = 2(23.0)+12.0+3(16.0)=106.0\). M1
\( n = 2.50/106.0 = 0.023585\ \text{mol} \)
Step 2
Ions per formula unit = 2 \(\ce{Na+}\) + 1 \(\ce{CO3^2-}\) = 3 ions (the \(\ce{CO3^2-}\) is ONE ion, not 4). M1
\( n(\text{ions}) = 0.023585 \times 3 = 0.070755\ \text{mol} \)
Step 3
\( \times L \). A1
\( 0.070755 \times 6.02\times10^{23} = 4.26\times10^{22} \)
Solution hidden — click Reveal
What this drilled
Formula units → ions. Yesterday you circled this — "do I multiply by the coefficient?" Here's the rule cold: count the ions the formula dissociates into (\(\ce{Na2CO3}\to 2\ce{Na+}+\ce{CO3^2-}\) = 3), a polyatomic ion counts as one, then \(\times n \times L\). "Formula units" = \(n \times L\); "ions" = that \(\times\) ions-per-unit.

Question 2

\( 25.0\ \text{cm}^3 \) of a gaseous hydrocarbon \(\ce{C}_x\ce{H}_y\) is mixed with \( 200\ \text{cm}^3 \) of oxygen (an excess) and sparked. After cooling to the original room temperature and pressure, the residual gas has a volume of \( 150.0\ \text{cm}^3 \). On shaking with aqueous KOH the volume falls to \( 75.0\ \text{cm}^3 \). Deduce the molecular formula. [4]
Solution
Step 1 — read the two clues
On cooling, water is liquid (drop it from the gas volumes). KOH absorbs \(\ce{CO2}\), so \(\ce{CO2} = 150.0-75.0 = 75.0\); the \(75.0\) left is unused \(\ce{O2}\). M1
Step 2 — contraction table
Track volumes (all cm³, same T,P). M1
\(\ce{C}_x\ce{H}_y\)\(\ce{O2}\)\(\ce{CO2}\)\(\ce{H2O}(l)\)
initial25.020000
final075.075.0—
reacted−25.0−125+75.0—
Step 3 — find x
\(\ce{C}_x\ce{H}_y:\ce{CO2}=25.0:75.0=1:3\). M1
\( x = 3 \)
Step 4 — find y from the O₂ used
\(\ce{O2}\) used \(=125\), ratio to hydrocarbon \(=125/25=5 = x + \dfrac{y}{4}\). A1
\( 5 = 3 + \dfrac{y}{4} \Rightarrow y = 8 \Rightarrow \mathbf{\ce{C3H8}} \)
Trap: after cooling, water is liquid → it does NOT count in the residual gas. If the question said "measured at 120 °C" the water is a gas and stays in the table.

Question 3

\( 0.440\ \text{g} \) of a hydrocarbon burns completely to give \( 1.320\ \text{g} \) of \(\ce{CO2}\) and \( 0.720\ \text{g} \) of \(\ce{H2O}\). Its relative molecular mass is \( 44.0 \).
(i) Determine its molecular formula. [3]
(ii) Hence calculate the volume of oxygen, at s.t.p., needed to burn \( 0.440\ \text{g} \) of it completely. [2]

Step 1 — (i) moles of C and H
All C → \(\ce{CO2}\), all H → \(\ce{H2O}\). Note \(\ce{H2O}\) has 2 H. M1
\( n(\ce{C}) = 1.320/44.0 = 0.03000 \); \( n(\ce{H}) = 2 \times 0.720/18.0 = 0.08000 \)
Step 2 — ratio → EF → MF
\(\ce{C}:\ce{H}=0.03000:0.08000=3:8\); EF \(=\ce{C3H8}\ (M_r 44.0)\), and \(44.0/44.0=1\). M1 A1
\( \mathbf{\ce{C3H8}} \)
Step 3 — (ii) carry the MF forward
\(\ce{C3H8 + 5O2 -> 3CO2 + 4H2O}\); \( n(\ce{C3H8}) = 0.440/44.0 = 0.01000 \). M1
\( n(\ce{O2}) = 5 \times 0.01000 = 0.05000 \)
Step 4
A1
\( V = 0.05000 \times 22.7 = 1.14\ \text{dm}^3 \)

Carry the earlier answer forward + the ×2 for H. You said this is where you struggle — restarting from scratch instead of reusing part (i). Part (ii) is impossible without the MF from (i). And \(n(\ce{H}) = 2\times n(\ce{H2O})\) because water holds two H — the ×2 you flagged.

Question 4

A \( 0.500\ \text{g} \) sample of impure sodium hydrogencarbonate is dissolved and reacted with \( 40.0\ \text{cm}^3 \) of \( 0.200\ \text{mol dm}^{-3} \) hydrochloric acid (an excess). \(\ce{NaHCO3 + HCl -> NaCl + H2O + CO2}\). The unreacted acid requires \( 18.0\ \text{cm}^3 \) of \( 0.150\ \text{mol dm}^{-3} \) sodium hydroxide to neutralise. Calculate the percentage purity of the sodium hydrogencarbonate. [4]
Solution
Step 1
Total acid, then excess (= acid that met the NaOH, 1:1). M1 M1
\( n(\ce{HCl})_\text{total}=0.200\times0.0400=8.000\times10^{-3} \)
\( n(\ce{HCl})_\text{excess}=n(\ce{NaOH})=0.150\times0.0180=2.700\times10^{-3} \)
Step 2
Acid that reacted with the solid; \(\ce{NaHCO3}:\ce{HCl}=1:1\). M1
\( n(\ce{HCl})_\text{reacted}=8.000\times10^{-3}-2.700\times10^{-3}=5.300\times10^{-3} \Rightarrow n(\ce{NaHCO3})=5.300\times10^{-3} \)
Step 3 — finish the job
Mass then %, \(M_r(\ce{NaHCO3})=84.0\). A1
\( m = 5.300\times10^{-3}\times84.0=0.4452\ \text{g} \Rightarrow \dfrac{0.4452}{0.500}\times100 = 89.0\% \)
Trap (your own): don't stop at moles or mass — the answer is a percentage. Yesterday you did the hard middle right on the back-titration and dropped the last step. Total − excess = reacted → moles → mass → %.

What this drilled
Finishing a back-titration. The chain is always total − excess = reacted, then all the way through to the % the question asks for. The examiner puts the last mark on the final conversion — that's the one you left on the table.

Question 5

(i) Calculate the mass of nitrogen present. [2]
(ii) Calculate the number of molecules present. [1]

Step 3 — (ii)
A1

Question 6

Calculate the number of ions present in \( 4.30\ \text{g} \) of hydrated sodium sulfate, \(\ce{Na2SO4.10H2O}\). [3]
Solution
Step 1
Include the water of crystallisation in \(M_r\): \(M_r = 142.1 + 10(18.0) = 322.1\). M1
\( n = 4.30/322.1 = 0.013350\ \text{mol} \)
Step 2
Ions per formula unit = 2 \(\ce{Na+}\) + 1 \(\ce{SO4^2-}\) = 3. The \(\ce{10H2O}\) is not ionic — it does not count. M1
\( n(\text{ions}) = 0.013350\times3 = 0.040050 \)
Step 3
A1
\( 0.040050\times6.02\times10^{23} = 2.41\times10^{22} \)
Trap: the \(\ce{10H2O}\) must be in the \(M_r\) (it's part of the mass) but must NOT be counted as ions (water is covalent, stays as molecules).

What this drilled
Formula units, harder. Same idea as Q1 but the hydrate splits the two roles of the water: it adds to the mass (so it's in \(M_r\)) yet contributes zero ions. Get both right and formula units are dead to you.

Question 7

An iron tablet of mass \( 1.00\ \text{g} \) is dissolved in excess dilute sulfuric acid and the solution made up to \( 100\ \text{cm}^3 \). A \( 25.0\ \text{cm}^3 \) portion is titrated with \( 0.0200\ \text{mol dm}^{-3} \) acidified \(\ce{KMnO4}\); \( 21.50\ \text{cm}^3 \) is required.
\(\ce{MnO4- + 5Fe^2+ + 8H+ -> Mn^2+ + 5Fe^3+ + 4H2O}\)
Calculate the percentage by mass of iron in the tablet. [4]
Solution
Step 1
Moles \(\ce{MnO4-}\). M1
\( n(\ce{MnO4-}) = 0.0200\times0.02150 = 4.300\times10^{-4} \)
Step 2
Ratio \(\ce{MnO4-}:\ce{Fe^2+}=1:5\), in the \(25.0\ \text{cm}^3\) portion. M1
\( n(\ce{Fe^2+})_{25} = 5\times4.300\times10^{-4} = 2.150\times10^{-3} \)
Step 3 — scale to the whole tablet
\( 25.0\ \text{cm}^3 \) is \(\tfrac14\) of \( 100\ \text{cm}^3 \), so \(\times 4\). M1
\( n(\ce{Fe})_\text{tablet} = 4\times2.150\times10^{-3} = 8.600\times10^{-3} \)
Step 4 — finish
Mass then %; \(A_r(\ce{Fe})=55.8\). A1
\( m = 8.600\times10^{-3}\times55.8 = 0.4799\ \text{g} \Rightarrow \dfrac{0.4799}{1.00}\times100 = 48.0\% \)
Solution hidden — click Reveal
What this drilled
"One question solves many" — the carry-forward chain. Four linked steps off one titre: titre → \(\ce{MnO4-}\) → \(\ce{Fe^2+}\) (×5) → whole tablet (×4 for the aliquot) → mass → %. Miss the \(\times 4\) aliquot scaling or stop before the % and you lose it. This is exactly the multi-step threading you flagged.
