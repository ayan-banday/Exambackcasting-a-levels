# Pre-Study Sheet — CSE H2 > Governance

> Big picture only. Light. Read before diving into clusters. Skip if you've done your own pre-study.

---

## What this topic is fundamentally about

**How does the Chinese Communist Party maintain political power while managing complex governance challenges?** The party-state system (monopoly power + centralized decision-making) gives the CCP the tools to act quickly—but it also creates the problems it must solve: unchecked corruption, fiscal misalignment between centre and local governments, weak rule of law, ethnic tensions. The system's legitimacy rests on **performance** (economic growth, poverty reduction) more than ideology alone. This tension between effectiveness and fragility is the spine of every governance essay.

---

## How the clusters connect

- **Party-State System & Legitimacy** — the foundational layer. Understand why the CCP has monopoly power, how it justifies it (ideology + performance), and why this structure shapes all other governance challenges. Everything below depends on this.

- **Anti-Corruption (反腐)** — the most visible governance challenge and most-tested node. Corruption erodes both performance legitimacy and public trust. The party's response (运动式反腐 vs 制度反腐) reveals the broader tension: rapid enforcement vs. deep reform. High-yield for 2026.

- **Central-Local Relations (央地关系)** — structural problem baked into the 1994 fiscal system: centre collects revenue, locals bear spending → locals must find informal revenue (land sales, debt) → systemic risk. This is the hardest problem to solve without restructuring the entire system.

- **Rule of Law & Judicial Reform (法治/司法改革)** — the party's contradiction: it wants rule **of law** (governance by law) but refuses rule **by law** (constraints on party power). Judicial reform moves forward (员额制, circuit courts) but hits the ceiling: judges still answer to party committees, not courts. This limits all other reforms.

- **Ethnic Minority Governance (少数民族问题)** — the highest-stakes governance challenge for soft power. Official strategy (assimilation + economic development) clashes with international criticism and minority identity concerns. The July 2026 Ethnic Unity Law is a live event at exam time.

- **Political Participation & Internet Governance (政治参与/网络治理)** — how the party channels dissent (信访, 网络问政, 12345 hotlines, 协商民主) without sharing power. The National Cyber ID (July 2025) is new teachable material for this cluster.

**The throughline:** All six clusters are **responses to the party-state system's inherent tension**: it is **effective at mobilization** (can lock down quickly, collect taxes, execute policy) but **fragile at legitimacy** (no way to correct errors, no checks on abuse, depends entirely on performance delivery). When performance is strong (2000–2015), this works. When growth slows (post-2015), the system reveals its limits.

---

## The single most important concept

**Legitimacy = Performance > Ideology.**

The CCP's right to rule is primarily earned through delivering results (GDP growth, poverty reduction, national strength), secondarily supported by its ideology (中国特色社会主义). When the economy grows, the party barely needs to mention ideology—results speak. When growth falters (as it has post-2015), the party must actively push ideology (two established truths, 2021) to fill the gap. This hierarchy explains why:

- Economic slowdown is the party's **biggest governance risk** — not a side issue, but the core legitimacy threat.
- Anti-corruption campaigns are performative (running up prosecution numbers restores faith temporarily) but structurally limited (they don't address unchecked power itself).
- Ethnic minority policy faces international backlash precisely because it prioritizes party control (three evils: 分裂/极端/恐怖) over minority voice — if performance legitimacy (economic development in Xinjiang) were truly working, the party could afford to listen more.

**Exam implication:** Any governance essay that doesn't ground itself in this legitimacy hierarchy will miss the analytical spine. The most likely 2026 question will either directly test legitimacy (因素是非题: ideology vs. performance) or use legitimacy as the hidden benchmark against which other challenges are measured.
