Card 1 — Trig Powers: Identity-First Reflex

How the exam asks it
Any integrand with \( \sin^{2}, \cos^{2}, \tan^{2}, \sin^{2}(ax+b), \cos^{2}(kx) \), etc. Even part of a longer chain (substitution-led integral that ends up with \( \cos^{2}\theta \)).
Concept
Trig powers have NO direct antiderivative formula. You ALWAYS rewrite using a double-angle identity first, then integrate. The single exception is \( \sec^{2}x \) — that integrates directly to \( \tan x + C \) because it's literally the derivative of \( \tan x \) (and similarly \( \csc^{2}x \to -\cot x \), though not in MF27). Identities to remember: \( \sin^{2}x = \tfrac{1-\cos 2x}{2},\;\cos^{2}x = \tfrac{1+\cos 2x}{2},\;\tan^{2}x = \sec^{2}x - 1 \).
Example
Find \( \displaystyle\int 4\cos^{2}(3x)\,dx \). [4]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Apply the identity \( \cos^{2}\theta = \tfrac{1+\cos 2\theta}{2} \) with \( \theta = 3x \). M1
\( 4\cos^{2}(3x) = 4 \cdot \dfrac{1 + \cos 6x}{2} = 2 + 2\cos 6x \).
Step 2
Integrate term by term. Recall \( \int \cos(kx)\,dx = \tfrac{1}{k}\sin(kx) \). M1 A1
\( \int (2 + 2\cos 6x)\,dx = 2x + 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{6}\sin 6x + C = 2x + \tfrac{1}{3}\sin 6x + C \).
Step 3
Don't forget \( +C \). A1
\[ \int 4\cos^{2}(3x)\,dx = 2x + \tfrac{1}{3}\sin 6x + C \]

Practice
Find \( \displaystyle\int (3\sin^{2}x + 2\sec^{2}x)\,dx \). [4]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
For \( 3\sin^{2}x \), apply identity. For \( 2\sec^{2}x \), integrate directly (do NOT rewrite as \( 1/\cos^{2}x \)). M1
\( 3\sin^{2}x = 3 \cdot \dfrac{1 - \cos 2x}{2} = \dfrac{3}{2} - \dfrac{3}{2}\cos 2x \).
\( 2\sec^{2}x \) stays as is — its antiderivative is \( 2\tan x \).
Step 2
Integrate each piece. M1 A1
\( \int \tfrac{3}{2}\,dx = \tfrac{3}{2}x \).
\( \int -\tfrac{3}{2}\cos 2x\,dx = -\tfrac{3}{2} \cdot \tfrac{1}{2}\sin 2x = -\tfrac{3}{4}\sin 2x \).
\( \int 2\sec^{2}x\,dx = 2\tan x \).
Step 3
Combine. A1
\[ = \tfrac{3}{2}x - \tfrac{3}{4}\sin 2x + 2\tan x + C \]

Card 2 — Standard MF27 Forms with Non-Unit Coefficients

How the exam asks it
Integrals like \( \dfrac{1}{4 + 9x^{2}},\;\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{9 - 4x^{2}}},\;\dfrac{1}{25 - 16x^{2}} \). The constant in the denominator is NOT 1, AND \( x^{2} \) has a coefficient. You can't read \( a \) straight off the formula.
Concept
For all MF27 standard forms (\( \dfrac{1}{a^{2} \pm x^{2}},\;\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{a^{2} - x^{2}}} \)), the variable inside MUST be pure \( x^{2} \) (no coefficient). Two-step procedure: (1) FACTOR out the coefficient of \( x^{2} \) so the inside reads \( a^{2} + x^{2} \) (or similar). (2) Identify \( a \) by square-rooting the resulting constant. \( a \) is the number, \( a^{2} \) is its square. Confusing the two costs an A1 every time.
Example
Find \( \displaystyle\int \dfrac{1}{16 + 25x^{2}}\,dx \). [3]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Factor out the coefficient of \( x^{2} \) so the inside reads as constant + \( x^{2} \). M1
\( 16 + 25x^{2} = 25\!\left(\dfrac{16}{25} + x^{2}\right) \).
Step 2
Identify \( a^{2} \) and \( a \). Here \( a^{2} = \tfrac{16}{25} \), so \( a = \tfrac{4}{5} \).
Step 3
Pull constant out and apply MF27 form \( \int \tfrac{1}{a^{2}+x^{2}}\,dx = \tfrac{1}{a}\tan^{-1}\!\tfrac{x}{a} \). M1
\( \int \dfrac{1}{16 + 25x^{2}}\,dx = \dfrac{1}{25}\int \dfrac{1}{(4/5)^{2} + x^{2}}\,dx = \dfrac{1}{25} \cdot \dfrac{1}{4/5}\tan^{-1}\!\dfrac{x}{4/5} + C \)
Step 4
Simplify the constants and the inner fraction. A1
\[ = \dfrac{1}{25} \cdot \dfrac{5}{4}\tan^{-1}\!\dfrac{5x}{4} + C = \tfrac{1}{20}\tan^{-1}\!\dfrac{5x}{4} + C \]

Practice
Find \( \displaystyle\int \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{9 - 4x^{2}}}\,dx \). [3]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
Factor inside the square root. M1
\( 9 - 4x^{2} = 4\!\left(\dfrac{9}{4} - x^{2}\right) \), so \( \sqrt{9 - 4x^{2}} = 2\sqrt{\tfrac{9}{4} - x^{2}} \).
Step 2
\( a^{2} = \tfrac{9}{4} \Rightarrow a = \tfrac{3}{2} \).
Step 3
Pull out and apply MF27 form \( \int \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{a^{2}-x^{2}}}\,dx = \sin^{-1}\!\tfrac{x}{a} \). M1 A1
\[ \int \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{9 - 4x^{2}}}\,dx = \dfrac{1}{2}\int \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{(3/2)^{2} - x^{2}}}\,dx = \tfrac{1}{2}\sin^{-1}\!\dfrac{2x}{3} + C \]

Card 3 — Substitution Chains with Mid-Way Trig Identity

How the exam asks it
"Use the substitution \( x = a\sin\theta \) (or \( x = a\tan\theta \)) to find \( \int \sqrt{a^{2} - x^{2}}\,dx \) (or similar)." After substituting, the integrand becomes \( a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta \) or \( a^{2}\sec^{2}\theta \) — STOP and apply the trig identity before integrating.
Concept
Two techniques chained: substitution to remove the radical, then trig-identity rewrite to integrate the resulting trig power. The trap: forgetting Step 2 (identity rewrite) and trying to integrate \( \cos^{2}\theta \) directly. Also: when \( x = a\sin\theta \), it's \( \dfrac{dx}{d\theta} = a\cos\theta \) (x is the one given, so x is the subject of derivative).
Example
Use the substitution \( x = 3\sin\theta \) to find \( \displaystyle\int \sqrt{9 - x^{2}}\,dx \). [6]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Differentiate the substitution. Note it is \( \dfrac{dx}{d\theta} \) (x is what was given). B1
\( x = 3\sin\theta \Rightarrow \dfrac{dx}{d\theta} = 3\cos\theta \Rightarrow dx = 3\cos\theta\,d\theta \).
Step 2
Simplify the radical using \( \sin^{2} + \cos^{2} = 1 \). M1
\( \sqrt{9 - x^{2}} = \sqrt{9 - 9\sin^{2}\theta} = \sqrt{9\cos^{2}\theta} = 3\cos\theta \) (positive root).
Step 3
Substitute everything.
\( \int \sqrt{9 - x^{2}}\,dx = \int 3\cos\theta \cdot 3\cos\theta\,d\theta = \int 9\cos^{2}\theta\,d\theta \).
Step 4
STOP. \( \cos^{2}\theta \) needs the identity. Apply \( \cos^{2}\theta = \tfrac{1+\cos 2\theta}{2} \). M1
\( 9\cos^{2}\theta = \tfrac{9}{2}(1 + \cos 2\theta) \).
Step 5
Integrate. M1
\( \int \tfrac{9}{2}(1 + \cos 2\theta)\,d\theta = \tfrac{9}{2}\theta + \tfrac{9}{4}\sin 2\theta + C \).
Step 6
Back-substitute. \( \theta = \sin^{-1}\!\tfrac{x}{3} \). For \( \sin 2\theta = 2\sin\theta\cos\theta \): \( \sin\theta = \tfrac{x}{3} \), \( \cos\theta = \tfrac{\sqrt{9-x^{2}}}{3} \), so \( \sin 2\theta = \tfrac{2x\sqrt{9-x^{2}}}{9} \). A1
\[ \int \sqrt{9 - x^{2}}\,dx = \tfrac{9}{2}\sin^{-1}\!\tfrac{x}{3} + \tfrac{9}{4} \cdot \tfrac{2x\sqrt{9-x^{2}}}{9} + C = \tfrac{9}{2}\sin^{-1}\!\tfrac{x}{3} + \tfrac{x}{2}\sqrt{9-x^{2}} + C \]

Practice
Use the substitution \( x = \sin\theta \) to find \( \displaystyle\int \sqrt{1 - x^{2}}\,dx \). [5]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
Differentiate substitution. B1
\( \dfrac{dx}{d\theta} = \cos\theta \Rightarrow dx = \cos\theta\,d\theta \).
Step 2
Simplify radical.
\( \sqrt{1 - x^{2}} = \sqrt{1 - \sin^{2}\theta} = \cos\theta \).
Step 3
Substitute. M1
\( \int \cos\theta \cdot \cos\theta\,d\theta = \int \cos^{2}\theta\,d\theta \).
Step 4
Apply identity, then integrate. M1
\( \int \tfrac{1+\cos 2\theta}{2}\,d\theta = \tfrac{1}{2}\theta + \tfrac{1}{4}\sin 2\theta + C \).
Step 5
Back-substitute. \( \theta = \sin^{-1}x \), \( \sin 2\theta = 2x\sqrt{1-x^{2}} \). A1
\[ \int \sqrt{1-x^{2}}\,dx = \tfrac{1}{2}\sin^{-1}x + \tfrac{x}{2}\sqrt{1-x^{2}} + C \]

Card 4 — IBP Execution Discipline

How the exam asks it
Integrand is a product of two distinct types of functions (e.g. \( x\,e^{x},\;x\ln x,\;x\sin x,\;x^{2}\ln x \)). The exam expects clean IBP working with the formula stated.
Concept
Three habits that prevent dropped marks:
  1. Memorise the formula: \( \int u\,dv = uv - \int v\,du \). Write it at the top of your working.
  2. LIATE for \( u \): Log, Inverse-trig, Algebraic, Trig, Exponential — earliest in the list goes as \( u \).
  3. Simplify \( v\,du \) algebraically BEFORE integrating. If \( v = \tfrac{x^{2}}{2} \) and \( du = \tfrac{1}{x}\,dx \), then \( v\,du = \tfrac{x}{2}\,dx \) — cancel before integrating, don't bury the simplification inside the integral.
Example
Find \( \displaystyle\int x^{2}\ln x\,dx \). [5]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
LIATE: Log beats Algebraic. B1
\( u = \ln x,\; dv = x^{2}\,dx \).
Step 2
Differentiate \( u \), integrate \( dv \).
\( du = \dfrac{1}{x}\,dx,\; v = \dfrac{x^{3}}{3} \).
Step 3
Apply the formula. M1
\( \int x^{2}\ln x\,dx = \dfrac{x^{3}}{3}\ln x - \int \dfrac{x^{3}}{3} \cdot \dfrac{1}{x}\,dx \).
Step 4
SIMPLIFY the integrand of the remaining integral BEFORE integrating. M1
\( \dfrac{x^{3}}{3} \cdot \dfrac{1}{x} = \dfrac{x^{2}}{3} \). So the remaining integral is \( \int \dfrac{x^{2}}{3}\,dx \).
Step 5
Integrate and combine. A1
\( \int \dfrac{x^{2}}{3}\,dx = \dfrac{x^{3}}{9} \). \[ \int x^{2}\ln x\,dx = \dfrac{x^{3}}{3}\ln x - \dfrac{x^{3}}{9} + C \]

Practice
Find \( \displaystyle\int x\cos 2x\,dx \). [4]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
LIATE: Algebraic beats Trig. B1
\( u = x,\; dv = \cos 2x\,dx \).
Step 2
Differentiate / integrate.
\( du = dx,\; v = \tfrac{1}{2}\sin 2x \).
Step 3
Apply formula. M1
\( \int x\cos 2x\,dx = \tfrac{x}{2}\sin 2x - \int \tfrac{1}{2}\sin 2x\,dx \).
Step 4
Integrate. M1 A1
\( \int \tfrac{1}{2}\sin 2x\,dx = -\tfrac{1}{4}\cos 2x \). \[ \int x\cos 2x\,dx = \tfrac{x}{2}\sin 2x + \tfrac{1}{4}\cos 2x + C \]

Card 5 — Repeated IBP

How the exam asks it
Integrand is \( x^{2}e^{x},\;x^{2}\sin x,\;(\ln x)^{2} \), or similar — one IBP leaves a remaining integral that needs IBP AGAIN. Also: \( e^{x}\sin x \) type — IBP twice produces a cyclic equation in the original integral.
Concept
Recognise when one IBP isn't enough: if after IBP the remaining \( \int v\,du \) is STILL a product of two distinct function types, apply IBP again. For \( (\ln y)^{2} \), the second IBP integrand is \( \int \ln y\,dy \). For \( x^{n}e^{x} \), each IBP reduces the polynomial power by 1.
Example
Find \( \displaystyle\int x^{2}\,e^{x}\,dx \). [5]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
First IBP. LIATE: \( u = x^{2},\; dv = e^{x}\,dx \). So \( du = 2x\,dx,\; v = e^{x} \). M1
\( \int x^{2}e^{x}\,dx = x^{2}e^{x} - \int 2x\,e^{x}\,dx \).
Step 2
The remaining integral \( \int 2x\,e^{x}\,dx \) is STILL Algebraic × Exponential. IBP again. \( u = 2x,\; dv = e^{x}\,dx \). So \( du = 2\,dx,\; v = e^{x} \). M1
\( \int 2x\,e^{x}\,dx = 2x\,e^{x} - \int 2e^{x}\,dx = 2x\,e^{x} - 2e^{x} \).
Step 3
Substitute back into Step 1's result. M1
\( \int x^{2}e^{x}\,dx = x^{2}e^{x} - (2x\,e^{x} - 2e^{x}) = x^{2}e^{x} - 2x\,e^{x} + 2e^{x} + C \).
Step 4
Factor for tidiness. A1 A1
\[ \int x^{2}e^{x}\,dx = e^{x}(x^{2} - 2x + 2) + C \]

Practice
Find \( \displaystyle\int (\ln x)^{2}\,dx \). [5]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
First IBP. \( u = (\ln x)^{2},\; dv = dx \). So \( du = \dfrac{2\ln x}{x}\,dx,\; v = x \). M1
\( \int (\ln x)^{2}\,dx = x(\ln x)^{2} - \int x \cdot \dfrac{2\ln x}{x}\,dx \).
Step 2
Simplify the new integrand BEFORE integrating: \( x \cdot \dfrac{2\ln x}{x} = 2\ln x \). M1
\( = x(\ln x)^{2} - \int 2\ln x\,dx \).
Step 3
Second IBP on \( \int 2\ln x\,dx \). \( u = \ln x,\; dv = dx \). So \( du = \dfrac{1}{x}\,dx,\; v = x \). M1
\( \int 2\ln x\,dx = 2\!\left[x\ln x - \int x \cdot \dfrac{1}{x}\,dx\right] = 2(x\ln x - x) = 2x\ln x - 2x \).
Step 4
Substitute back into Step 2. A1 A1
\[ \int (\ln x)^{2}\,dx = x(\ln x)^{2} - 2x\ln x + 2x + C \]

Card 6 — Partial Fractions: When to Use

How the exam asks it
Integrand is a rational function (polynomial over polynomial). Question may explicitly ask "express in partial fractions and hence integrate", or may just give the integral and expect you to recognise the trigger.
Concept
Three diagnostic questions, in order:
FormMethod
Polynomial only (no denominator)Integrate term by term using power rule
\( \dfrac{1}{x + a} \) or \( \dfrac{\text{something}}{x+a} \) with single linear factorUse \( \int \tfrac{f'}{f}\,dx = \ln\|f\| \) directly (Block 28)
\( \dfrac{1}{a^{2}+x^{2}},\;\dfrac{1}{a^{2}-x^{2}} \) etc.MF27 standard form (Block 31–34)
\( \dfrac{P(x)}{(x+a)(x+b)\ldots} \) with TWO OR MORE distinct factors in denominatorPartial fractions, then integrate each piece
Partial fractions are the trigger ONLY when the denominator factors into multiple distinct pieces. A single denominator factor → don't bother, use direct integration.
Example
Find \( \displaystyle\int \dfrac{2x + 1}{x^{2} + x - 2}\,dx \). [5]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Factor the denominator.
\( x^{2} + x - 2 = (x+2)(x-1) \). Two distinct linear factors → partial fractions trigger.
Step 2
Decompose. M1
\( \dfrac{2x+1}{(x+2)(x-1)} = \dfrac{A}{x+2} + \dfrac{B}{x-1} \).
Step 3
Clear denominators and solve for \( A, B \). M1
\( 2x + 1 = A(x-1) + B(x+2) \).
At \( x = 1 \): \( 3 = 3B \Rightarrow B = 1 \).
At \( x = -2 \): \( -3 = -3A \Rightarrow A = 1 \).
Step 4
Rewrite and integrate each piece using Block 28's \( \int \tfrac{1}{x+a}\,dx = \ln\|x+a\| \). M1
\( \int \dfrac{1}{x+2}\,dx = \ln|x+2| \); \( \int \dfrac{1}{x-1}\,dx = \ln|x-1| \).
Step 5
Combine using log laws. A1
\[ \int \dfrac{2x+1}{x^{2}+x-2}\,dx = \ln|x+2| + \ln|x-1| + C = \ln|(x+2)(x-1)| + C \]

Practice
Find \( \displaystyle\int \dfrac{5x - 7}{(x-1)(x-2)}\,dx \). [4]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
Decompose. M1
\( \dfrac{5x-7}{(x-1)(x-2)} = \dfrac{A}{x-1} + \dfrac{B}{x-2} \).
Step 2
Solve. M1
\( 5x - 7 = A(x-2) + B(x-1) \).
At \( x = 1 \): \( -2 = -A \Rightarrow A = 2 \).
At \( x = 2 \): \( 3 = B \).
Step 3
Integrate. M1 A1
\[ \int \dfrac{5x-7}{(x-1)(x-2)}\,dx = 2\ln|x-1| + 3\ln|x-2| + C \]

Card 7 — Volume of Revolution Requiring IBP

How the exam asks it
Curve given as \( y = e^{kx} \), \( y = \ln x \), or \( y = x e^{kx} \), rotated about the y-axis. After rearranging \( x = g(y) \) and squaring, the integrand is \( (\ln y)^{2} \), \( y e^{...} \), or similar — needs IBP (often twice).
Concept
The hidden two-step trap: (1) Volume formula gives you \( \pi\int x^{2}\,dy \), so you need \( x \) in terms of \( y \) AND squared. (2) Squaring an exponential or log produces something IBP-shaped. Recognise the chain early so you don't get stuck mid-question.
Example
The region bounded by the curve \( y = e^{2x} \), the y-axis, and the line \( y = e^{2} \) is rotated through \( 360^{\circ} \) about the y-axis. Find the exact volume of the solid formed. [7]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Rearrange the curve to give \( x \) in terms of \( y \). B1
\( y = e^{2x} \Rightarrow 2x = \ln y \Rightarrow x = \tfrac{1}{2}\ln y \). So \( x^{2} = \tfrac{1}{4}(\ln y)^{2} \).
Step 2
Find y-limits. The region runs from where the curve meets the y-axis (\( x = 0 \Rightarrow y = 1 \)) up to \( y = e^{2} \). B1
\( y \) from 1 to \( e^{2} \).
Step 3
Set up the volume integral. M1
\( V = \pi\int_{1}^{e^{2}} x^{2}\,dy = \dfrac{\pi}{4}\int_{1}^{e^{2}} (\ln y)^{2}\,dy \).
Step 4
Recognise \( \int (\ln y)^{2}\,dy \) needs IBP twice. (From Card 5.) M1
\( \int (\ln y)^{2}\,dy = y(\ln y)^{2} - 2y\ln y + 2y + C \).
Step 5
Evaluate at limits. M1
At \( y = e^{2} \): \( e^{2}(2)^{2} - 2e^{2}(2) + 2e^{2} = 4e^{2} - 4e^{2} + 2e^{2} = 2e^{2} \).
At \( y = 1 \): \( 1(0)^{2} - 2(1)(0) + 2(1) = 2 \).
Difference: \( 2e^{2} - 2 \).
Step 6
Multiply by \( \tfrac{\pi}{4} \) and simplify. A1
\[ V = \tfrac{\pi}{4}(2e^{2} - 2) = \tfrac{\pi}{2}(e^{2} - 1) \]

Card 8 — DE Pattern Recognition: Three Standard Rate Equations

How the exam asks it
"A quantity \( N \) changes such that ... [physical description]. Formulate a differential equation." The exam tests whether you can map the words to one of three canonical patterns.
Concept
Three patterns, snap-recognise from the verbal trigger:
Verbal triggerDEBehaviourExamples
"Rate \( \propto N \)" (only the quantity)\( \dfrac{dN}{dt} = \pm kN \)Exponential growth (+) or decay (−)Bacterial growth, radioactive decay, simple interest with continuous compounding
"Rate \( \propto (K - N) \)" (chasing a target K)\( \dfrac{dN}{dt} = k(K - N) \)Approaches K from below; rate slows as you near KNewton's cooling (T → room temp), drug clearance to baseline
"Rate \( \propto N(K - N) \)" (product of current and remaining capacity)\( \dfrac{dN}{dt} = kN(K - N) \)Logistic S-curve, levels off at K (carrying capacity)Population with finite resources, virus spread in a population
Sign rule: positive coefficient if quantity is INCREASING; negative if DECREASING. Always state the initial condition.
Example
A hot metal bar at \( 200\,^{\circ}\text{C} \) is placed in a room at \( 25\,^{\circ}\text{C} \). The rate of cooling is proportional to the temperature difference between the bar and the room. Formulate a differential equation for the temperature \( T \) of the bar at time \( t \). [3]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Identify the verbal pattern. "Rate proportional to difference (T − 25)" → second pattern (chasing K = 25). M1
Step 2
Apply the sign rule. Bar is cooling, so \( T \) is DECREASING → negative coefficient. M1
Step 3
Write the DE plus initial condition. A1
\[ \frac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - 25),\quad k > 0,\quad T(0) = 200 \]

Card 9 — DE by Given Substitution: Full Mechanics

How the exam asks it
"Show, by using the substitution \( u = \ldots \), that the DE \( \ldots \) can be written as \( \ldots \). Hence solve the DE." The substitution always reduces a non-separable DE to a separable one.
Concept
WHY differentiate the substitution? The original DE contains \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} \). To rewrite in terms of \( u \), you need to express \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} \) in terms of \( u, x, \dfrac{du}{dx} \). Differentiating the substitution gives that relationship.

Six-step procedure (always the same):
  1. Differentiate the substitution w.r.t. \( x \). Use product rule if needed.
  2. Solve for \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} \) in terms of \( u, x, \dfrac{du}{dx} \).
  3. Substitute \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} \) AND any \( y \)-terms in the original DE.
  4. Simplify — the new DE in \( u \) and \( x \) should be separable.
  5. Solve the separable DE.
  6. Back-substitute to give the answer in original variables.
Example
Use the substitution \( y = ux \) to find the general solution of the differential equation \( x\dfrac{dy}{dx} = y + x \). [6]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Differentiate \( y = ux \) w.r.t. \( x \) using the product rule. M1
\( \dfrac{dy}{dx} = u + x\dfrac{du}{dx} \).
Step 2
Substitute \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} \) and \( y = ux \) into the DE. M1
\( x\!\left(u + x\dfrac{du}{dx}\right) = ux + x \).
Step 3
Simplify. The \( ux \) terms cancel. M1
\( ux + x^{2}\dfrac{du}{dx} = ux + x \Rightarrow x^{2}\dfrac{du}{dx} = x \Rightarrow \dfrac{du}{dx} = \dfrac{1}{x} \).
Step 4
Separate and integrate. M1
\( du = \dfrac{1}{x}\,dx \Rightarrow u = \ln|x| + C \).
Step 5
Back-substitute \( u = \dfrac{y}{x} \). M1
\( \dfrac{y}{x} = \ln|x| + C \).
Step 6
Solve for \( y \). A1
\[ y = x\ln|x| + Cx \]

Card 10 — DE Interpretation Vocabulary

How the exam asks it
"Find the [long-term behaviour / equilibrium / carrying capacity / half-life] of …" — vocabulary-precision questions. Often a 1–3 mark interpretation rider on a longer DE question.
Concept
Definitions, all in one place:
TermPrecise meaningHow to find
Long-term behaviourWhat \( y \) approaches as \( t \to \infty \)Substitute \( t \to \infty \) into the solved \( y(t) \); simplify
Equilibrium / steady stateA value \( y^{*} \) where \( \dfrac{dy}{dt} = 0 \)Set RHS of original DE = 0, solve for \( y \)
Carrying capacity (K)The maximum equilibrium for a logistic model \( \dfrac{dN}{dt} = kN(1 - N/K) \)Read off from DE: the value of \( N \) where the rate becomes zero (other than \( N = 0 \))
Half-lifeTime for an exponentially decaying quantity to halveFor \( y = y_{0}e^{-kt} \): solve \( y_{0}/2 = y_{0}e^{-kt} \Rightarrow t_{1/2} = (\ln 2)/k \)
Maximum growth rate (logistic)The \( N \)-value where \( \dfrac{dN}{dt} \) is maximumInflection of S-curve: \( N = K/2 \)
Example
A population satisfies \( \dfrac{dP}{dt} = 0.3\,P\!\left(1 - \dfrac{P}{2000}\right) \) with \( P(0) = 100 \).
(i) State the carrying capacity. [1]
(ii) Find the equilibrium values. [2]
(iii) State the value of \( P \) at which the population is growing fastest. [1]
(iv) Describe the long-term behaviour of \( P \). [1]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i) Carrying capacity
Recognise logistic form \( \dfrac{dP}{dt} = kP(1 - P/K) \) with \( k = 0.3,\; K = 2000 \). B1
\( K = 2000 \).
Step 2 — (ii) Equilibrium values
Set \( \dfrac{dP}{dt} = 0 \) and solve. M1
\( 0.3 P (1 - P/2000) = 0 \Rightarrow P = 0 \text{ or } P = 2000 \). A1
Step 3 — (iii) Maximum growth rate
Inflection of logistic S-curve is at \( P = K/2 \). B1
\( P = 1000 \).
Step 4 — (iv) Long-term behaviour
Starting from \( P(0) = 100 \), below the carrying capacity, growth continues until reaching the upper equilibrium. B1
\( P \to 2000 \) as \( t \to \infty \).

A drug concentration \( C(t) \) (in mg/L) satisfies \( \dfrac{dC}{dt} = -0.1C \) with \( C(0) = 80 \).
(i) Solve to find \( C(t) \). [3]
(ii) Describe the long-term behaviour of \( C \). [1]
(iii) Find the half-life of the drug, giving your answer to 3 significant figures. [2]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i) Solve
Separable. M1
\( \dfrac{1}{C}\,dC = -0.1\,dt \Rightarrow \ln|C| = -0.1t + K_{\text{const}} \).
Step 2
Exponentiate. M1
\( C = A\,e^{-0.1t} \) (where \( A = e^{K_{\text{const}}} \)).
Step 3
Apply initial condition. A1
\( C(0) = 80 \Rightarrow A = 80 \Rightarrow C(t) = 80\,e^{-0.1t} \).
Step 4 — (ii) Long-term behaviour
As \( t \to \infty \), \( e^{-0.1t} \to 0 \). B1
\( C \to 0 \). The drug is fully cleared from the body in the long run.
Step 5 — (iii) Half-life
Solve \( C(t_{1/2}) = 40 \). M1
\( 80\,e^{-0.1\,t_{1/2}} = 40 \Rightarrow e^{-0.1\,t_{1/2}} = \tfrac{1}{2} \Rightarrow -0.1\,t_{1/2} = -\ln 2 \).
Step 6
Solve for \( t_{1/2} \). A1
\( t_{1/2} = \dfrac{\ln 2}{0.1} = 10\ln 2 \approx 6.93 \) time units.
