Card 1 — Reverse Chain Rule (pattern recognition)

How the exam asks it
"Find \( \int f(x)\,dx \)" with no hint — you must spot which of the three reverse chain rule patterns applies: \( \dfrac{f'(x)}{f(x)},\;\;f'(x)\,e^{f(x)},\;\;f'(x)\,[f(x)]^{n} \). Typical 2–4m, opener.
Concept
Look for the structure first: is the integrand a fraction with the numerator equal to the derivative of the denominator → \( \ln|f(x)| \). Is it something times \( e \) to a function → \( e^{f(x)} \). Is it a function-derivative times the function raised to a power → \( [f(x)]^{n+1}/(n+1) \). Sometimes you need to factor out or adjust a constant.
Example
Find \( \displaystyle\int \dfrac{6x^{2}}{x^{3} + 4}\,dx \). [3]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Spot the pattern. Denominator is \( x^{3} + 4 \). Derivative of denominator is \( 3x^{2} \). Numerator is \( 6x^{2} = 2 \cdot 3x^{2} \) — pattern \( \dfrac{f'}{f} \) with a constant multiplier. M1
Step 2
Pull out the constant. M1
\( \int \dfrac{6x^{2}}{x^{3}+4}\,dx = 2\int \dfrac{3x^{2}}{x^{3}+4}\,dx \).
Step 3
Apply the pattern (\( x^{3}+4 > 0 \) for all \( x \), so drop modulus). A1
\[ = 2\ln(x^{3}+4) + C \]

Practice
Find \( \displaystyle\int x^{2}\,e^{x^{3}+2}\,dx \). [3]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
Pattern: \( f'(x)\,e^{f(x)} \). Here \( f(x) = x^{3} + 2,\; f'(x) = 3x^{2} \). Integrand has \( x^{2} = \tfrac{1}{3} \cdot 3x^{2} \). M1
Step 2
Pull out the \( \tfrac{1}{3} \). M1
\( \int x^{2}\,e^{x^{3}+2}\,dx = \tfrac{1}{3}\int 3x^{2}\,e^{x^{3}+2}\,dx \).
Step 3
Apply the formula. A1
\[ = \tfrac{1}{3}\,e^{x^{3}+2} + C \]

Card 2 — Trig Integration via Identities

How the exam asks it
"Find \( \int \sin^{2}(2x)\,dx \)" or "Find the exact value of \( \int_{0}^{\pi/4}\cos^{2}x\,dx \)". Almost always uses one of: \( \sin^{2}\theta = \tfrac{1-\cos 2\theta}{2},\; \cos^{2}\theta = \tfrac{1+\cos 2\theta}{2},\; \tan^{2}\theta = \sec^{2}\theta - 1 \).
Example
Find the exact value of \( \displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/4} \sin^{2}(2x)\,dx \). [4]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Apply identity \( \sin^{2}\theta = \tfrac{1-\cos 2\theta}{2} \) with \( \theta = 2x \). M1
\( \sin^{2}(2x) = \dfrac{1 - \cos 4x}{2} \).
Step 2
Integrate. M1
\( \int \dfrac{1-\cos 4x}{2}\,dx = \tfrac{1}{2}x - \tfrac{1}{8}\sin 4x + C \).
Step 3
Evaluate at limits. M1
At \( x = \pi/4 \): \( \tfrac{\pi}{8} - \tfrac{1}{8}\sin\pi = \tfrac{\pi}{8} - 0 = \tfrac{\pi}{8} \).
At \( x = 0 \): \( 0 - 0 = 0 \).
Step 4
Subtract. A1
\[ \int_{0}^{\pi/4} \sin^{2}(2x)\,dx = \dfrac{\pi}{8} \]

Practice
Find the exact value of \( \displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/3}\tan^{2}x\,dx \). [4]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
Apply identity \( \tan^{2}x = \sec^{2}x - 1 \). M1
Step 2
Integrate. M1
\( \int (\sec^{2}x - 1)\,dx = \tan x - x + C \).
Step 3
Evaluate. M1
At \( x = \pi/3 \): \( \tan(\pi/3) - \pi/3 = \sqrt{3} - \pi/3 \).
At \( x = 0 \): \( 0 - 0 = 0 \).
Step 4
Subtract. A1
\[ \int_{0}^{\pi/3}\tan^{2}x\,dx = \sqrt{3} - \dfrac{\pi}{3} \]

Card 3 — MF27 Inverse-Trig Forms

How the exam asks it
"Find \( \int \dfrac{1}{a^{2}+x^{2}}\,dx \)" or "Find the exact value of \( \int_{0}^{a} \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{a^{2}-x^{2}}}\,dx \)". Direct application of MF27 \( \int \tfrac{1}{a^{2}+x^{2}}\,dx = \tfrac{1}{a}\tan^{-1}\tfrac{x}{a} \) and \( \int \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{a^{2}-x^{2}}}\,dx = \sin^{-1}\tfrac{x}{a} \).
Example
Find the exact value of \( \displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{16 - x^{2}}}\,dx \). [3]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Identify \( a^{2} = 16 \Rightarrow a = 4 \). MF27 form: \( \sin^{-1}\!\tfrac{x}{a} \). M1
Step 2
Apply. A1
\( \int \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{16-x^{2}}}\,dx = \sin^{-1}\!\dfrac{x}{4} + C \).
Step 3
Evaluate. A1
At \( x=2 \): \( \sin^{-1}(1/2) = \pi/6 \). At \( x=0 \): \( 0 \). \[ \int_{0}^{2}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{16-x^{2}}}\,dx = \dfrac{\pi}{6} \]

Practice
Find the exact value of \( \displaystyle\int_{0}^{3}\dfrac{1}{9 + x^{2}}\,dx \). [3]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
\( a^{2} = 9 \Rightarrow a = 3 \). Form: \( \tfrac{1}{a}\tan^{-1}\!\tfrac{x}{a} \). M1
Step 2
Apply. A1
\( \int \dfrac{1}{9+x^{2}}\,dx = \tfrac{1}{3}\tan^{-1}\!\tfrac{x}{3} + C \).
Step 3
Evaluate. A1
At \( x=3 \): \( \tfrac{1}{3}\tan^{-1}(1) = \tfrac{1}{3}\cdot\tfrac{\pi}{4} = \tfrac{\pi}{12} \). At \( x=0 \): \( 0 \). \[ \int_{0}^{3}\dfrac{1}{9+x^{2}}\,dx = \dfrac{\pi}{12} \]

Card 4 — MF27 Log Forms

How the exam asks it
"Find \( \int \dfrac{1}{a^{2}-x^{2}}\,dx \) or \( \int \dfrac{1}{x^{2}-a^{2}}\,dx \)". Direct MF27 application: \( \int \tfrac{1}{a^{2}-x^{2}}\,dx = \tfrac{1}{2a}\ln\!\left|\tfrac{a+x}{a-x}\right| \) and \( \int \tfrac{1}{x^{2}-a^{2}}\,dx = \tfrac{1}{2a}\ln\!\left|\tfrac{x-a}{x+a}\right| \). Check the SIGN of the constant carefully to pick the right form.
Example
Find \( \displaystyle\int \dfrac{1}{x^{2} - 9}\,dx \). [2]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Form \( \dfrac{1}{x^{2}-a^{2}} \) with \( a = 3 \). M1
Step 2
Apply. A1
\[ \int \dfrac{1}{x^{2}-9}\,dx = \tfrac{1}{6}\ln\!\left|\dfrac{x-3}{x+3}\right| + C \]
Solution hidden — click Reveal
Practice
Find the exact value of \( \displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{1}{4 - x^{2}}\,dx \). [3]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
Form \( \dfrac{1}{a^{2}-x^{2}} \) with \( a = 2 \). M1
Step 2
Antiderivative. M1
\( \int \dfrac{1}{4-x^{2}}\,dx = \tfrac{1}{4}\ln\!\left|\dfrac{2+x}{2-x}\right| + C \).
Step 3
Evaluate. A1
At \( x=1 \): \( \tfrac{1}{4}\ln 3 \). At \( x=0 \): \( \tfrac{1}{4}\ln 1 = 0 \). \[ \int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{1}{4-x^{2}}\,dx = \dfrac{1}{4}\ln 3 \]

Card 5 — Integration by Given Substitution

How the exam asks it
"Use the substitution \( u = g(x) \) (or \( x = h(\theta) \)) to find …" — substitution is ALWAYS given. Five steps: differentiate substitution → rewrite integrand → change limits if definite → integrate in new variable → substitute back / evaluate.
Example
Use the substitution \( u = 1 + x^{2} \) to find \( \displaystyle\int_{0}^{1} \dfrac{x}{(1 + x^{2})^{3}}\,dx \). [5]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Differentiate the substitution. B1
\( u = 1 + x^{2} \Rightarrow \dfrac{du}{dx} = 2x \Rightarrow du = 2x\,dx \Rightarrow x\,dx = \tfrac{1}{2}\,du \).
Step 2
Change limits. At \( x=0 \): \( u=1 \). At \( x=1 \): \( u=2 \). B1
Step 3
Rewrite the integrand. M1
\( \int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{x}{(1+x^{2})^{3}}\,dx = \int_{1}^{2}\dfrac{1}{u^{3}}\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}\,du = \tfrac{1}{2}\int_{1}^{2}u^{-3}\,du \).
Step 4
Integrate. M1
\( \tfrac{1}{2}\!\left[\dfrac{u^{-2}}{-2}\right]_{1}^{2} = -\tfrac{1}{4}\!\left[\dfrac{1}{u^{2}}\right]_{1}^{2} = -\tfrac{1}{4}(\tfrac{1}{4} - 1) = -\tfrac{1}{4}\cdot(-\tfrac{3}{4}) \).
Step 5
Simplify. A1
\[ = \dfrac{3}{16} \]
Solution hidden — click Reveal
Practice
Use the substitution \( u = \ln x \) to find \( \displaystyle\int_{1}^{e}\dfrac{(\ln x)^{2}}{x}\,dx \). [5]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
Differentiate. B1
\( u = \ln x \Rightarrow du = \tfrac{1}{x}\,dx \).
Step 2
Change limits. At \( x=1 \): \( u=0 \). At \( x=e \): \( u=1 \). B1
Step 3
Rewrite. \( (\ln x)^{2}\cdot\tfrac{1}{x}\,dx = u^{2}\,du \). M1
\( \int_{1}^{e}\dfrac{(\ln x)^{2}}{x}\,dx = \int_{0}^{1}u^{2}\,du \).
Step 4
Integrate. M1
\( \left[\dfrac{u^{3}}{3}\right]_{0}^{1} = \dfrac{1}{3} - 0 \).
Step 5
Final. A1
\[ = \dfrac{1}{3} \]

Card 6 — Integration by Parts (single application)

How the exam asks it
"Find \( \int x\sin x\,dx \)" or "Find the exact value of \( \int_{0}^{1} x\,e^{-x}\,dx \)". Single IBP — integrand is product of two distinct function types.
Example
Find the exact value of \( \displaystyle\int_{0}^{1} x\,e^{-x}\,dx \). [5]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
LIATE: \( u = x,\; dv = e^{-x}\,dx \). B1
\( du = dx,\; v = -e^{-x} \).
Step 2
Apply IBP. M1
\( \int_{0}^{1} x\,e^{-x}\,dx = [-x\,e^{-x}]_{0}^{1} - \int_{0}^{1} -e^{-x}\,dx = [-x\,e^{-x}]_{0}^{1} + \int_{0}^{1} e^{-x}\,dx \).
Step 3
Compute each piece. M1 M1
\( [-x\,e^{-x}]_{0}^{1} = -e^{-1} - 0 = -\tfrac{1}{e} \).
\( \int_{0}^{1} e^{-x}\,dx = [-e^{-x}]_{0}^{1} = -e^{-1} - (-1) = 1 - \tfrac{1}{e} \).
Step 4
Combine. A1
\[ -\tfrac{1}{e} + 1 - \tfrac{1}{e} = 1 - \dfrac{2}{e} \]
Solution hidden — click Reveal
Practice
Find \( \displaystyle\int x\sin 3x\,dx \). [4]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
LIATE: \( u = x,\; dv = \sin 3x\,dx \). B1
\( du = dx,\; v = -\tfrac{1}{3}\cos 3x \).
Step 2
Apply IBP. M1
\( \int x\sin 3x\,dx = -\tfrac{x}{3}\cos 3x - \int -\tfrac{1}{3}\cos 3x\,dx = -\tfrac{x}{3}\cos 3x + \tfrac{1}{3}\int \cos 3x\,dx \).
Step 3
Integrate. M1
\( \tfrac{1}{3}\int \cos 3x\,dx = \tfrac{1}{9}\sin 3x \).
Step 4
Combine. A1
\[ \int x\sin 3x\,dx = -\dfrac{x}{3}\cos 3x + \dfrac{1}{9}\sin 3x + C \]

Card 7 — IBP Cyclic (\( e^{x}\sin x \) type)

How the exam asks it
"Find \( \int e^{x}\sin x\,dx \)" or similar. After IBP twice, the original integral reappears. Solve algebraically: set the original integral as \( I \), get an equation in \( I \), solve.
Concept
\( e^{x}\sin x \) and \( e^{x}\cos x \) cycle under differentiation (sin → cos → -sin → -cos → sin). Two applications of IBP bring the original integrand back with a coefficient. Solve like a linear equation.
Example
Find \( \displaystyle\int e^{x}\sin x\,dx \). [6]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Let \( I = \int e^{x}\sin x\,dx \). First IBP: pick \( u = \sin x,\; dv = e^{x}\,dx \), so \( du = \cos x\,dx,\; v = e^{x} \). M1
\( I = e^{x}\sin x - \int e^{x}\cos x\,dx \).
Step 2
Second IBP on the remaining \( \int e^{x}\cos x\,dx \). Same orientation: \( u = \cos x,\; dv = e^{x}\,dx \), so \( du = -\sin x\,dx,\; v = e^{x} \). M1
\( \int e^{x}\cos x\,dx = e^{x}\cos x - \int e^{x}(-\sin x)\,dx = e^{x}\cos x + \int e^{x}\sin x\,dx = e^{x}\cos x + I \).
Step 3
Substitute back into Step 1. M1
\( I = e^{x}\sin x - (e^{x}\cos x + I) = e^{x}\sin x - e^{x}\cos x - I \).
Step 4
Solve algebraically for \( I \). M1
\( 2I = e^{x}\sin x - e^{x}\cos x \).
Step 5
Divide by 2 and add \( + C \). A1 A1
\[ I = \dfrac{1}{2}e^{x}(\sin x - \cos x) + C \]
Solution hidden — click Reveal
Practice
Find \( \displaystyle\int e^{2x}\cos x\,dx \). [6]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
Let \( I = \int e^{2x}\cos x\,dx \). First IBP: \( u = \cos x,\; dv = e^{2x}\,dx \). So \( du = -\sin x\,dx,\; v = \tfrac{1}{2}e^{2x} \). M1
\( I = \tfrac{1}{2}e^{2x}\cos x + \tfrac{1}{2}\int e^{2x}\sin x\,dx \).
Step 2
Second IBP on \( \int e^{2x}\sin x\,dx \). \( u = \sin x,\; dv = e^{2x}\,dx \). \( du = \cos x\,dx,\; v = \tfrac{1}{2}e^{2x} \). M1
\( \int e^{2x}\sin x\,dx = \tfrac{1}{2}e^{2x}\sin x - \tfrac{1}{2}\int e^{2x}\cos x\,dx = \tfrac{1}{2}e^{2x}\sin x - \tfrac{1}{2}I \).
Step 3
Substitute back. M1
\( I = \tfrac{1}{2}e^{2x}\cos x + \tfrac{1}{2}\!\left[\tfrac{1}{2}e^{2x}\sin x - \tfrac{1}{2}I\right] = \tfrac{1}{2}e^{2x}\cos x + \tfrac{1}{4}e^{2x}\sin x - \tfrac{1}{4}I \).
Step 4
Solve. M1
\( I + \tfrac{1}{4}I = \tfrac{1}{2}e^{2x}\cos x + \tfrac{1}{4}e^{2x}\sin x \Rightarrow \tfrac{5}{4}I = \tfrac{e^{2x}}{4}(2\cos x + \sin x) \).
Step 5
Final. A1 A1
\[ I = \dfrac{e^{2x}}{5}(2\cos x + \sin x) + C \]

Card 8 — Partial Fractions then Integrate

How the exam asks it
"Express \( f(x) \) in partial fractions and hence find \( \int f(x)\,dx \)." Or just "Find \( \int f(x)\,dx \)" where \( f(x) \) is a rational function with a factorable denominator.
Example
(i) Express \( \dfrac{3x+5}{(x+1)(x-2)} \) in partial fractions. [3]
(ii) Hence find \( \displaystyle\int \dfrac{3x+5}{(x+1)(x-2)}\,dx \). [3]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i)
Set up decomposition. M1
\( \dfrac{3x+5}{(x+1)(x-2)} = \dfrac{A}{x+1} + \dfrac{B}{x-2} \).
Step 2
Clear and solve. M1
\( 3x+5 = A(x-2) + B(x+1) \).
At \( x = 2 \): \( 11 = 3B \Rightarrow B = \tfrac{11}{3} \).
At \( x = -1 \): \( 2 = -3A \Rightarrow A = -\tfrac{2}{3} \).
Step 3
Write the decomposition. A1
\( \dfrac{3x+5}{(x+1)(x-2)} = -\dfrac{2/3}{x+1} + \dfrac{11/3}{x-2} \).
Step 4 — (ii)
Integrate term by term. M1 M1
\( \int -\dfrac{2/3}{x+1}\,dx = -\tfrac{2}{3}\ln|x+1| \).
\( \int \dfrac{11/3}{x-2}\,dx = \tfrac{11}{3}\ln|x-2| \).
Step 5
Combine. A1
\[ \int \dfrac{3x+5}{(x+1)(x-2)}\,dx = -\tfrac{2}{3}\ln|x+1| + \tfrac{11}{3}\ln|x-2| + C \]
Solution hidden — click Reveal
Practice
Find \( \displaystyle\int \dfrac{4}{x^{2} - 4}\,dx \), using partial fractions. [5]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
Factor: \( x^{2} - 4 = (x-2)(x+2) \). Decompose. M1
\( \dfrac{4}{(x-2)(x+2)} = \dfrac{A}{x-2} + \dfrac{B}{x+2} \).
Step 2
Solve. M1
\( 4 = A(x+2) + B(x-2) \).
At \( x=2 \): \( 4 = 4A \Rightarrow A = 1 \).
At \( x=-2 \): \( 4 = -4B \Rightarrow B = -1 \).
Step 3
Integrate. M1 M1
\( \int \!\left[\dfrac{1}{x-2} - \dfrac{1}{x+2}\right]dx = \ln|x-2| - \ln|x+2| + C \).
Step 4
Combine with log laws. A1
\[ \int \dfrac{4}{x^{2}-4}\,dx = \ln\!\left|\dfrac{x-2}{x+2}\right| + C \]

Card 9 — Area Between Two Curves

How the exam asks it
"Find the exact area of the region enclosed by [curve 1] and [curve 2]." Or with a line. You need to (a) find intersections (these are the limits), (b) decide which curve is upper, (c) integrate (upper − lower).
Concept
Intersection \( x \)-values become your limits: SMALLER value is the lower limit, LARGER is the upper. To decide which curve is "upper", pick any \( x \) between the limits and substitute — the one giving the bigger \( y \) is upper. Integrate (upper − lower). If the order is wrong, you get a negative; take absolute value.
Example
Find the exact area of the region enclosed by the curve \( y = x^{2} \) and the line \( y = 2x + 3 \). [6]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Find intersections. M1
\( x^{2} = 2x + 3 \Rightarrow x^{2} - 2x - 3 = 0 \Rightarrow (x-3)(x+1) = 0 \Rightarrow x = 3 \text{ or } x = -1 \).
Step 2
Decide upper. At \( x = 0 \): parabola \( = 0 \), line \( = 3 \). Line is upper. B1
Step 3
Set up. M1
\( A = \int_{-1}^{3} [(2x+3) - x^{2}]\,dx = \int_{-1}^{3}(2x + 3 - x^{2})\,dx \).
Step 4
Antiderivative.
\( \int (2x + 3 - x^{2})\,dx = x^{2} + 3x - \tfrac{x^{3}}{3} \).
Step 5
Evaluate. M1
At \( x = 3 \): \( 9 + 9 - 9 = 9 \).
At \( x = -1 \): \( 1 - 3 + \tfrac{1}{3} = -\tfrac{5}{3} \).
Difference: \( 9 - (-\tfrac{5}{3}) = \tfrac{32}{3} \).
Step 6
State exact value. A1
\[ A = \dfrac{32}{3} \;\text{units}^{2} \]
Solution hidden — click Reveal
Practice
Find the exact area of the region enclosed by the curves \( y = x^{3} \) and \( y = x \). [6]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
Intersections. M1
\( x^{3} = x \Rightarrow x^{3} - x = 0 \Rightarrow x(x-1)(x+1) = 0 \Rightarrow x = -1, 0, 1 \).
Step 2
Two enclosed regions (one on \( [-1, 0] \), one on \( [0, 1] \)). Decide upper in each. At \( x = 0.5 \): \( y = x \to 0.5,\; y = x^{3} \to 0.125 \). So \( y = x \) is upper on \( [0, 1] \). At \( x = -0.5 \): \( y = x \to -0.5,\; y = x^{3} \to -0.125 \). \( y = x^{3} \) is upper on \( [-1, 0] \). B1
Step 3
Set up two integrals (by symmetry, both give the same value). M1
\( A = \int_{-1}^{0}(x^{3} - x)\,dx + \int_{0}^{1}(x - x^{3})\,dx \).
Step 4
Evaluate the second (\( [0,1] \)). M1
\( \int_{0}^{1}(x - x^{3})\,dx = \left[\tfrac{x^{2}}{2} - \tfrac{x^{4}}{4}\right]_{0}^{1} = \tfrac{1}{2} - \tfrac{1}{4} = \tfrac{1}{4} \).
Step 5
By symmetry the first also gives \( \tfrac{1}{4} \). M1
Step 6
Sum. A1
\[ A = \dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{1}{2} \;\text{units}^{2} \]

Card 10 — Volume of Revolution

How the exam asks it
"The region bounded by … is rotated through \( 360^{\circ} \) about the [x / y]-axis. Find the exact volume of the solid formed." About x-axis: \( V = \pi\int y^{2}\,dx \). About y-axis: rearrange to \( x = g(y) \), then \( V = \pi\int x^{2}\,dy \). \( \pi \) is mandatory.
Example
The region bounded by \( y = x^{2} + 1 \), the x-axis, and the lines \( x = 0 \) and \( x = 2 \) is rotated through \( 360^{\circ} \) about the x-axis. Find the exact volume of the solid formed. [5]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
About x-axis: \( V = \pi\int_{0}^{2} y^{2}\,dx \). B1
Step 2
Compute \( y^{2} \). M1
\( y^{2} = (x^{2}+1)^{2} = x^{4} + 2x^{2} + 1 \).
Step 3
Integrate term by term. M1
\( \int (x^{4} + 2x^{2} + 1)\,dx = \tfrac{x^{5}}{5} + \tfrac{2x^{3}}{3} + x \).
Step 4
Evaluate at limits. M1
At \( x=2 \): \( \tfrac{32}{5} + \tfrac{16}{3} + 2 \). Common denominator 15: \( \tfrac{96+80+30}{15} = \tfrac{206}{15} \).
At \( x=0 \): \( 0 \).
Step 5
Multiply by \( \pi \). A1
\[ V = \dfrac{206\pi}{15} \;\text{units}^{3} \]
Solution hidden — click Reveal
Practice
The region bounded by \( y = \sqrt{4 - x^{2}} \) and the x-axis is rotated through \( 360^{\circ} \) about the x-axis. Find the exact volume of the solid formed. [5]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
The curve is the upper semicircle \( x^{2}+y^{2}=4 \). Limits: \( y = 0 \Rightarrow x = \pm 2 \). B1
Step 2
Set up. M1
\( V = \pi\int_{-2}^{2} y^{2}\,dx = \pi\int_{-2}^{2}(4 - x^{2})\,dx \).
Step 3
Integrate. M1
\( \int (4 - x^{2})\,dx = 4x - \tfrac{x^{3}}{3} \).
Step 4
Evaluate. M1
At \( x=2 \): \( 8 - \tfrac{8}{3} = \tfrac{16}{3} \). At \( x=-2 \): \( -8 + \tfrac{8}{3} = -\tfrac{16}{3} \). Difference: \( \tfrac{32}{3} \).
Step 5
Multiply by \( \pi \). (Sanity check: this is the volume of a sphere of radius 2 = \( \tfrac{4}{3}\pi(8) = \tfrac{32\pi}{3} \). ✓) A1
\[ V = \dfrac{32\pi}{3} \;\text{units}^{3} \]
