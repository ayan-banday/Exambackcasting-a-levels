Question 1

Example
Find \( \displaystyle\int \dfrac{6x^{2}}{x^{3} + 4}\,dx \). [3]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Denominator derivative = \( 3x^{2} \), numerator = \( 6x^{2} = 2 \cdot 3x^{2} \). Pattern \( \dfrac{f'}{f} \) with constant multiplier. M1
Step 2
Pull the 2 out. M1
\( 2\int \dfrac{3x^{2}}{x^{3}+4}\,dx \).
Step 3
Apply. Denominator \( > 0 \) always, drop modulus. A1
\[ = 2\ln(x^{3}+4) + C \]

Find \( \displaystyle\int x^{2}\,e^{x^{3}+2}\,dx \). [3]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
Pattern \( f'(x)e^{f(x)} \) with \( f(x) = x^{3}+2,\; f'(x) = 3x^{2} \). Integrand has \( x^{2} = \tfrac{1}{3} \cdot 3x^{2} \). M1
Step 2
Pull constant. M1
\( \tfrac{1}{3}\int 3x^{2}e^{x^{3}+2}\,dx \).
Step 3
Apply. A1
\[ = \tfrac{1}{3}e^{x^{3}+2} + C \]
Answer hidden — click Reveal
Archetype revealed
Reverse Chain Rule (pattern recognition).

"Find \( \int f(x)\,dx \)" with no hint — you must spot which reverse-chain pattern applies: \( \tfrac{f'}{f} \to \ln \), \( f'e^{f} \to e^{f} \), or \( f' f^{n} \to \tfrac{f^{n+1}}{n+1} \). Adjust for constant multipliers if needed.

Question 2

Example
Find the exact value of \( \displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/4} \sin^{2}(2x)\,dx \). [4]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Identity \( \sin^{2}\theta = \tfrac{1-\cos 2\theta}{2} \) with \( \theta = 2x \). M1
\( \sin^{2}(2x) = \tfrac{1 - \cos 4x}{2} \).
Step 2
Integrate. M1
\( \int \tfrac{1 - \cos 4x}{2}\,dx = \tfrac{x}{2} - \tfrac{\sin 4x}{8} + C \).
Step 3
Evaluate. M1 A1
At \( x = \pi/4 \): \( \tfrac{\pi}{8} - \tfrac{\sin\pi}{8} = \tfrac{\pi}{8} \). At \( x = 0 \): \( 0 \). \[ \int_{0}^{\pi/4} \sin^{2}(2x)\,dx = \tfrac{\pi}{8} \]

Practice
Find the exact value of \( \displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/3}\tan^{2}x\,dx \). [4]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
Identity \( \tan^{2}x = \sec^{2}x - 1 \). M1
Step 2
Integrate. M1
\( \int (\sec^{2}x - 1)\,dx = \tan x - x + C \).
Step 3
Evaluate. M1 A1
At \( x = \pi/3 \): \( \sqrt{3} - \pi/3 \). At \( x = 0 \): \( 0 \). \[ \int_{0}^{\pi/3}\tan^{2}x\,dx = \sqrt{3} - \tfrac{\pi}{3} \]

Trig Integration via Identities.

Integrands with \( \sin^{2},\; \cos^{2},\; \tan^{2} \) always need the double-angle identity or \( \tan^{2} = \sec^{2} - 1 \) rewrite first.

Step 2
Apply. A1
\( \int \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{16 - x^{2}}}\,dx = \sin^{-1}\!\tfrac{x}{4} + C \).
Step 3
Evaluate. A1
At \( x = 2 \): \( \sin^{-1}(1/2) = \pi/6 \). At \( x = 0 \): \( 0 \). \[ = \tfrac{\pi}{6} \]

Question 5

Example
Use the substitution \( u = 1 + x^{2} \) to find \( \displaystyle\int_{0}^{1} \dfrac{x}{(1 + x^{2})^{3}}\,dx \). [5]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Differentiate substitution. B1
\( u = 1 + x^{2} \Rightarrow du = 2x\,dx \Rightarrow x\,dx = \tfrac{1}{2}\,du \).
Step 2
Change limits. At \( x = 0,\; u = 1 \). At \( x = 1,\; u = 2 \). B1
Step 3
Rewrite. M1
\( \int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{x}{(1+x^{2})^{3}}\,dx = \tfrac{1}{2}\int_{1}^{2}u^{-3}\,du \).
Step 4
Integrate. M1
\( \tfrac{1}{2}\!\left[\dfrac{u^{-2}}{-2}\right]_{1}^{2} = -\tfrac{1}{4}\!\left[\dfrac{1}{u^{2}}\right]_{1}^{2} = -\tfrac{1}{4}(\tfrac{1}{4} - 1) \).
Step 5
Simplify. A1
\[ = \tfrac{3}{16} \]
Solution hidden — click Reveal
Practice
Use the substitution \( u = \ln x \) to find \( \displaystyle\int_{1}^{e}\dfrac{(\ln x)^{2}}{x}\,dx \). [5]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
\( u = \ln x \Rightarrow du = \tfrac{1}{x}\,dx \). B1
Step 2
Change limits. At \( x = 1,\; u = 0 \). At \( x = e,\; u = 1 \). B1
Step 3
Rewrite. M1
\( \int_{1}^{e}\dfrac{(\ln x)^{2}}{x}\,dx = \int_{0}^{1}u^{2}\,du \).
Step 4
Integrate. M1
\( \left[\dfrac{u^{3}}{3}\right]_{0}^{1} = \tfrac{1}{3} \).
Step 5
Final. A1
\[ = \tfrac{1}{3} \]
Answer hidden — click Reveal
Archetype revealed
Integration by Given Substitution.

Substitution always given. Five steps: differentiate → substitute integrand → change limits (definite) → integrate in \( u \) → substitute back / evaluate.

Question 6

Example
Find the exact value of \( \displaystyle\int_{0}^{1} x\,e^{-x}\,dx \). [5]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
LIATE: \( u = x,\; dv = e^{-x}\,dx \). So \( du = dx,\; v = -e^{-x} \). B1
Step 2
Apply IBP. M1
\( \int_{0}^{1} x\,e^{-x}\,dx = [-xe^{-x}]_{0}^{1} + \int_{0}^{1} e^{-x}\,dx \).
Step 3
Compute each. M1 M1
\( [-xe^{-x}]_{0}^{1} = -\tfrac{1}{e} \).
\( \int_{0}^{1} e^{-x}\,dx = [-e^{-x}]_{0}^{1} = 1 - \tfrac{1}{e} \).
Step 4
Combine. A1
\[ = -\tfrac{1}{e} + 1 - \tfrac{1}{e} = 1 - \tfrac{2}{e} \]
Solution hidden — click Reveal
Practice
Find \( \displaystyle\int x\sin 3x\,dx \). [4]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
LIATE: \( u = x,\; dv = \sin 3x\,dx \). So \( du = dx,\; v = -\tfrac{1}{3}\cos 3x \). B1
Step 2
Apply. M1
\( \int x\sin 3x\,dx = -\tfrac{x}{3}\cos 3x + \tfrac{1}{3}\int \cos 3x\,dx \).
Step 3
Integrate. M1
\( \tfrac{1}{3}\int \cos 3x\,dx = \tfrac{1}{9}\sin 3x \).
Step 4
Combine. A1
\[ = -\tfrac{x}{3}\cos 3x + \tfrac{1}{9}\sin 3x + C \]
Answer hidden — click Reveal
Archetype revealed
Integration by Parts (single application).

Integrand is product of two distinct function types. LIATE picks \( u \). Formula \( \int u\,dv = uv - \int v\,du \).

Question 7

Example
Find \( \displaystyle\int e^{x}\sin x\,dx \). [6]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Let \( I = \int e^{x}\sin x\,dx \). First IBP: \( u = \sin x,\; dv = e^{x}\,dx \). So \( du = \cos x\,dx,\; v = e^{x} \). M1
\( I = e^{x}\sin x - \int e^{x}\cos x\,dx \).
Step 2
Second IBP: same orientation. \( u = \cos x,\; dv = e^{x}\,dx \), so \( du = -\sin x\,dx,\; v = e^{x} \). M1
\( \int e^{x}\cos x\,dx = e^{x}\cos x + \int e^{x}\sin x\,dx = e^{x}\cos x + I \).
Step 3
Substitute back. M1
\( I = e^{x}\sin x - (e^{x}\cos x + I) \).
Step 4
Solve for \( I \). M1
\( 2I = e^{x}(\sin x - \cos x) \).
Step 5
Final. A1 A1
\[ I = \tfrac{1}{2}e^{x}(\sin x - \cos x) + C \]
Solution hidden — click Reveal
Practice
Find \( \displaystyle\int e^{2x}\cos x\,dx \). [6]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
Let \( I = \int e^{2x}\cos x\,dx \). First IBP: \( u = \cos x,\; dv = e^{2x}\,dx \). \( du = -\sin x\,dx,\; v = \tfrac{1}{2}e^{2x} \). M1
\( I = \tfrac{1}{2}e^{2x}\cos x + \tfrac{1}{2}\int e^{2x}\sin x\,dx \).
Step 2
Second IBP: \( u = \sin x,\; dv = e^{2x}\,dx \). \( du = \cos x\,dx,\; v = \tfrac{1}{2}e^{2x} \). M1
\( \int e^{2x}\sin x\,dx = \tfrac{1}{2}e^{2x}\sin x - \tfrac{1}{2}I \).
Step 3
Substitute back. M1
\( I = \tfrac{1}{2}e^{2x}\cos x + \tfrac{1}{4}e^{2x}\sin x - \tfrac{1}{4}I \).
Step 4
Solve. M1
\( \tfrac{5}{4}I = \tfrac{e^{2x}}{4}(2\cos x + \sin x) \).
Step 5
Final. A1 A1
\[ I = \tfrac{e^{2x}}{5}(2\cos x + \sin x) + C \]
Answer hidden — click Reveal
Archetype revealed
IBP Cyclic (\( e^{x}\sin x \) type).

Two applications of IBP reproduce the original integrand. Set the integral \( = I \), get a linear equation in \( I \), solve.

Question 8

Example
(i) Express \( \dfrac{3x+5}{(x+1)(x-2)} \) in partial fractions. [3]
(ii) Hence find \( \displaystyle\int \dfrac{3x+5}{(x+1)(x-2)}\,dx \). [3]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i)
Decompose. M1
\( \dfrac{3x+5}{(x+1)(x-2)} = \dfrac{A}{x+1} + \dfrac{B}{x-2} \).
Step 2
Clear and solve. M1
\( 3x+5 = A(x-2) + B(x+1) \). At \( x = 2 \): \( 11 = 3B \Rightarrow B = \tfrac{11}{3} \). At \( x = -1 \): \( 2 = -3A \Rightarrow A = -\tfrac{2}{3} \).
Step 3
Write. A1
\( = -\dfrac{2/3}{x+1} + \dfrac{11/3}{x-2} \).
Step 4 — (ii)
Integrate. M1 M1
\( \int -\dfrac{2/3}{x+1}\,dx + \int \dfrac{11/3}{x-2}\,dx = -\tfrac{2}{3}\ln|x+1| + \tfrac{11}{3}\ln|x-2| + C \).
Step 5
Answer. A1
\[ = -\tfrac{2}{3}\ln|x+1| + \tfrac{11}{3}\ln|x-2| + C \]
Solution hidden — click Reveal
Practice
Find \( \displaystyle\int \dfrac{4}{x^{2} - 4}\,dx \), using partial fractions. [5]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
Factor: \( x^{2}-4 = (x-2)(x+2) \). Decompose. M1
\( \dfrac{4}{(x-2)(x+2)} = \dfrac{A}{x-2} + \dfrac{B}{x+2} \).
Step 2
Solve. M1
\( 4 = A(x+2) + B(x-2) \). At \( x = 2 \): \( 4 = 4A \Rightarrow A = 1 \). At \( x = -2 \): \( 4 = -4B \Rightarrow B = -1 \).
Step 3
Integrate. M1 M1
\( \int \tfrac{1}{x-2}\,dx - \int \tfrac{1}{x+2}\,dx = \ln|x-2| - \ln|x+2| + C \).
Step 4
Combine using log law. A1
\[ = \ln\!\left|\tfrac{x-2}{x+2}\right| + C \]
Answer hidden — click Reveal
Archetype revealed
Partial Fractions then Integrate.

Rational integrand with denominator factorable into two or more distinct factors. Decompose first, then integrate each piece using \( \int \tfrac{1}{x+c}\,dx = \ln|x+c| \).

Question 9

Example
Find the exact area of the region enclosed by the curve \( y = x^{2} \) and the line \( y = 2x + 3 \). [6]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Find intersections. M1
\( x^{2} = 2x + 3 \Rightarrow (x-3)(x+1) = 0 \Rightarrow x = 3 \) or \( x = -1 \).
Step 2
Decide upper. At \( x = 0 \): parabola \( = 0 \), line \( = 3 \). Line is upper. B1
Step 3
Set up. M1
\( A = \int_{-1}^{3}(2x + 3 - x^{2})\,dx \).
Step 4
Antiderivative.
\( \int (2x + 3 - x^{2})\,dx = x^{2} + 3x - \tfrac{x^{3}}{3} \).
Step 5
Evaluate. M1
At \( x = 3 \): \( 9 + 9 - 9 = 9 \). At \( x = -1 \): \( 1 - 3 + \tfrac{1}{3} = -\tfrac{5}{3} \). Difference: \( 9 + \tfrac{5}{3} = \tfrac{32}{3} \).
Step 6
Exact value. A1
\[ A = \tfrac{32}{3} \;\text{units}^{2} \]
Solution hidden — click Reveal
Practice
Find the exact area of the region enclosed by the curves \( y = x^{3} \) and \( y = x \). [6]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
Intersections. M1
\( x^{3} = x \Rightarrow x(x-1)(x+1) = 0 \Rightarrow x = -1, 0, 1 \). Two enclosed regions.
Step 2
Decide upper per region. On \( (0, 1) \): line above cubic. On \( (-1, 0) \): cubic above line. B1
Step 3
Two integrals. By symmetry, they're equal. M1
\( A = 2\int_{0}^{1}(x - x^{3})\,dx \).
Step 4
Integrate. M1
\( \int_{0}^{1}(x - x^{3})\,dx = \left[\tfrac{x^{2}}{2} - \tfrac{x^{4}}{4}\right]_{0}^{1} = \tfrac{1}{4} \).
Step 5
Double it. A1 A1
\[ A = 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{4} = \tfrac{1}{2} \;\text{units}^{2} \]
Answer hidden — click Reveal
Archetype revealed
Area Between Two Curves.

Find intersections → decide upper by test point → integrate (upper − lower) between limits. Watch for curves that cross each other — split at intersections.

Question 10

Example
The region bounded by \( y = x^{2} + 1 \), the x-axis, and the lines \( x = 0 \) and \( x = 2 \) is rotated through \( 360^{\circ} \) about the x-axis. Find the exact volume of the solid formed. [5]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
About x-axis: \( V = \pi\int_{a}^{b} y^{2}\,dx \). B1
Step 2
Compute \( y^{2} \). M1
\( y^{2} = (x^{2}+1)^{2} = x^{4} + 2x^{2} + 1 \).
Step 3
Integrate. M1
\( \int (x^{4} + 2x^{2} + 1)\,dx = \tfrac{x^{5}}{5} + \tfrac{2x^{3}}{3} + x \).
Step 4
Evaluate. M1
At \( x = 2 \): \( \tfrac{32}{5} + \tfrac{16}{3} + 2 = \tfrac{96 + 80 + 30}{15} = \tfrac{206}{15} \). At \( x = 0 \): 0.
Step 5
Multiply by \( \pi \). A1
\[ V = \tfrac{206\pi}{15} \;\text{units}^{3} \]
