Question 1 [6]
Gap drill · fresh
(i) Differentiate \(\tan^{-1}(2x)\) with respect to \(x\). [1] (ii) Hence, or otherwise, find \(\displaystyle\int \tan^{-1}(2x)\,\mathrm dx\). [3] (iii) Find the exact value of \(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1/2} \tan^{-1}(2x)\,\mathrm dx\). [2]
Before you reveal — say it out loud:  Confidence:  high  /  medium  /  guessed
Solution
(i) inverse-trig derivative, chain rule
MF27: \(\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm du}\tan^{-1}u=\frac{1}{1+u^2}\), here \(u=2x\) so \(u'=2\). \[ \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}\tan^{-1}(2x)=\frac{2}{1+4x^2}. \](1)
(ii) the inverse-trig is \(u\) in by parts; \(\mathrm dv=\mathrm dx\)
A lone inverse-trig integrand is always integration by parts with the inverse function as \(u\) (you can only differentiate it, not integrate it) and \(\mathrm dv=\mathrm dx\), so \(v=x\). \[ \int \tan^{-1}(2x)\,\mathrm dx = x\tan^{-1}(2x)-\int x\cdot\frac{2}{1+4x^2}\,\mathrm dx. \](1) The remaining integral is \(\frac{f'}{f}\) form: \(\int\frac{2x}{1+4x^2}\,\mathrm dx=\frac14\int\frac{8x}{1+4x^2}\,\mathrm dx=\frac14\ln(1+4x^2)\). \[ = x\tan^{-1}(2x)-\frac14\ln(1+4x^2)+C. \](1)(1)
(iii) apply the limits
At \(x=\tfrac12\): \(\tan^{-1}1=\tfrac{\pi}{4}\) and \(1+4(\tfrac14)=2\). At \(x=0\) both terms vanish. \[ \Big[x\tan^{-1}(2x)-\tfrac14\ln(1+4x^2)\Big]_{0}^{1/2}=\Big(\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac{\pi}{4}-\tfrac14\ln 2\Big)-0=\frac{\pi}{8}-\frac{\ln 2}{4}. \](1)(1)
Closes: the #1 gap — a lone inverse-trig integral is integration by parts with the inverse function as \(u\) and \(\mathrm dv=\mathrm dx\); the leftover resolves as \(\frac{f'}{f}\to\ln\).

Question 2 [6]
Gap drill · fresh
(a) Find \(\displaystyle\int x\big[(2-5x^2)^4+e^{1-x^2}\big]\,\mathrm dx\). [3] (b) Find \(\dfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}\sin^{-1}(x^3)\). Hence find the exact value of \(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{x^2}{\sqrt{1-x^6}}\,\mathrm dx\). [3]
Before you reveal — say it out loud:  Confidence:  high  /  medium  /  guessed
Solution
(a) reverse chain rule — balance with the reciprocal constant
\(\int x(2-5x^2)^4\,\mathrm dx\): inside \(2-5x^2\) differentiates to \(-10x\); the integrand has only \(x\), so pull out \(-\tfrac1{10}\), raise the power, divide by 5. \(-\tfrac1{10}\cdot\tfrac15=-\tfrac1{50}\). \(\int xe^{1-x^2}\,\mathrm dx\): inside \(1-x^2\) differentiates to \(-2x\); pull out \(-\tfrac12\). \[ \int x\big[(2-5x^2)^4+e^{1-x^2}\big]\,\mathrm dx=-\frac{(2-5x^2)^5}{50}-\tfrac12 e^{1-x^2}+C. \](1)(1)(1)
(b) differentiate the inverse-trig, then run it backwards
MF27: \(\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm du}\sin^{-1}u=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-u^2}}\) with \(u=x^3\), \(u'=3x^2\) — the derivative of the inside sits on top. \[ \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}\sin^{-1}(x^3)=\frac{3x^2}{\sqrt{1-x^6}}. \](1) The integrand is \(\tfrac13\) of that derivative; \(\sin^{-1}1=\tfrac{\pi}{2}\). \[ \int_{0}^{1}\frac{x^2}{\sqrt{1-x^6}}\,\mathrm dx=\tfrac13\Big[\sin^{-1}(x^3)\Big]_{0}^{1}=\tfrac13\cdot\tfrac{\pi}{2}=\frac{\pi}{6}. \](1)(1)
Closes: reverse-chain constant balancing (the recurring \(-\tfrac1{10}\) / \(-\tfrac1{50}\) slip) and the inverse-trig derivative where the numerator is the derivative of the inside, not \(1\).

Question 3 [7]
Gap drill · fresh
The curve \(C\) has equation \(y=x^3 e^{-x}\). (i) Find the exact coordinates of the stationary points of \(C\). [3] (ii) Using the second derivative test, determine the nature of each stationary point. [4]
Before you reveal — say it out loud:  Confidence:  high  /  medium  /  guessed
Solution
(i) product rule, set \(\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}=0\)
\(y=x^3e^{-x}\): product of \(x^3\) (derivative \(3x^2\)) and \(e^{-x}\) (derivative \(-e^{-x}\)). \[ \frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}=3x^2e^{-x}-x^3e^{-x}=x^2e^{-x}(3-x). \](1) \(e^{-x}\neq0\), so \(x^2(3-x)=0\Rightarrow x=0\) or \(x=3\). \[ (0,0)\quad\text{and}\quad\big(3,\,27e^{-3}\big). \](1)(1)
(ii) second derivative
Differentiate \(\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}=e^{-x}(3x^2-x^3)\) by the product rule again. \[ \frac{\mathrm d^2y}{\mathrm dx^2}=-e^{-x}(3x^2-x^3)+e^{-x}(6x-3x^2)=e^{-x}\big(x^3-6x^2+6x\big). \](1) At \(x=3\): \(e^{-3}(27-54+18)=-9e^{-3}<0\). \[ \frac{\mathrm d^2y}{\mathrm dx^2}\Big|_{x=3}<0\Rightarrow (3,27e^{-3})\text{ is a maximum.} \](1) At \(x=0\): \(\frac{\mathrm d^2y}{\mathrm dx^2}=0\), so the test is inconclusive — fall back to the first derivative test. Near \(x=0\), \(\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}=x^2e^{-x}(3-x)\): \(x^2\ge0\) and \(3-x>0\), so \(\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}\ge0\) on both sides. \[ (0,0)\text{ is a stationary point of inflexion.} \](1)(1)
Closes: the second-derivative test end to end — including the trap where \(\frac{\mathrm d^2y}{\mathrm dx^2}=0\) forces you back to the first-derivative sign test.

Question 4 [9]
Gap drill · fresh
A curve \(C\) has parametric equations \(x=t^2\), \(y=t-\tfrac13 t^3\), for \(t\ge 0\). (i) Find \(\dfrac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}\) in terms of \(t\). [2] (ii) Find the coordinates of the point where the tangent to \(C\) is horizontal, and show that the tangent is vertical only at the origin. [3] (iii) The curve meets the \(x\)-axis again at the point \(P\). Find the exact area of the region enclosed by \(C\) and the \(x\)-axis. [4]
Before you reveal — say it out loud:  Confidence:  high  /  medium  /  guessed
Solution
(i) parametric differentiation
\(\frac{\mathrm dx}{\mathrm dt}=2t\), \(\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dt}=1-t^2\). \[ \frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}=\frac{1-t^2}{2t}. \](1)(1)
(ii) horizontal \(\Leftrightarrow \frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dt}=0\); vertical \(\Leftrightarrow \frac{\mathrm dx}{\mathrm dt}=0\)
Horizontal: \(1-t^2=0\Rightarrow t=1\) (\(t\ge0\)); then \(x=1\), \(y=1-\tfrac13=\tfrac23\). \[ \left(1,\tfrac23\right). \](1)(1) Vertical: \(2t=0\Rightarrow t=0\), giving \((0,0)\) — the origin only. \[ \text{tangent vertical only at }(0,0). \](1)
(iii) parametric area \(\int y\,\frac{\mathrm dx}{\mathrm dt}\,\mathrm dt\); find the \(t\)-limits first
On the \(x\)-axis \(y=0\): \(t(1-\tfrac13 t^2)=0\Rightarrow t=0\) or \(t=\sqrt3\). So \(P\) is at \(t=\sqrt3\). \[ \text{Area}=\int_{0}^{\sqrt3}\!\Big(t-\tfrac13 t^3\Big)(2t)\,\mathrm dt=\int_{0}^{\sqrt3}\!\Big(2t^2-\tfrac23 t^4\Big)\mathrm dt. \](1)(1) \[ =\Big[\tfrac23 t^3-\tfrac{2}{15}t^5\Big]_{0}^{\sqrt3}=\tfrac23(3\sqrt3)-\tfrac{2}{15}(9\sqrt3)=2\sqrt3-\tfrac{6\sqrt3}{5}=\frac{4\sqrt3}{5}\ \text{units}^2. \](1)(1)
Closes: parametric gap in a non-trig shape — horizontal/vertical tangents from \(\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dt}\) and \(\frac{\mathrm dx}{\mathrm dt}\) separately, and the parametric area with the \(t\)-limits found from \(y=0\).

Question 5 [6]
Gap drill · fresh
(a) Find \(\displaystyle\int x\tan^{-1}x\,\mathrm dx\). [4] (b) Hence find the exact value of \(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1} x\tan^{-1}x\,\mathrm dx\). [2]
Before you reveal — say it out loud:  Confidence:  high  /  medium  /  guessed
Solution
(a) by parts with the inverse-trig as \(u\); the leftover needs division
\(u=\tan^{-1}x\) (\(u'=\frac{1}{1+x^2}\)), \(\mathrm dv=x\,\mathrm dx\Rightarrow v=\tfrac12 x^2\). \[ \int x\tan^{-1}x\,\mathrm dx=\tfrac12 x^2\tan^{-1}x-\tfrac12\int\frac{x^2}{1+x^2}\,\mathrm dx. \](1) The leftover is improper: \(\frac{x^2}{1+x^2}=1-\frac{1}{1+x^2}\) (divide first), so \(\int\frac{x^2}{1+x^2}\mathrm dx=x-\tan^{-1}x\). \[ =\tfrac12 x^2\tan^{-1}x-\tfrac12\big(x-\tan^{-1}x\big)+C=\tfrac12(x^2+1)\tan^{-1}x-\tfrac12 x+C. \](1)(1)(1)
(b) apply the limits
At \(x=1\): \(\tfrac12(2)\tan^{-1}1-\tfrac12=\tan^{-1}1-\tfrac12=\tfrac{\pi}{4}-\tfrac12\). At \(x=0\): \(0\). \[ \int_{0}^{1} x\tan^{-1}x\,\mathrm dx=\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac12. \](1)(1)
Closes: the inverse-trig by-parts again — but now with a polynomial multiplier, so the leftover integral is improper and needs \(\frac{x^2}{1+x^2}=1-\frac{1}{1+x^2}\) before you can finish.

Question 6 [8]
Gap drill · fresh
A drinking cup is a cone with its vertex pointing down. Taking the axis as the \(y\)-axis with the vertex at the origin, the sloping side is the line \(x=\tfrac12 y\), and the depth of liquid is \(h\) cm. (i) Show, by integration, that the volume of liquid is \(V=\dfrac{\pi h^3}{12}\). [3] (ii) Liquid is poured in at a constant rate of \(8\) cm\(^3\)s\(^{-1}\). Find the exact rate at which the depth is increasing when \(h=4\). [3] (iii) Find the exact rate at which the radius of the liquid surface is increasing at that same instant. [2]
Before you reveal — say it out loud:  Confidence:  high  /  medium  /  guessed
Solution
(i) volume of revolution about the \(y\)-axis: \(V=\pi\int x^2\,\mathrm dy\)
Rotating the line \(x=\tfrac12 y\) about the \(y\)-axis makes \(x\) the radius of each disc; integrate from \(y=0\) to \(y=h\). \[ V=\pi\int_{0}^{h}\Big(\tfrac12 y\Big)^2\,\mathrm dy=\pi\int_{0}^{h}\tfrac14 y^2\,\mathrm dy=\pi\Big[\tfrac{y^3}{12}\Big]_{0}^{h}=\frac{\pi h^3}{12}. \](1)(1)(1)
(ii) connected rates: \(\frac{\mathrm dh}{\mathrm dt}=\frac{\mathrm dV}{\mathrm dt}\div\frac{\mathrm dV}{\mathrm dh}\)
\(\frac{\mathrm dV}{\mathrm dt}=8\); \(\frac{\mathrm dV}{\mathrm dh}=\frac{\pi h^2}{4}\). \[ \frac{\mathrm dh}{\mathrm dt}=\frac{8}{\pi h^2/4}=\frac{32}{\pi h^2};\qquad h=4:\ \frac{\mathrm dh}{\mathrm dt}=\frac{32}{16\pi}=\frac{2}{\pi}\ \text{cm s}^{-1}. \](1)(1)(1)
(iii) surface radius \(r=\tfrac12 h\)
The surface radius satisfies \(r=\tfrac12 h\), so \(\frac{\mathrm dr}{\mathrm dt}=\tfrac12\frac{\mathrm dh}{\mathrm dt}\). \[ \frac{\mathrm dr}{\mathrm dt}=\tfrac12\cdot\frac{2}{\pi}=\frac{1}{\pi}\ \text{cm s}^{-1}. \](1)(1)
Closes: the same prove-a-volume-then-relate-rates skill as the bowl, but a different vessel and a second rate — the surface radius — chained off the first.

Question 7 [6]
Gap drill · fresh
The region \(R\) is bounded by the curve \(y=\dfrac{1}{x^2+1}\), the \(x\)-axis, and the lines \(x=0\) and \(x=1\). \(R\) is rotated through \(2\pi\) about the \(x\)-axis. Using the substitution \(x=\tan\theta\), find the exact volume generated. [6]
Before you reveal — say it out loud:  Confidence:  high  /  medium  /  guessed
Solution
volume of revolution, then the substitution (all three parts)
\(V=\pi\int_0^1 y^2\,\mathrm dx=\pi\int_0^1\frac{1}{(x^2+1)^2}\mathrm dx\). Sub \(x=\tan\theta\): \(\mathrm dx=\sec^2\theta\,\mathrm d\theta\); \((x^2+1)^2=(\sec^2\theta)^2=\sec^4\theta\); limits \(x=0\to\theta=0\), \(x=1\to\theta=\tfrac{\pi}{4}\). \[ V=\pi\int_{0}^{\pi/4}\frac{\sec^2\theta}{\sec^4\theta}\,\mathrm d\theta=\pi\int_{0}^{\pi/4}\cos^2\theta\,\mathrm d\theta. \](1)(1)
now a \(\cos^2\) integral — double angle
\(\cos^2\theta=\tfrac12(1+\cos 2\theta)\). \[ V=\pi\int_{0}^{\pi/4}\tfrac12(1+\cos 2\theta)\,\mathrm d\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}\Big[\theta+\tfrac12\sin 2\theta\Big]_{0}^{\pi/4}. \](1)(1) \[ =\frac{\pi}{2}\Big(\tfrac{\pi}{4}+\tfrac12\Big)=\frac{\pi^2}{8}+\frac{\pi}{4}\ \text{units}^3. \](1)(1)
Closes: \(x=\tan\theta\) where the integrand is \(\frac{1}{(x^2+1)^2}\), so after the substitution you still have to finish with the \(\cos^2\) double-angle step — two of your gaps in one integral.

Question 8 [6]
Gap drill · fresh
(a) By using integration by parts twice, find \(\displaystyle\int e^{x}\sin x\,\mathrm dx\). [4] (b) Hence find the exact value of \(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi} e^{x}\sin x\,\mathrm dx\). [2]
Before you reveal — say it out loud:  Confidence:  high  /  medium  /  guessed
Solution
(a) cyclic by parts — the integral reappears, so name it \(I\)
Let \(I=\int e^x\sin x\,\mathrm dx\). By parts (\(u=\sin x\), \(\mathrm dv=e^x\mathrm dx\)): \(I=e^x\sin x-\int e^x\cos x\,\mathrm dx\). By parts again on \(\int e^x\cos x\,\mathrm dx\) (\(u=\cos x\)): \(=e^x\cos x+\int e^x\sin x\,\mathrm dx=e^x\cos x+I\). \[ I=e^x\sin x-\big(e^x\cos x+I\big)=e^x(\sin x-\cos x)-I. \](1)(1) The original \(I\) is back on the right, so solve for it: \(2I=e^x(\sin x-\cos x)\). \[ I=\tfrac12 e^x(\sin x-\cos x)+C. \](1)(1)
(b) apply the limits
At \(x=\pi\): \(\tfrac12 e^{\pi}(0-(-1))=\tfrac12 e^{\pi}\). At \(x=0\): \(\tfrac12(0-1)=-\tfrac12\). \[ \int_{0}^{\pi} e^{x}\sin x\,\mathrm dx=\tfrac12 e^{\pi}-\big(-\tfrac12\big)=\frac{e^{\pi}+1}{2}. \](1)(1)
Closes: cyclic by parts (the "let \(I=\)" trick where the integral returns and you solve for it) — a different beast from the repeated-by-parts on \(x^2e^{-3x}\), which just terminates.

Question 9 [6]
Gap drill · fresh
Using the substitution \(u^2=1+x^2\), find \(\displaystyle\int x^3\sqrt{1+x^2}\,\mathrm dx\). [6]
Before you reveal — say it out loud:  Confidence:  high  /  medium  /  guessed
Solution
change everything to \(u\) — including the spare \(x^2\)
\(u^2=1+x^2\Rightarrow 2u\,\mathrm du=2x\,\mathrm dx\), so \(x\,\mathrm dx=u\,\mathrm du\); the surd \(\sqrt{1+x^2}=u\); and the leftover \(x^2=u^2-1\). Split \(x^3=x^2\cdot x\) so one \(x\) pairs with \(\mathrm dx\). \[ \int x^3\sqrt{1+x^2}\,\mathrm dx=\int x^2\cdot\sqrt{1+x^2}\cdot x\,\mathrm dx=\int (u^2-1)\,u\,\cdot u\,\mathrm du. \](1)(1)
integrate in \(u\), then back-substitute
\[ =\int (u^4-u^2)\,\mathrm du=\frac{u^5}{5}-\frac{u^3}{3}+C. \](1)(1) Return to \(x\) with \(u=(1+x^2)^{1/2}\). \[ =\frac{(1+x^2)^{5/2}}{5}-\frac{(1+x^2)^{3/2}}{3}+C. \](1)(1)
Closes: the substitution discipline — every \(x\) becomes \(u\), including the leftover \(x^2=u^2-1\) that is easy to forget; harder than the \(u^2=2-x\) case because the spare factor is a full \(x^2\).

Question 10 [7]
Gap drill · fresh
(a) Express \(\dfrac{2x^3+x^2+3}{(x-1)(x^2+1)}\) in the form \(A+\dfrac{B}{x-1}+\dfrac{Cx+D}{x^2+1}\). [3] (b) Hence find \(\displaystyle\int \frac{2x^3+x^2+3}{(x-1)(x^2+1)}\,\mathrm dx\). [4]
Before you reveal — say it out loud:  Confidence:  high  /  medium  /  guessed
Solution
(a) the fraction is improper — divide FIRST
Numerator degree \(3\) = denominator degree \(3\), so long-divide before splitting. Denominator \((x-1)(x^2+1)=x^3-x^2+x-1\). \(2x^3+x^2+3=2(x^3-x^2+x-1)+(3x^2-2x+5)\), so \(A=2\) and the remainder is \(\frac{3x^2-2x+5}{(x-1)(x^2+1)}\). Split the remainder: \(3x^2-2x+5=B(x^2+1)+(Cx+D)(x-1)\). \(x=1\): \(6=2B\Rightarrow B=3\). Compare \(x^2\): \(3=B+C\Rightarrow C=0\). Compare constants: \(5=B-D\Rightarrow D=-2\). \[ \frac{2x^3+x^2+3}{(x-1)(x^2+1)}=2+\frac{3}{x-1}-\frac{2}{x^2+1}. \](1)(1)(1)
(b) integrate term by term
\(\int 2\,\mathrm dx=2x\); \(\int\frac{3}{x-1}\mathrm dx=3\ln|x-1|\); \(\int\frac{2}{x^2+1}\mathrm dx=2\tan^{-1}x\) (MF27). \[ \int\frac{2x^3+x^2+3}{(x-1)(x^2+1)}\,\mathrm dx=2x+3\ln|x-1|-2\tan^{-1}x+C. \](1)(1)(1)(1)
Closes: partial fractions when the fraction is improper — you must polynomial-divide first (the constant \(A=2\)) before the irreducible-quadratic split; a level up from the proper fraction in the redrill.
