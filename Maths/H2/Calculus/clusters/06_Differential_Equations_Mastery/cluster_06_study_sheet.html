Meta-rules — read before every card

Rule 1: Three patterns and their triggers

PatternDE formTrigger words
1\( \dfrac{dN}{dt} = \pm kN \)"proportional to the amount / mass / population itself"; only one quantity in play; no ceiling mentioned
2\( \dfrac{dN}{dt} = -k(N - K) \)"proportional to the difference / gap / deficit"; two temperatures / two values named; system tending toward a target
3\( \dfrac{dN}{dt} = kN(K - N) \)"proportional to the product of \(x\) and \(y\)"; both current and remaining-capacity present

Sign of \( k \): always state \( k > 0 \). The physical direction (growth/decay) is set by the sign in front of the RHS, not by \( k \) itself.

Rule 2: What is \( A \)? (this is the biggest omission in most notes)

PatternGeneral solution form\( A \) equals
1\( N = A\,e^{\pm kt} \)initial value \( N_0 \)
2\( N = K + A\,e^{-kt} \)\( N_0 - K \) — the initial gap. Can be negative if starting below the target.
3\( \dfrac{N}{K - N} = A\,e^{Kkt} \)\( \dfrac{N_0}{K - N_0} \) — a ratio, not a value.

Rule 3: Method for Pattern 2 — DO NOT EXPAND

Keep the bracket \( (N - K) \) or \( (K - N) \) intact. Then:

  • \( \displaystyle \int \dfrac{1}{N - K}\,dN = \ln|N - K| \)
  • \( \displaystyle \int \dfrac{1}{K - N}\,dN = -\ln|K - N| \)  — chain rule: derivative of \( (K - N) \) is \( -1 \), so a minus falls out

If you expand into a linear expression like \( 25 - T \) and integrate the two pieces separately, you cannot combine cleanly, you lose track of the constant, and the exponentiation fails.

Rule 4: Method for Pattern 3 — partial fractions

The template:

\[ \dfrac{1}{N(K - N)} = \dfrac{1}{K}\left[\dfrac{1}{N} + \dfrac{1}{K - N}\right] \]

Then integrate each piece (watching the chain-rule sign on the second one), group the two logs with \( \ln a - \ln b = \ln(a/b) \), and multiply through by \( K \) to clear the leading \( 1/K \). The exponent coefficient in the final answer is always \( Kk \), never just \( k \).

Rule 5: Setup for tank / mixing / compartment problems

Always start with the physical accounting:

\[ \dfrac{dS}{dt} = (\text{rate in}) - (\text{rate out}) \]
  • Rate in = (source concentration) × (inflow rate). A constant.
  • Rate out = (current tank concentration) × (outflow rate) = \( \left(\dfrac{S}{V}\right) \cdot F_{\text{out}} \)

If inflow = outflow, volume \( V \) stays constant, so rate out is linear in \( S \). The result is always of the form \( \dfrac{dS}{dt} = C - rS \), which factorises to \( -r\!\left(S - \dfrac{C}{r}\right) \) — Pattern 2 with target \( K = C/r \).

Rule 6: The universal seven-step recipe

  1. Identify the pattern from the keywords.
  2. Set the sign of \( k \) (growth: \(+\); decay/cooling: \(-\)). Always state \( k > 0 \).
  3. Separate variables — every \( N \) on the left, every \( t \) on the right.
  4. Integrate both sides. Pattern 2: don't expand. Pattern 3: partial fractions.
  5. Isolate the \( \ln \), add \( +C \), then exponentiate. Merge \( \pm e^C \) into a single constant \( A \).
  6. Substitute the initial condition into the exponentiated form (not the log form) to find \( A \).
  7. Present the explicit function, then answer whatever the question actually asks (long-term / find \( t \) / find \( k \) / describe behaviour).

Rule 7: What questions typically ask (per pattern)

  • Pattern 1: long-term behaviour (\( N \to 0 \) or \( N \to \infty \)); comment on model suitability (population can't grow indefinitely).
  • Pattern 2: limiting value \( K \); time to reach a given \( N \); state that fastest change happens at \( t = 0 \) when the gap \( |N_0 - K| \) is largest.
  • Pattern 3: carrying capacity \( K \); equilibrium values (\( N = 0 \) and \( N = K \)); value at fastest growth (\( N = K/2 \)); long-term (\( N \to K \)).

Question 2

Example
A radioactive substance of mass \( M \) g decays at a rate proportional to the mass remaining, with positive constant \( k \). Initially the mass is \( 200 \) g. After \( 5 \) years, the mass is \( 150 \) g.

(i) Write down the differential equation and initial condition. [2]
(ii) Solve the differential equation, giving \( M \) in terms of \( t \) and \( k \). [4]
(iii) Find the value of \( k \) correct to 4 decimal places. [2]
(iv) Find the half-life of the substance. [3]
(v) Describe the long-term behaviour of \( M \). [1]
Solution
(i) DE and IC
B1 B1
\[ \dfrac{dM}{dt} = -kM, \quad k > 0, \quad M(0) = 200 \]
(ii) Solve
M1 M1 M1 A1
Separate: \[ \int \dfrac{1}{M}\,dM = \int -k\,dt \] \[ \ln|M| = -kt + C \] Exponentiate: \[ |M| = e^{-kt + C} = e^C \, e^{-kt} \] Since \( M > 0 \), write \( M = A e^{-kt} \) with \( A = e^C > 0 \). Apply IC \( M(0) = 200 \): \[ 200 = A e^0 = A \implies A = 200 \] \[ \boxed{M = 200\,e^{-kt}} \]
(iii) Find \(k\)
M1 A1
Given \( M(5) = 150 \): \[ 150 = 200\,e^{-5k} \] \[ e^{-5k} = \dfrac{3}{4} \] \[ -5k = \ln\dfrac{3}{4} \] \[ k = \dfrac{1}{5}\ln\dfrac{4}{3} = 0.0575 \; (\text{4 dp}) \]
(iv) Half-life
M1 M1 A1
Half-life is the time \( t_h \) such that \( M(t_h) = 100 \). \[ 100 = 200\,e^{-k t_h} \implies e^{-k t_h} = \dfrac{1}{2} \] \[ t_h = \dfrac{\ln 2}{k} = \dfrac{5 \ln 2}{\ln(4/3)} \approx 12.05 \; \text{years} \]
(v) Long-term
B1
As \( t \to \infty \), \( e^{-kt} \to 0 \), so \( M \to 0 \). The substance decays away completely.

Question 4

Example
A cold drink at \( 5^{\circ}\text{C} \) is placed in a room at \( 25^{\circ}\text{C} \). The rate of warming is proportional to the temperature difference between the drink and the room, with positive constant \( k \). After \( 6 \) minutes, the drink is at \( 12^{\circ}\text{C} \).

(i) Write down the differential equation and initial condition. [2]
(ii) Show that \( T = 25 - 20\,e^{-kt} \). [4]
(iii) Find \( k \) correct to 4 decimal places. [2]
(iv) Find the time taken for the drink to reach \( 20^{\circ}\text{C} \). [3]
Solution
Trap
The drink is BELOW room temperature, so it warms UP. In the DE, the "temperature difference" is now \( (25 - T) \), which is positive. Sign of the RHS is \( +k(25 - T) = -k(T - 25) \). The gap \( A = T_0 - K = 5 - 25 = -20 \) is negative. Do not force \( A \) to be positive.
(i) DE and IC
B1 B1
\[ \dfrac{dT}{dt} = k(25 - T), \quad k > 0, \quad T(0) = 5 \] Equivalent: \( \dfrac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - 25) \).
(ii) Solve
M1 M1 M1 A1
Keep the bracket \( (T - 25) \): \[ \int \dfrac{1}{T - 25}\,dT = \int -k\,dt \] \[ \ln|T - 25| = -kt + C \implies T - 25 = A\,e^{-kt} \] Apply IC \( T(0) = 5 \): \[ 5 - 25 = A \implies A = -20 \] \[ T = 25 - 20\,e^{-kt} \text{ (shown)} \]
(iii) Find \(k\)
M1 A1
\[ 12 = 25 - 20\,e^{-6k} \implies 20\,e^{-6k} = 13 \implies e^{-6k} = \dfrac{13}{20} \] \[ k = \dfrac{1}{6}\ln\dfrac{20}{13} = 0.0718 \; (\text{4 dp}) \]
(iv) Time to \(20^{\circ}\text{C}\)
M1 M1 A1
\[ 20 = 25 - 20\,e^{-kt} \implies 20\,e^{-kt} = 5 \implies e^{-kt} = \dfrac{1}{4} \] \[ t = \dfrac{\ln 4}{k} = \dfrac{6 \ln 4}{\ln(20/13)} \approx 19.31 \; \text{minutes} \]

Question 5

Example
A drug is administered to a patient by continuous intravenous injection at a constant rate of \( 4 \) mg per hour. The body simultaneously removes the drug at a rate proportional to the amount \( D \) mg present in the bloodstream, with positive constant \( 0.5 \). Initially there is no drug in the body.

(i) By considering the rate at which the drug enters and leaves the body, show that \( \dfrac{dD}{dt} = 4 - 0.5\,D \). [2]
(ii) Show that \( D = 8\!\left(1 - e^{-t/2}\right) \). [5]
(iii) State the long-term amount of drug in the body. [1]
(iv) Find the time taken for \( D \) to reach \( 6 \) mg. [2]
Solution
Trap
This is not straight Pattern 2 — the DE looks unfamiliar. Do not try to solve it directly. Factorise \( 4 - 0.5D = -0.5(D - 8) \) first: this reveals it as Pattern 2 with target \( K = 4 / 0.5 = 8 \). The steady state (long-term value) is always \( K = \text{rate in} / \text{sink rate} \).
(i) Rate in – rate out
B1 B1
Rate in = \( 4 \) mg/hr (constant).
Rate out = \( 0.5\,D \) mg/hr (proportional to \( D \)).
By the accumulation principle: \[ \dfrac{dD}{dt} = 4 - 0.5\,D \text{ (shown)} \]
(ii) Solve
M1 M1 M1 M1 A1
Factorise: \[ \dfrac{dD}{dt} = -0.5(D - 8) \] Separate: \[ \int \dfrac{1}{D - 8}\,dD = \int -0.5\,dt \] \[ \ln|D - 8| = -0.5\,t + C \implies D - 8 = A\,e^{-t/2} \] Apply IC \( D(0) = 0 \): \[ 0 - 8 = A \implies A = -8 \] \[ D = 8 - 8\,e^{-t/2} = 8\!\left(1 - e^{-t/2}\right) \text{ (shown)} \]
(iii) Long-term
B1
As \( t \to \infty \), \( e^{-t/2} \to 0 \), so \( D \to 8 \) mg.
(iv) Time to 6 mg
M1 A1
\[ 6 = 8\!\left(1 - e^{-t/2}\right) \implies e^{-t/2} = \dfrac{1}{4} \] \[ t = 2\ln 4 = 4\ln 2 \approx 2.77 \; \text{hours} \]

Question 6

Example
In a town of \( 10\,000 \) people, a disease spreads such that the rate of increase of the number \( I \) of infected people is proportional to the product of the number of infected and the number not yet infected. The constant of proportionality is \( 2 \times 10^{-4} \). Initially \( 100 \) people are infected.

(i) Write down the differential equation and initial condition. [2]
(ii) Show that \( \dfrac{I}{10\,000 - I} = \dfrac{1}{99}\,e^{2t} \). [6]
(iii) Find the number of infected people after \( 3 \) time units, to the nearest whole number. [2]
(iv) Find the time at which half of the town is infected. [2]
(v) State what happens to \( I \) in the long term. [1]
Solution
Trap
The exponent in the final answer is \( Kk\,t \), not \( kt \). Here \( K = 10\,000 \) and \( k = 2 \times 10^{-4} \), so \( Kk = 2 \). Missing this factor is the most common Pattern 3 error.
(i) DE and IC
B1 B1
\[ \dfrac{dI}{dt} = 2 \times 10^{-4}\,I(10\,000 - I), \quad I(0) = 100 \]

M1 M1 M1 M1 M1 A1

(ii) Solve the differential equation, giving \( S \) in terms of \( t \). [3]
(iii) Find the time taken for the salt in the tank to halve. [2]
(iv) At time \( t = 0 \), the fresh water source is replaced with brine of concentration \( 0.5 \) kg/L, flowing in at \( 5 \) L/min (with mixed brine still leaving at \( 5 \) L/min). Show that \( \dfrac{dS}{dt} = 2.5 - \dfrac{S}{20} \), and find \( S \) in terms of \( t \). [5]
(v) State the long-term amount of salt in the tank under the new inflow. [1]
Solution
Trap
Part (i) is Pattern 1 (fresh water in → rate in = 0). Part (iv) is Pattern 2 (constant source + proportional sink → collapses via factorisation to Pattern 2 with target \( K = 2.5 / (1/20) = 50 \)). Same tank, different regime — this is the classic synthesis exam question.
(i) Rate-in – rate-out (fresh water regime)
B1 B1
Rate in = \( 0 \) kg/L \( \times \) \( 5 \) L/min = \( 0 \).
Rate out = concentration in tank \( \times \) outflow = \( \dfrac{S}{100} \times 5 = \dfrac{S}{20} \) kg/min. \[ \dfrac{dS}{dt} = 0 - \dfrac{S}{20} = -\dfrac{S}{20} \text{ (shown)} \]
(ii) Solve (Pattern 1)
M1 M1 A1
\[ \int \dfrac{1}{S}\,dS = \int -\dfrac{1}{20}\,dt \implies \ln|S| = -\dfrac{t}{20} + C \] \[ S = A\,e^{-t/20}, \quad S(0) = 10 \Rightarrow A = 10 \] \[ \boxed{S = 10\,e^{-t/20}} \]
(iii) Half-life of salt
M1 A1
\[ 5 = 10\,e^{-t/20} \implies e^{-t/20} = \dfrac{1}{2} \implies t = 20 \ln 2 \approx 13.86 \; \text{minutes} \]
(iv) New regime — rate-in – rate-out, then solve
B1 B1 M1 M1 A1
Rate in = \( 0.5 \times 5 = 2.5 \) kg/min. Rate out = \( \dfrac{S}{20} \) kg/min (unchanged). \[ \dfrac{dS}{dt} = 2.5 - \dfrac{S}{20} \text{ (shown)} \] Factorise: \[ \dfrac{dS}{dt} = -\dfrac{1}{20}(S - 50) \] \[ \ln|S - 50| = -\dfrac{t}{20} + C \implies S - 50 = A\,e^{-t/20} \] At the moment of switching, \( S = 10 \) (from part ii evaluated at \( t = 0 \) of the new regime): \[ A = 10 - 50 = -40 \] \[ \boxed{S = 50 - 40\,e^{-t/20}} \]
(v) Long-term
B1
As \( t \to \infty \), \( S \to 50 \) kg.
