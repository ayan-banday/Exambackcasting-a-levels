Question 1

Example
A curve has equation \( x^{2} + y^{2} - xy = 7 \).
(i) Find \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} \) in terms of \( x \) and \( y \). [3]
(ii) Find the coordinates of the points on the curve where the tangent is parallel to the x-axis. [4]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i)
Differentiate every term w.r.t. \( x \). Chain rule on \( y^{2} \) (gives \( 2y\dfrac{dy}{dx} \)), product rule on \( xy \). M1
\( 2x + 2y\dfrac{dy}{dx} - \left(y + x\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right) = 0 \).
Step 2
Group \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} \) terms. M1
\( (2y - x)\dfrac{dy}{dx} = y - 2x \).
Step 3
Solve. A1
\[ \dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{y - 2x}{2y - x} \]
Step 4 — (ii)
Tangent parallel to x-axis \(\Leftrightarrow \dfrac{dy}{dx} = 0 \Leftrightarrow \) numerator \( = 0 \). M1
\( y - 2x = 0 \Rightarrow y = 2x \).
Step 5
Substitute \( y = 2x \) into the original curve. M1
\( x^{2} + 4x^{2} - 2x^{2} = 7 \Rightarrow 3x^{2} = 7 \Rightarrow x = \pm\sqrt{7/3} \).
Step 6
Corresponding \( y \)-coordinates. A1 A1
Points: \( \left(\sqrt{\tfrac{7}{3}},\; 2\sqrt{\tfrac{7}{3}}\right) \) and \( \left(-\sqrt{\tfrac{7}{3}},\; -2\sqrt{\tfrac{7}{3}}\right) \).

A curve has equation \( x^{2}y + y^{3} = 6 \).
(i) Show that \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{2xy}{x^{2} + 3y^{2}} \). [3]
(ii) Find the exact coordinates of the points on the curve where the tangent is parallel to the y-axis. [3]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i)
Differentiate: product rule on \( x^{2}y \), chain rule on \( y^{3} \). M1
\( 2xy + x^{2}\dfrac{dy}{dx} + 3y^{2}\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 0 \).
Step 2
Group and solve. M1 A1 (AG)
\( (x^{2} + 3y^{2})\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -2xy \Rightarrow \dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{2xy}{x^{2} + 3y^{2}} \).
Step 3 — (ii)
Tangent parallel to y-axis \(\Leftrightarrow\) denominator \( = 0 \) (vertical tangent). M1
\( x^{2} + 3y^{2} = 0 \).
Step 4
Analyse. Since \( x^{2} \ge 0 \) and \( 3y^{2} \ge 0 \), only \( x = 0, y = 0 \) works. M1
Check in curve: \( 0 = 6 \) — false.
Step 5
Conclusion. A1
No such points exist on the curve.

Question 2

Example
A curve has parametric equations \( x = t + \dfrac{1}{t},\; y = t - \dfrac{1}{t} \) for \( t \ne 0 \).
(i) Find \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} \) in terms of \( t \). [2]
(ii) Show that the equation of the tangent at the point where \( t = p \) is \( (p^{2} - 1)x - (p^{2} + 1)y = \dfrac{4}{p} \). [4]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i)
Differentiate each parametric equation w.r.t. \( t \). M1
\( \dfrac{dx}{dt} = 1 - \dfrac{1}{t^{2}} = \dfrac{t^{2} - 1}{t^{2}} \); \( \dfrac{dy}{dt} = 1 + \dfrac{1}{t^{2}} = \dfrac{t^{2} + 1}{t^{2}} \).
Step 2
Divide. A1
\[ \dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{t^{2} + 1}{t^{2} - 1} \]
Step 3 — (ii)
Find point and gradient at \( t = p \). B1
Point: \( \left(p + \tfrac{1}{p},\; p - \tfrac{1}{p}\right) \). Gradient \( m = \dfrac{p^{2} + 1}{p^{2} - 1} \).
Step 4
Apply point-slope form. M1
\( y - \left(p - \tfrac{1}{p}\right) = \dfrac{p^{2} + 1}{p^{2} - 1}\!\left[x - \left(p + \tfrac{1}{p}\right)\right] \).
Step 5
Multiply both sides by \( p(p^{2} - 1) \) to clear fractions, expand, tidy. M1 A1 (AG)
\[ (p^{2} - 1)x - (p^{2} + 1)y = \dfrac{4}{p} \]

(i) Find \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} \) in terms of \( t \). [2]
(ii) Show that the equation of the tangent at the point with parameter \( p \) is \( y = px - p^{3} \). [3]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i)
Differentiate. M1
\( \dfrac{dx}{dt} = 6t \); \( \dfrac{dy}{dt} = 6t^{2} \).
Step 2
Divide. A1
\( \dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{6t^{2}}{6t} = t \).
Step 3 — (ii)
Point and gradient at \( t = p \). B1
Point \( (3p^{2}, 2p^{3}) \); gradient \( m = p \).
Step 4
Point-slope form. M1
\( y - 2p^{3} = p(x - 3p^{2}) \).
Step 5
Expand and tidy. A1 (AG)
\[ y = px - 3p^{3} + 2p^{3} = px - p^{3} \]

Question 3

Example
The curve \( C \) has equation \( y = x^{3} - 2x^{2} \). The point \( A \) on \( C \) has x-coordinate 2.
(i) Find the equation of the normal to \( C \) at \( A \). [3]
(ii) Determine whether the normal meets \( C \) again. [4]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i)
Find \( y \)-coordinate at \( x = 2 \).
\( y = 8 - 8 = 0 \). So \( A = (2, 0) \).
Step 2
Find tangent gradient. M1
\( \dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3x^{2} - 4x \). At \( x = 2 \): \( m = 12 - 8 = 4 \).
Step 3
Normal gradient. M1
\( -\dfrac{1}{m} = -\dfrac{1}{4} \).
Step 4
Point-slope form and simplify. A1
\( y - 0 = -\tfrac{1}{4}(x - 2) \Rightarrow y = -\tfrac{1}{4}x + \tfrac{1}{2} \).
Step 5 — (ii)
Solve system: set curve = normal. M1
\( x^{3} - 2x^{2} = -\tfrac{1}{4}x + \tfrac{1}{2} \). Multiply by 4: \( 4x^{3} - 8x^{2} + x - 2 = 0 \).
Step 6
\( x = 2 \) is a known root (at \( A \)). Factor. M1
\( (x - 2)(4x^{2} + 1) = 0 \).
Step 7
Analyse. \( 4x^{2} + 1 > 0 \) for all real \( x \), so no other real roots. A1
Step 8
Conclusion. A1
The normal does not meet \( C \) again.

(i) Show that \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{x^{2} - 2x}{(x - 1)^{2}} \). [2]
(ii) Find the equation of the tangent to \( C \) at the point where \( x = 2 \). [3]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i)
Quotient rule: \( u = x^{2},\; v = x-1 \). M1
\( u' = 2x,\; v' = 1 \).
Step 2
Apply and simplify. A1 (AG)
\( \dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{2x(x-1) - x^{2}}{(x-1)^{2}} = \dfrac{x^{2} - 2x}{(x-1)^{2}} \).
Step 3 — (ii)
Find \( y \)-coordinate at \( x = 2 \). B1
\( y = \dfrac{4}{1} = 4 \). Point \( (2, 4) \).
Step 4
Gradient at \( x = 2 \). M1
\( m = \dfrac{4 - 4}{1} = 0 \). Tangent is horizontal.
Step 5
Equation. A1
\( y = 4 \).

Question 4

Example
A right circular cone with semi-vertical angle \( 30^{\circ} \) is held vertex downward with its axis vertical. Water is poured in at a constant rate of \( 8 \) cm³ s\(^{-1}\). Find the rate at which the depth of water is increasing when the depth is \( 10 \) cm. [5]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Geometry: relate \( r \) to \( h \) using the half-angle. B1
\( \tan 30^{\circ} = \dfrac{r}{h} \Rightarrow r = \dfrac{h}{\sqrt{3}} \).
Step 2
Volume in terms of \( h \) only. M1
\( V = \tfrac{1}{3}\pi r^{2} h = \tfrac{1}{3}\pi\cdot\tfrac{h^{2}}{3}\cdot h = \dfrac{\pi h^{3}}{9} \).
Step 3
Differentiate. M1
\( \dfrac{dV}{dh} = \dfrac{\pi h^{2}}{3} \). At \( h = 10 \): \( \dfrac{dV}{dh} = \dfrac{100\pi}{3} \).
Step 4
Chain rule: \( \dfrac{dV}{dt} = \dfrac{dV}{dh}\cdot\dfrac{dh}{dt} \). M1
\( 8 = \dfrac{100\pi}{3}\cdot\dfrac{dh}{dt} \).
Step 5
Solve. A1
\( \dfrac{dh}{dt} = \dfrac{6}{25\pi} \approx 0.0764 \) cm s\(^{-1}\).

Practice
A spherical raindrop evaporates so that its volume decreases at \( 0.5 \) mm³ s\(^{-1}\). Find the rate at which the radius is decreasing when \( r = 2 \) mm. [5]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
Volume of sphere. B1
\( V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi r^{3} \Rightarrow \dfrac{dV}{dr} = 4\pi r^{2} \).
Step 2
Evaluate at \( r = 2 \). M1 A1
\( \dfrac{dV}{dr} = 16\pi \).
Step 3
Chain rule. \( V \) is DECREASING, so \( \dfrac{dV}{dt} = -0.5 \) (sign matters). M1
\( -0.5 = 16\pi\cdot\dfrac{dr}{dt} \).
Step 4
Solve. A1
\( \dfrac{dr}{dt} = -\dfrac{1}{32\pi} \approx -0.00995 \) mm s\(^{-1}\). Radius decreasing at \( \dfrac{1}{32\pi} \) mm s\(^{-1}\).

Question 5

Example
A closed cylindrical can of fixed volume \( 500 \) cm³ is to be made. The top and bottom cost twice as much per unit area as the curved surface. Find the radius and height of the can that minimise the total cost. (You need not show that your answer gives a minimum.) [7]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Constraint. B1
\( V = \pi r^{2}h = 500 \Rightarrow h = \dfrac{500}{\pi r^{2}} \).
Step 2
Cost function. Let \( k \) = cost per unit area of curved surface. Top+bottom area = \( 2\pi r^{2} \), curved area = \( 2\pi r h \). M1
\( C = 2k(2\pi r^{2}) + k(2\pi r h) = 4\pi k r^{2} + 2\pi k r h \).
Step 3
Eliminate \( h \). M1
\( C = 4\pi k r^{2} + \dfrac{1000k}{r} \).
Step 4
Differentiate and set to zero. M1
\( \dfrac{dC}{dr} = 8\pi k r - \dfrac{1000k}{r^{2}} = 0 \Rightarrow 8\pi r^{3} = 1000 \).
Step 5
Solve for \( r \). A1
\( r^{3} = \dfrac{125}{\pi} \Rightarrow r = \dfrac{5}{\sqrt[3]{\pi}} \approx 3.41 \) cm.
Step 6
Find \( h \) from the constraint. A1
\( h = \dfrac{500}{\pi r^{2}} \approx 6.83 \) cm. (Clean ratio: \( h = 2r \).)
Step 7
State the optimum. A1
Radius \( \approx 3.41 \) cm, height \( \approx 6.83 \) cm.

Practice
A rectangle is inscribed in a semicircle of radius \( 10 \) cm, with one side on the diameter. Find the dimensions of the rectangle that maximises its area, and the maximum area. (You need not show your answer is a maximum.) [7]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
Set up coordinates. Let rectangle vertices be \( (\pm x, 0) \) and \( (\pm x, y) \) on the semicircle. B1
Constraint: \( x^{2} + y^{2} = 100 \Rightarrow y = \sqrt{100 - x^{2}} \).
Step 2
Area function. M1
\( A = 2xy = 2x\sqrt{100 - x^{2}} \).
Step 3
Differentiate using product rule + chain rule. M1
\( \dfrac{dA}{dx} = 2\sqrt{100 - x^{2}} + 2x\cdot\dfrac{-x}{\sqrt{100 - x^{2}}} = \dfrac{200 - 4x^{2}}{\sqrt{100 - x^{2}}} \).
Step 4
Set to zero. M1 A1
\( 200 - 4x^{2} = 0 \Rightarrow x^{2} = 50 \Rightarrow x = 5\sqrt{2} \).
Step 5
Find \( y \). A1
\( y = \sqrt{100 - 50} = \sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2} \).
Step 6
Maximum area and dimensions. A1
Width \( 2x = 10\sqrt{2} \approx 14.1 \) cm; height \( y = 5\sqrt{2} \approx 7.07 \) cm.
Max area \( A = 100 \) cm².

Question 6

Example
A function \( f \) satisfies \( f'(x) = (x - 1)(x - 3)^{2} \).
(i) State the values of \( x \) where \( f \) has a local max or min, with reasons. [3]
(ii) Sketch \( y = f'(x) \), indicating x-axis crossings and behaviour. [2]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i)
Find zeros of \( f' \). B1
\( f'(x) = 0 \) at \( x = 1 \) (simple root) and \( x = 3 \) (double root).
Step 2
Sign analysis of \( f'(x) \) around each zero.
  • For \( x < 1 \): \( (x-1) < 0,\; (x-3)^{2} > 0 \Rightarrow f' < 0 \). \( f \) decreasing.
  • For \( 1 < x < 3 \): \( (x-1) > 0,\; (x-3)^{2} > 0 \Rightarrow f' > 0 \). \( f \) increasing.
  • For \( x > 3 \): both factors positive \(\Rightarrow f' > 0 \). \( f \) still increasing.
Step 3
Classify. M1 A1
At \( x = 1 \): \( f' \) changes \( - \to + \Rightarrow \) local min at \( x = 1 \).
At \( x = 3 \): \( f' \) does NOT change sign (touches zero and stays positive) \(\Rightarrow\) stationary point of inflexion, NOT a max or min.
Step 4 — (ii)
Sketch (see graph above). Cubic shape, crosses at \( x = 1 \), touches at \( x = 3 \). B1 B1

Practice
The graph of \( y = f(x) \) has a local maximum at \( (-2, 5) \), a local minimum at \( (1, -3) \), and a non-stationary point of inflexion at \( x = 4 \). \( f \) is increasing on \( (-\infty, -2) \cup (1, \infty) \) and decreasing on \( (-2, 1) \). Sketch \( y = f'(x) \), labelling all x-axis crossings and turning points. [4]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
Translate features of \( f \) into features of \( f' \).
  • Local max of \( f \) at \( x = -2 \) \(\Rightarrow f'(-2) = 0 \), \( f' \) crosses from \( + \) to \( - \). B1
  • Local min of \( f \) at \( x = 1 \) \(\Rightarrow f'(1) = 0 \), \( f' \) crosses from \( - \) to \( + \). B1
  • Inflexion of \( f \) at \( x = 4 \) \(\Rightarrow f''(4) = 0 \), so \( f' \) has a turning point at \( x = 4 \) (does NOT cross axis there). B1
Step 2
Sign of \( f'(x) \) on each interval. B1
\( f' > 0 \) on \( (-\infty, -2) \), \( f' < 0 \) on \( (-2, 1) \), \( f' > 0 \) on \( (1, \infty) \) with a turning point (local max) at \( x = 4 \).

Question 7

Example
Using the standard series in the List of Formulae and Results, expand \( \dfrac{1}{(1 - 2x)^{3}} \) up to and including the term in \( x^{3} \). State the range of values of \( x \) for which the expansion is valid. [4]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Rewrite. M1
\( \dfrac{1}{(1 - 2x)^{3}} = (1 + (-2x))^{-3} \). So \( n = -3 \), replace \( x \) by \( -2x \).
Step 2
Substitute into standard MF27 series.
\( (1 + u)^{-3} = 1 + (-3)u + \dfrac{(-3)(-4)}{2}u^{2} + \dfrac{(-3)(-4)(-5)}{6}u^{3} + \cdots = 1 - 3u + 6u^{2} - 10u^{3} + \cdots \)
Step 3
Set \( u = -2x \) and simplify. M1 A1
\[ = 1 + 6x + 24x^{2} + 80x^{3} + \cdots \]
Step 4
Validity. B1
\( |-2x| < 1 \Rightarrow |x| < \tfrac{1}{2} \).

Question 8

Example
It is given that \( f(x) = \sec 2x \).
(i) Find \( f(0),\; f'(0),\; f''(0) \). [4]
(ii) Hence find the Maclaurin series for \( f(x) \) up to and including the term in \( x^{2} \). [2]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i)
\( f(0) \). B1
\( f(0) = \sec 0 = 1 \).
Step 2
\( f'(x) \). M1 A1
\( f'(x) = 2\sec 2x \tan 2x \). \( f'(0) = 2(1)(0) = 0 \).
Step 3
\( f''(x) \) using product rule. A1
\( f''(x) = 4\sec 2x \tan^{2} 2x + 4\sec^{3} 2x \). \( f''(0) = 0 + 4 = 4 \).
Step 4 — (ii)
Assemble. M1 A1
\( f(x) \approx 1 + 0\cdot x + \dfrac{4}{2!}x^{2} = 1 + 2x^{2} \).

Question 9

Example
Find the first three non-zero terms in the Maclaurin series of \( \ln(\cos 2x) \). [5]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Expand \( \cos 2x \) using the standard MF27 series. M1
\( \cos 2x = 1 - 2x^{2} + \tfrac{2}{3}x^{4} - \cdots \).
Step 2
Let \( u = -2x^{2} + \tfrac{2}{3}x^{4} - \cdots \), so \( \cos 2x = 1 + u \). Use \( \ln(1+u) = u - \tfrac{u^{2}}{2} + \tfrac{u^{3}}{3} - \cdots \). M1
Step 3
Compute \( u^{2},\; u^{3} \) up to \( x^{6} \).
\( u^{2} = 4x^{4} - \tfrac{8}{3}x^{6} + \cdots \); \( u^{3} = -8x^{6} + \cdots \).
Step 4
Assemble. M1
\( \ln(\cos 2x) = (-2x^{2} + \tfrac{2}{3}x^{4}) - \tfrac{1}{2}(4x^{4} - \tfrac{8}{3}x^{6}) + \tfrac{1}{3}(-8x^{6}) + \cdots \).
Step 5
Collect like terms. A1 A1
\[ = -2x^{2} - \tfrac{4}{3}x^{4} - \tfrac{4}{3}x^{6} + \cdots \]

Question 10

Example
It is given that \( y \) satisfies \( y^{3} + y^{2} + y = x^{2} - 2x \) and \( y = 0 \) when \( x = 0 \).
(i) Show that \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} = -2 \) when \( x = 0 \). [2]
(ii) Find \( \dfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} \) when \( x = 0 \). [3]
(iii) Hence find the Maclaurin series for \( y \) up to \( x^{2} \). [2]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i)
Differentiate implicitly. M1
\( 3y^{2}\dfrac{dy}{dx} + 2y\dfrac{dy}{dx} + \dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2x - 2 \).
Step 2
Sub \( x = 0, y = 0 \). A1 (AG)
\( 0 + 0 + \dfrac{dy}{dx} = -2 \Rightarrow \dfrac{dy}{dx} = -2 \).
Step 3 — (ii)
Differentiate the differentiated equation again. M1
