Question 1

Example
Find the particular solution of \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{2x}{y^{2} + 1} \) for which \( y = 0 \) when \( x = 1 \). [6]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Separate. M1
\( (y^{2}+1)\,dy = 2x\,dx \).
Step 2
Integrate. M1 A1
\( \tfrac{y^{3}}{3} + y = x^{2} + C \).
Step 3
Apply IC. M1
At \( x = 1, y = 0 \): \( 0 = 1 + C \Rightarrow C = -1 \).
Step 4
State solution. Since \( y \) can't be isolated cleanly, leave in implicit form. A1 A1
\[ \tfrac{y^{3}}{3} + y = x^{2} - 1 \quad\text{or}\quad y^{3} + 3y = 3x^{2} - 3 \]

Question 2

Example
By using the substitution \( u = x^{2} \), find the general solution of \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2x\,y \). [5]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Differentiate substitution. B1
\( u = x^{2} \Rightarrow \dfrac{du}{dx} = 2x \Rightarrow du = 2x\,dx \).
Step 2
Rewrite DE. RHS \( 2xy\,dx = y\,du \). M1
\( dy = y\,du \Rightarrow \dfrac{dy}{du} = y \).
Step 3
Separable in \( u \). Solve. M1
\( \ln|y| = u + C \).
Step 4
Back-substitute. M1
\( \ln|y| = x^{2} + C \).
Step 5
Exponentiate. A1
\[ y = A\,e^{x^{2}} \]

Practice
By using the substitution \( u = \dfrac{y}{x} \), find the general solution of \( x\dfrac{dy}{dx} = y + 2x^{2}\cos x \). [6]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
\( y = ux \Rightarrow \dfrac{dy}{dx} = u + x\dfrac{du}{dx} \). M1
Step 2
Substitute. M1
\( x(u + x\dfrac{du}{dx}) = ux + 2x^{2}\cos x \).
Step 3
Simplify — \( ux \) terms cancel. M1
\( x^{2}\dfrac{du}{dx} = 2x^{2}\cos x \Rightarrow \dfrac{du}{dx} = 2\cos x \).
Step 4
Integrate. M1
\( u = 2\sin x + C \).
Step 5
Back-substitute. A1 A1
\[ y = 2x\sin x + Cx \]

Question 3

Example
A radioactive substance decays at a rate proportional to the mass remaining. Initially the mass is 200 grams. After 5 years, the mass is 150 grams.
(i) Show that the mass \( M \) at time \( t \) (in years) satisfies \( \dfrac{dM}{dt} = -kM \) for some positive constant \( k \), and state the initial condition. [2]
(ii) Solve the DE and find the value of \( k \), correct to 4 d.p. [5]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i)
Rate \( \propto M \), decreasing → negative sign. M1 A1
\( \dfrac{dM}{dt} = -kM,\; k > 0,\; M(0) = 200 \).
Step 2 — (ii)
Separable. M1
\( \ln|M| = -kt + C \Rightarrow M = A\,e^{-kt} \).
Step 3
Apply IC. M1
\( A = 200 \), so \( M = 200\,e^{-kt} \).
Step 4
Use \( M(5) = 150 \) to find \( k \). M1
\( 150 = 200\,e^{-5k} \Rightarrow e^{-5k} = 0.75 \Rightarrow k = -\dfrac{\ln 0.75}{5} \).
Step 5
Compute. A1
\( k = 0.0575 \) (4 d.p.).

Practice
A population of bacteria \( P \) doubles every 3 hours. Initially \( P = 1000 \).
(i) Set up a DE for \( P \) at time \( t \) (in hours), assuming the growth rate is proportional to the current population. [2]
(ii) Solve for \( P(t) \) and find the value of the growth constant. [5]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i)
Rate \( \propto P \), growing → positive. M1 A1
\( \dfrac{dP}{dt} = kP,\; k > 0,\; P(0) = 1000 \).
Step 2 — (ii)
Solve. M1
\( P = A\,e^{kt} \). \( P(0) = 1000 \Rightarrow A = 1000 \). So \( P = 1000\,e^{kt} \). M1
Step 3
Doubles every 3 hours: \( P(3) = 2000 \). M1
\( 2000 = 1000\,e^{3k} \Rightarrow k = \dfrac{\ln 2}{3} \approx 0.231 \) per hour.
Step 4
State. A1
\( P(t) = 1000 \cdot 2^{t/3} \).

Question 4

Example
Coffee at \( 90\,^{\circ}\text{C} \) is left in a room at \( 20\,^{\circ}\text{C} \). The rate of cooling is proportional to the temperature difference. After 10 minutes the coffee is at \( 60\,^{\circ}\text{C} \).
(i) Show that the temperature \( T \) at time \( t \) (in minutes) satisfies \( T = 20 + 70\,e^{-kt} \). [5]
(ii) Find the value of \( k \), correct to 4 d.p. [2]
(iii) Find the temperature after 20 minutes. [2]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i)
Set up DE. B1
\( \dfrac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - 20),\; k > 0,\; T(0) = 90 \).
Step 2
Separate, integrate. M1 M1
\( \ln|T - 20| = -kt + C \).
Step 3
Exponentiate. \( T > 20 \), drop modulus. M1
\( T - 20 = A\,e^{-kt} \).
Step 4
IC \( T(0) = 90 \). A1
\( A = 70 \). So \( T = 20 + 70\,e^{-kt} \). (AG)
Step 5 — (ii)
Use \( T(10) = 60 \). M1 A1
\( 60 = 20 + 70\,e^{-10k} \Rightarrow e^{-10k} = \tfrac{4}{7} \Rightarrow k = 0.0560 \) (4 d.p.).
Step 6 — (iii)
Substitute \( t = 20 \). M1 A1
\( T(20) = 20 + 70\,e^{-1.12} \approx 20 + 22.9 \approx 42.9\,^{\circ}\text{C} \).

Practice
A cold drink at \( 5\,^{\circ}\text{C} \) is placed in a room at \( 25\,^{\circ}\text{C} \). The rate of warming is proportional to the temperature difference. After 6 minutes the drink is at \( 12\,^{\circ}\text{C} \).
(i) Formulate and solve the DE for \( T(t) \). [6]
(ii) Find the time taken for the drink to reach \( 20\,^{\circ}\text{C} \), correct to the nearest minute. [3]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i)
DE. \( T \) rising toward 25, deficit \( (T - 25) < 0 \). Write as chasing 25. B1
\( \dfrac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - 25),\; k > 0,\; T(0) = 5 \).
Step 2
Solve. M1 M1
\( T = 25 + A\,e^{-kt} \).
Step 3
IC. M1
\( T(0) = 5 \Rightarrow A = -20 \). So \( T = 25 - 20\,e^{-kt} \).
Step 4
Use \( T(6) = 12 \). M1
\( 12 = 25 - 20\,e^{-6k} \Rightarrow e^{-6k} = 0.65 \Rightarrow k \approx 0.0718 \).
Step 5
State. A1
\( T(t) = 25 - 20\,e^{-0.0718 t} \).
Step 6 — (ii)
Solve \( T = 20 \). M1 M1
\( 20 = 25 - 20\,e^{-0.0718 t} \Rightarrow e^{-0.0718 t} = 0.25 \Rightarrow t = -\dfrac{\ln 0.25}{0.0718} \approx 19.3 \).
Step 7
Round. A1
\( t \approx 19 \) min.

Archetype revealed
Newton's Cooling / Warming (chasing K).

Rate \( \propto (T - T_{R}) \). Solution \( T = T_{R} + (T_{0} - T_{R})\,e^{-kt} \). \( T \to T_{R} \) as \( t \to \infty \).

Question 5

Example
A virus spreads through a town of 10,000 people according to \( \dfrac{dI}{dt} = 0.0002\,I(10000 - I) \) where \( I \) is the number infected at time \( t \) (in days). Initially \( I(0) = 100 \).
(i) Show that \( I = \dfrac{10000}{1 + 99\,e^{-2t}} \). [6]
(ii) Find the number infected after 3 days. [2]
(iii) State the long-term behaviour of \( I \). [1]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i)
Separate. M1
\( \dfrac{1}{I(10000 - I)}\,dI = 0.0002\,dt \).
Step 2
Partial fractions. M1
\( \dfrac{1}{I(10000 - I)} = \tfrac{1}{10000}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{I} + \tfrac{1}{10000 - I}\right) \).
Step 3
Integrate. Multiply by 10000. M1
\( \ln\!\left|\dfrac{I}{10000 - I}\right| = 2t + C \).
Step 4
Exponentiate and IC \( I(0) = 100 \). M1 M1
\( \dfrac{I}{10000 - I} = D\,e^{2t} \). At \( t = 0 \): \( D = \tfrac{100}{9900} = \tfrac{1}{99} \).
Step 5
Solve for \( I \). A1
\[ I = \dfrac{10000}{1 + 99\,e^{-2t}} \quad\text{(AG)} \]
Step 6 — (ii)
At \( t = 3 \). M1 A1
\( I(3) = \dfrac{10000}{1 + 99\,e^{-6}} \approx 8030 \) people.
Step 7 — (iii)
Long-term. B1
\( I \to 10000 \) as \( t \to \infty \). Whole population infected.

(i) Solve for \( R(t) \). [6]
(ii) Find \( R \) when \( t = 2 \). [2]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
Separate. M1
\( \dfrac{1}{R(5000 - R)}\,dR = 0.0004\,dt \).
Step 2
Partial fractions and integrate. M1 M1
\( \tfrac{1}{5000}\ln\!\left|\dfrac{R}{5000 - R}\right| = 0.0004t + C \Rightarrow \ln\!\left|\dfrac{R}{5000-R}\right| = 2t + C' \).
Step 3
Exponentiate and IC \( R(0) = 50 \). M1
\( D = \tfrac{50}{4950} = \tfrac{1}{99} \).
Step 4
Solve for \( R \). M1 A1
\[ R = \dfrac{5000}{1 + 99\,e^{-2t}} \]
Step 5 — (ii)
\( t = 2 \). M1 A1
\( R(2) = \dfrac{5000}{1 + 99\,e^{-4}} \approx 1777 \) people.

Logistic Growth.

Population \( \propto \) product of current and remaining capacity. Solve via partial fractions. Answer takes form \( \dfrac{K}{1 + A\,e^{-kt}} \). \( P \to K \) as \( t \to \infty \).

Question 6

Example
A radioactive isotope decays according to \( \dfrac{dN}{dt} = -0.0231\,N \) where \( N \) is in grams and \( t \) is in years, with \( N(0) = 500 \).
(i) Solve for \( N(t) \). [3]
(ii) Find the half-life of the isotope. [2]
(iii) Describe the long-term behaviour of \( N \). [1]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i)
Separable. M1 A1
\( N(t) = 500\,e^{-0.0231 t} \).
Step 2 — (ii)
Half-life: solve \( N = 250 \). M1
\( e^{-0.0231 t_{1/2}} = \tfrac{1}{2} \Rightarrow t_{1/2} = \dfrac{\ln 2}{0.0231} \approx 30.0 \) years. A1
Step 3 — (iii)
Long-term. B1
\( N \to 0 \) as \( t \to \infty \). The isotope decays away completely.

Question 7

Example
A drug \( D \) (in mg) is injected into a patient at a constant rate of 4 mg per hour. The body simultaneously removes the drug at a rate proportional to the amount present, with constant of proportionality 0.5 per hour. Initially there is no drug in the body.
(i) Show that \( \dfrac{dD}{dt} = 4 - 0.5D \). [2]
(ii) Solve to find \( D(t) \). [5]
(iii) Find the long-term amount of drug in the body. [2]
(iv) Find how long it takes for the drug level to reach 6 mg, correct to the nearest minute. [3]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i)
Net rate = input − output. Input: \( +4 \). Output: \( 0.5D \). M1 A1 (AG)
\( \dfrac{dD}{dt} = 4 - 0.5D \).
Step 2 — (ii)
Separate. M1
\( \dfrac{dD}{4 - 0.5D} = dt \).
Step 3
Integrate. M1
\( -2\ln|4 - 0.5D| = t + C \Rightarrow \ln|4 - 0.5D| = -\tfrac{t}{2} + C' \).
Step 4
Exponentiate. M1
\( 4 - 0.5D = A\,e^{-t/2} \).
Step 5
IC \( D(0) = 0 \). M1 A1
\( A = 4 \). So \( D = 8(1 - e^{-t/2}) \).
Step 6 — (iii)
Long-term. M1 A1
\( D \to 8 \) mg as \( t \to \infty \).
Step 7 — (iv)
Solve \( D = 6 \). M1
\( 6 = 8(1 - e^{-t/2}) \Rightarrow e^{-t/2} = \tfrac{1}{4} \Rightarrow t = 4\ln 2 \approx 2.77 \) hours.
Step 8
Convert. M1 A1
\( 2.77 \times 60 \approx 166 \) minutes.

Rewrite \( \tfrac{50 - S}{100} \) and separate. M1
\( \dfrac{dS}{50 - S} = \dfrac{dt}{100} \).

Integrate. M1

Step 4
Exponentiate. M1
\( 50 - S = A\,e^{-t/100} \).
Step 5
IC \( S(0) = 0 \). M1 A1
\( A = 50 \). So \( S = 50(1 - e^{-t/100}) \).
Step 6 — (iii)
Long-term. M1 A1
\( S \to 50 \) kg.

\( S = 25 \). M1 M1 A1
\( 25 = 50(1 - e^{-t/100}) \Rightarrow e^{-t/100} = \tfrac{1}{2} \Rightarrow t = 100\ln 2 \approx 69.3 \) min.
