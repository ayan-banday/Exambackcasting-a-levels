Card 1 — Separable DE + Particular Solution

How the exam asks it
"Find the particular solution of the DE \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} = f(x)g(y) \) for which \( y = y_{0} \) when \( x = x_{0} \). Give your answer in the form \( y = … \)." Typical 5–7m.
Example
Find the particular solution of \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{2x}{y^{2} + 1} \) for which \( y = 0 \) when \( x = 1 \). [6]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Separate variables. M1
\( (y^{2}+1)\,dy = 2x\,dx \).
Step 2
Integrate both sides. M1 A1
\( \dfrac{y^{3}}{3} + y = x^{2} + C \).
Step 3
Substitute initial condition. M1
At \( x=1, y=0 \): \( 0 + 0 = 1 + C \Rightarrow C = -1 \).
Step 4
Write particular solution. The answer is implicit in \( y \) — that's acceptable when \( y \) can't be isolated. A1 A1
\[ \dfrac{y^{3}}{3} + y = x^{2} - 1 \quad\text{or equivalently}\quad y^{3} + 3y = 3x^{2} - 3 \]

Practice
Find the particular solution of \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} = y\cos x \) for which \( y = 3 \) when \( x = 0 \). [5]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
Separate. M1
\( \dfrac{1}{y}\,dy = \cos x\,dx \).
Step 2
Integrate. M1 A1
\( \ln|y| = \sin x + C \).
Step 3
Substitute. M1
At \( x = 0, y = 3 \): \( \ln 3 = 0 + C \Rightarrow C = \ln 3 \).
Step 4
Exponentiate. \( y = 3 > 0 \), drop modulus. A1
\( \ln|y| = \sin x + \ln 3 \Rightarrow y = e^{\sin x + \ln 3} = 3\,e^{\sin x} \). \[ y = 3\,e^{\sin x} \]

Card 2 — DE by Given Substitution

How the exam asks it
"Show, by using the substitution \( u = \ldots \) (or \( v = \ldots \)), that the DE can be written as \( \ldots \). Hence find the general solution." The substitution reduces a non-separable DE to a separable one. (Full mechanics drilled in Cluster 4 Card 9.)
Example
By using the substitution \( u = x^{2} \), find the general solution of \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2x\,y \). [5]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1
Differentiate substitution. B1
\( u = x^{2} \Rightarrow \dfrac{du}{dx} = 2x \).
Step 2
Rewrite the DE using \( 2x\,dx = du \). The RHS \( 2xy\,dx = y\,du \). So \( dy = y\,du \). M1
\( \dfrac{dy}{du} = y \).
Step 3
Separable. Solve. M1
\( \dfrac{1}{y}\,dy = du \Rightarrow \ln|y| = u + C \).
Step 4
Back-substitute \( u = x^{2} \). M1
\( \ln|y| = x^{2} + C \).
Step 5
Exponentiate. A1
\[ y = A\,e^{x^{2}} \quad (\text{where } A = \pm e^{C}) \]
Solution hidden — click Reveal
Practice
By using the substitution \( u = \dfrac{y}{x} \), find the general solution of \( x\dfrac{dy}{dx} = y + 2x^{2}\cos x \). [6]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
\( y = ux \Rightarrow \dfrac{dy}{dx} = u + x\dfrac{du}{dx} \). M1
Step 2
Substitute into DE. M1
\( x\!\left(u + x\dfrac{du}{dx}\right) = ux + 2x^{2}\cos x \).
Step 3
Simplify — \( ux \) terms cancel. M1
\( x^{2}\dfrac{du}{dx} = 2x^{2}\cos x \Rightarrow \dfrac{du}{dx} = 2\cos x \).
Step 4
Integrate. M1
\( u = 2\sin x + C \).
Step 5
Back-substitute. A1 A1
\( \dfrac{y}{x} = 2\sin x + C \Rightarrow y = 2x\sin x + Cx \). \[ y = 2x\sin x + Cx \]

Card 3 — Formulating a DE from Context

How the exam asks it
Words to DE. The exam expects: (1) right pattern from the verbal trigger, (2) right sign from the direction of change, (3) initial condition stated. (Pattern recognition drilled in Cluster 4 Card 8.)
Example
A radioactive substance decays at a rate proportional to the mass remaining. Initially the mass is 200 grams. After 5 years, the mass is 150 grams.
(i) Show that the mass \( M \) at time \( t \) (in years) satisfies \( \dfrac{dM}{dt} = -kM \) for some positive constant \( k \), and state the initial condition. [2]
(ii) Solve the DE and find the value of \( k \), correct to 4 d.p. [5]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i) Pattern
"Rate \( \propto M \)" → pattern 1 (\( dN/dt = \pm kN \)). Decay → negative. M1 A1
\( \dfrac{dM}{dt} = -kM,\; k > 0,\; M(0) = 200 \).
Step 2 — (ii) Solve
Separable. M1
\( \dfrac{1}{M}\,dM = -k\,dt \Rightarrow \ln|M| = -kt + C \Rightarrow M = A\,e^{-kt} \).
Step 3
Use \( M(0) = 200 \). M1
\( A = 200 \), so \( M = 200\,e^{-kt} \).
Step 4
Use \( M(5) = 150 \) to find \( k \). M1
\( 150 = 200\,e^{-5k} \Rightarrow e^{-5k} = 0.75 \Rightarrow -5k = \ln 0.75 \Rightarrow k = -\dfrac{\ln 0.75}{5} \).
Step 5
Compute. A1
\( k = 0.0575 \) (4 d.p.).

Practice
A population of bacteria \( P \) doubles every 3 hours. Initially \( P = 1000 \).
(i) Set up a DE for \( P \) at time \( t \) (in hours), assuming the growth rate is proportional to the current population. [2]
(ii) Solve for \( P(t) \) and find the value of the growth constant. [5]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i)
Pattern 1, growing → positive. M1 A1
\( \dfrac{dP}{dt} = kP,\; k > 0,\; P(0) = 1000 \).
Step 2 — (ii) Solve
Separable. M1
\( \ln|P| = kt + C \Rightarrow P = A\,e^{kt} \).
Step 3
\( P(0) = 1000 \Rightarrow A = 1000 \). M1
\( P = 1000\,e^{kt} \).
Step 4
"Doubles every 3 hours": \( P(3) = 2000 \). M1
\( 2000 = 1000\,e^{3k} \Rightarrow e^{3k} = 2 \Rightarrow k = \dfrac{\ln 2}{3} \).
Step 5
Compute. A1
\( k \approx 0.231 \) per hour. So \( P(t) = 1000\,e^{(\ln 2)t/3} = 1000 \cdot 2^{t/3} \).

Card 4 — Newton's Cooling (chasing K)

How the exam asks it
"Coffee at \( T_{0} \) is left in a room of temperature \( T_{R} \). The cooling rate is proportional to the temperature difference …" Pattern 2: \( \dfrac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - T_{R}) \). Solution: \( T = T_{R} + (T_{0} - T_{R})e^{-kt} \).
Concept
Recognise the limiting value \( T_{R} \) — that's the room temp (equilibrium). The \( (T_{0} - T_{R}) \) term is the initial deficit. The temperature approaches \( T_{R} \) exponentially as \( t \to \infty \).
Example
Coffee at \( 90\,^{\circ}\text{C} \) is left in a room at \( 20\,^{\circ}\text{C} \). The rate of cooling is proportional to the temperature difference. After 10 minutes the coffee is at \( 60\,^{\circ}\text{C} \).
(i) Show that the temperature \( T \) at time \( t \) (in minutes) satisfies \( T = 20 + 70\,e^{-kt} \). [5]
(ii) Find the value of \( k \), correct to 4 d.p. [2]
(iii) Find the temperature after 20 minutes. [2]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i) Set up DE
Cooling, decreasing T → negative coefficient. B1
\( \dfrac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - 20),\; k > 0,\; T(0) = 90 \).
Step 2
Separate and integrate. M1 M1
\( \dfrac{1}{T - 20}\,dT = -k\,dt \Rightarrow \ln|T - 20| = -kt + C \).
Step 3
Exponentiate. \( T - 20 > 0 \) (always above room temp), so drop modulus. M1
\( T - 20 = A\,e^{-kt} \).
Step 4
Apply \( T(0) = 90 \). A1
\( 90 - 20 = A \Rightarrow A = 70 \). So \( T = 20 + 70\,e^{-kt} \). (AG)
Step 5 — (ii) Find k
Use \( T(10) = 60 \). M1
\( 60 = 20 + 70\,e^{-10k} \Rightarrow e^{-10k} = \tfrac{40}{70} = \tfrac{4}{7} \Rightarrow k = -\dfrac{\ln(4/7)}{10} \).
Step 6
Compute. A1
\( k = 0.0560 \) (4 d.p.).
Step 7 — (iii) Temp at t = 20
Substitute. M1 A1
\( T(20) = 20 + 70\,e^{-0.0560 \times 20} = 20 + 70\,e^{-1.12} \approx 20 + 70(0.3265) \approx 42.9\,^{\circ}\text{C} \).

Practice
A cold drink at \( 5\,^{\circ}\text{C} \) is placed in a room at \( 25\,^{\circ}\text{C} \). The rate of warming is proportional to the temperature difference. After 6 minutes the drink is at \( 12\,^{\circ}\text{C} \).
(i) Formulate and solve the DE for \( T(t) \). [6]
(ii) Find the time taken for the drink to reach \( 20\,^{\circ}\text{C} \), correct to the nearest minute. [3]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i) DE
Warming, T increasing toward 25 → positive coefficient, but the deficit \( (T - 25) \) is negative, so we use \( -k(T - 25) \) to get positive rate. B1
\( \dfrac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - 25),\; k > 0,\; T(0) = 5 \).
Step 2
Solve. M1 M1
\( \ln|T - 25| = -kt + C \). Since \( T < 25 \), \( T - 25 < 0 \), so \( |T - 25| = 25 - T \). Or: write \( T = 25 + A\,e^{-kt} \) with \( A < 0 \).
Step 3
Apply initial condition. M1
\( T(0) = 5 \Rightarrow 5 = 25 + A \Rightarrow A = -20 \). So \( T = 25 - 20\,e^{-kt} \).
Step 4
Use \( T(6) = 12 \) to find \( k \). M1
\( 12 = 25 - 20\,e^{-6k} \Rightarrow 20\,e^{-6k} = 13 \Rightarrow e^{-6k} = 0.65 \Rightarrow k = -\dfrac{\ln 0.65}{6} \approx 0.0718 \).
Step 5
State the solution. A1
\( T(t) = 25 - 20\,e^{-0.0718t} \).
Step 6 — (ii) Time to reach 20
Solve \( 20 = 25 - 20\,e^{-0.0718t} \). M1
\( 20\,e^{-0.0718t} = 5 \Rightarrow e^{-0.0718t} = 0.25 \Rightarrow t = -\dfrac{\ln 0.25}{0.0718} \approx 19.3 \) min.
Step 7
Round to nearest minute. M1 A1
\( t \approx 19 \) minutes.

Card 5 — Logistic Growth

How the exam asks it
"Population \( P \) grows according to \( \dfrac{dP}{dt} = kP\!\left(1 - \dfrac{P}{K}\right) \) … " The S-curve. Solve using partial fractions (often a "show that" form like \( P = K/(1 + Ae^{-kt}) \)).
Concept
Logistic DE is separable, but solving it requires partial fractions because \( \dfrac{1}{P(1 - P/K)} = \dfrac{1}{P} + \dfrac{1/K}{1 - P/K} \) (after manipulation). The solution form \( P = \dfrac{K}{1 + A\,e^{-kt}} \) is standard. \( P \to K \) as \( t \to \infty \).
Example
A virus spreads through a town of 10,000 people according to \( \dfrac{dI}{dt} = 0.0002\,I(10000 - I) \) where \( I \) is the number infected at time \( t \) (in days). Initially \( I(0) = 100 \).
(i) Show that \( I = \dfrac{10000}{1 + 99\,e^{-2t}} \). [6]
(ii) Find the number infected after 3 days. [2]
(iii) State the long-term behaviour of \( I \). [1]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i) Separate
Separate and set up partial fractions. M1
\( \dfrac{1}{I(10000 - I)}\,dI = 0.0002\,dt \).
Step 2
Partial fractions: \( \dfrac{1}{I(10000 - I)} = \dfrac{A}{I} + \dfrac{B}{10000 - I} \). Solve: \( 1 = A(10000 - I) + BI \). At \( I = 0 \): \( A = \tfrac{1}{10000} \). At \( I = 10000 \): \( B = \tfrac{1}{10000} \). M1
So \( \dfrac{1}{I(10000 - I)} = \dfrac{1}{10000}\!\left(\dfrac{1}{I} + \dfrac{1}{10000 - I}\right) \).
Step 3
Integrate. M1
\( \tfrac{1}{10000}(\ln|I| - \ln|10000 - I|) = 0.0002t + C \).
Multiply by 10000: \( \ln\!\left|\dfrac{I}{10000 - I}\right| = 2t + C' \).
Step 4
Exponentiate. M1
\( \dfrac{I}{10000 - I} = D\,e^{2t} \) (where \( D = \pm e^{C'} \)).
Step 5
Apply \( I(0) = 100 \). M1
\( \dfrac{100}{9900} = D \Rightarrow D = \tfrac{1}{99} \).
So \( \dfrac{I}{10000 - I} = \dfrac{e^{2t}}{99} \).
Step 6
Solve for \( I \). A1
\( 99 I = (10000 - I)\,e^{2t} \Rightarrow 99 I + I\,e^{2t} = 10000\,e^{2t} \Rightarrow I(99 + e^{2t}) = 10000\,e^{2t} \).
Divide top and bottom by \( e^{2t} \): \[ I = \dfrac{10000}{1 + 99\,e^{-2t}} \quad\text{(AG)} \]
Step 7 — (ii) After 3 days
Substitute. M1 A1
\( I(3) = \dfrac{10000}{1 + 99\,e^{-6}} = \dfrac{10000}{1 + 99(0.00248)} \approx \dfrac{10000}{1.245} \approx 8030 \) people.
Step 8 — (iii) Long-term
As \( t \to \infty,\; e^{-2t} \to 0 \). B1
\( I \to 10000 \). Eventually the whole population is infected (carrying capacity).

Practice
A rumour spreads through a community of 5000 people, modelled by \( \dfrac{dR}{dt} = 0.0004\,R(5000 - R) \) with \( R(0) = 50 \).
(i) Solve for \( R(t) \). [6]
(ii) Find \( R \) when \( t = 2 \). [2]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1
Separate. M1
\( \dfrac{1}{R(5000 - R)}\,dR = 0.0004\,dt \).
Step 2
Partial fractions. M1
\( \dfrac{1}{R(5000 - R)} = \dfrac{1}{5000}\!\left(\dfrac{1}{R} + \dfrac{1}{5000 - R}\right) \).
Step 3
Integrate. M1
\( \tfrac{1}{5000}\ln\!\left|\dfrac{R}{5000 - R}\right| = 0.0004t + C \Rightarrow \ln\!\left|\dfrac{R}{5000 - R}\right| = 2t + C' \).
Step 4
Exponentiate and apply IC \( R(0) = 50 \). M1
\( \dfrac{R}{5000 - R} = D\,e^{2t} \). At \( t=0 \): \( \dfrac{50}{4950} = D \Rightarrow D = \tfrac{1}{99} \).
Step 5
Solve for \( R \). M1 A1
\[ R = \dfrac{5000}{1 + 99\,e^{-2t}} \]
Step 6 — (ii)
Substitute \( t = 2 \). M1 A1
\( R(2) = \dfrac{5000}{1 + 99\,e^{-4}} = \dfrac{5000}{1 + 99(0.01832)} \approx \dfrac{5000}{2.814} \approx 1777 \) people.

Card 6 — Interpretation: Equilibrium, Half-life, Long-term

How the exam asks it
Final part of a DE question: "State the long-term value of \( y \) / Find the half-life / Find the equilibrium population / Describe what happens to \( P \) as \( t \) becomes large." 1–3 marks each — vocabulary precision.
Concept
Definitions in one place: long-term = \( y \) as \( t \to \infty \). Equilibrium = \( y \) where \( dy/dt = 0 \). Carrying capacity = max equilibrium in a logistic model. Half-life = time for exponential decay to halve = \( (\ln 2)/k \). (Full glossary in Cluster 4 Card 10.)
Example
A radioactive isotope decays according to \( \dfrac{dN}{dt} = -0.0231\,N \) where \( N \) is in grams and \( t \) is in years, with \( N(0) = 500 \).
(i) Solve for \( N(t) \). [3]
(ii) Find the half-life of the isotope. [2]
(iii) Describe the long-term behaviour of \( N \). [1]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i)
Solve separable. M1 A1
\( N(t) = 500\,e^{-0.0231 t} \).
Step 2 — (ii) Half-life
Solve \( N(t_{1/2}) = 250 \). M1
\( 500\,e^{-0.0231 t_{1/2}} = 250 \Rightarrow e^{-0.0231 t_{1/2}} = \tfrac{1}{2} \Rightarrow t_{1/2} = \dfrac{\ln 2}{0.0231} \approx 30.0 \) years. A1
Step 3 — (iii) Long-term
\( e^{-0.0231 t} \to 0 \). B1
\( N \to 0 \) as \( t \to \infty \). The isotope decays away completely in the long run.

Practice
A fish population \( P \) satisfies \( \dfrac{dP}{dt} = 0.2 P\!\left(1 - \dfrac{P}{5000}\right) \) with \( P(0) = 200 \).
(i) State the carrying capacity. [1]
(ii) Find the equilibrium values. [2]
(iii) State the value of \( P \) at which the population is growing fastest. [1]
(iv) Describe the long-term behaviour of \( P \). [1]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i)
Read \( K \) off the logistic form. B1
\( K = 5000 \).
Step 2 — (ii) Equilibria
Set \( dP/dt = 0 \). M1 A1
\( 0.2P(1 - P/5000) = 0 \Rightarrow P = 0 \) or \( P = 5000 \).
Step 3 — (iii)
Maximum at \( K/2 \). B1
\( P = 2500 \).
Step 4 — (iv)
Starting from \( P_{0} = 200 \), below \( K \), population grows toward carrying capacity. B1
\( P \to 5000 \) as \( t \to \infty \).

Card 7 — Applied DE Closer (P1 12-14m style)

How the exam asks it
The classic P1 closer. Real-world context (drug clearance, draining tank, water in a reservoir, terminal velocity, drone descent, virus spread, loan repayment). Multi-part: (a) formulate the DE from words, (b) solve, (c) find a specific value, (d) interpret long-term. Typical 10–14m, AO2-heavy.
Example
A drug \( D \) (in mg) is injected into a patient at a constant rate of 4 mg per hour. The body simultaneously removes the drug at a rate proportional to the amount present, with constant of proportionality 0.5 per hour. Initially there is no drug in the body.
(i) Show that \( \dfrac{dD}{dt} = 4 - 0.5D \). [2]
(ii) Solve to find \( D(t) \). [5]
(iii) Find the long-term amount of drug in the body. [2]
(iv) Find how long it takes for the drug level to reach 6 mg, correct to the nearest minute. [3]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i) Formulate
Net rate = input rate − output rate. M1
Input: \( +4 \) mg/hr. Output: \( 0.5D \) (proportional to amount). So \( \dfrac{dD}{dt} = 4 - 0.5D \). A1 (AG)
Step 2 — (ii) Solve
Separate. M1
\( \dfrac{dD}{4 - 0.5D} = dt \).
Step 3
Integrate. M1
\( -\dfrac{1}{0.5}\ln|4 - 0.5D| = t + C \Rightarrow -2\ln|4 - 0.5D| = t + C \Rightarrow \ln|4 - 0.5D| = -\dfrac{t}{2} + C' \).
Step 4
Exponentiate. M1
\( 4 - 0.5D = A\,e^{-t/2} \).
Step 5
Apply \( D(0) = 0 \). M1 A1
\( 4 - 0 = A \Rightarrow A = 4 \). So \( 4 - 0.5D = 4\,e^{-t/2} \Rightarrow D = 8(1 - e^{-t/2}) \).
Step 6 — (iii) Long-term
As \( t \to \infty,\; e^{-t/2} \to 0 \). M1 A1
\( D \to 8 \) mg. (Equivalently: equilibrium where \( dD/dt = 0 \Rightarrow 4 = 0.5D \Rightarrow D = 8 \).)
Step 7 — (iv) Time to reach 6 mg
Solve \( 6 = 8(1 - e^{-t/2}) \). M1
\( 1 - e^{-t/2} = \tfrac{3}{4} \Rightarrow e^{-t/2} = \tfrac{1}{4} \Rightarrow t = -2\ln\tfrac{1}{4} = 2\ln 4 = 4\ln 2 \approx 2.77 \) hours.
Step 8
Convert to minutes. M1 A1
\( 2.77 \times 60 \approx 166 \) minutes.

Practice
A tank initially holds 500 litres of pure water. Salt water containing 0.1 kg of salt per litre flows in at 5 litres per minute, and the well-mixed solution drains out at the same rate. Let \( S(t) \) be the amount of salt (in kg) in the tank at time \( t \) (in minutes).
(i) Show that \( \dfrac{dS}{dt} = 0.5 - \dfrac{S}{100} \). [3]
(ii) Solve for \( S(t) \). [5]
(iii) Find the long-term amount of salt in the tank. [2]
(iv) Find the time taken for the salt content to reach 25 kg, correct to 1 d.p. [3]
Answer with mark scheme
Step 1 — (i)
Rate in = \( 5 \cdot 0.1 = 0.5 \) kg/min. Rate out = (concentration) × (flow rate) = \( \dfrac{S}{500} \cdot 5 = \dfrac{S}{100} \). M1 M1
\( \dfrac{dS}{dt} = 0.5 - \dfrac{S}{100} \). A1 (AG)
Step 2 — (ii) Solve
Rewrite \( 0.5 - \dfrac{S}{100} = \dfrac{50 - S}{100} \). Separable. M1
\( \dfrac{dS}{50 - S} = \dfrac{dt}{100} \).
Step 3
Integrate. M1
\( -\ln|50 - S| = \dfrac{t}{100} + C \Rightarrow \ln|50 - S| = -\dfrac{t}{100} + C' \).
Step 4
Exponentiate. M1
\( 50 - S = A\,e^{-t/100} \).
Step 5
IC \( S(0) = 0 \). M1 A1
\( 50 = A \Rightarrow S = 50(1 - e^{-t/100}) \).
Step 6 — (iii) Long-term
As \( t \to \infty,\; S \to 50 \) kg. M1 A1
Step 7 — (iv) Reach 25
\( 25 = 50(1 - e^{-t/100}) \Rightarrow e^{-t/100} = \tfrac{1}{2} \Rightarrow t = 100\ln 2 \approx 69.3 \) min. M1 M1 A1
