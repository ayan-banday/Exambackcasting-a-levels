Question 1

Example
Find \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} \) in terms of \( x \) and \( y \) for the curve \( x^{3} + 2xy - y^{2} = 5 \). [4]
Solution with mark scheme
Concept — chain rule on every y-term
When you differentiate a relation implicitly, every \( y \)-term carries an extra factor of \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} \) because \( y \) is implicitly a function of \( x \). So:
  • \( \dfrac{d}{dx}(y) = \dfrac{dy}{dx} \)
  • \( \dfrac{d}{dx}(y^{2}) = 2y\cdot\dfrac{dy}{dx} \) — NOT just \( 2y \)
  • \( \dfrac{d}{dx}(y^{n}) = n\,y^{n-1}\cdot\dfrac{dy}{dx} \)
  • \( \dfrac{d}{dx}(xy) = y + x\dfrac{dy}{dx} \) (product rule; \( y \) is a function of \( x \))
Step 1
Differentiate every term w.r.t. \( x \). Apply chain rule on all \( y \)-terms. Product rule on \( 2xy \). M1
\( 3x^{2} + \left(2y + 2x\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right) - 2y\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 0 \).
Step 2
Group \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} \) terms. M1
\( 2x\dfrac{dy}{dx} - 2y\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -3x^{2} - 2y \).
Step 3
Factor. M1
\( (2x - 2y)\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -(3x^{2} + 2y) \).
Step 4
Solve. A1
\[ \dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{-(3x^{2} + 2y)}{2(x - y)} = \dfrac{3x^{2} + 2y}{2(y - x)} \]

Question 3

Example
Given \( y \) satisfies \( y^{2} + xy = 4 \) with \( y = 2 \) when \( x = 0 \), find \( \dfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} \) when \( x = 0 \). [5]
Solution with mark scheme
Concept — sign hygiene when substituting negative values
When intermediate values are negative and get raised to a power, the sign flips per the exponent rules:
  • \( (-a)^{2} = +a^{2} \) — squaring a negative gives POSITIVE
  • \( (-a)^{3} = -a^{3} \) — cubing preserves the sign
  • \( -a^{2} \ne (-a)^{2} \) — the placement of the minus sign matters
Habit: circle every intermediate value with its sign before substituting. If you compute \( y' = -3 \) and later need \( (y')^{2} \), that's \( (-3)^{2} = 9 \) (positive), not \( -9 \).
Step 1
Differentiate the original implicit equation to find \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} \). M1
\( 2y\dfrac{dy}{dx} + y + x\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 0 \).
Step 2
At \( x = 0, y = 2 \). M1
\( 4\dfrac{dy}{dx} + 2 + 0 = 0 \Rightarrow \dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{1}{2} \). Circle: \( y' = -1/2 \) (negative).
Step 3
Differentiate the PRE-GROUPED equation again (not the isolated form). Product rule on every product. M1
\( \dfrac{d}{dx}\!\left[2y\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right] + \dfrac{d}{dx}(y) + \dfrac{d}{dx}\!\left[x\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right] = 0 \).
\( 2\!\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^{2} + 2y\dfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} + \dfrac{dy}{dx} + \dfrac{dy}{dx} + x\dfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} = 0 \).
Step 4
Substitute \( x = 0, y = 2, y' = -\tfrac{1}{2} \). M1
\( 2\!\cdot\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2} + 4\dfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} + \left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\right) + \left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\right) + 0 = 0 \).
Note \( (-1/2)^{2} = +1/4 \) (positive): \( 2\cdot\tfrac{1}{4} + 4y'' - 1 = 0 \Rightarrow \tfrac{1}{2} + 4y'' - 1 = 0 \).
Step 5
Solve. A1
\( 4y'' = \tfrac{1}{2} \Rightarrow \dfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} = \tfrac{1}{8} \).

Question 4

Example
A function \( f \) satisfies \( f'(x) = (x + 2)(x - 1)^{2}(x - 4) \). Sketch \( y = f'(x) \) indicating all x-axis crossings and behaviour. State the values of \( x \) where \( f \) has local max / min / stationary inflexion. [5]
Solution with mark scheme
Concept — sign diagram method
The sign diagram method for sketching \( f' \) (or classifying stationary points of \( f \)):
  1. Find zeros of \( f'(x) \). Note multiplicities: SIMPLE root → crosses axis; DOUBLE root → touches axis; TRIPLE → crosses with flat tangent.
  2. Draw a number line with the zeros marked.
  3. For each interval, pick a test point and evaluate the sign of \( f'(x) \).
  4. Sketch \( f' \) as a curve: crossing at simple roots, touching at double roots.
  5. Classify stationary points of \( f \): sign of \( f' \) changes \( -\to + \) is a min; \( +\to - \) is a max; no change is a stationary inflexion.
Step 1
Zeros of \( f' \). B1
\( x = -2 \) (simple), \( x = 1 \) (double), \( x = 4 \) (simple).
Step 2
Sign diagram. Pick test points in each interval.
  • \( x < -2 \), e.g. \( x = -3 \): \( (-1)(16)(-7) = +112 \) → \( f' > 0 \). \( f \) increasing.
  • \( -2 < x < 1 \), e.g. \( x = 0 \): \( (2)(1)(-4) = -8 \) → \( f' < 0 \). \( f \) decreasing.
  • \( 1 < x < 4 \), e.g. \( x = 2 \): \( (4)(1)(-2) = -8 \) → \( f' < 0 \). \( f \) still decreasing.
  • \( x > 4 \), e.g. \( x = 5 \): \( (7)(16)(1) = +112 \) → \( f' > 0 \). \( f \) increasing.
Step 3
Sketch: crosses at \( -2 \) (\( - \to + \)), touches at \( 1 \) (stays negative on both sides... wait, both sides give \( f'<0 \), so it touches from BELOW), crosses at \( 4 \) (\( - \to + \)). B1 B1
Step 4
Classify stationary points of \( f \). M1 A1
  • \( x = -2 \): \( f' \) changes \( + \to - \) → local max.
  • \( x = 1 \): \( f' \) does NOT change sign (\(-\) both sides) → stationary point of inflexion.
  • \( x = 4 \): \( f' \) changes \( - \to + \) → local min.

Question 5

Example
The curve \( C \) has equation \( x^{2} + xy - y^{3} = 3 \). The point \( P \) on \( C \) has coordinates \( (1, -1) \).
(i) Find \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} \) at \( P \). [3]
(ii) Find \( \dfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} \) at \( P \). [4]
Solution with mark scheme
Concept — always differentiate the PRE-GROUPED equation for f''
After the first implicit differentiation you have \( P(x, y, y') = 0 \). To find \( y'' \):
  • WRONG: isolate \( y' = \dfrac{\text{numerator}}{\text{denominator}} \), then quotient-rule. Massively over-complicated.
  • RIGHT: differentiate \( P(x, y, y') = 0 \) directly, treating \( y' \) as a function of \( x \) (so \( \dfrac{d}{dx}(y') = y'' \)). Product rule on any product involving \( y \) or \( y' \).
This was your Cluster 1 Q10 gap. Habit fix: after finding \( y' \), don't touch the isolated form; differentiate the pre-grouped equation instead.
Step 1 — (i)
Differentiate implicitly. M1
\( 2x + y + x\dfrac{dy}{dx} - 3y^{2}\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 0 \).
Step 2
Substitute \( x = 1, y = -1 \). M1
\( 2 - 1 + \dfrac{dy}{dx} - 3\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 0 \Rightarrow 1 - 2\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 0 \Rightarrow \dfrac{dy}{dx} = \tfrac{1}{2} \). A1
Step 3 — (ii)
Differentiate the PRE-GROUPED equation from Step 1 again. Product rule on \( x\dfrac{dy}{dx} \) and \( 3y^{2}\dfrac{dy}{dx} \). M1
\( 2 + \dfrac{dy}{dx} + \dfrac{dy}{dx} + x\dfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} - 6y\!\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^{2} - 3y^{2}\dfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} = 0 \).
Step 4
Substitute \( x = 1, y = -1, y' = \tfrac{1}{2} \). M1
\( 2 + \tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{2} + y'' - 6(-1)\!\left(\tfrac{1}{4}\right) - 3(1)y'' = 0 \).
\( (1/2)^{2} = 1/4 \) (positive), \( 6y \cdot 1/4 = -3/2 \) since \( y = -1 \): \( 3 + y'' + \tfrac{3}{2} - 3y'' = 0 \).
Step 5
Solve. A1
\( \tfrac{9}{2} - 2y'' = 0 \Rightarrow \dfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} = \tfrac{9}{4} \).

Practice

Question 9

Example
Given \( f(x) = e^{x}\sec x \), find \( f(0),\; f'(0),\; f''(0) \), and hence find the Maclaurin series of \( f(x) \) up to and including the term in \( x^{2} \). [6]
Solution with mark scheme
Concept — the general Maclaurin formula anchor
When \( f(x) \) is NOT one of the MF27 standard series, use the general formula: \[ f(x) \approx f(0) + f'(0)x + \dfrac{f''(0)}{2!}x^{2} + \dfrac{f'''(0)}{3!}x^{3} + \cdots \] This is the "Maclaurin expansion" formula in MF27 page 2.
