Archetype 1 — Implicit Differentiation (find \( \tfrac{dy}{dx} \), then a tangent / stationary condition)

How the exam asks it
"A curve has equation [relation in \( x, y \)]. (i) Find \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} \) in terms of \( x \) and \( y \). (ii) Find the coordinates of the points on the curve where the tangent is [parallel to the \( x \)-axis / vertical / equal to a given gradient]." Typical: (i) 3m, (ii) 4m.
Example
A curve has equation \( x^{2} + y^{2} - xy = 7 \).
(i) Find \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} \) in terms of \( x \) and \( y \). [3]
(ii) Find the coordinates of the points on the curve where the tangent is parallel to the \( x \)-axis. [4]
Solution with mark scheme
(i) Differentiate both sides w.r.t. \( x \) (chain rule on \( y^{2} \), product rule on \( xy \)): \[ 2x + 2y\frac{dy}{dx} - \left(y + x\frac{dy}{dx}\right) = 0 \qquad \text{M1} \] Group \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} \) terms: \[ (2y - x)\frac{dy}{dx} = y - 2x \qquad \text{M1} \] \[ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{y - 2x}{2y - x} \qquad \text{A1} \] (ii) Tangent parallel to \( x \)-axis \(\Leftrightarrow \dfrac{dy}{dx} = 0 \Leftrightarrow y = 2x \). M1
Substitute into the curve: \( x^{2} + 4x^{2} - 2x^{2} = 7 \Rightarrow 3x^{2} = 7 \Rightarrow x = \pm\sqrt{7/3} \). M1
Points: \( \left(\sqrt{\tfrac{7}{3}},\; 2\sqrt{\tfrac{7}{3}}\right) \) and \( \left(-\sqrt{\tfrac{7}{3}},\; -2\sqrt{\tfrac{7}{3}}\right) \). A1 A1
Solution hidden — attempt the example first
Practice
A curve has equation \( x^{2}y + y^{3} = 6 \).
(i) Show that \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{2xy}{x^{2} + 3y^{2}} \). [3]
(ii) Find the exact coordinates of the points on the curve where the tangent is parallel to the \( y \)-axis. [3]
Answer with mark scheme
(i) Differentiate (product rule on \( x^{2}y \)): \[ 2xy + x^{2}\frac{dy}{dx} + 3y^{2}\frac{dy}{dx} = 0 \qquad \text{M1} \] \[ (x^{2} + 3y^{2})\frac{dy}{dx} = -2xy \qquad \text{M1} \] \[ \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{2xy}{x^{2} + 3y^{2}} \qquad \text{A1 (AG)} \] (ii) Tangent parallel to \( y \)-axis \(\Leftrightarrow\) denominator \( = 0 \): \( x^{2} + 3y^{2} = 0 \). M1
Since \( x^{2}, y^{2} \ge 0 \), only \( x = y = 0 \), but \( (0,0) \) does not satisfy the curve. M1
Therefore no such points exist. A1

Archetype 2 — Parametric Differentiation (find \( \tfrac{dy}{dx} \), then tangent at \( P \))

How the exam asks it
"A curve has parametric equations \( x = f(t),\; y = g(t) \). (i) Show that \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} = [\text{simplified form}] \). (ii) Find the tangent at the point where \( t = p \). (iii) (often) find a locus or intersection condition."
Example
A curve has parametric equations \( x = t + \dfrac{1}{t},\; y = t - \dfrac{1}{t} \) for \( t \ne 0 \).
(i) Find \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} \) in terms of \( t \). [2]
(ii) Show that the equation of the tangent at the point where \( t = p \) is \( (p^{2} - 1)x - (p^{2} + 1)y = \dfrac{4}{p} \). [4]
Solution with mark scheme
(i) \[ \frac{dx}{dt} = 1 - \frac{1}{t^{2}} = \frac{t^{2} - 1}{t^{2}};\qquad \frac{dy}{dt} = 1 + \frac{1}{t^{2}} = \frac{t^{2} + 1}{t^{2}} \qquad \text{M1} \] \[ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{t^{2} + 1}{t^{2} - 1} \qquad \text{A1} \] (ii) At \( t = p \): point \( \left(p + \tfrac{1}{p},\; p - \tfrac{1}{p}\right) \), gradient \( m = \dfrac{p^{2} + 1}{p^{2} - 1} \). B1
Tangent: \( y - \left(p - \tfrac{1}{p}\right) = \dfrac{p^{2} + 1}{p^{2} - 1}\!\left[x - \left(p + \tfrac{1}{p}\right)\right] \). M1
Multiply both sides by \( p(p^{2} - 1) \), expand and simplify: \[ (p^{2} - 1)x - (p^{2} + 1)y = \frac{4}{p} \qquad \text{M1 A1 (AG)} \]
Solution hidden — attempt the example first
Practice
A curve has parametric equations \( x = 3t^{2},\; y = 2t^{3} \).
(i) Find \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} \) in terms of \( t \). [2]
(ii) Show that the equation of the tangent at the point with parameter \( p \) is \( y = px - p^{3} \). [3]
Answer with mark scheme
(i) \( \dfrac{dx}{dt} = 6t,\; \dfrac{dy}{dt} = 6t^{2} \). M1 \[ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{6t^{2}}{6t} = t \qquad \text{A1} \] (ii) At \( t = p \): point \( (3p^{2}, 2p^{3}) \), gradient \( m = p \). B1
Tangent: \( y - 2p^{3} = p\,(x - 3p^{2}) \). M1 \[ y = px - 3p^{3} + 2p^{3} = px - p^{3} \qquad \text{A1 (AG)} \]

Archetype 3 — Tangent / Normal Equations (pure or applied)

How the exam asks it
"Find the equation of the tangent / normal to \( y = f(x) \) at the point …", often followed by "find where the normal meets the curve again." 3–7m, often a P1 mid-paper question.
Example
The curve \( C \) has equation \( y = x^{3} - 2x^{2} \). The point \( A \) on \( C \) has \( x \)-coordinate \( 2 \).
(i) Find the equation of the normal to \( C \) at \( A \). [3]
(ii) Determine whether the normal meets \( C \) again. [4]
Solution with mark scheme
(i) At \( x = 2 \): \( y = 8 - 8 = 0 \). \( A = (2, 0) \).
\( \dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3x^{2} - 4x \). At \( x = 2 \): \( m = 4 \). M1
Normal gradient \( = -\tfrac{1}{4} \). Equation: M1 \[ y - 0 = -\tfrac{1}{4}(x - 2) \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; y = -\tfrac{1}{4}x + \tfrac{1}{2} \qquad \text{A1} \] (ii) Solve \( x^{3} - 2x^{2} = -\tfrac{1}{4}x + \tfrac{1}{2} \). Multiply by 4: M1 \[ 4x^{3} - 8x^{2} + x - 2 = 0 \] \( x = 2 \) is a root. Factor: \( (x - 2)(4x^{2} + 1) = 0 \). M1
\( 4x^{2} + 1 > 0 \) for all real \( x \), so no other real root. A1
The normal does not meet \( C \) again. A1
Solution hidden — attempt the example first
Practice
The curve \( C \) has equation \( y = \dfrac{x^{2}}{x - 1} \) for \( x \ne 1 \).
(i) Show that \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{x^{2} - 2x}{(x - 1)^{2}} \). [2]
(ii) Find the equation of the tangent to \( C \) at the point where \( x = 2 \). [3]
Answer with mark scheme
(i) Quotient rule with \( u = x^{2},\; v = x - 1 \): \( u' = 2x,\; v' = 1 \). \[ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{2x(x-1) - x^{2}}{(x-1)^{2}} = \frac{x^{2} - 2x}{(x-1)^{2}} \qquad \text{M1 A1 (AG)} \] (ii) At \( x = 2 \): \( y = 4 \), point \( (2, 4) \). B1
Gradient \( = \dfrac{4 - 4}{1} = 0 \). Tangent is horizontal. M1
Equation: \( y = 4 \). A1

Archetype 4 — Connected Rates of Change (real-world: tank, balloon, cone, meteorite)

How the exam asks it
A physical situation with two rates linked by geometry. Common contexts: cone draining, spherical balloon expanding, ice melting, oil spilling. Find one rate given another at a specific instant. Typical 4–6m, P1/P2 opener.
Example
A right circular cone with semi-vertical angle \( 30^{\circ} \) is held vertex downward with its axis vertical. Water is poured in at \( 8 \) cm³ s\(^{-1}\). Find the rate at which the depth of water is increasing when the depth is \( 10 \) cm. [5]
Solution with mark scheme
Geometry: \( \tan 30^{\circ} = \dfrac{r}{h} \Rightarrow r = \dfrac{h}{\sqrt{3}} \). B1 \[ V = \tfrac{1}{3}\pi r^{2} h = \tfrac{1}{3}\pi\cdot\tfrac{h^{2}}{3}\cdot h = \frac{\pi h^{3}}{9} \qquad \text{M1} \] \[ \frac{dV}{dh} = \frac{\pi h^{2}}{3} \qquad \text{M1} \] At \( h = 10 \): \( \dfrac{dV}{dh} = \dfrac{100\pi}{3} \). Chain rule \( \dfrac{dV}{dt} = \dfrac{dV}{dh}\cdot\dfrac{dh}{dt} \): \[ 8 = \frac{100\pi}{3}\cdot\frac{dh}{dt} \qquad \text{M1} \] \[ \frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{6}{25\pi} \approx 0.0764 \;\text{cm s}^{-1} \qquad \text{A1 (with units)} \]
Solution hidden — attempt the example first
Practice
A spherical raindrop evaporates so that its volume decreases at \( 0.5 \) mm³ s\(^{-1}\). Find the rate at which the radius is decreasing when \( r = 2 \) mm. [5]
Answer with mark scheme
\( V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi r^{3} \Rightarrow \dfrac{dV}{dr} = 4\pi r^{2} \). B1 M1
At \( r = 2 \): \( \dfrac{dV}{dr} = 16\pi \). A1
Volume decreasing, so \( \dfrac{dV}{dt} = -0.5 \) (sign matters): \[ -0.5 = 16\pi\cdot\frac{dr}{dt} \qquad \text{M1} \] \[ \frac{dr}{dt} = -\frac{1}{32\pi} \approx -0.00995 \;\text{mm s}^{-1} \qquad \text{A1} \]

Archetype 5 — Optimisation (max / min in a context)

How the exam asks it
Minimise material / cost / distance, OR maximise area / volume / profit. (1) Express the quantity in one variable via a constraint. (2) Differentiate, set to zero, solve. (3) Verify max/min (often "(You need not show that this is a min/max)"). Typical 6–10m.
Example
A closed cylindrical can of fixed volume \( 500 \) cm³ is to be made. The top and bottom cost twice as much per unit area as the curved surface. Find the radius and height of the can that minimise the total cost. (You need not show that your answer gives a minimum.) [7]
Solution with mark scheme
Constraint: \( \pi r^{2}h = 500 \Rightarrow h = \dfrac{500}{\pi r^{2}} \). B1
Cost (let \( k \) = cost per unit area of curved surface): \[ C = 2k(2\pi r^{2}) + k(2\pi r h) = 4\pi k r^{2} + 2\pi k r h \qquad \text{M1} \] Substitute: \( C = 4\pi k r^{2} + \dfrac{1000k}{r} \). M1
Differentiate and set to zero: \[ \frac{dC}{dr} = 8\pi k r - \frac{1000k}{r^{2}} = 0 \qquad \text{M1} \] \[ 8\pi r^{3} = 1000 \;\Rightarrow\; r = \frac{5}{\sqrt[3]{\pi}} \approx 3.41 \;\text{cm} \qquad \text{A1} \] \[ h = \frac{500}{\pi r^{2}} \approx 6.83 \;\text{cm} \qquad \text{A1} \] Note: \( h = 2r \) at the optimum — a clean ratio result. A1
Solution hidden — attempt the example first
Practice
A rectangle is inscribed in a semicircle of radius \( 10 \) cm, with one side on the diameter. Find the dimensions and the maximum area. (You need not show your answer is a maximum.) [7]
Answer with mark scheme
Semicircle: \( x^{2} + y^{2} = 100,\; y \ge 0 \). Rectangle vertices \( (\pm x, 0),\; (\pm x, y) \). Width \( 2x \), height \( y \). B1
Area: \( A = 2xy = 2x\sqrt{100 - x^{2}} \). M1
Product rule + chain: \[ \frac{dA}{dx} = 2\sqrt{100 - x^{2}} + 2x\cdot\frac{-x}{\sqrt{100 - x^{2}}} = \frac{200 - 4x^{2}}{\sqrt{100 - x^{2}}} \qquad \text{M1 A1} \] Set to zero: \( 200 - 4x^{2} = 0 \Rightarrow x = 5\sqrt{2} \). M1
\( y = \sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2} \). A1
Max area: \( A = 2 \cdot 5\sqrt{2} \cdot 5\sqrt{2} = 100 \;\text{cm}^{2} \). A1
Dimensions: width \( 10\sqrt{2} \approx 14.1 \) cm, height \( 5\sqrt{2} \approx 7.07 \) cm.

Archetype 6 — Sketching \( y = f'(x) \) from \( y = f(x) \), or Sign Reasoning

How the exam asks it
Given a sketch of \( y = f(x) \), sketch \( y = f'(x) \). Turning points of \( f \) become \( x \)-axis crossings of \( f' \); intervals where \( f \) increases / decreases give the sign of \( f' \); inflexion points of \( f \) become turning points of \( f' \). Typical 3–5m opener.
Example
A function \( f \) satisfies \( f'(x) = (x - 1)(x - 3)^{2} \).
(i) State the values of \( x \) where \( f \) has a local max or min, with reasons. [3]
(ii) Sketch \( y = f'(x) \), indicating \( x \)-axis crossings and behaviour. [2]
Solution with mark scheme
(i) \( f'(x) = 0 \) at \( x = 1 \) (simple) and \( x = 3 \) (double). B1
Sign of \( f'(x) \):
  • \( x < 1 \): \( (-)\cdot(+) < 0 \). \( f \) decreasing.
  • \( 1 < x < 3 \): \( (+)\cdot(+) > 0 \). \( f \) increasing.
  • \( x > 3 \): \( (+)\cdot(+) > 0 \). \( f \) still increasing.
\( f' \) changes \( - \to + \) at \( x = 1 \) → local min at \( x = 1 \). M1 A1
No sign change at \( x = 3 \) → stationary point of inflexion (not max/min).
(ii) Sketch: cubic shape, crosses axis at \( x = 1 \), touches axis at \( x = 3 \). B1 B1
Solution hidden — attempt the example first
Practice
The graph of \( y = f(x) \) has a local maximum at \( (-2, 5) \), a local minimum at \( (1, -3) \), and a non-stationary point of inflexion at \( x = 4 \). \( f \) is increasing on \( (-\infty, -2) \cup (1, \infty) \) and decreasing on \( (-2, 1) \). Sketch \( y = f'(x) \), labelling all \( x \)-axis crossings and turning points. [4]
Answer with mark scheme
Key features of \( f'(x) \):
  • \( f'(-2) = 0 \) (turning point of \( f \)) — crosses \( x \)-axis from \( + \) to \( - \). B1
  • \( f'(1) = 0 \) — crosses from \( - \) to \( + \). B1
  • \( f''(4) = 0 \) (inflexion of \( f \)) — \( f' \) has a turning point at \( x = 4 \) (does NOT cross). B1
  • Shape: \( f' > 0 \) on \( (-\infty, -2) \), \( f' < 0 \) on \( (-2, 1) \), \( f' > 0 \) on \( (1, \infty) \) with a local min at \( x = 4 \). B1

Archetype 7 — Maclaurin by Substituting into Standard MF27 Series

How the exam asks it
"Using the standard series in the List of Formulae and Results, expand … up to and including the term in \( x^{n} \). State the range of \( x \) for which the expansion is valid." Pure mechanical recognition. Typical 3–5m.
Example
Using the standard series in MF27, expand \( \dfrac{1}{(1 - 2x)^{3}} \) up to and including the term in \( x^{3} \). State the range of \( x \) for which the expansion is valid. [4]
Solution with mark scheme
Recognise \( (1 + u)^{n} \) with \( u = -2x,\; n = -3 \). M1 \[ (1+u)^{-3} = 1 - 3u + 6u^{2} - 10u^{3} + \cdots \] Substitute: \[ = 1 + 6x + 24x^{2} + 80x^{3} + \cdots \qquad \text{M1 A1} \] Validity: \( |-2x| < 1 \Rightarrow |x| < \tfrac{1}{2} \). B1
Solution hidden — attempt the example first
Practice
Using the standard series in MF27, expand \( \sqrt{1 + 3x} \) up to and including the term in \( x^{3} \). State the range of \( x \) for which the expansion is valid. [4]
Answer with mark scheme
\( (1 + 3x)^{1/2} \) with \( u = 3x,\; n = \tfrac{1}{2} \). M1 \[ (1+u)^{1/2} = 1 + \tfrac{1}{2}u - \tfrac{1}{8}u^{2} + \tfrac{1}{16}u^{3} + \cdots \] Substitute: \[ = 1 + \tfrac{3}{2}x - \tfrac{9}{8}x^{2} + \tfrac{27}{16}x^{3} + \cdots \qquad \text{M1 A1} \] Validity: \( |3x| < 1 \Rightarrow |x| < \tfrac{1}{3} \). B1

Archetype 8 — Maclaurin by Repeated Differentiation

How the exam asks it
"Given \( f(x) = [\text{non-standard function}] \), find \( f(0),\; f'(0),\; f''(0),\; \ldots \), and hence the Maclaurin series up to \( x^{n} \)." Used when the function isn't in MF27 (e.g. \( \sec x,\; \tan x,\; \sec 2x \)).
Example
It is given that \( f(x) = \sec 2x \).
(i) Find \( f(0),\; f'(0),\; f''(0) \). [4]
(ii) Hence find the Maclaurin series for \( f(x) \) up to and including the term in \( x^{2} \). [2]
Solution with mark scheme
(i) \[ f(0) = \sec 0 = 1 \qquad \text{B1} \] \[ f'(x) = 2\sec 2x\tan 2x \Rightarrow f'(0) = 2\cdot 1\cdot 0 = 0 \qquad \text{M1 A1} \] \( f''(x) = 4\sec 2x\tan^{2} 2x + 4\sec^{3} 2x \). At \( x = 0 \): \( f''(0) = 0 + 4 = 4 \). A1
(ii) \[ f(x) \approx 1 + 0\cdot x + \frac{4}{2}x^{2} = 1 + 2x^{2} \qquad \text{M1 A1} \]
Solution hidden — attempt the example first
Practice
It is given that \( f(x) = \tan x \).
(i) Find \( f(0),\; f'(0),\; f''(0),\; f'''(0) \). [5]
(ii) Hence find the Maclaurin series for \( \tan x \) up to and including the term in \( x^{3} \). [2]
Answer with mark scheme
(i) \[ f(0) = 0;\quad f'(x) = \sec^{2}x \Rightarrow f'(0) = 1 \qquad \text{B1 B1} \] \[ f''(x) = 2\sec^{2}x\tan x \Rightarrow f''(0) = 0 \qquad \text{M1 A1} \] \( f'''(x) = 4\sec^{2}x\tan^{2}x + 2\sec^{4}x \). At \( x = 0 \): \( f'''(0) = 2 \). A1
(ii) \[ \tan x \approx 0 + x + \frac{0}{2}x^{2} + \frac{2}{6}x^{3} = x + \tfrac{1}{3}x^{3} \qquad \text{M1 A1} \]

Archetype 9 — Maclaurin by Combining Standard Series

How the exam asks it
"Using standard series in MF27, find the Maclaurin series of \( e^{2x}\cos x \) (or \( \ln(\cos 2x),\; \dfrac{1}{1 - \sin x} \), etc.) up to the term in \( x^{n} \)." Multiply or compose standard series; collect terms. Faster than repeated differentiation when applicable. Typical 4–6m.
Example
Find the first three non-zero terms in the Maclaurin series of \( \ln(\cos 2x) \). [5]
Solution with mark scheme
Step 1: \( \cos 2x = 1 - 2x^{2} + \tfrac{2}{3}x^{4} - \cdots \). M1
Step 2: Let \( u = -2x^{2} + \tfrac{2}{3}x^{4} - \cdots \), then \( \cos 2x = 1 + u \). Apply \( \ln(1 + u) = u - \tfrac{u^{2}}{2} + \tfrac{u^{3}}{3} - \cdots \). M1
Step 3: Compute up to \( x^{6} \): \[ u = -2x^{2} + \tfrac{2}{3}x^{4};\qquad u^{2} = 4x^{4} - \tfrac{8}{3}x^{6} + \cdots;\qquad u^{3} = -8x^{6} + \cdots \] \[ \ln(\cos 2x) = u - \tfrac{u^{2}}{2} + \tfrac{u^{3}}{3} \] \[ = \left(-2x^{2} + \tfrac{2}{3}x^{4}\right) - \left(2x^{4} - \tfrac{4}{3}x^{6}\right) + \left(-\tfrac{8}{3}x^{6}\right) \qquad \text{M1} \] \[ = -2x^{2} - \tfrac{4}{3}x^{4} - \tfrac{4}{3}x^{6} + \cdots \qquad \text{A1 A1} \]
Solution hidden — attempt the example first
Practice
Using standard series, find the Maclaurin series of \( e^{x}\sin 2x \) up to and including the term in \( x^{3} \). [4]
Answer with mark scheme
\( e^{x} = 1 + x + \tfrac{x^{2}}{2} + \tfrac{x^{3}}{6} + \cdots \); \( \sin 2x = 2x - \tfrac{4}{3}x^{3} + \cdots \). M1 M1
Multiply, keep up to \( x^{3} \): \[ (1)(2x) + (x)(2x) + (\tfrac{x^{2}}{2})(2x) + (1)(-\tfrac{4}{3}x^{3}) \] \[ = 2x + 2x^{2} + x^{3} - \tfrac{4}{3}x^{3} = 2x + 2x^{2} - \tfrac{1}{3}x^{3} + \cdots \qquad \text{M1 A1} \]

Archetype 10 — Maclaurin by Repeated Implicit Differentiation

How the exam asks it
"Given that \( y \) satisfies \( [\text{implicit equation}] \) and \( y = y_{0} \) when \( x = 0 \), find the first few terms of the Maclaurin series of \( y \)." Differentiate the relation implicitly, substitute \( (x, y) = (0, y_{0}) \) at each step to find \( y'(0), y''(0), \ldots \).
Example
It is given that \( y \) satisfies \( y^{3} + y^{2} + y = x^{2} - 2x \) and \( y = 0 \) when \( x = 0 \).
(i) Show that \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} = -2 \) when \( x = 0 \). [2]
(ii) Find \( \dfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} \) when \( x = 0 \). [3]
(iii) Hence find the Maclaurin series for \( y \) up to \( x^{2} \). [2]
Solution with mark scheme
(i) Differentiate (chain rule on \( y \)-terms): \[ 3y^{2}\frac{dy}{dx} + 2y\frac{dy}{dx} + \frac{dy}{dx} = 2x - 2 \qquad \text{M1} \] At \( x = 0,\, y = 0 \): \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} = -2 \). A1 (AG)
(ii) Differentiate again: \[ 6y\!\left(\tfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^{2} + 3y^{2}\tfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} + 2\!\left(\tfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^{2} + 2y\tfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} + \tfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} = 2 \qquad \text{M1} \] At \( x = 0,\, y = 0,\, \dfrac{dy}{dx} = -2 \): \[ 0 + 0 + 2(4) + 0 + \tfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} = 2 \Rightarrow \tfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} = -6 \qquad \text{M1 A1} \] (iii) \[ y \approx 0 + (-2)x + \tfrac{-6}{2}x^{2} = -2x - 3x^{2} \qquad \text{M1 A1} \]
Solution hidden — attempt the example first
Practice
It is given that \( y \) satisfies \( e^{y} = 1 + \sin x \) and \( y = 0 \) when \( x = 0 \).
(i) Show that \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} = 1 \) when \( x = 0 \). [2]
(ii) Find \( \dfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} \) when \( x = 0 \). [3]
(iii) Hence find the Maclaurin series for \( y \) up to \( x^{2} \). [2]
Answer with mark scheme
(i) \[ e^{y}\frac{dy}{dx} = \cos x \qquad \text{M1} \] At \( x = 0,\,y = 0 \): \( 1\cdot\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 1 \). A1 (AG)
(ii) Differentiate \( e^{y}\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \cos x \) (product rule on LHS): \[ e^{y}\!\left(\tfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^{2} + e^{y}\tfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} = -\sin x \qquad \text{M1} \] At \( x = 0,\,y = 0,\,\tfrac{dy}{dx} = 1 \): \( 1 + \tfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} = 0 \Rightarrow \tfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} = -1 \). M1 A1
(iii) \[ y \approx 0 + x - \tfrac{1}{2}x^{2} \qquad \text{M1 A1} \]

Archetype 11 — Range of Convergence / Validity

How the exam asks it
"State the range of values of \( x \) for which the expansion is valid" — usually 1–2m at the end of a Maclaurin question. \( (1+x)^{n} \to |x| < 1 \); \( e^{x},\sin x,\cos x \to \) all \( x \); \( \ln(1+x) \to -1 < x \le 1 \). When \( x \to g(x) \), substitute and solve the inequality.
Example
A function is expanded as \( \ln(1 + 3x^{2}) = 3x^{2} - \tfrac{9}{2}x^{4} + 9x^{6} - \cdots \). State the range of values of \( x \) for which the expansion is valid. [2]
Solution with mark scheme
Base series \( \ln(1 + u) \) valid for \( -1 < u \le 1 \). Here \( u = 3x^{2} \). M1

Example
