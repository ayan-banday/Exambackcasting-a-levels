The vectors toolkit — memorise cold (NOT in MF27 unless flagged)
Magnitude \( |\mathbf{v}| \)\( \sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}} \) — Pythagoras extended to 3-D
Unit vector \( \hat{\mathbf{v}} \)\( \dfrac{\mathbf{v}}{|\mathbf{v}|} \) — same direction, magnitude 1
Displacement \( \overrightarrow{AB} \)\( \overrightarrow{OB} - \overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a} \)
Distance between points\( |\overrightarrow{AB}| = |\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}| \)
Collinearity of \(A, B, C\)Show \( \overrightarrow{AB} = k\,\overrightarrow{AC} \) for some scalar \(k\) (parallel + shared point). Equivalent: \( \overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC} = \mathbf{0} \).
Ratio theorem — \(P\) divides \(AB\) in ratio \(\lambda:\mu\)\( \overrightarrow{OP} = \dfrac{\mu\mathbf{a} + \lambda\mathbf{b}}{\lambda + \mu} \)   — the weight goes with the OPPOSITE endpoint. Midpoint: \( \overrightarrow{OM} = \dfrac{\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}}{2} \).
Scalar (dot) product\( \mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} = |\mathbf{a}||\mathbf{b}|\cos\theta = a_{1}b_{1}+a_{2}b_{2}+a_{3}b_{3} \). Perpendicular iff \( \mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}=0 \). Note \( \mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{a}=|\mathbf{a}|^{2} \).
Vector (cross) product\( |\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}| = |\mathbf{a}||\mathbf{b}|\sin\theta \). Direction: perpendicular to both, right-hand rule. Parallel iff \( \mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{0} \). Anti-commutative: \( \mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b} = -\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{a} \). And \( \mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{0} \).
Angle between vectors\( \cos\theta = \dfrac{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}}{|\mathbf{a}||\mathbf{b}|} \)
Scalar projection of \(\mathbf{a}\) on \(\hat{\mathbf{n}}\)\( \mathbf{a}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}} \)   — a signed length. Vector projection \( = (\mathbf{a}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}})\hat{\mathbf{n}} \). Perpendicular-component magnitude \( = |\mathbf{a}\times\hat{\mathbf{n}}| \).
Area of triangle\( \tfrac{1}{2}|\overrightarrow{AB}\times\overrightarrow{AC}| \)
Area of parallelogram\( |\overrightarrow{AB}\times\overrightarrow{AD}| \)   (adjacent sides)
Excluded in 9758: triple products \( \mathbf{a}\cdot(\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c}) \) and \( \mathbf{a}\times(\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c}) \). Never use them.

Question 1

Question
The points \( A \) and \( B \) have position vectors \( \overrightarrow{OA} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} \) and \( \overrightarrow{OB} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 0 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix} \) referred to the origin \( O \).

(i) Find \( \overrightarrow{AB} \) and state its magnitude in exact form. [2]
(ii) Find the unit vector in the direction of \( \overrightarrow{AB} \). [1]
(iii) The point \( C \) has position vector \( \overrightarrow{OC} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ k \end{pmatrix} \). Find the possible values of \( k \) for which \( |\overrightarrow{OA}| = |\overrightarrow{OC}| \). [2]
Solution
Step 1 — (i) Displacement and magnitude
M1 A1
\[ \overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{OB} - \overrightarrow{OA} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 0 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ -5 \end{pmatrix} \] \[ |\overrightarrow{AB}| = \sqrt{2^{2} + 1^{2} + (-5)^{2}} = \sqrt{30} \text{ units} \]
Step 2 — (ii) Unit vector
A1
\[ \hat{\mathbf{u}} = \dfrac{\overrightarrow{AB}}{|\overrightarrow{AB}|} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{30}}\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ -5 \end{pmatrix} \]
Step 3 — (iii) Equal magnitude condition
M1 A1
\( |\overrightarrow{OA}|^{2} = 4 + 1 + 9 = 14 \).   \( |\overrightarrow{OC}|^{2} = 1 + 4 + k^{2} = 5 + k^{2} \).
\[ 5 + k^{2} = 14 \;\Longrightarrow\; k^{2} = 9 \;\Longrightarrow\; k = \pm 3 \] \[ \therefore k = 3 \text{ or } k = -3 \]
Trap: squaring gives \(k = \pm 3\) — both roots must be reported. A single "\(k = 3\)" answer loses the A mark.
Solution hidden — click Reveal
Archetype revealed
Basics — displacement, magnitude, unit vector, magnitude equation.

The bread-and-butter opener for any vectors question. Trap: forgetting the second root when solving \(k^{2} = c\); also, giving the unit vector as \( \overrightarrow{AB} \) itself (must divide by magnitude).

Question 2

Question
The points \( P \) and \( Q \) have position vectors \( \overrightarrow{OP} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 3 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} \) and \( \overrightarrow{OQ} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -1 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} \).

(i) Find \( \overrightarrow{PQ} \) and state its exact magnitude. [2]
(ii) Find the unit vector in the direction of \( \overrightarrow{QP} \). [1]
(iii) The point \( R \) has position vector \( \overrightarrow{OR} = \begin{pmatrix} t \\ 2 \\ t-1 \end{pmatrix} \). Find the values of \( t \) for which \( |\overrightarrow{OR}| = 3 \). [3]
Solution
Step 1 — (i)
M1 A1
\[ \overrightarrow{PQ} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -1 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 3 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ -4 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}, \quad |\overrightarrow{PQ}| = \sqrt{16+16+4} = \sqrt{36} = 6 \text{ units} \]
Step 2 — (ii)
A1
\( \overrightarrow{QP} = -\overrightarrow{PQ} = \begin{pmatrix} -4 \\ 4 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix} \), \( |\overrightarrow{QP}| = 6 \). \[ \hat{\mathbf{u}} = \dfrac{1}{6}\begin{pmatrix} -4 \\ 4 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix} = \dfrac{1}{3}\begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 2 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} \]
Step 3 — (iii)
M1 M1 A1
\( |\overrightarrow{OR}|^{2} = t^{2} + 4 + (t-1)^{2} = 9 \)
\( t^{2} + 4 + t^{2} - 2t + 1 = 9 \)  \(\Longrightarrow\)  \( 2t^{2} - 2t - 4 = 0 \)  \(\Longrightarrow\)  \( t^{2} - t - 2 = 0 \) \[ (t-2)(t+1) = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \therefore t = 2 \text{ or } t = -1 \]
Trap: quadratic in \(t\) — two roots. Also, \(\overrightarrow{QP}\) is asked for, not \(\overrightarrow{PQ}\) — reverse the sign before unitising.
Solution hidden — click Reveal
Archetype revealed
Basics again — same mechanic on a different stem, plus the "which direction was asked?" trap.

The quadratic in (iii) is where reps count: \(|\mathbf{r}|^{2} = c\) always produces at least two roots when the components involve \(t\). Simplify unit vectors by pulling out common factors.

Question 3

Question
The points \( A \), \( B \), \( C \) have position vectors \( \overrightarrow{OA} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} \), \( \overrightarrow{OB} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 0 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} \), \( \overrightarrow{OC} = \begin{pmatrix} x \\ 4 \\ y \end{pmatrix} \).

Given that \( A \), \( B \) and \( C \) are collinear, find the values of \( x \) and \( y \). [4]
Solution
Step 1 — Set up the collinearity condition
B1
Since \(A\), \(B\), \(C\) are collinear, \( \overrightarrow{AC} = k\,\overrightarrow{AB} \) for some scalar \(k\).
Step 2 — Compute both displacement vectors
M1
\[ \overrightarrow{AB} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 0 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 2 \\ -4 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \overrightarrow{AC} = \begin{pmatrix} x-1 \\ 6 \\ y-3 \end{pmatrix} \]
Step 3 — Compare components
M1
\[ \begin{pmatrix} x-1 \\ 6 \\ y-3 \end{pmatrix} = k\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 2 \\ -4 \end{pmatrix} \] From the \(j\)-component: \(6 = 2k \;\Longrightarrow\; k = 3\).
From the \(i\)-component: \(x - 1 = 2(3) = 6 \;\Longrightarrow\; x = 7\).
From the \(k\)-component: \(y - 3 = -4(3) = -12 \;\Longrightarrow\; y = -9\).
Step 4 — Conclude
A1
\[ \therefore x = 7, \; y = -9 \]
Trap: writing "\( |\overrightarrow{AB}| / |\overrightarrow{AC}| = k \)" or reasoning from magnitudes alone. Magnitudes on their own do not establish collinearity — you must show the vectors are parallel (scalar multiple) and share a point.
Solution hidden — click Reveal
Archetype revealed
Collinearity via parallel displacement vectors.

The template: \( \overrightarrow{AC} = k\,\overrightarrow{AB} \). Solve for \(k\) from the "clean" component (the one without unknowns), then substitute into the others. HCI Tutorial 7A Q2 explicitly warns: cannot use magnitude ratios alone — vectors must be shown parallel.

Question 4

Question
Referred to the origin \(O\), points \(P\), \(Q\), \(R\) have position vectors \( \mathbf{p} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} \), \( \mathbf{q} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \), \( \mathbf{r} = \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \\ 5 \end{pmatrix} \).

(i) Show that \(P\) and \(Q\) are distinct, and determine \(\overrightarrow{PQ}\). [1]
(ii) Given that \(P\), \(Q\), \(R\) are collinear, find \(a\) and \(b\). [3]
Solution
Step 1 — (i)
B1
\( \overrightarrow{PQ} = \mathbf{q} - \mathbf{p} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 2 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} \neq \mathbf{0} \), so \(P \neq Q\).
Step 2 — (ii)
M1 M1 A1
\( \overrightarrow{PR} = \begin{pmatrix} a-2 \\ b-1 \\ 6 \end{pmatrix} \). For collinearity, \( \overrightarrow{PR} = k \overrightarrow{PQ} = k\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 2 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} \).
\(k\)-component: \(6 = 2k \;\Longrightarrow\; k = 3\).
\(i\): \(a - 2 = 6 \;\Longrightarrow\; a = 8\).   \(j\): \(b - 1 = 6 \;\Longrightarrow\; b = 7\). \[ \therefore a = 8, \; b = 7 \]
Solution hidden — click Reveal
Archetype revealed
Collinearity again — this time the unknowns are the components of one point.

The "clean" component here is the \(k\)-component (no unknowns) — always work it first. Same technique as Q3, different unknowns.

Question 5

Question
Points \(A\) and \(B\) have position vectors \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\) referred to the origin \(O\).

(i) Point \(M\) is the midpoint of \(AB\). Write down \(\overrightarrow{OM}\) in terms of \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\). [1]
(ii) Point \(P\) lies on \(AB\) such that \(AP : PB = 2 : 3\). Find \(\overrightarrow{OP}\) in terms of \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\). [2]
(iii) Point \(Q\) lies on \(AB\) extended (beyond \(B\)) such that \(B\) is the midpoint of \(AQ\). Find \(\overrightarrow{OQ}\) in terms of \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\). [2]
Solution
Step 1 — (i) Midpoint
B1
\[ \overrightarrow{OM} = \dfrac{\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}}{2} \]
Step 2 — (ii) Ratio theorem, internal division
M1 A1
By ratio theorem, with \(\lambda : \mu = 2 : 3\): \[ \overrightarrow{OP} = \dfrac{\mu\mathbf{a} + \lambda\mathbf{b}}{\lambda + \mu} = \dfrac{3\mathbf{a} + 2\mathbf{b}}{5} \]
Step 3 — (iii) External point
M1 A1
\(B\) is the midpoint of \(AQ\), so \( \mathbf{b} = \dfrac{\mathbf{a} + \overrightarrow{OQ}}{2} \). \[ \therefore \overrightarrow{OQ} = 2\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a} \]
Trap (T4): in \( (\mu\mathbf{a} + \lambda\mathbf{b})/(\lambda + \mu) \), the weight goes with the opposite endpoint. Ratio \(AP:PB = 2:3\) puts 3 next to \(\mathbf{a}\) and 2 next to \(\mathbf{b}\). Writing \( (2\mathbf{a} + 3\mathbf{b})/5 \) is a straight sign/weight error.
Solution hidden — click Reveal
Archetype revealed
Ratio theorem — midpoint, internal division, and external point.

The weight-opposite-endpoint rule is the single most-tested facet. External division phrases ("midpoint of the extension", "B produced to Q with…") collapse into a simple linear rearrangement — no need for a special formula.

Question 6

Question
In triangle \(OAB\), \(\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}\) and \(\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf{b}\). Point \(P\) lies on \(AB\) such that \(AP : PB = 3 : 2\). Point \(Q\) lies on \(OA\) such that \(OQ : QA = 2 : 5\). Point \(R\) is the midpoint of \(BQ\).

(i) Find \(\overrightarrow{OP}\) in terms of \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\). [2]
(ii) Find \(\overrightarrow{OR}\) in terms of \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\). [3]
Solution
Step 1 — (i)
M1 A1
By ratio theorem, with \(AP : PB = 3 : 2\): \[ \overrightarrow{OP} = \dfrac{2\mathbf{a} + 3\mathbf{b}}{5} \]
Step 2 — (ii)
M1 M1 A1
\(Q\) divides \(OA\) with \(OQ : QA = 2 : 5\), so \(\overrightarrow{OQ} = \tfrac{2}{7}\mathbf{a}\).
\(R\) is midpoint of \(BQ\): \[ \overrightarrow{OR} = \dfrac{\overrightarrow{OB} + \overrightarrow{OQ}}{2} = \dfrac{1}{2}\left(\mathbf{b} + \tfrac{2}{7}\mathbf{a}\right) = \dfrac{1}{7}\mathbf{a} + \dfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{b} \]
Solution hidden — click Reveal
Archetype revealed
Ratio theorem stacked — two ratios on different sides of a triangle plus a midpoint of a derived segment.

The chained ratios exercise: (ii) makes you apply the ratio theorem TWICE — once to find \(\overrightarrow{OQ}\), then once more (midpoint) to combine \(\overrightarrow{OQ}\) with \(\overrightarrow{OB}\). Every multi-part vectors question inside a triangle looks like this.

Question 7

Question
In triangle \(OAB\), \(\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}\) and \(\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf{b}\). Point \(X\) lies on \(OA\) such that \(OX : XA = 1 : 2\), and point \(Y\) lies on \(OB\) such that \(OY : YB = 1 : 3\). Lines \(AY\) and \(BX\) intersect at \(P\).

By expressing \(\overrightarrow{OP}\) in two different ways, find \(\overrightarrow{OP}\) in terms of \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\). [6]
Solution
Step 1 — Set up two expressions for \(\overrightarrow{OP}\)
M1 M1
\(\overrightarrow{OX} = \tfrac{1}{3}\mathbf{a}\), \(\overrightarrow{OY} = \tfrac{1}{4}\mathbf{b}\).
Since \(P\) lies on \(AY\), \( \overrightarrow{OP} = \overrightarrow{OA} + t\,\overrightarrow{AY} = \mathbf{a} + t\!\left(\tfrac{1}{4}\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}\right) = (1-t)\mathbf{a} + \tfrac{t}{4}\mathbf{b} \)   …(1)
Since \(P\) lies on \(BX\), \( \overrightarrow{OP} = \overrightarrow{OB} + s\,\overrightarrow{BX} = \mathbf{b} + s\!\left(\tfrac{1}{3}\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}\right) = \tfrac{s}{3}\mathbf{a} + (1-s)\mathbf{b} \)   …(2)
Step 2 — Equate coefficients of \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\)
M1
Since \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\) are not parallel, by comparison: \[ 1 - t = \tfrac{s}{3} \quad \text{…(3)}, \qquad \tfrac{t}{4} = 1 - s \quad \text{…(4)} \]
Step 3 — Solve the simultaneous system
M1 A1
From (4): \(s = 1 - \tfrac{t}{4}\). Substitute into (3): \( 1 - t = \tfrac{1}{3}\!\left(1 - \tfrac{t}{4}\right) = \tfrac{1}{3} - \tfrac{t}{12} \).
Multiply through by 12: \(12 - 12t = 4 - t \;\Longrightarrow\; 11t = 8 \;\Longrightarrow\; t = \tfrac{8}{11} \).
Then \( s = 1 - \tfrac{2}{11} = \tfrac{9}{11} \).
Step 4 — Substitute back
A1
Using (1): \( \overrightarrow{OP} = \left(1 - \tfrac{8}{11}\right)\mathbf{a} + \tfrac{8}{44}\mathbf{b} = \tfrac{3}{11}\mathbf{a} + \tfrac{2}{11}\mathbf{b} \). \[ \therefore \overrightarrow{OP} = \tfrac{1}{11}(3\mathbf{a} + 2\mathbf{b}) \]
Trap: comparing coefficients only works because \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\) are not parallel — you must state this. If they were parallel, one equation would collapse.
Solution hidden — click Reveal
Archetype revealed
Intersection of two lines via "two different expressions" — the standard cevians-in-a-triangle trick.

The technique HCI Tutorial 7A Q3 formalises: write the unknown position vector in two ways (one along each line through it), equate, then compare coefficients. This is the go-to method for any intersection question in position-vector form.

Question 8

Question
Vectors \( \mathbf{a} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} \) and \( \mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} \).

(i) Find \( \mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} \). [1]
(ii) Find \(|\mathbf{a}|\) and \(|\mathbf{b}|\), giving your answers in exact form. [2]
(iii) Find the exact value of \(\cos\theta\), where \(\theta\) is the angle between \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\). [2]
(iv) Hence state whether \(\theta\) is acute, obtuse, or a right angle, giving a reason. [1]
Solution
Step 1 — (i)
A1
\[ \mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} = (3)(1) + (2)(-1) + (-1)(4) = 3 - 2 - 4 = -3 \]
Step 2 — (ii)
A1 A1
\( |\mathbf{a}| = \sqrt{9 + 4 + 1} = \sqrt{14} \),   \( |\mathbf{b}| = \sqrt{1 + 1 + 16} = \sqrt{18} = 3\sqrt{2} \).
Step 3 — (iii)
M1 A1
\[ \cos\theta = \dfrac{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}}{|\mathbf{a}||\mathbf{b}|} = \dfrac{-3}{\sqrt{14}\cdot 3\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{-1}{\sqrt{28}} = -\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{7}} \]
Step 4 — (iv)
B1
\( \cos\theta < 0 \), so \( \theta \) is obtuse.
Solution hidden — click Reveal
Archetype revealed
Scalar (dot) product — angle between two vectors.

Sign of \(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}\) instantly tells you the type of angle: positive = acute, zero = perpendicular, negative = obtuse. Keep the answer in exact form when asked. Rationalising the denominator is a good habit but not required by the syllabus.

Question 9

Question
Given \( \mathbf{u} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ p \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} \) is perpendicular to \( \mathbf{v} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} \).

(i) Find the value of \(p\). [2]
(ii) Using the value of \(p\) from part (i), find the scalar projection of \( \mathbf{u} \) onto \( \mathbf{w} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} \), giving your answer in exact form. [3]
(iii) Hence, find the vector projection of \( \mathbf{u} \) onto \( \mathbf{w} \). [2]
Solution
Step 1 — (i) Perpendicularity
M1 A1
\( \mathbf{u} \perp \mathbf{v} \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = 0 \): \[ (2)(1) + (p)(2) + (3)(-1) = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; 2p - 1 = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; p = \tfrac{1}{2} \]
Step 2 — (ii) Scalar projection
M1 M1 A1
With \(p = \tfrac{1}{2}\), \( \mathbf{u} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1/2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} \). And \( |\mathbf{w}| = \sqrt{4+1+4} = 3 \).
Scalar projection \( = \mathbf{u}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{w}} = \dfrac{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{w}}{|\mathbf{w}|} \): \[ \mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{w} = (2)(2) + \tfrac{1}{2}(1) + (3)(2) = 4 + \tfrac{1}{2} + 6 = \tfrac{21}{2} \] \[ \therefore \mathbf{u}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{w}} = \dfrac{21/2}{3} = \dfrac{7}{2} \]
Step 3 — (iii) Vector projection
M1 A1
Vector projection \( = (\mathbf{u}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{w}})\,\hat{\mathbf{w}} = \tfrac{7}{2}\cdot\dfrac{1}{3}\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} = \dfrac{7}{6}\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} \).
Trap (T1): scalar projection \(\mathbf{u}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{w}}\) is a signed number (a length). Vector projection \((\mathbf{u}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{w}})\hat{\mathbf{w}}\) is a vector. Do not conflate them — the question wording pins down which one is required.
Solution hidden — click Reveal
Archetype revealed
Perpendicularity constraint + scalar/vector projection.

Two facets of the dot product in one question: (a) \(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} = 0\) locks a perpendicularity condition to find an unknown component; (b) projections. The scalar vs vector projection distinction is a syllabus-flagged trap.

Question 10

Question
The points \(A\), \(B\), \(C\) have position vectors \( \overrightarrow{OA} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} \), \( \overrightarrow{OB} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ -1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} \), \( \overrightarrow{OC} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix} \).

(i) Find \( \overrightarrow{AB} \) and \( \overrightarrow{AC} \). [1]
(ii) Compute \( \overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC} \). [2]
(iii) State the exact area of triangle \(ABC\). [2]
(iv) Deduce a unit vector perpendicular to the plane containing \(A\), \(B\) and \(C\). [2]
Solution
Step 1 — (i)
B1
\[ \overrightarrow{AB} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -3 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \overrightarrow{AC} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} \]
Step 2 — (ii) Cross product
M1 A1
\[ \overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC} = \begin{pmatrix} (-3)(2) - (-1)(1) \\ (-1)(1) - (3)(2) \\ (3)(1) - (-3)(1) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -5 \\ -7 \\ 6 \end{pmatrix} \]
Step 3 — (iii) Area of triangle
M1 A1
\[ \text{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2}\left|\overrightarrow{AB}\times\overrightarrow{AC}\right| = \tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{25 + 49 + 36} = \tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{110} \text{ units}^{2} \]
Step 4 — (iv) Unit normal
M1 A1
The cross product is perpendicular to both \( \overrightarrow{AB} \) and \( \overrightarrow{AC} \), hence perpendicular to the plane through \(A\), \(B\), \(C\). \[ \hat{\mathbf{n}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{110}}\begin{pmatrix} -5 \\ -7 \\ 6 \end{pmatrix} \]
Trap: when computing \(\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}\) by expansion, the middle component is \((a_{3}b_{1} - a_{1}b_{3})\), not \((a_{1}b_{3} - a_{3}b_{1})\). Get the sign wrong here and both the area and the normal direction flip.
Solution hidden — click Reveal
Archetype revealed
Vector (cross) product — area of triangle + perpendicular direction.

Two applications of \(\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}\) in one card: magnitude gives area (\(\tfrac{1}{2}\) for triangle, no \(\tfrac{1}{2}\) for parallelogram); direction gives a normal to the plane. The unit normal is used constantly in Cluster 3 (planes).

Question 11

Question
Referred to the origin \(O\), let \(P\), \(Q\), \(R\) be distinct points with position vectors \(\mathbf{p}\), \(\mathbf{q}\), \(\mathbf{r}\) respectively.

(a) Show that \( (\mathbf{q} - \mathbf{p}) \times (\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{p}) = \mathbf{p}\times\mathbf{q} + \mathbf{q}\times\mathbf{r} + \mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{p} \). [3]
(b) Explain, with reference to part (a), why \( \tfrac{1}{2}|\mathbf{p}\times\mathbf{q} + \mathbf{q}\times\mathbf{r} + \mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{p}| \) is the area of triangle \(PQR\). [2]
(c) Given that \( \mathbf{p}\times\mathbf{q} + \mathbf{q}\times\mathbf{r} + \mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{p} = \mathbf{0} \), and that \(P\), \(Q\), \(R\) are distinct, deduce that \(P\), \(Q\), \(R\) are collinear. [3]
Solution
Step 1 — (a) Expand using distributivity
M1 M1 A1
\[ (\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{p}) \times (\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{p}) = \mathbf{q}\times\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{q}\times\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{p}\times\mathbf{r} + \mathbf{p}\times\mathbf{p} \] Using \( \mathbf{p}\times\mathbf{p} = \mathbf{0} \) and \( \mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b} = -\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{a} \): \[ = \mathbf{q}\times\mathbf{r} + \mathbf{p}\times\mathbf{q} + \mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{p} = \mathbf{p}\times\mathbf{q} + \mathbf{q}\times\mathbf{r} + \mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{p} \text{ (shown)} \]
Step 2 — (b) Geometrical meaning
B1 B1
From (a): \( \mathbf{p}\times\mathbf{q} + \mathbf{q}\times\mathbf{r} + \mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{p} = \overrightarrow{PQ}\times\overrightarrow{PR} \). Hence \[ \tfrac{1}{2}\left|\mathbf{p}\times\mathbf{q} + \mathbf{q}\times\mathbf{r} + \mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{p}\right| = \tfrac{1}{2}\left|\overrightarrow{PQ}\times\overrightarrow{PR}\right| = \text{area of }\triangle PQR. \]
Step 3 — (c) Collinearity
M1 M1 A1
Given the sum is \(\mathbf{0}\), by part (a), \( \overrightarrow{PQ} \times \overrightarrow{PR} = \mathbf{0} \).
Since \(P\), \(Q\), \(R\) are distinct, \( \overrightarrow{PQ} \neq \mathbf{0} \) and \( \overrightarrow{PR} \neq \mathbf{0} \). A non-zero cross product is zero only when the two vectors are parallel. \[ \therefore \overrightarrow{PQ} \parallel \overrightarrow{PR}, \text{ and since they share the point } P, \; P, Q, R \text{ are collinear.} \]
Trap: in the expansion, order matters — \( \mathbf{q}\times\mathbf{p} = -\mathbf{p}\times\mathbf{q} \). A student who assumes commutativity for the cross product will produce \(-\mathbf{p}\times\mathbf{q}\) as \(\mathbf{p}\times\mathbf{q}\) and the identity will fail to close.

Question
Referred to the origin \(O\), points \(A\), \(B\), \(C\) have non-zero, non-parallel position vectors \(\mathbf{a}\), \(\mathbf{b}\), \(\mathbf{c}\) respectively. Point \(P\) divides \(AB\) internally in the ratio \(1 : 3\), and point \(Q\) divides \(AC\) internally in the ratio \(2 : 1\).

(i) Find \(\overrightarrow{OP}\) and \(\overrightarrow{OQ}\) in terms of \(\mathbf{a}\), \(\mathbf{b}\), \(\mathbf{c}\). [2]
(ii) Show that \( \overrightarrow{PQ} = -\tfrac{5}{12}\mathbf{a} - \tfrac{1}{4}\mathbf{b} + \tfrac{2}{3}\mathbf{c} \). [3]
(iii) The lines \(BQ\) and \(CP\) intersect at \(R\). By expressing \(\overrightarrow{OR}\) in two different ways, find \(\overrightarrow{OR}\) in terms of \(\mathbf{a}\), \(\mathbf{b}\), \(\mathbf{c}\). [5]
Solution
Step 1 — (i)
A1 A1
By ratio theorem, with \(AP : PB = 1 : 3\): \[ \overrightarrow{OP} = \dfrac{3\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}}{4} \] With \(AQ : QC = 2 : 1\): \[ \overrightarrow{OQ} = \dfrac{\mathbf{a} + 2\mathbf{c}}{3} \]
Step 2 — (ii)
M1 M1 A1
\[ \overrightarrow{PQ} = \overrightarrow{OQ} - \overrightarrow{OP} = \dfrac{\mathbf{a} + 2\mathbf{c}}{3} - \dfrac{3\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}}{4} \] Common denominator 12: \[ = \dfrac{4\mathbf{a} + 8\mathbf{c}}{12} - \dfrac{9\mathbf{a} + 3\mathbf{b}}{12} = \dfrac{-5\mathbf{a} - 3\mathbf{b} + 8\mathbf{c}}{12} \] \[ \therefore \overrightarrow{PQ} = -\tfrac{5}{12}\mathbf{a} - \tfrac{1}{4}\mathbf{b} + \tfrac{2}{3}\mathbf{c} \text{ (shown)} \]
Step 3 — (iii) \(\overrightarrow{OR}\) two ways
M1 M1 M1 M1 A1
Since \(R\) lies on \(BQ\): \( \overrightarrow{OR} = \mathbf{b} + t(\overrightarrow{OQ} - \mathbf{b}) = (1-t)\mathbf{b} + t\!\left(\tfrac{\mathbf{a} + 2\mathbf{c}}{3}\right) = \tfrac{t}{3}\mathbf{a} + (1-t)\mathbf{b} + \tfrac{2t}{3}\mathbf{c} \) …(1)
Since \(R\) lies on \(CP\): \( \overrightarrow{OR} = \mathbf{c} + s(\overrightarrow{OP} - \mathbf{c}) = (1-s)\mathbf{c} + s\!\left(\tfrac{3\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}}{4}\right) = \tfrac{3s}{4}\mathbf{a} + \tfrac{s}{4}\mathbf{b} + (1-s)\mathbf{c} \) …(2)
Since \(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}\) are non-zero and non-parallel, by comparison: \[ \tfrac{t}{3} = \tfrac{3s}{4} \quad (3), \qquad 1 - t = \tfrac{s}{4} \quad (4), \qquad \tfrac{2t}{3} = 1 - s \quad (5) \] From (3): \(t = \tfrac{9s}{4}\). Substitute into (4): \(1 - \tfrac{9s}{4} = \tfrac{s}{4} \;\Longrightarrow\; 1 = \tfrac{10s}{4} \;\Longrightarrow\; s = \tfrac{2}{5}\).

Trap: the check via the third equation is not optional — with three unknowns and three equations, if consistency fails, one of the algebraic steps is wrong. HCI Tutorial 7A Q3 formalises this as "substitute answers into remaining unused equations to check for consistency."

Question 13

Question
Referred to the origin \(O\), the points \(A\) and \(B\) have position vectors \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\), where \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\) are non-parallel unit vectors. Point \(N\) is the foot of the perpendicular from \(B\) to the line through \(O\) and \(A\).

(i) Show that \(\overrightarrow{ON} = (\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b})\mathbf{a}\). [3]
(ii) Deduce that the exact length of the projection of \(\overrightarrow{OB}\) onto the direction of \(\mathbf{a}\) is \(|\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}|\). [1]
(iii) Hence, find the exact perpendicular distance from \(B\) to the line \(OA\) in terms of \(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}\). [3]
(iv) Given further that the angle between \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\) is \(60^{\circ}\), find the exact perpendicular distance from part (iii). [2]
Solution
Step 1 — (i)
M1 M1 A1
\(N\) lies on the line through \(O\) and \(A\), so \(\overrightarrow{ON} = \lambda \mathbf{a}\) for some \(\lambda \in \mathbb{R}\).
\(BN \perp OA\), so \(\overrightarrow{NB} \cdot \mathbf{a} = 0\): \[ (\mathbf{b} - \lambda\mathbf{a}) \cdot \mathbf{a} = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} - \lambda(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{a}) = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \lambda = \dfrac{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}}{|\mathbf{a}|^{2}} = \mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} \] (Since \(\mathbf{a}\) is a unit vector, \(|\mathbf{a}|^{2} = 1\).) \[ \therefore \overrightarrow{ON} = (\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b})\mathbf{a} \text{ (shown)} \]
Step 2 — (ii)
B1
The projection length \( = |\overrightarrow{ON}| = |(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b})\mathbf{a}| = |\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}| \cdot |\mathbf{a}| = |\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}| \).
Step 3 — (iii) Perpendicular distance
M1 M1 A1
The perpendicular distance is \(|\overrightarrow{NB}|\). Since \(ONB\) is right-angled at \(N\): \[ |\overrightarrow{OB}|^{2} = |\overrightarrow{ON}|^{2} + |\overrightarrow{NB}|^{2} \] \[ |\overrightarrow{NB}|^{2} = |\mathbf{b}|^{2} - (\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b})^{2} = 1 - (\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b})^{2} \] \[ \therefore |\overrightarrow{NB}| = \sqrt{1 - (\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b})^{2}} \]
Step 4 — (iv)
M1 A1
\( \mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} = (1)(1)\cos 60^{\circ} = \tfrac{1}{2} \). \[ |\overrightarrow{NB}| = \sqrt{1 - \tfrac{1}{4}} = \sqrt{\tfrac{3}{4}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \text{ units} \]
Trap (T1): \(\overrightarrow{ON}\) is the vector projection of \(\mathbf{b}\) onto \(\mathbf{a}\) — its magnitude is the scalar projection \(|\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}|\). Writing \(\overrightarrow{ON} = \mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}\) alone is a category error (scalar ≠ vector).

Closing notes

The four highest-EV rules from this cluster, in mark-loss order:

  1. Ratio-theorem weight rule — the weight goes with the OPPOSITE endpoint. Rewrite the formula in the working every time until it's automatic.
  2. Collinearity is proved by parallel displacement vectors, not by magnitude ratios. \(\overrightarrow{AB} = k \overrightarrow{AC}\).
  3. Scalar projection vs vector projection vs perpendicular-component — three distinct quantities. Read the question wording carefully.
  4. Two-different-expressions method for intersections. Always state "since \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\) are non-parallel, by comparison…" before equating coefficients.
