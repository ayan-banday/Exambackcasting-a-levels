## The question he called his best, and got right the hard way

**A single base substitution deep inside an intron abolishing both proteins.** He opened with
"missense mutation", which is wrong, then rebuilt the whole chain himself:

5' and 3' splice sites **flank the ends of the intron** → they allow **recognition and binding of
snRNPs by complementary base pairing** → which **recruits other proteins to form the spliceosome** →
which **excises the intron and ligates the flanking exons**. Destroy the splice site and the **intron
is retained in the mature mRNA**; because **intron length is usually not a multiple of three**, this
causes a **frameshift**, giving a truncated non-functional polypeptide.

*"Oh my god, that's actually quite smart. That's actually a very good question, bro."*

He also asked mid-answer whether this was out of scope, since it looked like a mutation question. It
is not: the mechanism being tested is splicing, which is LO (c) and (i), and the frameshift is the
consequence rather than the content. Worth telling him.
