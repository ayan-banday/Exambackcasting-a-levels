**Sitting being prepared for:** Nov 2026 (this cycle = 9-10 years old; LOWER priority than 2023-2025 but valuable for transition-era pattern coverage)

**Files processed (text-extracted):** ~120 PDFs across 14 JCs (2016) + 15 JCs (2017). 6 deep-reads + 22 skimmed papers. Image-scanned PDFs (HCI 2016, CJC 2016, IJC 2016, JJC 2016, TJC 2016, RVHS 2017) failed text extraction — see Gaps section.

## Paper Format — Locked (2016-2017 era)

| Paper | Duration | Total | Structure | Question count |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | 3 hours | 100 marks | Pure Math only | 11-13 questions (typ. ~12) |
| P2 Section A | (within 3h) | 40 marks | Pure Math | 4 questions |
| P2 Section B | (within 3h) | 60 marks | Probability & Statistics | 6-8 questions |

**Syllabus codes appearing on paper headers:**
- 2016: `9740/01` and `9740/02` (universally — last full 9740 year)
- 2017: Mix. HCI/NJC/MJC/ACJC/CJC/AJC/SAJC/TJC/PJC/YJC use `9758` (new). MI still uses `9740`. YJC ships BOTH 9740 AND 9758 versions side-by-side — a unique transition-year artifact.
- RI 2017: ships P1+P2 alongside FM (Further Math) P1+P2, distinct papers — RI was running both H2 Math and H2 FM concurrently.

**Standard instructions (verbatim block on every paper):**
- "Give non-exact numerical answers correct to 3 significant figures, or 1 decimal place in the case of angles in degrees, unless a different level of accuracy is specified in the question."
- "You are expected to use a [graphing/graphic/approved graphing] calculator." (slight variation in phrasing across JCs)
- "Unsupported answers from a [graphic/graphing] calculator are allowed unless a question specifically states otherwise."
- "Where unsupported answers from a graphing calculator are not allowed in a question, you are required to present the mathematical steps using mathematical notations and not calculator commands."
- 2016 papers reference **List of Formulae (MF15)**. 2017 papers reference **List of Formulae (MF26)**. (Modern: MF27.)

## SYNTHESIS — H2 Math 2016-2017 Examiner Patterns

### 1. The 9740 → 9758 Transition

**2017 was the official switchover year.** Key markers:

- **Header code changes:** Papers carrying `9740/0X` are old syllabus. Papers carrying `9758/0X` are new. 2017 has both.
- **MF15 → MF26:** 2016 papers reference MF15 in additional materials block. 2017 papers reference MF26.
- **YJC published BOTH versions** of every 2017 paper (9740 and 9758, separately) — strong evidence of internal calibration during the transition.
- **MI alone stayed on 9740 for 2017** — they were one of the slower-transitioning JCs.
- **Topic-level transition: minimal at exam level.** Content overlap is ~95%+. The 9758 syllabus dropped some early-9740 material (like classical normal-distribution-based finite population corrections in some treatments) but added almost nothing new at this stage. Structure of papers identical: 12 Q × 100 m × 3 hr × P1-pure / P2-A-pure-B-stats.
- **Notation change visible in 2017:** 9758 papers use `MF26` formulae numbering. The List-of-Formulae now contains the same content as before but renumbered.
- **Cover-page mark allocation tables (ACJC, HCI, NJC) introduced in 2016 and standardised in 2017** — a quality-control pattern. HCI 2017 uniquely shows the FULL marks table per question on the cover.
- **No content changes in question style.** The questions in 2017 (9758) are stylistically indistinguishable from late 9740. The "spirit" of the syllabus shift was technical/curricular, not exam-aesthetic.

### 3. Common question stems + frequency (2016-17 era)

**Recurring stems that recur 5+ times across the 28 papers scanned:**

1. **"Find the equation of..."** + tangent / normal / curve / regression line — universal across all 12-question P1 papers
2. **"Use the substitution..."** — appears in integration (especially trig substitution like x = sin θ, x = sec θ, u = tan x), substitution to reduce DE (u = y/x), and induction setup. ~90% of P1s and P2-Section-A.
3. **"By the method of mathematical induction, prove that..."** — every paper, exactly 1 Q
4. **"Sketch ..., labelling clearly..."** — every paper, at least 1 Q
5. **"State an assumption..."** — emerging stem, very common in 2017 P2 stats Qs (Binomial, Poisson, Normal). Cambridge calibration since 2017.
6. **"State, in the context of this question, ..."** — increasingly common in 2017 papers vs 2016. Cambridge moves towards real-world contextualisation.
7. **"Hence" / "Hence or otherwise"** — Cambridge load-bearing word. Appears 4-7 times per paper. Marks the chain dependency in Cambridge questions.
8. **"Explain why..."** — 2-3 per P2. Used heavily in stats reasoning Qs about validity, distributional assumptions, model choice.
9. **"Show that..."** — used for partial proofs, identity derivations, intermediate results. 5-8 per paper.
10. **"Test, at the X% level of significance..."** — every hypothesis testing Q in P2 stats.
11. **"Real-world embedded scenarios"** — pervasive: financial loans, AP/GP investments, savings, business profits, durian masses, traffic queues, viewing angles, optical statues, hoists/pulleys, statue + pedestal, hotel guests, post offices, ice-cream sales, etc.

### 4. Notable 2016-17-specific question patterns

- **The "two-figure" optimisation problem (Fig 1 + Fig 2):** RI 2017 P1 Q7 (viewing angle), NYJC 2016 P1 Q7 (statue). Cambridge asks students to optimise in TWO different configurations of the same scenario.
- **The "show that…" sub-question chain:** ACJC 2016 P1 Q4 (g(x) Maclaurin), HCI 2017 P1 Q7 (Maclaurin). Cambridge breaks down a long Maclaurin derivation into Show-that scaffolds for partial credit.
- **The "find p, q, r, s integer constants" answer form:** NJC 2016 P1 Q11 (Σ formula proof), HCI 2017 P1 Q11 (hoist C: X + Y(0.98)^(n-1)). Answers must be exact integers identified from a derived expression.
- **Complex number "no calculator" preamble:** HCI 2017 P1 Q3, RI 2017 P1 Q9, NJC 2017 P1 Q5. Cambridge marks no-calc questions explicitly; entire question must be done by hand.
- **Real-world DE Qs with multiple model parts (a) (b):** RI 2017 P1 Q10 (forest fire — logistic + alternative model), NJC 2017 P1 Q10 (consumer-producer rice price). Cambridge embeds 2-3 layers of DE modelling in single question.
- **Self-inverse function questions:** NJC 2017 P2 Q2 (f²⁰⁰³(5) iteration). Tests whether student grasps f(f(x)) = x as the self-inverse property.
- **The "Sketch of expected scatter diagram" Q:** ACJC 2016 P2 Q10, RI 2017 P2 Q9. Students must produce TWO scatter shapes corresponding to TWO model forms before being given actual data.
- **Locus + arg combination:** ACJC 2016 P1 Q12, NJC 2016 P2 Q4. Two complex-locus conditions intersect → find z.
