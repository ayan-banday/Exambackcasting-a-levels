## Paper Format (UNIVERSAL across all 19 JCs, both years)

### Paper 1 (Pure Mathematics only)
- Duration: 3 hours
- Total marks: 100
- 10–12 questions, no sections
- Mark range per question: 4 to 13 marks
- No formal sections; questions ordered roughly by topic/difficulty
- MF26 formula list provided
- Graphing calculator expected

### Paper 2 (Pure A + Stats B)
- Duration: 3 hours
- Total marks: 100
- **Section A: Pure Mathematics [40 marks]** — typically Q1–4
- **Section B: Probability and Statistics [60 marks]** — typically Q5–10 or Q5–11
- 9–11 questions total
- Mark range per question: 4 to 13 marks

### Cambridge instruction boilerplate (verbatim, every JC)
- "Give non-exact numerical answers correct to 3 significant figures, or 1 decimal place in the case of angles in degrees, unless a different level of accuracy is specified in the question."
- "The use of an approved graphing calculator is expected, where appropriate."
- "Unsupported answers from a graphing calculator are allowed unless a question specifically states otherwise."
- "Where unsupported answers from a graphing calculator are not allowed in a question, you are required to present the mathematical steps using mathematical notations and not calculator commands."
- "You are reminded of the need for clear presentation in your answers."

### Cambridge command word usage (verbatim, all confirmed multiple JCs)

- **State** (1–2 marks): one-word or one-line answer expected
- **Find** (3+ marks): full working required
- **Show that** (mark + 1 to 2): result given, working must lead to it cleanly
- **Hence / Hence or otherwise**: use previous part's result (penalty if doesn't)
- **Deduce**: short logical step from a known result
- **Verify that**: substitute and confirm (1 mark typically)
- **Explain why** (1–2 marks): qualitative reasoning
- **Comment on**: usually about reliability of estimate, or context interpretation
- **Sketch**: include all stated features (asymptotes, stationary pts, intercepts)
- **Describe a sequence of transformations**: 2–4 marks; expects order matters
- **Justify your answer** (1–2 marks): mathematical or contextual justification

### Calibration value for Ethan's 2026 paper generation

**The 9758 syllabus has not changed since 2017**, so 2018–2019 papers are highly authoritative on:

1. **Paper architecture is fixed** — every paper generation should use the universal P1 (no sections, 100m, ~11 questions) and P2 (Section A 40m + Section B 60m) format.
2. **Mark allocation conventions** are stable. A "show that…" yields 2–4 marks. A Maclaurin to x³ yields 5–7. A full DE (solve + sketch + interpret) yields 10–13.
3. **Real-world context wrapping is essential** — generic "solve this DE" or "find P(X<a)" questions are below standard. Every applied question is dressed in plausible real-world context (drone navigation, drug dosing, traffic light delays, e-scooter speeds).
4. **Mark scheme cues that reward marks**:
   - Stating distributions used (e.g., "X~N(μ,σ²)") explicitly
   - Stating assumptions (e.g., "independence of …", "X normally distributed")
   - Defining null and alternative hypotheses with population parameter symbol explicitly defined
   - Comparing p-value vs significance level OR test statistic vs critical value with explicit conclusion
   - Stating "Conclude there is sufficient/insufficient evidence at the 5% level that…"
5. **Topic clusters that almost always co-occur in single questions**:
   - Maclaurin + binomial expansion (matching coefficients)
   - Vectors plane + line + foot of perpendicular + reflection
   - Parametric curve + tangent + area or volume
   - DE + sketch solution + interpret behavior as t→∞
   - Sampling + hypothesis test + meaning of significance level
   - Regression + PMCC + model selection (always 2+ models compared)
6. **Non-calculator instruction (`Do not use a calculator in answering this question`)** appears in 18/19 JCs, always paired with complex number questions and sometimes with inequality questions. Generated papers should include 1 such instruction.
7. **Recurring exam traps** (test design tips):
   - "Verify that A lies on plane p" before doing the main calculation (1-mark warm-up)
   - "Hence" stems requiring previous part's result (penalize if not used)
   - "State an assumption needed for…" (1–2 free marks if you remember the formula assumption)
   - Inequality + "Hence solve" with exponential or trig substitution (4+2 mark split)
   - DRV "Find the probability that the last throw is the kth success" (negative binomial concept disguised)
