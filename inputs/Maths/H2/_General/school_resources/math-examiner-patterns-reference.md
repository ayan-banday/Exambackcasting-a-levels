## 1. 9758 H2 Math Paper Format (Ethan's 2026 sitting)

- **P1: Pure Math only, 100m, 3h**, ~10-13 questions (11-12 typical), no sections, mark range per Q 3-15
- **P2: Section A Pure 40m + Section B Stats 60m, 100m, 3h**
  - Section A: 4-5 questions, ~8-12 marks each
  - Section B: 6-8 questions, 5-14 marks each
- **Total: 200m across both papers**
- Last question on each paper traditionally a 12-15m applied/synthesis question
- MF27 (current) is the formula list. MF26 pre-2024. MF15 in 9740 (legacy)
- Standard rubric (verbatim, on every paper, copy exactly into generated papers):
  - "Give non-exact numerical answers correct to 3 significant figures, or 1 decimal place in the case of angles in degrees, unless a different level of accuracy is specified in the question."
  - "You are expected to use an approved graphing calculator."
  - "Unsupported answers from a graphing calculator are allowed unless a question specifically states otherwise."
  - "Where unsupported answers from a graphing calculator are not allowed in a question, you must present the mathematical steps using mathematical notations and not calculator commands."
  - "You must show all necessary working clearly."
  - "You are reminded of the need for clear presentation in your answers."

## 3. 9740 → 9758 Syllabus Transition (2017)

### Switchover year markers (per 2016-2017 sub-reference)
- 2016 = full 9740. 2017 = official switchover. YJC 2017 shipped BOTH versions side-by-side.
- MI alone stayed on 9740 for 2017 (slow transition).
- Code visible on cover: `9740/0X` vs `9758/0X`.
- Formula list: MF15 (pre-2017) → MF26 (2017-2023) → MF27 (2024-current).

### Removed from 9758
- **Poisson distribution** (was in every 9740 P2)
- **Geometric distribution / negative binomial** (formal treatment removed)
- **t-test** (moved to FM 9649 only)
- **Sign test / Wilcoxon and other non-parametric tests** (removed)
- **Confidence intervals / point and interval estimation** (removed)
- **Second-order DEs** (e.g. d²y/dx² = ay + b, RLC circuits) (removed from 9758; 9758 has first-order only)
- **Polar coordinates** (was occasionally in 9740; removed)
- Pre-2017 questions on these are out-of-syllabus — flag as practice-only with caveat

### Added/emphasized in 9758
- Sampling distribution + CLT applied more explicitly
- DRV with given probability function (2017 Q5, 2018 Q8, 2020 Q5 Cambridge actual)
- Practical hypothesis testing (z-test only; one-tail/two-tail discussion + critical region in terms of n)
- Conditional probability with Bayes-style reasoning (2017 quick test, 2018 P2 Q6 bug-in-maze)
- Regression with non-linear transformations (y on 1/x, y on √x, y on x², y on ln x)
- Permutations with multi-case restrictions (2017 Q6, 2022 Q7 Cambridge actual)

### Style changes
- Longer applied questions in 9758 (contextual: Snell's law, rugby kick, terminal velocity, drone navigation, ice freezing)
- More integrated multi-topic questions per question (vectors+DE, complex+trig)
- "Show that" steps required in vectors more explicitly
- Explicit "Do not use a calculator" instructions more prominent
- Real-world dressing more standardised

### Practical impact
- ~15-20% of pre-2017 prelim questions are now off-syllabus (Poisson + DE removed bulk)
- Pure topics ~95% overlap: Functions, Vectors, Sequences, Maclaurin, Integration, Parametric, Curve sketching, Inequalities all transfer 1:1
- Stats ~80% overlap: kept Binomial, Normal, Sampling, Hypothesis testing (z-only), Correlation/regression, PnC, Probability

## 5. Locked-In Question Architecture Patterns

Synthesized from cross-references:

- **Every P1 ends with vectors (12-14m) or applied closer (12-15m)** — either 3D vectors with real-world dressing OR a sequence/series real-world AP/GP recurrence, OR a long DE modelling question, OR a complex multi-stage optimisation
- **Every P2 Section A includes Maclaurin (mandatory or near-mandatory)** + one Vectors Q + one Complex OR Parametric OR Integration Q + sometimes a DE Q (alternates with P1 DE)
- **Every P2 Section B includes Normal + Hypothesis Test as the longest questions (10-14m each)** — Normal Q tests single + linear combination + sample mean + CLT; Hypothesis Test Q tests 1-tail/2-tail + critical region + meaning + assumptions
- **Every paper has at least one no-calc complex number question** — explicit instruction "Do not use a calculator"
- **Multi-part question depth norms:** 5-7 sub-parts for 12-15m closers; 3-5 sub-parts for mid-marks Qs (6-10m); 1-2 sub-parts for openers (3-5m)
- **"Show that" scaffolding:** every question 8+ marks has at least one "Show that" step (forces intermediate result rigour)
- **Final sub-part rule:** final sub-part is highest mark in the question and requires synthesis across prior parts
- **"Hence" chains:** Hence parts force use of previous result; "Hence or otherwise" allows alternative methods

### P1 most likely structure (12 questions, 100m, in approximate order):
1. Opener: inequality (4-5m) OR simple curve/derivative (4-5m) OR linear system (3-4m)
2. Functions / composite / inverse (6-8m)
3. Vectors (2D) or graph transformations (5-9m)
4. Sequences/series — sigma OR AP/GP (6-9m)
5. Curve sketching with parametric/rational (7-9m)
6. Differentiation: related rates OR optimisation (8-10m)
7. Complex numbers (no calculator) (8-12m)
8. Integration (techniques) (8-10m)
9. Differential equations (8-12m)
10. Vectors (3D, long, real-world) (12-14m)
11. Sequences/series (real-world AP/GP/recurrence) (12-13m) — alternates with closer

### P2 most likely structure:
**Section A (40m, 4-5 Qs):**
1. Maclaurin series (8-10m)
2. Vectors or complex numbers (7-10m)
3. Differential equation OR parametric (8-12m)
4. Integration application OR proof (8-10m)

**Section B (60m, 6-8 Qs):**
5. P&C — anagrams / arrangements with restrictions (5-7m)
6. Probability — conditional / independence / mutually exclusive (5-7m)
7. Discrete RV (E, Var) (6-8m)
8. Binomial distribution + assumptions (5-8m)
9. Linear regression — linearise + scatter + reliability (8-12m)
10. Normal distribution — sample mean + CLT + comparison (10-14m)
11. Hypothesis testing — meaning, critical region, multi-step (10-14m)

## 6. Cambridge Actual vs Prelim Style

### Cambridge actual papers (HIGH AUTHORITY ground truth) tend to:
- Use cleaner contextual setups (ice freezing, Snell's law, rugby kick angle, terminal velocity, drone navigation)
- Specify exactly what form the answer should take ("in the form p{(x+q)³+r}", "where a, b, c are integers")
- Ask multi-stage applied questions (especially in 9758): 2019 Q12 Snell's law, 2020 Q11 rugby kick
- Have shorter sub-parts (2-4 marks each) but more of them
- Use "Hence" deliberately to force linking parts
- Test interpretation and judgment more (especially in Stats: "Comment on the suitability", "Explain why…")
- More integrated multi-topic questions

### Prelim papers (JC mocks) tend to:
- Have longer sub-parts with more marks each (5-8 mark sub-parts common)
- Be more algebraically heavy without context
- Include more "trap" questions designed to catch syllabus subtleties
- Use more obscure substitution choices in integration
- Have somewhat random / disjointed topic blends (less integrated)
- Test recognition of standard MF26/27 forms
- May include questions slightly beyond core syllabus (vectors, complex loci)

### For paper-generation
- Lean toward Cambridge actual style for **authenticity** (clean context, shorter sub-parts, multi-stage)
- Use prelim style for **difficulty calibration** (heavier algebra, longer sub-parts)
- For 2026 prep specifically: use 2023-2025 prelim style for current cohort calibration; 2017-2022 Cambridge actual for syllabus-faithful examples

### Patterns to discount as "school style" (avoid)
- Random multi-topic blends (complex + vectors + integration all in one Q)
- Questions requiring obscure factor formula manipulations beyond MF26/27 standard
- Heavy reliance on "by GC" for non-trivial polynomial roots
- Questions where algebra is the main challenge rather than the concept

## 7. Diagram Conventions

- **3D vectors:** real-world model diagrams with O marked + i, j, k unit vectors labelled relative to a reference plane. Real-world dressings: canopy, gallery, prism, pyramid, room model, sculpture, drone, helicopter
- **Argand diagrams:** Re/Im axes labelled, O origin marked. When points are plotted, capital letters (P, Q, R, A, B, W₁) used. Provide empty grid in answer booklet; student plots
- **Graph sketches:** ask for shape, asymptotes (dashed), turning points (coordinates), axial intercepts (coordinates), labelled axes y/x with O at origin. Specified points (P, Q) labelled
- **Distribution diagrams:** bell curve sketches for normal; rare for tree/Venn (usually scenarios described algebraically)
- **Statistics scatter:** labelled axes, all data points marked. Optionally one point P labelled as "anomalous" for student to omit. Do NOT pre-plot — student plots; provide grid only
- **Parametric curves:** show direction of increase of parameter, key points, asymptotes if any
- **Periodic functions:** show one full period plus extra each side, with period boundary markings
- Marks: shape (1m), asymptotes (1m), turning points (1m), intercepts (1m) — full sketch can yield 4 marks

## 10. H3 Difficulty Markers (for advanced practice)

From older-h3-ib sub-reference: how Cambridge constructs hard questions. Transferable to H2 advanced sub-questions (the 1-2 questions designed to separate A from A*). H3 itself NOT in Ethan's schedule — do not attempt full H3 Qs.

### Difficulty markers transferable to H2 final sub-parts:
1. **Multi-step proof chain with bridging insight** — sub-part (i) gives identity → (ii) uses identity → (iii) builds on (ii); missing a step breaks chain
2. **Unfamiliar context wrapping a familiar technique** — H3 2020 RI Q2 substitution x = 1/t looks unfamiliar but reduces to standard substitution
3. **Synthesis across topics** — combines DE + Maclaurin + bifurcation analysis in one Q
4. **Reverse direction of standard reasoning** — given regression lines, deduce inequality (rather than derive line)
5. **Existence/uniqueness arguments** — show solution to y' = f(y) unique using Lipschitz
6. **Combinatorial bijections** — arrangements with derangement constraints
7. **Modular arithmetic / number theory** — Wilson's theorem, Fermat's little theorem (rare in H2 but transferable thinking)
8. **Inequality bounding with creative function choice** — AM-GM on five "1"s + x^6
9. **Recurrence to closed form via substitution or generating function** — coin scoring p_n = (1/3)p_{n-1} + (2/3)p_{n-2}
10. **Pigeonhole on unexpected partition**

### Implication for H2 prep
Last sub-part of any 12-mark Q in H2 may use techniques 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, or 9. Practice the bridging skill: when stuck on a final sub-part, ask "what did the previous part give me?" — Cambridge always plants a key earlier.

## 13. Calibration Rules for Paper Generation (most important section)

### Hard rules (always)
- **P1 topic balance:** must include vectors closer (12-14m) + DE (8-12m) + complex (8-12m) + at least 1 inequality opener
- **P2 stats:** must include normal (10-14m) + hypothesis test (10-14m) as the longest Section B Qs
- **P2 Section A:** must include Maclaurin + Vectors (these two are near-mandatory)
- **Mark allocation per stem type:**
  - "Show that" steps: 2-4m each (1 mark per logical step)
  - Sketches: 2-3m (1 for shape, 1 for asymptotes/turning points, 1 for intercepts)
  - "Hence find" follow-ups: 1-3m
  - Multi-part complex/vectors: 8-14m total
  - Hypothesis test core: 4-6m + 2m for assumptions/critical region/meaning
  - Long DE: 6-10m for setup + solve + interpret
  - Long linear regression: scatter (1m) + r-value (1m) + model choice + reliability + linear transform
- **Sub-question depth norms:** no question >7 sub-parts; total marks 8-15 for long closers; openers 3-5m single-part
- **Final sub-part rule:** highest-mark sub-part requires synthesis across prior parts
- **Stem phrasing pool — use verbatim Cambridge-style:**
  - Always "Show that" when downstream part depends on result
  - Always end variable graphs with: "stating the equations of any asymptotes, the coordinates of any stationary points, and the points where the graph crosses the axes."
  - Always end DE Qs with: "find the general/particular solution for y in terms of x" or "solve the differential equation"
  - Always preface hypothesis testing with: "Test, at the X% significance level, whether..."
  - Always preface stats with: "In this question you should state the parameters of any distributions you use."
  - Always end optimisation with: "(You need not show that your answer gives a maximum/minimum.)" when context makes it obvious
  - Always end inequalities with "Hence ..." OR "find the set of values of x..."
  - Use "in the context of the question" for stats interpretation Qs
- **Diagram standards:**
  - 3D vectors: labelled diagram with O origin and named points + axes labelled with i, j, k
  - Argand diagrams: grid with O marked; student plots
  - Statistics scatter: grid only; student plots
  - Piecewise/periodic: function definition stated; student sketches
- **"Without calculator" reserved for** complex numbers + inequalities + polynomial factorisation (1-2 per paper)

### Avoid (out of 9758 syllabus)
- **Poisson distribution** — removed from 9758 (was every 9740 P2)
- **Geometric / negative binomial distribution** — removed (still mentioned in 9740 contexts)
- **t-test** — moved to FM only; H2 9758 uses z-test only
- **Sign test / non-parametric tests** — removed
- **Confidence intervals** — removed
- **Second-order DEs** — removed (9758 has first-order DEs only)
- **Polar coordinates** — was rare in 9740; not in 9758
- Complex roots of unity beyond simple polar form

### Recurring exam traps (test design tips)
- "Verify that A lies on plane p" before doing the main calculation (1-mark warm-up baits the student into using the wrong vector form)
- "Hence" stems requiring previous part's result — penalty if student does it independently
- "State an assumption needed for…" — 1-2 free marks if formula assumption remembered
- Inequality + "Hence solve" with exponential or trig substitution (4+2 mark split)
- DRV "Find the probability that the last throw is the kth success" — negative binomial concept disguised
- Real-world DE Qs with multiple model parts (a)/(b) — different DE for same data
- Self-inverse function questions f(f(x)) = x — tests whether student notices f⁻¹ = f
- "Find p, q, r, s integer constants" — answers must be exact integers identified from a derived expression
