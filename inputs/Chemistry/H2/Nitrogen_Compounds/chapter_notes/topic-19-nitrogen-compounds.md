## UNIT 1 — Introduction & basicity (naming, bp/solubility, the basicity spine)

### Definitions & classification
- **Amine = NH₃ derivative**, one or more H replaced by alkyl/aryl. **Classify by the number of C atoms directly attached to N:** **1°** (RNH₂), **2°** (R₂NH), **3°** (R₃N), **quaternary ammonium ion** (R₄N⁺). **Phenylamine = N attached directly to a benzene ring** (C₆H₅NH₂).
- **Amide = carboxylic acid derivative, RCONH₂** (1°) or N-substituted. **Amino acid = both –CO₂H and –NH₂ on the same molecule.**

### Naming (LO 11.1a)
- **Amines:** suffix **-amine** on the longest alkyl chain (ethylamine), or replace alkane's -e with -amine; **N-** locates substituents on N (N-methylethylamine, N,N-dimethylphenylamine). If a higher-priority group is present, –NH₂ is the prefix **amino-**.
- **Amides:** replace acid's **-oic acid with -amide** (ethanamide); **N-** for substituents on N.

### Physical properties — H-bonding (but weaker than alcohols)
- **bp: 1° & 2° amines H-bond** (N–H polar) → higher bp; **3° amines have no N–H → no H-bonding → only pd–pd → lower bp** than 1°/2° of the same Mr. bp rises with chain length (dispersion).
- **Amine bp < alcohol of similar electron count** (e.g. methylamine −6 < methanol 65) because **N–H is less polar than O–H** (N less electronegative than O) → amine H-bonds are weaker. (But amine bp ≫ alkane — methylamine −6 ≫ ethane −89 — because H-bonding > dispersion.)
- **Amides: extensive H-bonding (polar N–H) → high mp/bp; mostly white crystalline solids** (methanamide is liquid).
- **Solubility:** aliphatic amines with **≤4 C are water-soluble** (H-bond with water); **phenylamine and longer-chain amines are virtually insoluble** (non-polar skeleton; dispersion outweighs the H-bonding gained) but dissolve in organic solvents. **Simple amides very soluble** (extensive H-bonding with water).

### Basicity (LO 11.9c, d, g) ★★ — the conceptual spine
Amines have an **N lone pair → can accept a proton (Brønsted base) / donate the pair to an acid (Lewis base).** **Basicity = availability of the N lone pair.**

**(a) Aliphatic amines in the GAS phase (LO 11.9c) — interpret as Lewis bases:**
- **Alkyl groups are electron-donating → increase electron density on N → make the lone pair more available.** So gas-phase basicity: **3° > 2° > 1° > NH₃** (more alkyl groups = more basic).

**(b) Aliphatic amines in AQUEOUS medium (background):** the order shifts because the **conjugate acid (R₃NH⁺) must be stabilised by solvation (ion-dipole with water).** 3° cations have **three bulky alkyl groups blocking solvation → conjugate acid less stable → 3° amine ionises less than expected.** (So the neat gas-phase order doesn't hold in water.)

**(c) Phenylamine (LO 11.9d) — much weaker base than ammonia:**
- The **N lone pair is delocalised into the benzene ring → decreases electron density on N → lone pair less available to bond a proton.** → phenylamine is far less basic than ammonia.
- **Substituted phenylamines:** **EDG on the ring** (e.g. –CH₃) → ↑ring electron density → lone pair more available → **more basic** (4-methylphenylamine pKb 8.92). **EWG** (e.g. –NO₂) → ↓ → **less basic** (4-nitrophenylamine pKb 13.0). (Phenylamine pKb 9.40.)

**(d) Amides are NEUTRAL / non-basic (LO 11.9g):**
- The **N lone pair is delocalised into the adjacent C=O → electron density on N reduced → lone pair NOT available for protonation** → amides give neutral solutions. (This delocalisation also makes amides very stable → the **least reactive carboxylic-acid derivative.**)

**Overall aqueous basicity ladder (LO 11.9d): ethylamine > ammonia > phenylamine > amide**
(pKb: ethylamine 3.35 < ammonia 4.74 < phenylamine 9.40 < ethanamide 14.50; smaller pKb = stronger base). Argue: ethylamine's alkyl group donates (more available lone pair) > NH₃ > phenylamine (delocalised into ring) > amide (delocalised into C=O).

## UNIT 2 — Reactions of amines, phenylamines & amides

### Preparation (LO 11.9a, f)
- **Amines from:** nucleophilic substitution of halogenoalkanes (T15, NH₃/ethanol); **reduction of nitriles** (LiAlH₄ in dry ether, or H₂/Ni — T15/T17); **reduction of amides** (LiAlH₄).
- **Phenylamine from reduction of nitrobenzene:** **Sn + concentrated HCl, heat** (forms the salt C₆H₅NH₃⁺Cl⁻), **then NaOH(aq)** to liberate free phenylamine. *(LiAlH₄/NaBH₄ NOT used: NaBH₄ doesn't reduce –NO₂ at all; LiAlH₄ gives azo compounds C₆H₅N=NC₆H₅ instead.)* Aliphatic nitro compounds aren't reduced by this.
- **Amides from:** **condensation of acyl chlorides** with NH₃/amines (T18). **Carboxylic acids can't be used** (just acid-base → ammonium carboxylate).

### Reactions of amines (lone-pair-driven: base, nucleophile, ligand)
- **With acids → salts (LO 11.9b):** amine (aliphatic or phenylamine) + mineral/carboxylic acid → **ionic ammonium salt** (soluble in water via ion-dipole; insoluble in organic solvents). **Use:** convert a water-insoluble amine to a soluble salt to **separate it from other organics**; regenerate the free amine with **NaOH(aq)** (stronger base displaces weaker).
- **Nucleophilic substitution (LO):** amine + halogenoalkane → next-higher amine (1°→2°→3°→quaternary salt) — T15.
- **Condensation (LO):** **1° and 2° amines (aliphatic OR phenylamine) + acyl chloride → amide** (T18).
- **Electrophilic substitution of phenylamine (LO 11.9e) ★** — like phenol: the **N lone pair delocalises into the ring → highly activates it (no catalyst needed)**, 2,4-directing. **Phenylamine + aqueous bromine, rt → white ppt of 2,4,6-tribromophenylamine** (yellow-orange Br₂ decolourised; HBr fumes may not show — dissolves in water). **No Lewis-acid catalyst** (contrast benzene, which needs AlBr₃/FeBr₃). **Aliphatic amines don't react with bromine.**

### Reactions of amides (LO 11.9h) — least reactive derivative
- **Amides do NOT act as nucleophiles** (N lone pair tied up in C=O) and are **neutral.**
- **Reduction → amine:** **LiAlH₄ in dry ether** (`RCONH₂ → RCH₂NH₂`).
- **Hydrolysis (acid or base), heat under reflux** — same bond cleaved, products depend on pH:
  - **Acid hydrolysis (dil H₂SO₄, reflux) → carboxylic acid + ammonium salt** (NH₄⁺ from a 1° amide, or amine salt from substituted).
  - **Base hydrolysis (dil NaOH, reflux, then acidify) → carboxylate salt + NH₃** (1° amide) or **amine** (substituted). **NH₃ evolved on alkaline hydrolysis of a 1° amide is a distinguishing test.**

## UNIT 3 — Amino acids & proteins

### Structure & acid-base properties (LO 11.9i)
- **α-amino acid:** both –NH₂ and –CO₂H on the **same (α) carbon.** General `H₂N–CHR–CO₂H`; 20 naturally-occurring, differing only in the side chain R; the simplest are **chiral → optically active** (except glycine, R=H). *(β-amino acids like GABA exist but aren't the protein monomers.)*
- **Amphoteric:** the –CO₂H (acidic) and –NH₂ (basic) groups react with both acids and bases, and can undergo an **intramolecular acid-base reaction.**

### Zwitterion & predominant species vs pH ★
- **Zwitterion = a dipolar ion** (–CO₂⁻ and –NH₃⁺ on the same molecule) with **no overall net charge** — the predominant species at intermediate pH (the **isoelectric point, pI**).
- **At low pH (excess H⁺): –CO₂H protonated → cation** (–NH₃⁺, –CO₂H). **At high pH (excess OH⁻): –NH₃⁺ deprotonated → anion** (–NH₂, –CO₂⁻). **At pI: zwitterion.**
- **Predicting the species at a given pH:** assign each pKa to its group; **a group with pKa < solution pH is deprotonated (–CO₂⁻, –NH₂); pKa > pH is protonated (–CO₂H, –NH₃⁺).** For acidic side chains, the –CO₂H *nearer the electron-withdrawing –NH₃⁺* is the stronger acid (lower pKa). pI: neutral side chain ≈6, basic (lysine) higher ≈9.7, acidic (aspartic) lower ≈2.8.

### Physical properties (mp & solubility) — ionic, because of the zwitterion ★
- Amino acids exist as **zwitterions in the solid and in solution**, so they behave like **ionic compounds:**
  - **High melting points** (e.g. glycine 262 °C) — **strong electrostatic/ionic forces between oppositely-charged ions** in the lattice; lots of energy to overcome.
  - **More soluble in water than in organic solvents** — **strong ion-dipole interactions** between the zwitterion's ions and water.

### Peptide bonds & proteins (LO 11.10c) ★
- **Proteins = condensation polymers of α-amino acid monomers, linked by peptide (amide) bonds.**
- A **peptide bond is an amide bond formed by condensation between the –CO₂H of one α-amino acid and the –NH₂ of another (on their α-carbons, NOT the side chains), eliminating H₂O.** Dipeptide = 2 amino acids/1 bond; tripeptide = 3/2 bonds; **polypeptide** ≤ ~50 residues; **protein** = larger (Mr > ~5000). *(In the lab, acid + amine would just form a salt; living organisms use enzymes to make peptide bonds.)*

### Hydrolysis of proteins/peptides (LO 11.10d) ★
- **Heat under reflux with aqueous acid OR aqueous alkali (for several hours)** — breaks the peptide bonds back into the **constituent amino acids** (or their ions, depending on pH; e.g. **dilute H₂SO₄** → the amino acids in their **cationic** forms). An n-peptide has (n−1) peptide bonds → on full hydrolysis gives n amino acids and (n−1) H₂O consumed.

## TOP EXAM TRAPS (Topic 19)
- **Basicity = lone-pair availability.** EDG/↑e⁻ density on N → stronger base; delocalisation into a ring (phenylamine) or C=O (amide) → weaker. Always phrase it this way.
- **Gas phase: 3°>2°>1°>NH₃ (pure inductive). Aqueous: order shifts** — 3° amine's conjugate acid is poorly solvated (bulky alkyls block ion-dipole). Don't quote the gas-phase order for aqueous.
- **Aqueous ladder: ethylamine > ammonia > phenylamine > amide.** Amide is NEUTRAL (lone pair in C=O) — a common "is it basic?" trap.
- **Amine bp < alcohol** (N–H less polar than O–H → weaker H-bonds); **3° amines have no N–H → no H-bonding.**
- **Phenylamine reduction: Sn + conc HCl, heat, THEN NaOH** (not LiAlH₄/NaBH₄). Phenylamine + Br₂(aq) → **white ppt of 2,4,6-tribromophenylamine, NO catalyst** (activated ring) — mirror of phenol.
- **Amides made from acyl chloride, not the carboxylic acid** (acid just gives a salt). Amide hydrolysis: acid → carboxylic acid + ammonium/amine salt; base → carboxylate salt + NH₃/amine (**NH₃ evolved = test for a 1° amide**).
- **Zwitterion has NO net charge** (not "two charges that count"). Predominant at pI. Low pH → cation; high pH → anion.
- **Amino acids: high mp + water-soluble because they're zwitterionic (ionic)** — quote ionic bonding / ion-dipole, NOT covalent reasoning.
- **Peptide bond forms between the α-CO₂H and α-NH₂, NOT the side chains** — and it's an amide bond by condensation (−H₂O).
- An **n-peptide → n amino acids on hydrolysis** (n−1 bonds broken).
