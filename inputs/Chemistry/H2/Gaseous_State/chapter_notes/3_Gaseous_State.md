    ## 🔬 **Challenging Variations on Pressure and Gases**
    
    ### 🔹 **Question 1: Industrial Gas Cylinder**
    
    A 5.0 dm³ cylinder contains 4.4 kg of carbon dioxide at 298 K.
    
    (a) Calculate the pressure exerted using the ideal gas law.
    
    (b) The actual measured pressure is significantly lower than your result. Explain this deviation in terms of molecular interactions.
    
    (c) The cylinder is cooled to 273 K and the gas begins to liquefy. Explain why liquefaction occurs at lower temperatures even if volume is constant.
    
    ---
    
    ### 🔹 **Question 2: Hot Air Balloon System**
    
    A hot air balloon contains 200 m³ of air at 290 K and 1.00 × 10⁵ Pa.
    
    (a) Calculate the number of moles of air in the balloon.
    
    (b) If the air is heated to 330 K, find the new pressure assuming volume remains constant.
    
    (c) Explain the effect of temperature on pressure using kinetic molecular theory.
    
    ---
    
    ### 🔹 **Question 3: Spacecraft Cabin Leak**
    
    A spacecraft cabin has a pressure of 8.0 × 10⁴ Pa and a volume of 120 m³ at 293 K.
    
    (a) Calculate the number of moles of air present.
    
    (b) If a micrometeorite causes a slow leak and the volume drops to 115 m³ at constant temperature, calculate the new pressure.
    
    (c) Discuss why the drop in pressure affects the rate of oxygen diffusion into astronaut blood.
    
    ---
    
    ### 🔹 **Question 4: Liquefaction under Compression**
    
    A sample of a gas is compressed from 2.0 dm³ to 0.25 dm³ at constant temperature.
    
    (a) Use the ideal gas law to describe how pressure changes.
    
    (b) The gas begins to liquefy at high pressure. Explain why this behavior cannot be predicted by the ideal gas law.
    
    (c) Discuss the role of intermolecular forces in the liquefaction of real gases.
