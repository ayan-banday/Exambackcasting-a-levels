## Why it matters
Transition-element chemistry is the highest-yield inorganic topic in P2/P3: it stitches together electrochemistry (E°cell on every redox/catalysis question), kinetics (catalyst mode of action), equilibrium (ligand exchange Kc, Le Chatelier), acid-base (aqua complex hydrolysis) and QA observations. The examiner rewards **mechanistic chains explained with the right vocabulary**, not facts. Almost every part is "explain", "describe", "predict using E°", or "construct equation."

## Mark-scheme-first 核心 — what earns the marks
1. **Definition of transition element** must be quoted exactly: *a d-block element whose atom has an incomplete d subshell, OR which gives rise to at least one cation with an incomplete d subshell.* This wording is the marked answer for "why is Zn / Sc not a transition element."
2. **Electronic configs** — write 4s before 3d in the config but **remove 4s electrons FIRST** when forming ions. Cr (3d⁵4s¹) and Cu (3d¹⁰4s¹) exceptions are recurring 1-mark traps.
3. **Variable oxidation states** — "the 3d and 4s orbitals are *close in energy*, so a variable number of electrons can be lost." This phrase is the mark.
4. **Redox feasibility** — every redox/catalysis answer: quote E° half-equations from the Data Booklet, compute E°cell = E°(red) − E°(ox), state **E°cell > 0 ⟹ feasible.**
5. **Catalysis** — name the type, give the *reason rooted in transition-metal property* (heterogeneous: partially filled 3d orbitals adsorb reactants; homogeneous: variable oxidation states act as electron-transfer intermediary), and for homogeneous show **two steps, each with positive E°cell.**
6. **Colour** — the chain: ligands split degenerate d-orbitals into two levels (ΔE) → electron absorbs visible light → **d-d transition** → colour seen is the **complement of light absorbed.** No d electrons (3d⁰) or full d (3d¹⁰) ⟹ no d-d transition ⟹ colourless.

## UNIT 1 — Intro, Physical & Chemical Properties [LO 13(a)]

**Definition (quote it):** A transition element is a d-block element whose atom has an *incomplete d subshell*, or which gives rise to *cation(s) with an incomplete d subshell.*
- **Sc excluded?** Sc forms only Sc³⁺ = [Ar]3d⁰ (empty, not incomplete) → borderline; HCI treats Ti–Cu as the canonical transition metals.
- **Zn excluded:** Zn atom = 3d¹⁰4s², Zn²⁺ = 3d¹⁰ (full). No incomplete d subshell ⟹ **not a transition element.**

**Electronic configurations Sc→Zn** (fill 4s before 3d):
- Normal: Sc [Ar]3d¹4s² … but **Cr = [Ar]3d⁵4s¹** and **Cu = [Ar]3d¹⁰4s¹** (half-filled / fully-filled 3d extra stability).
- **Ion formation: lose 4s electrons before 3d.** e.g. Fe = 3d⁶4s² → Fe²⁺ = 3d⁶ (not 3d⁴4s²); Fe³⁺ = 3d⁵; Cu²⁺ = 3d⁹.

**Physical properties (vs s-block, e.g. Ca):**
- **Atomic radii & ionisation energies are relatively invariant** across the series — added electrons go into inner 3d, which screens the increasing nuclear charge, so effective nuclear charge stays roughly constant (recap T1).
- **Higher melting points** — both 3d AND 4s electrons are delocalised into the metallic bonding ⟹ more electrons per atom, stronger metallic bond.
- **Higher density** — smaller atomic radii + higher relative atomic mass.

**Four characteristic chemical properties:** (1) variable oxidation states, (2) catalytic behaviour, (3) form complex ions, (4) form coloured compounds/ions.

## UNIT 2 — Variable Oxidation States [LO 13(g), 13(h)]

**Why variable:** 3d and 4s orbitals are *close in energy*, so successive electrons can be removed with comparable, attainable energy → a range of oxidation states.
- **Max O.N.** = total of (3d + 4s) electrons available, e.g. Mn = 3d⁵4s² → max +7 (MnO₄⁻).
- **Trend in NUMBER of states:** rises Sc/Ti → **peaks at Mn**, then falls Fe → Cu. After 3d⁵ electrons begin pairing; paired d-electrons are harder to remove/involve, so fewer states for the later metals.
- **Lower states** → ionic, basic (e.g. Mn²⁺); **higher states** → covalent, found in oxoanions/acidic oxides (MnO₄⁻, CrO₄²⁻).

**Three named redox systems (memorise colours + half-eqns):**
| System | Half-equation | Colour change | E° / V |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ | Fe³⁺ + e⁻ ⇌ Fe²⁺ | yellow → pale green | +0.77 |
| MnO₄⁻/Mn²⁺ | MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ ⇌ Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O | purple → colourless | +1.52 |
| Cr₂O₇²⁻/Cr³⁺ | Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6e⁻ ⇌ 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O | orange → green | +1.33 |
- High O.N. species (Cr₂O₇²⁻, MnO₄⁻) = oxidising agents; low O.N. (Cr²⁺, V²⁺) = reducing agents.
- **Predicting feasibility:** combine half-equations, E°cell = E°(red) − E°(ox); **E°cell > 0 ⟹ reaction feasible.**

**Worked example (atmospheric oxidation):** green Fe(OH)₂ → brown Fe(OH)₃ on standing in air.
`O₂ + 2H₂O + 4Fe(OH)₂ → 4Fe(OH)₃`; E°cell = +0.40 − (−0.56) = **+0.96 V > 0** ⟹ spontaneous. (O₂ is the oxidising agent — feasible even though slow.)

## UNIT 3 — Catalytic Properties [LO 13(m)]

Catalyst = provides an alternative pathway of lower Ea; not consumed. Speeds forward AND reverse equally; does not change yield or ΔH.

**Heterogeneous (catalyst ≠ phase of reactants; usually solid catalyst, gas/liquid reactants):**
- **Reason transition metal works:** partially filled 3d orbitals can *accept electron pairs from / donate to* reactant molecules → adsorption onto active sites.
- **Mode of action (3 steps):** (1) **Adsorption** — reactants diffuse to surface, adsorb onto active sites via weak forces → raises surface concentration, weakens bonds within reactant molecules, orients them correctly → lowers Ea. (2) **Reaction** on surface. (3) **Desorption** — product leaves, frees active sites.
- **Named examples:** Fe (or Fe₂O₃) — Haber `N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃`; V₂O₅ — Contact `2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃`; Ni — hydrogenation of vegetable oils → margarine; TiCl₃/Al₂(C₂H₅)₆ Ziegler-Natta — poly(ethene).

**Homogeneous (catalyst same phase as reactants):**
- **Reason:** ease of interconversion between oxidation states lets the metal ion act as an **electron-transfer intermediary**; regenerated at the end.
- **Classic example: Fe³⁺ catalysing S₂O₈²⁻ + 2I⁻ → 2SO₄²⁻ + I₂.** Uncatalysed step has high Ea because both reactants are negatively charged (electrostatic repulsion). Fe³⁺ route:
  - Step 1: 2Fe³⁺ + 2I⁻ → 2Fe²⁺ + I₂, E°cell = +0.77 − 0.54 = **+0.23 V**
  - Step 2: 2Fe²⁺ + S₂O₈²⁻ → 2Fe³⁺ + 2SO₄²⁻, E°cell = +2.01 − 0.77 = **+1.24 V**
  - Both steps involve oppositely-charged ions ⟹ lower Ea. Fe³⁺ regenerated. **Both E°cell must be positive.**
- **Generalisation (exam favourite):** any M^(n+1)/M^(n) couple works as catalyst provided its E° lies **between +0.54 V and +2.01 V** so both steps are feasible. (Co²⁺/Co³⁺ is a stock exercise answer.)

## UNIT 4 — Transition Element Complexes [LO 13(i), 13(j)]

**Definitions (quote):**
- **Complex** = a molecule or ion formed by a central metal atom/ion surrounded by one or more ligands.
- **Ligand** = an ion or molecule with one or more lone pairs available to donate into vacant orbitals of the central metal, forming a **coordinate (dative) bond.**
- Net charge of complex = O.N. of central metal + sum of ligand charges.

**Why transition metals form complexes:** small, highly charged cations → high charge density / high polarising power → tendency to covalent (dative) bonding; plus vacant 3d/4s/4p orbitals to accept ligand lone pairs.

**Ligand denticity:** monodentate (1 donor atom, e.g. H₂O, NH₃, Cl⁻, CN⁻, CO), bidentate (2, e.g. ethanedioate C₂O₄²⁻, en), polydentate/**chelating** (e.g. EDTA⁴⁻ hexadentate, porphyrin tetradentate) — form stable rings, grip like a claw.

**Coordination number = number of dative bonds** (common 4 and 6; also 2 for Cu(I)/Ag(I)):
| Complex | C.N. | Geometry |
|---|---|---|
| [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ | 2 | linear |
| [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ | 4 | square planar |
| [CuCl₄]²⁻ | 4 | tetrahedral |
| [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻ | 6 | octahedral |
(C.N. 4 can be square planar OR tetrahedral; *which one is NOT in syllabus.*)

**Acidity of aqua complexes** (links to T14 salt hydrolysis): high-charge-density M³⁺ aqua ions hydrolyse → mildly acidic.
`[Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ + H₂O ⇌ [Fe(H₂O)₅(OH)]²⁺ + H₃O⁺` (similarly Cr³⁺, like Al³⁺). For O.N. > +3 polarising power is so great that oxoanions form instead — e.g. [Cr(H₂O)₆]⁶⁺ does not exist; Cr(VI) exists as CrO₄²⁻ / Cr₂O₇²⁻.

**Chromium complexes & oxoanions (amphoteric pattern):**
- Cr³⁺ + 3OH⁻ (dropwise) → grey-green Cr(OH)₃ ppt (acid-base, Cr³⁺ is acidic).
- + excess OH⁻ → dark green [Cr(OH)₆]³⁻ solution.
- CrO₄²⁻ (yellow) + 2H⁺ ⇌ Cr₂O₇²⁻ (orange) + H₂O — **O.N. unchanged (+6), NOT redox.**

### Ligand exchange [LO 13(j)] — readily occurs if new complex is more stable; usually a colour change; often reversible (Kc; [H₂O] omitted as solvent).
- **Fe³⁺ + SCN⁻:** `[Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ + SCN⁻ ⇌ [Fe(SCN)(H₂O)₅]²⁺ + H₂O` — yellow → **blood red** (large Kc, lies far right).
- **Cu²⁺ with NH₃ (QA staple):** dropwise NH₃ → pale blue Cu(OH)₂ ppt; excess NH₃ → ligand exchange → **dark blue [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺**. Add dilute H₂SO₄: removes NH₃, equilibria shift back → pale blue Cu(OH)₂ ppt reappears, then dissolves to pale blue solution (neutralisation).
  `[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4NH₃ ⇌ [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ + 4H₂O`
- **Cu²⁺ with conc HCl:** `[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CuCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O` — pale blue → **yellow**; green seen mid-way = mixture of blue aqua + yellow chloro complex.

### Effect of ligand exchange on E° (application)
Changing ligands changes reduction potential. Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺: with H₂O ligands E° = **+0.77 V** (so [Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ oxidises I⁻ → I₂, E°cell = +0.77−0.54 = +0.23 V > 0, dark brown). With CN⁻ ligands E° drops to **+0.36 V**, so [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻ **cannot** oxidise I⁻ (E°cell = +0.36−0.54 = −0.18 V < 0, not feasible).

### Ligand exchange in haemoglobin [LO 13(j)]
- Haemoglobin: Fe(II) at centre of a porphyrin ring (tetradentate, 4 N donors), a 5th N from histidine (globin), 6th site reversibly binds **O₂ → oxyhaemoglobin** → transports O₂.
- **CO replaces O₂** (ligand exchange) → **carboxyhaemoglobin**; binding is essentially irreversible, ~210× stronger affinity than O₂ → blocks O₂ transport → toxicity/death.

## UNIT 5 — Colours of Transition Element Complexes [LO 13(k), 13(l)]

**Light & colour:** white light = all visible wavelengths. Colour seen = **complement** of colour absorbed (colour wheel — green/red, blue/orange etc.). E ∝ 1/λ (red = long λ, low E; blue/violet = short λ, high E). Object absorbs some λ, transmits/reflects the rest.

**Crystal field theory:** ligand lone pairs modelled as point negative charges; they attract the central cation (stabilise complex) but **repel the d-orbital electrons.**

**d-orbital splitting in an octahedral field [LO 13(k)]:**
- Free ion: all five 3d orbitals **degenerate.**
- Ligands approach along x, y, z axes → repel all d-electrons (all rise in energy), but **unequally**: d(x²−y²) and d(z²) point *directly at* ligands → repelled more → higher energy; d(xy), d(yz), d(xz) point *between* ligands → lower energy.
- Result: two levels separated by energy gap **ΔE** (lower set of 3, upper set of 2).

**d-d transition & why coloured [LO 13(l)]:**
- An electron in the lower set absorbs a photon of visible light whose energy matches ΔE and is promoted to the upper set — a **d-d transition.**
- The colour observed = complement of the wavelengths absorbed.
- e.g. **[Ti(H₂O)₆]³⁺** (Ti³⁺ = 3d¹) absorbs green/yellow → transmits red/orange/blue/violet → looks **purple.**
- **Colourless ions:** Sc³⁺, Ti⁴⁺ (3d⁰) and Cu⁺, Zn²⁺ (3d¹⁰) — no partially filled d-orbitals ⟹ **no d-d transition possible** ⟹ colourless.

**Factors affecting ΔE / colour:**
1. **Oxidation state of metal** — different d-electron count → different ΔE → different colour. e.g. V³⁺(3d², green) vs V²⁺(3d³, violet).
2. **Nature of ligand** — different ligands split d-orbitals by different amounts (different ΔE → different colour). [Relative ligand field strength order is **NOT required.**]
3. **Coordination number / geometry** — different splitting pattern. (Only octahedral splitting need be recalled; other patterns may appear as application Qs.)

**Colour reference table (QA-critical):**
| Species | Colour |
|---|---|
| Cr³⁺(aq) | green |
| CrO₄²⁻ | yellow · Cr₂O₇²⁻ orange |
| Mn²⁺(aq) | faint pink / colourless if dilute |
| MnO₂ | dark brown ppt · MnO₄²⁻ dark green · MnO₄⁻ purple |
| Fe²⁺(aq) | pale green · Fe(OH)₂ green ppt |
| Fe³⁺(aq) | yellow · Fe(OH)₃ reddish-brown ppt · [Fe(SCN)(H₂O)₅]²⁺ blood red |
| [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ | pale blue · Cu(OH)₂ pale blue ppt |
| [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ | dark blue · [CuCl₄]²⁻ yellow |

**Colorimetry (planning, e.g. N2013/P2/Q1):**
- *Concentration:* choose filter of complementary colour → measure absorbance of standards of known conc → plot calibration graph (absorbance vs conc) → read off unknown. (UV-Vis: fix λ at max absorbance instead of a filter.)
- *Stoichiometry:* **method of continuous variation** — mix M and ligand in varying molar ratios (10:0, 9:1, 8:2…), keep total moles constant; the ratio giving maximum colour intensity gives the complex formula.

## TOP EXAM TRAPS
1. **Writing ion configs by removing 3d before 4s.** Always strip 4s first: Fe²⁺ = 3d⁶, NOT 3d⁴4s².
2. **Forgetting Cr/Cu exceptions** (3d⁵4s¹, 3d¹⁰4s¹).
3. **Calling CrO₄²⁻ ⇌ Cr₂O₇²⁻ a redox reaction** — it is acid-base; Cr stays +6.
4. **Stating colour without the d-d chain.** "Coloured because transition metal" earns 0. You must say: partially filled d-orbitals split by ligands → ΔE → electron absorbs visible light (d-d transition) → colour seen is complement of absorbed.
5. **Homogeneous catalysis with only one step or a negative E°cell.** Must show TWO steps, both E°cell > 0, catalyst regenerated.
6. **Omitting "E°cell > 0 ⟹ feasible"** when predicting redox — quote the values from the Data Booklet.
7. **Saying ΔE depends on ligand field order** — not required; just state "different ligands give different ΔE."
8. **Forgetting the "close in energy" phrase** for variable oxidation states.
9. **Heterogeneous catalysis reason** is *partially filled 3d orbitals* (adsorption), NOT variable oxidation states — that's the homogeneous reason. Don't swap them.
10. **[Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ not [Cu(NH₃)₆]²⁺** — only 4 NH₃ replace, 2 axial H₂O remain.
