## UNIT 1 — Types of polymers & their formation

### Definitions (LO 11.10a) ★ word-perfect
- **Monomer = a small molecule that can react with other small molecules (same or different type) to form long chains called polymers** — the building blocks.
- **Polymer = a macromolecule built up from very large numbers of monomers**, with **average Mr ≥ 1000 or ≥ 100 repeat units.**
- **Repeat unit = the smallest unit from which the polymer chain can be built up by repetition.** Drawn in **square brackets with subscript n** (n = number of repeat units = chain length); **the bonds must extend out through the brackets** (the chain continues). Repeat units are linked by **covalent bonds.**

### Addition vs condensation (LO 11.10b) ★★ — the central distinction
| | **Addition polymer** (e.g. poly(alkenes)) | **Condensation polymer** (e.g. polyesters, polyamides) |
|---|---|---|
| **Repeat unit** | **same empirical formula** as monomer | **different empirical formula (fewer atoms)** than monomer |
| **Formed by** | **addition reaction, NO loss of atoms** | **condensation reaction, loss of small molecules** (H₂O, HCl) |
| **Backbone** | **C–C bonds** (monomers have **C=C** that open: C=C → C–C, each C makes a new single bond) | contains **ester and/or amide groups** |
| **Monomers** | alkene / substituted alkene (one C=C) | **two different functional groups** — carboxylic-acid/acyl-chloride + alcohol/amine — on **two different monomers** OR **one monomer bearing both** |

### Addition polymers (poly(alkenes))
- **The C=C opens to a C–C single bond** and the two carbons each bond to the next unit — exactly an alkene addition (T11). Made from alkene or **substituted alkene** monomers.
- **Examples (monomer → polymer → use):** chloroethene → **PVC** (pipes, insulation); tetrafluoroethene → **PTFE/Teflon** (non-stick); propene → **PP** (containers); phenylethene/styrene → **polystyrene** (packaging foam); ethene → **poly(ethene)** (packaging).
- *(Ring-opening polymerisation of cyclic monomers is another addition class — bonds form with no small molecule lost.)*

### Condensation polymers (polyesters & polyamides)
- **Polyester = repeat units linked by ester bonds (–COO–); polyamide = linked by amide bonds (–CONH–).**
- **Two routes:** (1) **two different monomers** — one with two –CO₂H (or two –COCl), the other with two –OH (or two –NH₂); (2) **one monomer with two different functional groups** that condense together.
- **One small molecule (H₂O) is eliminated for every ester/amide linkage formed** (HCl if an acyl chloride is used).
- **Examples:**
  - **PET / Terylene (polyester):** ethane-1,2-diol + benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid → eliminates H₂O per ester bond. (Bottles, textiles, clothing.)
  - **Nylon-6,10 (polyamide):** hexane-1,6-diamine + decanedioic acid → eliminates H₂O per amide bond. (Nylons named by the C count in each monomer.)
  - **PLA, poly(lactic acid) (polyester):** lactic acid (one monomer, –OH and –CO₂H) → eliminates H₂O. (3D-printing filament; biodegradable.)
- **Peptide bonds (proteins) are amide bonds — proteins are condensation polymers of α-amino acids** (Topic 19, LO 11.10c/d).

### Drawing & deducing (LO 11.10b)
- **Draw repeat unit from monomers:** addition → open the C=C, put the two carbons in brackets with side groups, bonds through brackets. Condensation → join the two functional groups into the ester/amide linkage, remove H₂O, bracket from mid-linkage to mid-linkage.
- **Deduce monomers from a polymer section:** **cut at each ester/amide linkage** and **add back the eliminated H₂O** (–CO– gets –OH → –CO₂H; –O– or –NH– gets –H → –OH or –NH₂). For addition polymers, **put the C=C back** into the repeat unit.

## UNIT 2 — Properties, biodegradability, disposal & recycling

### Properties from bonding (LO — application)
- **A polymer's properties follow from the functional groups in its backbone/side groups** (same bonding logic as the molecules):
  - **Nylon is strong:** backbone **amide linkages H-bond extensively** between adjacent chains.
  - **PVA (poly(vinyl alcohol)) is water-soluble:** many **–OH side groups H-bond with water** (used in dissolvable detergent capsules).
  - **PVC is water-resistant:** backbone/side groups **cannot H-bond with water.**
  - *(Conjugated poly(ethyne) conducts: overlapping p orbitals → delocalised electrons free to move.)*

### Biodegradability (LO 11.10e, f) ★
- **Poly(alkenes) are chemically inert → difficult to biodegrade:** they're **effectively very long-chain alkanes**, with **strong, non-polar C–C and C–H bonds**, so they don't react with most chemicals (acids, alkalis) and **microorganisms have not evolved enzymes to break them down for food** → persist in the environment.
- **Polyesters & polyamides are generally biodegradable by hydrolysis:** like simple esters/amides, their linkages are **hydrolysed by strong acids/alkalis** — and **enzymes (esterases, peptidases) in soil bacteria hydrolyse the ester/amide linkages over time.**
- **Drawing hydrolysis products (LO 11.10f) ★:** acid or base hydrolysis cleaves each ester/amide linkage back to the monomers — **ester → carboxylic acid + alcohol** (acid hydrolysis) or **carboxylate salt + alcohol** (base); **amide → carboxylic acid + amine** (acid) or **carboxylate + amine** (base). *(e.g. PET hydrolyses back to benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid + ethane-1,2-diol; PLA → lactic acid, then metabolised to CO₂ + H₂O.)*
- **Atom economy:** addition polymerisation = **100% atom economy** (no by-product); condensation = **<100%** (eliminates H₂O/HCl → a by-product → some waste).

### Disposal & recycling (LO 11.10g) — the discursive block
- **Finite resource:** petroleum (from crude oil) is **finite** (doesn't renew fast enough for sustainable extraction). Most polymer **monomers are made from petroleum**, so plastic use depletes a limited supply. (Plastics still take far **less manufacturing energy** than aluminium/glass/steel equivalents.)
- **Why disposal is unsustainable** (plastic waste is largely non-biodegradable; in Singapore it's incinerated + ash to landfill):
  - **Environmental:** incineration → **noxious fumes (CO, dioxin)** + **CO₂ (global warming)**; additives **leach into water/landfill**; breakdown into **microplastics** entering food chains.
  - **Economic:** **land for landfill is scarce** (competes with housing/agriculture in Singapore — Semakau fills by ~2035); incineration **consumes energy and incurs cost.**
- **Importance of recycling** (3rd of "Reduce, Reuse, Recycle"); many polymers can be **melted and remoulded** — but must be **sorted by type first** (hence the **Resin Identification Code**, RIC). **Advantages:**
  - **Environmental:** conserves finite raw materials (petroleum, trees, minerals) & habitats; reduces incineration/landfill (toxic/greenhouse emissions, water pollution); reduces litter harming wildlife (seabirds, marine entanglement).
  - **Economic:** reduces energy & cost of making products from raw materials and of waste disposal.
  - **Social:** creates **jobs/opportunities** in the recycling industry.
- **Challenges:** plastics **must be sorted + shredded before melting**; poorly sorted recyclate has **inconsistent properties** → **higher cost/time**, so recycled plastic can be **dearer than virgin plastic from petroleum**; may need **more energy** than recycling aluminium/glass; depends on **public willingness** to sort/reuse.
- *(Incineration note: must be **high, controlled temperature** to avoid poisonous CO and COCl₂/phosgene; PVC can give **dioxin** unless burned hot; preferred by-product is HCl, absorbed in water.)*

## TOP EXAM TRAPS (Topic 20)
- **Repeat unit drawing: square brackets, subscript n, BONDS THROUGH the brackets.** Missing the continuation bonds is the classic lost mark.
- **Addition vs condensation = (i) same vs different empirical formula and (ii) no atom loss vs small-molecule loss.** State both. Addition backbone = C–C (from C=C opening); condensation backbone = ester/amide groups.
- **Deduce monomers: cut the ester/amide linkage and ADD BACK the eliminated H₂O** (–CO– → –CO₂H; –O–/–NH– → –OH/–NH₂). For addition, restore the C=C.
- **Poly(alkenes) hard to biodegrade because they're inert long-chain alkanes** (strong non-polar C–C/C–H; no enzymes). **Polyesters/polyamides biodegrade by HYDROLYSIS** of the ester/amide linkages.
- **Hydrolysis products = the monomers:** ester → acid + alcohol (or carboxylate salt + alcohol in base); amide → acid + amine. Draw them, don't just name.
- **Addition = 100% atom economy; condensation < 100%** (eliminates a small molecule).
- **Discursive recycling answer: give SEPARATE economic / environmental / social points** — and note recycling's *challenges* (sorting, cost vs virgin plastic), not only its benefits.
- **Properties from H-bonding:** –OH side groups → water-soluble (PVA); amide backbone → strong (nylon, H-bonds between chains); no H-bond capacity → water-resistant (PVC).
