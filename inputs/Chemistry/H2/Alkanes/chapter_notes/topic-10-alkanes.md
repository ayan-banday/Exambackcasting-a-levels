## UNIT 1 — Introduction, naming, isomerism, physical properties

- **Alkane = saturated hydrocarbon**: only C and H, only **single bonds** (each C has 4 bonds), all carbons **sp³ hybridised**. General formula **aliphatic CₙH₂ₙ₊₂**; **cycloalkanes CₙH₂ₙ** (isomeric with alkenes).
- **Naming:** straight-chain (meth–dec + **-ane**); branched (longest chain = parent; number from the end **nearer the first substituent**; substituents **alphabetical**; di/tri for repeats; complex substituents e.g. (1-methylethyl)). Cycloalkanes: **cyclo-** prefix.
- **Isomerism (recap Topic 9):** constitutional isomers grow fast (C₅H₁₂ = 3, C₁₀H₂₂ = 75). **Smallest chiral alkane = 3-methylhexane** (a C bonded to four different groups: H, CH₃, C₂H₅, C₃H₇).
- **Physical properties — all from weak dispersion forces** (C–H bond is essentially **non-polar**: EN of C = 2.55, H = 2.20):
  - **State (r.t.p.):** C₁–C₄ gases, C₅–C₁₇ liquids, ≥C₁₈ solids.
  - **Boiling point ↑ regularly with chain length:** more electrons → larger, more polarisable electron cloud → **stronger dispersion forces** → more energy to overcome. **Branched < straight-chain** (smaller surface area of contact → weaker dispersion). 
  - **Melting point: less regular** — depends on **packing in the crystal lattice** as well as size.
  - **Solubility: soluble in non-polar solvents** (benzene, CCl₄ — dispersion-with-solvent comparable to dispersion broken); **insoluble in water** (energy from pd–id/dispersion with water < energy to break water's H-bonding + alkane dispersion).
  - **Density:** less dense than water (floats), rises slightly with chain length. **Viscosity:** rises with chain length (stronger dispersion).

## UNIT 2 — Reactions

### General unreactivity (LO 11.3c)
- C–C and C–H bonds are **strong and non-polar** (C and H have similar electronegativities) → the molecule has **no significant δ+/δ− centre** → **unreactive towards polar reagents** (nucleophiles/electrophiles). Only two reactions: **combustion** and **free-radical substitution**.

### Combustion
- **Complete (excess O₂) → CO₂ + H₂O**, highly exothermic (CH₄: ΔHc = −890 kJ mol⁻¹) but **high Ea** (stable until a spark, then accelerates explosively). Alkanes **only burn in the gaseous state**.
- **Incomplete (limited O₂) → CO + C (soot) + H₂O** — environmentally harmful.

### Free-radical substitution (LO 11.4a, 11.3k) — the key mechanism
- **Reagents/conditions: Cl₂(g) or Br₂(l), UV light (or heat), room temperature.** A **non-polar** (photochemical) reaction — the **only** one in the syllabus.
- **Observations:** greenish-yellow Cl₂ decolourised / reddish-brown Br₂ decolourised.
- **Mechanism (CH₄ + Cl₂):**
  1. **Initiation** (homolytic fission by UV): **Cl–Cl → 2Cl•**. (UV can't break the **stronger** C–H bond: BE Cl–Cl = 244 < C–H = 410.)
  2. **Propagation** (chain — regenerates a radical): **Cl• + CH₄ → HCl + •CH₃** then **•CH₃ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + Cl•**. The radical is regenerated → **self-sustaining chain reaction** (so only a *flash* of UV is needed, not prolonged radiation; the radical acts as a homogeneous catalyst).
  3. **Termination** (two radicals combine → removes radicals, ends the chain): **Cl• + Cl• → Cl₂**, **Cl• + •CH₃ → CH₃Cl**, **•CH₃ + •CH₃ → CH₃CH₃ (ethane)**.

### Challenges — extent & position hard to control
- **Extent:** a radical attacks almost any C–H, so **excess halogen → further substitution** (CH₃Cl → CH₂Cl₂ → CHCl₃ → CCl₄). **To favour mono-substitution: excess alkane + limited halogen** (a Cl• is more likely to meet an alkane than a product). **To favour CCl₄: excess halogen.**
- **Position (alkane with ≥3 C → isomeric products):** the ratio depends on **(1) the number of each type of H** and **(2) the stability of the radical intermediate.**
- **Carbon/H classification:** 1°/2°/3° by number of alkyl groups on the carbon; an H is named by the carbon it sits on.
- **Radical stability: 3° > 2° > 1° > methyl** — alkyl groups are **weakly electron-donating**, compensating the electron deficiency of the radical (so more substituted radicals form more easily).
- **Product ratio = (relative reactivity of that H type) × (number of H of that type).** Propane: 1°:2° H is 6:2, but the **actual 1-chloro:2-chloro ≈ 45:55 (≈1:1)** because both factors operate. *Worked (2-methylbutane, relative reactivities 1°:2°:3° = 1:3.5:5):* multiply reactivity × count for each product.
- **Halogen reactivity: F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂.** F₂ reacts violently **even in the dark** (low F–F BE, high C–F BE); Cl₂/Br₂ need UV/heat; **I₂ doesn't react** — propagation step 1 (•CH₃ + HI) is endothermic (weak H–I bond) and step 2 isn't exothermic enough, so overall ΔH is **positive (+22 kJ mol⁻¹)**.
- **Energetics:** **ΔHr = ΔH₁ + ΔH₂** (the two propagation steps); ΔH₁ = BE(C–H) − BE(H–X), ΔH₂ = BE(X–X) − BE(C–X).

## UNIT 3 — Recycling & environmental consequences

- **Alkanes as fuels:** crude oil → **fractional distillation** (petroleum gas → gasoline → kerosene → gas oil → residue, increasing chain length/bp). Petroleum is a **finite resource**.
- **Recycling matters** across **economic** (cheaper than virgin raw materials), **environmental** (less pollution/energy/landfill, lower greenhouse emissions), and **social** (preserves resources, saves landfill space) factors.

### Pollutants from the internal combustion engine (LO 11.4h)
- **CO + unburnt hydrocarbons — from incomplete combustion** (short reaction time, poor engine, low O₂). **CO binds irreversibly to haemoglobin** (forms a stable complex) → reduces O₂ transport → drowsiness/headache, fatal >2000 ppm; **odourless** (no warning). Unburnt HC + sunlight + O₂/O₃/NOₓ → **photochemical smog**.
- **Oxides of nitrogen (NOₓ = NO + NO₂)** — formed in the engine at **high temperature**: **N₂ + O₂ → 2NO**, then **2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂**. Cause lung irritation/bronchitis, **photochemical smog**, and **acid rain**:
  - **2NO₂ + H₂O → HNO₂ + HNO₃** (acid rain).
  - **NO₂ catalyses SO₂ → SO₃** (SO₂ + NO₂ → SO₃ + NO; SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄). NO₂ is regenerated by NO + ½O₂.
  - **Acid rain** → farmland too acidic for cultivation, corrodes buildings, harms marine life.
- **Catalytic converter:** **Pt/Rh alloy, a heterogeneous catalyst** (adsorb → react → desorb). Removes all three by oxidising/reducing:
  - **2NO + 2CO → N₂ + 2CO₂** (NO reduced, N +2→0; CO oxidised, C +2→+4)
  - **2CO + O₂ → 2CO₂**
  - **CₓHy + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O**
  - **Lead poisons the catalyst → use unleaded petrol.**

### Enhanced greenhouse effect (LO 11.4h-ii)
- **Greenhouse effect:** greenhouse gases let **shortwave (visible/UV)** sunlight through to warm the surface, then **absorb the re-radiated longwave (IR/heat)**, trapping it in the lower atmosphere. **Enhanced** = the **extra warming from human activities**, *in addition to* the natural effect → rising global temperatures.
- **Greenhouse gases:** **CO₂, methane, water vapour, nitrous oxide (N₂O)** (natural); **CFCs, HFCs, PFCs, SF₆** (man-made). **Methane** has a greenhouse factor ≈30 and ~20% of radiative forcing despite low concentration.

## TOP EXAM TRAPS (Topic 10)
- Free-radical substitution: **conditions = Cl₂(g)/Br₂(l), UV light (or heat), room temperature**. State all parts.
- Mechanism must be **three labelled stages**: initiation (homolytic, UV), propagation (**two** steps, regenerates the radical → chain), termination (two radicals combine). Use **fish-hook (half-head) arrows** if drawing.
- UV doesn't break C–H in initiation because **Cl–Cl is weaker than C–H**.
- **Radical stability: 3° > 2° > 1° > methyl** (alkyl electron-donating). **Product ratio = relative reactivity × number of that H.**
- **F₂ reacts in the dark; I₂ does not react** (overall ΔH positive — weak H–I bond).
- Combustion: alkanes only burn **gaseous**; incomplete → **CO + soot**.
- **CO binds irreversibly to haemoglobin and is odourless.**
- **NOₓ forms in the engine from N₂ + O₂** (not from N in petrol) and causes acid rain + photochemical smog.
- **Catalytic converter = Pt/Rh, heterogeneous; lead poisons it → unleaded petrol.** Quote oxidation-state changes if asked.
- **Enhanced greenhouse effect = extra, human-caused warming on top of the natural effect.** Gases: CO₂, CH₄, water vapour, N₂O, CFCs.
- **Branched alkanes have lower bp** (smaller surface area → weaker dispersion); melting point is **less regular** (lattice packing).
