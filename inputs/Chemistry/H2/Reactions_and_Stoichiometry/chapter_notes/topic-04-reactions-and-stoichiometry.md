## THE CORE TOOLKIT (commit to muscle memory)
- **n = m / Mr** ; **n = c × V(dm³)** ; **n = V(gas) / Vm** ; **V = n·Vm**.
- **Vm:** s.t.p. = 273 K, 1 bar → **22.7 dm³ mol⁻¹**; r.t.p. = 293 K, 1 atm → **24 dm³ mol⁻¹** (Data Booklet).
- 1 dm³ = 1000 cm³ = 10⁻³ m³. **c in g dm⁻³ = c in mol dm⁻³ × Mr.**
- **Always:** write a balanced equation → moles of known → mole ratio → moles of unknown → answer. Show working; mind sig figs.

## UNIT 1 — Formulae of compounds

### Definitions
- **Relative molecular mass (Mr):** sum of Ar of all atoms in the molecular formula. **Relative formula mass** = same idea for **ionic** compounds. Both **have no units** (ratio of masses).
- **Empirical formula:** simplest whole-number **ratio** of atoms of each element.
- **Molecular formula:** **actual number** of atoms of each element in one molecule. (ethanoic acid: EF = CH₂O, MF = C₂H₄O₂.)

### % composition by mass
- **% of element = (number of atoms × Ar) / Mr × 100%.** (Reverse: given %, deduce Mr or number of atoms — e.g. haemoglobin from 0.34% Fe with 4 Fe atoms.)

### Empirical/molecular formula calculations
- **From composition by mass:** mass (in 100 g) → moles (÷ Ar) → **divide all by the smallest** → ratio → whole numbers (×2, ×3 etc. if needed — ⚠️ **do NOT round 1.33 to 1; ×3 → 4**). Then MF: let MF = (EF)ₙ, **n = Mr / (EF mass)**.
- **From combustion data** (organic → CO₂ + H₂O, by conservation of matter):
  - **mass C = (12.0/44.0) × mass CO₂**; **mass H = (2.0/18.0) × mass H₂O**.
  - **mass of any third element (O, Fe…) = total sample mass − mass C − mass H** (⚠️ found by *difference*, never from the O₂).
  - Then proceed as composition-by-mass.

## UNIT 2 — Reactions and stoichiometry

- **Balancing** = conservation of matter/mass: same number of atoms of each element both sides (and balance charge for ionic equations).
- **Limiting reagent:** divide moles of each reactant by its coefficient — smallest wins; it's fully consumed and caps the product. The other is in **excess**. ⚠️ Identify it *before* calculating product amount.
- **Percentage yield = (actual yield / theoretical yield) × 100%** (theoretical from the limiting reagent).
- **Gas volumes (same T, P): volume ratio = mole ratio** (Avogadro's Law). For hydrocarbon combustion:
  - **CₓHy + (x + y/4) O₂ → x CO₂ + (y/2) H₂O.**
  - **CO₂ is absorbed by aqueous KOH/NaOH** → the contraction on adding alkali gives the CO₂ volume.
  - At r.t.p., **water is liquid** → don't count H₂O as a gas volume; at >100 °C it's steam (a gas). Use the "initial vs final volume" table; total contraction and CO₂-absorption give x and y.
- **Solutions:** concentration = moles solute per dm³ of solution. **Standard solution** = concentration accurately known. **Dilution:** moles of solute unchanged → **c₁V₁ = c₂V₂**. For concentration of a specific ion, multiply by its subscript (e.g. [Na⁺] = 2 × [Na₂CO₃]).
- Density problems: mass = density × volume; then convert to moles and a concentration.

## UNIT 3 — Acid-base reactions

### Three theories (definitions + limitation + when to use)
| Theory | Acid | Base | Notes / limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Arrhenius** | dissociates in water to give **H₃O⁺ (H⁺)** | dissociates in water to give **OH⁻** | **aqueous only**; best for classifying isolated substances |
| **Brønsted–Lowry** | **proton (H⁺) donor** (must contain H) | **proton acceptor** (must have a lone pair) | works in gas/non-aqueous; gives **conjugate acid–base pairs**; can't explain H-free acids (BF₃) |
| **Lewis** | **electron-pair acceptor** (e.g. BF₃) | **electron-pair donor** (e.g. NH₃) | most general; **use only when no proton transfer** (e.g. BF₃ + NH₃ → BF₃NH₃) |

- **Conjugate pair:** differ by one H⁺ (acid → its conjugate base + H⁺). A reaction transfers H⁺ from acid to base.
- **H₂O is amphoteric** (acid or base depending on partner). A species like HNO₃ can be a B–L acid (with NaOH) or B–L base (with stronger H₂SO₄).

### Titrations
- **Standard solution:** accurately known concentration. **Aliquot:** fixed volume pipetted into the conical flask. **Titre:** volume delivered from the burette.
- **Equivalence point:** reactants have reacted in exact stoichiometric ratio. **End point:** where the indicator changes colour (chosen to ≈ equivalence point). **pH working range:** the pH interval over which the indicator changes colour.
- **Indicator choice (acid–base):**
  - **Strong acid–strong base:** either (methyl orange or phenolphthalein).
  - **Strong acid–weak base → methyl orange** (3.1–4.4; red↔yellow, **end orange**).
  - **Weak acid–strong base → phenolphthalein** (8.0–9.6; colourless↔pink) or thymol blue.
  - **Weak acid–weak base → no suitable indicator** (no sharp jump).
  - ⚠️ State the colour change in the right **direction** (depends on which reagent is in the burette): strong acid in flask, base in burette → methyl orange **red → orange**; reverse the order → **yellow → orange**.
- **Calc method:** moles of standard → mole ratio from equation → moles of unknown → concentration. Repeat for consistent titres (within ±0.10 cm³).

### Back (indirect) titration
- A **known excess of A** reacts with unknown **B**; the **unreacted A** is then titrated with standard **C**.
- **Total A = A reacted with B + A reacted with C (titration).** So **A that reacted with B = total A − A found unreacted by titration**, then use stoichiometry to get B.
- Used when B is impure/insoluble/slow, or the gas escapes (e.g. impure Mg + excess HCl, then titrate leftover HCl with NaOH; or NH₄Cl warmed with excess NaOH).

## UNIT 4 — Redox reactions

### Definitions (OIL RIG)
- **Oxidation:** loss of electrons → **increase** in oxidation number. **Reduction:** gain of electrons → **decrease** in O.N.
- **Oxidising agent (oxidant):** takes electrons, **itself reduced** (e.g. MnO₄⁻, Cr₂O₇²⁻, H₂O₂, I₂).
- **Reducing agent (reductant):** gives electrons, **itself oxidised** (e.g. Fe²⁺, C₂O₄²⁻, I⁻, S₂O₃²⁻).
- **Disproportionation:** the **same species** is simultaneously oxidised AND reduced (e.g. 3Cl₂ + 6OH⁻ → 5Cl⁻ + ClO₃⁻ + 3H₂O).

### Oxidation number rules
1. Element in its free form = **0**. 2. Monoatomic ion = its **charge**. 3. Polyatomic ion: Σ O.N. = **charge**. 4. Neutral compound: Σ O.N. = **0**. 5. More electronegative element gets the **negative** O.N.
- **Reference values:** H **+1** (−1 in metal hydrides, NaH); O **−2** (−1 in peroxides H₂O₂; also exceptions like OF₂); Group 1 **+1**; Group 2 **+2**; Al **+3**; F **−1**.
- Rules give the **average** O.N. (e.g. C in C₂O₄²⁻ averages +3). First-principles (dot-cross + electronegativity boundary) gives **per-atom** O.N. (e.g. the two C in ethanol are −3 and −1) — only needed when atoms are in different environments.

### Constructing & balancing half-equations
1. Split into oxidation + reduction half-equations (rewrite molecular → ionic first).
2. Balance the redox element; **add electrons = change in O.N.** (× number of that atom in the formula).
3. **Balance charge with H⁺ (acidic) or OH⁻ (alkaline)**; **balance O/H with H₂O**.
4. Scale each half so **electrons lost = electrons gained**; add; cancel; check atoms **and** charge.
- **Memorise the key reductions:**
  - **MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O** (acidic; purple → colourless). In alkaline → MnO₂ (brown ppt).
  - **Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6e⁻ → 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O** (orange → green).
  - **Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ + e⁻** (pale green → yellow).

### Redox titrations (LO 13g)
- **KMnO₄ titration:** KMnO₄ in burette, reductant + **dilute H₂SO₄** in flask. **Self-indicating** — end point = first **permanent pink** (purple decolourises while reductant remains). ⚠️ **Use H₂SO₄, not HCl (oxidised to Cl₂) or HNO₃ (itself an oxidant).**
- **Iodine/thiosulfate (iodometric):** **2S₂O₃²⁻ + I₂ → 2I⁻ + S₄O₆²⁻.** Thiosulfate in burette, brown I₂ in flask; titrate to **pale yellow**, then add **starch** (blue-black complex), continue to **colourless** end point.
  - ⚠️ Add starch **late** (high [I₂] traps iodine in starch → early/false endpoint). Titrate **promptly** (I₂ volatile; cover flasks). **Ignore slow return of blue** afterward (atmospheric oxidation of I⁻ → I₂).
- **Iodometric back-titration of an oxidant:** oxidant + **excess KI** → liberates I₂ (e.g. H₂O₂ + 2I⁻ + 2H⁺ → 2H₂O + I₂); titrate the I₂ with standard thiosulfate → back-calculate the oxidant.
- **Electron-transfer method to find an unknown O.N./product:** moles e⁻ lost by reductant = moles e⁻ gained by oxidant; divide by moles of the species to get e⁻ transferred per formula unit → deduce the O.N. change/product (e.g. C₂O₄²⁻ → CO₂, O.N. C +3 → +4).

## TOP EXAM TRAPS (Topic 4)
- Empirical formula: never round a ratio like 1.33/1.5/1.25 — scale up (×3, ×2, ×4).
- Combustion: mass of the third element (O etc.) comes **by difference**, not from the oxygen.
- Identify the **limiting reagent** before any product calculation; % yield uses the *theoretical* (limiting-reagent) amount.
- Gas volume = mole ratio **only for gases at the same T,P**; water is liquid at r.t.p. — don't count it; **CO₂ is the gas absorbed by KOH/NaOH**.
- Brønsted **acid must contain H**; **base must have a lone pair**. Lewis only when there's no proton transfer.
- Indicator colour change must be in the **correct direction** for which reagent is in the burette; weak-acid/weak-base → no good indicator.
- Back titration: amount that reacted with the analyte = **total − unreacted (from titration)**.
- KMnO₄: **dilute H₂SO₄ only**; it's **self-indicating** (no extra indicator). Cr₂O₇²⁻ is not self-indicating.
- Iodometric: starch **added late**; ignore the slow blue return after the endpoint.
- Half-equations: balance **charge** (H⁺/OH⁻ by medium) as well as atoms.
