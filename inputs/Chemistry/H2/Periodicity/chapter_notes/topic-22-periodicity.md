## UNIT 1 — Period 3 oxides (LO 5e)

### Highest oxidation number trend (LO 5e-i)
- **Highest O.N. in oxides rises +1→+6 across Na→S** (Na₂O +1, MgO +2, Al₂O₃ +3, SiO₂ +4, P₄O₁₀ +5, SO₃ +6); in chlorides +1→+5 (PCl₅). **It equals the number of valence electrons** (all used in bonding).
- O.N. in oxides is always positive (O is more electronegative than every Period 3 element). **Na, Mg, Al show only ONE O.N. (lose all valence e⁻); P, S, Cl show a range** because they can **expand their octet** using vacant 3d orbitals.

### Structure & bonding vs electronegativity (LO 5e-ii) ★ — the spine
- **Across the period, the electronegativity difference between the element and oxygen decreases → bonding changes ionic → covalent.** (Covalent bonds form between atoms of similar electronegativity; ionic between very different.)
  - **Na₂O, MgO, Al₂O₃ = ionic** (giant ionic lattice, strong electrostatic forces between oppositely-charged ions).
  - **SiO₂ = giant molecular/macromolecular** (strong covalent bonds; each Si–4 O, each O–2 Si, diamond-like).
  - **P₄O₁₀, SO₃ = simple discrete covalent molecules** (weak dispersion forces between molecules).

### Melting point trend (LO 5c application) ★
- **Na₂O→MgO→Al₂O₃ all giant ionic:** mp high; **MgO > Na₂O** (Mg²⁺ smaller + higher charge → larger lattice energy → stronger ionic bonding). **Al₂O₃ mp < MgO** (Al³⁺ very high charge density polarises O²⁻ → covalent character lowers it below the lattice-energy prediction). **SiO₂ high** (giant molecular, break strong covalent bonds). **P₄O₁₀, SO₃ low** (simple molecular, weak dispersion forces). *(mp peaks at MgO/Al₂O₃, drops sharply at the molecular oxides.)*

### Reactions with water (LO 5e-iii) ★
| Oxide | Reaction with water | pH |
|---|---|---|
| **Na₂O** (basic) | vigorous, exothermic even cold; dissolves fully → `Na₂O + H₂O → 2Na⁺ + 2OH⁻` | ~13 (strongly alkaline) |
| **MgO** (basic) | very slow, sparingly soluble → `MgO + H₂O → Mg(OH)₂` (sparingly soluble) | ~9 (weakly alkaline) |
| **Al₂O₃** (amphoteric) | **insoluble — no reaction** | — |
| **SiO₂** (acidic) | **does not react/dissolve** | — |
| **P₄O₁₀** (acidic) | violent → `P₄O₁₀ + 6H₂O → 4H₃PO₄` (phosphoric(V) acid, triprotic, fairly strong) | ~2 |
| **SO₃** (acidic) | violent, very exothermic → `SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄` (strong dibasic acid, fully ionised) | ~1 |

### Acid-base behaviour (LO 5e-iv) ★ — basic → amphoteric → acidic
- **Ionic oxides are basic** (contain O²⁻, which hydrolyses: `O²⁻ + H₂O → 2OH⁻`). **Covalent oxides are acidic** (form oxoanions + H₃O⁺). **Al₂O₃ is the only amphoteric oxide in Period 3.**
- **Basic oxides/hydroxides + acid → salt + water:** `Na₂O + 2H⁺ → 2Na⁺ + H₂O`; `MgO + 2H⁺ → Mg²⁺ + H₂O`; `NaOH/Mg(OH)₂ + HCl → salt + H₂O` (NaOH white pellets dissolve exothermically; Mg(OH)₂ sparingly soluble white solid).
- **Amphoteric Al₂O₃ & Al(OH)₃ — react with BOTH acid and NaOH** ★ (Al³⁺ high charge density gives the ionic oxide covalent character):
  - **As a base (with acid):** `Al₂O₃ + 6H⁺ → 2Al³⁺ + 3H₂O`; `Al(OH)₃ + 3HCl → AlCl₃ + 3H₂O`.
  - **As an acid (with excess NaOH):** `Al₂O₃ + 2OH⁻ + 3H₂O → 2[Al(OH)₄]⁻`; `Al(OH)₃ + NaOH → Na[Al(OH)₄]` → the colourless **aluminate ion [Al(OH)₄]⁻**.
- **Acidic oxides + base:** SiO₂ is only **weakly acidic** (needs **hot, concentrated NaOH** to break the giant lattice) → `SiO₂ + 2OH⁻(conc) → SiO₃²⁻ + H₂O` (silicate). P₄O₁₀ → `+ 12OH⁻ → 4PO₄³⁻ + 6H₂O`. SO₃ → `+ 2OH⁻ → SO₄²⁻ + H₂O`.

### Predicting structure/bonding from properties (LO 5e-vi)
- **High mp + conducts when molten/aqueous + basic/dissolves to alkali → giant ionic.** **Very high mp + insoluble + doesn't conduct → giant molecular (SiO₂).** **Low mp + acidic + soluble → simple molecular.**

## UNIT 2 — Period 3 chlorides (LO 5e-ii, v, vi)

### Structure & bonding (LO 5e-ii) ★ — same electronegativity logic
- **Across the period, electronegativity difference with Cl decreases → ionic → covalent.**
  - **NaCl, MgCl₂ = giant ionic** (white crystalline solids, high mp 801/714 °C).
  - **AlCl₃ = borderline** (the LO *exception*): solid has an **ionic lattice with much covalent character** (Al³⁺ very high charge density polarises Cl⁻); just below 180 °C it **sublimes**, becoming **simple molecular Al₂Cl₆ dimers** (dative bonding) ⇌ AlCl₃ monomers (more monomer at higher T).
  - **SiCl₄ (colourless fuming liquid), PCl₅ (off-white solid) = simple molecular** (weak dispersion forces; SiCl₄ low mp; PCl₅ self-ionises to PCl₄⁺PCl₆⁻ in the solid → sublimes ~160 °C).

### Reactions with water (LO 5e-v) ★ — hydrolysis increases ionic→covalent
- **Ionic chlorides dissolve** (ions hydrated by ion-dipole). Cl⁻ is the conjugate base of strong HCl → no hydrolysis. **Metal cations hydrolyse depending on charge density.**
| Chloride | Reaction with water | pH |
|---|---|---|
| **NaCl** | dissolves → `NaCl + aq → Na⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq)`; Na⁺ low charge density, **no hydrolysis** | 7 |
| **MgCl₂** | dissolves; `[Mg(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + H₂O ⇌ [Mg(H₂O)₅OH]⁺ + H₃O⁺` (slight hydrolysis) | ~6.5 |
| **AlCl₃** | **few drops water → steamy white HCl fumes + white Al(OH)₃ solid** `AlCl₃ + 3H₂O → Al(OH)₃ + 3HCl`; **excess water → [Al(H₂O)₆]³⁺**, hydrolyses further (high charge density) → distinctly acidic | ~3 |
| **SiCl₄** | **violent, complete hydrolysis → HCl fumes** `SiCl₄ + 4H₂O → SiO₂·2H₂O + 4HCl` (hydrated silica) | ~1 |
| **PCl₅** | **violent, complete hydrolysis** `PCl₅ + 4H₂O → H₃PO₄ + 5HCl`; *dropwise* water → POCl₃ (`PCl₅ + H₂O → POCl₃ + 2HCl`) | ~1 |
- **Trend: ionic chlorides just dissolve; covalent chlorides hydrolyse to give acidic solutions + HCl fumes** (so must be kept in airtight containers; fume even in moist air). **As bonding goes ionic→covalent, the tendency to hydrolyse increases.** (SiCl₄/PCl₅ react with ROH the same way → the basis of the PCl₅ test for –OH, T16.)

## UNIT 3 — Group 2 (LO 5f-i, 5g-i)

### Reactivity as reducing agents (LO 5f-i) ★ — from E°
- **Reactivity increases down the group.** Down the group, atomic radius ↑ → metal loses electrons more readily (1st+2nd IE ↓) → forms M²⁺ more easily → **reducing power increases down the group**, shown by **increasingly negative E°** (Mg²⁺/Mg −2.38, Ca −2.87, Sr −2.89, Ba −2.90). *(More negative E° = stronger reducing agent — T21.)*

### Thermal stability of carbonates (LO 5g-i) ★
- `MCO₃(s) → MO(s) + CO₂(g)`. **Thermal stability INCREASES down the group** (decomposition temperature rises: Mg 400, Ca 900, Sr 1280, Ba 1360 °C). **Ease of decomposition decreases down the group.**
- **Explanation — charge density of cation × polarisability of anion:**
  - **Down the group, cationic radius ↑ (charge constant) → charge density ↓ → polarising power of the cation ↓ → it distorts/weakens the C–O bonds of the carbonate anion to a SMALLER extent → covalent bonds in CO₃²⁻ less likely to break → higher decomposition temperature.**
  - The carbonate anion is **large and polyatomic** (only polyatomic anions can decompose; larger anions are more polarisable). A high-charge-density cation pulls the anion's electron cloud, weakening a C–O bond → CO₂ + O²⁻ form.
- *(Same logic for Group 2 nitrates `→ MO + 2NO₂ + ½O₂` and hydroxides `→ MO + H₂O`. Group 1 carbonates are stable except Li₂CO₃ — Li⁺ has high charge density, the "diagonal" odd-one-out.)*

## UNIT 4 — Group 17 (LO 5d, 5f-ii, 5g-ii)

### Volatility trend (LO 5d) ★
- Halogens are **simple non-polar diatomic molecules with dispersion forces only.** **Down the group, electron-cloud size (and polarisability) ↑ → stronger dispersion forces → more energy to overcome → volatility DECREASES, mp/bp INCREASE.** (F₂ gas, Cl₂ gas, Br₂ liquid, I₂ solid.)

### Reactivity as oxidising agents (LO 5f-ii) ★ — from E°
- Halogens (ns²np⁵) tend to **gain** an electron → reduced → oxidising agents. **E° (`X₂ + 2e⁻ → 2X⁻`) becomes less positive down the group (F₂ +2.87, Cl₂ +1.36, Br₂ +1.07, I₂ +0.54) → oxidising power DECREASES down the group.**
- **Displacement:** a halogen displaces a halide below it (e.g. `Cl₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + I₂` — pale yellow Cl₂(aq) turns brown; brown is I₂ + excess I⁻ → I₃⁻).
- **With Fe²⁺ [E°(Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺) = +0.77]:** Cl₂ (+1.36−0.77 = +0.59) and Br₂ (+0.30) oxidise Fe²⁺→Fe³⁺ (spontaneous); **I₂ (−0.23) does NOT** (E°cell < 0). Read straight off E° — a classic exam item.
- **With thiosulfate S₂O₃²⁻:** Cl₂/Br₂ (stronger) oxidise it to **SO₄²⁻** (S: +2→+6); I₂ (weaker) only to **tetrathionate S₄O₆²⁻** (S: +2→+2.5).

### Thermal stability of hydrides (LO 5g-ii) ★ — from bond energy
- `2HX → H₂ + X₂`. **Down the group, atomic radius ↑ → H–X bond length ↑ → bond strength ↓ (H–F 562, H–Cl 431, H–Br 366, H–I 299 kJ mol⁻¹) → less energy needed to break H–X → thermal stability DECREASES down the group.** (HF/HCl don't decompose even on strong heating; HBr → brown Br₂ fumes; HI → violet I₂ fumes with a red-hot rod.)

## UNIT 5 — Applying periodicity (LO 5h, 5i)

- **Predict an element's properties** from its Group/Period using the trends above; **deduce an unknown element's identity/position** from given physical/chemical data (e.g. a chloride that's M₂Cl₆ in vapour → Group 13 like Al; oxide dissolves in acid → metal; coloured ionic salts → transition-metal-like).
- **Diagonal relationships (useful tool):** diagonally-adjacent Period 2/3 pairs (Li~Mg, Be~Al, B~Si) share properties because **the opposing trends across a period and down a group "cancel out."** Key examples: **BeO and Al₂O₃ both amphoteric**; Be²⁺ and Al³⁺ both weakly acidic (hydrolysis); B and Si both covalent semiconductors. → the first element of a group can differ slightly from the rest.
- **Electronegativity quick-rank** uses across-↑/down-↓: e.g. Al ≈ Be (diagonal), both ~1.5.

## TOP EXAM TRAPS (Topic 22)
- **Universal explanation: "electronegativity difference with O/Cl decreases across the period → bonding ionic→covalent."** Use it for oxides AND chlorides.
- **Every mp/volatility answer: structure → type of force → energy.** Giant ionic/covalent = strong bonds (high mp); simple molecular = weak dispersion (low mp); dispersion ↑ with electron-cloud size.
- **Al₂O₃ mp < MgO** despite the lattice-energy prediction — Al³⁺ polarises O²⁻ → covalent character.
- **Acid-base: basic (Na₂O, MgO) → amphoteric (Al₂O₃ ONLY) → acidic (SiO₂ weakly, P₄O₁₀, SO₃).** Al₂O₃/Al(OH)₃ react with **both acid and NaOH** → aluminate [Al(OH)₄]⁻. SiO₂ needs **hot conc NaOH**.
- **Chloride hydrolysis: NaCl none, MgCl₂ slight (~6.5), AlCl₃ more (~3), SiCl₄/PCl₅ complete (~1) with HCl fumes.** Bonding ionic→covalent ⇒ hydrolysis ↑.
- **AlCl₃ is the bonding "exception"** (borderline ionic/covalent; Al₂Cl₆ dimers in vapour).
- **Group 2: reactivity/reducing power ↑ down (E° more negative); carbonate thermal stability ↑ down** (cation charge density ↓ → polarises anion less). Don't invert the stability trend.
- **Group 17: volatility ↓ / mp ↑ down (dispersion); oxidising power ↓ down (E° less positive); hydride stability ↓ down (H–X bond weaker).**
- **Carbonate stability = cation charge density × anion polarisability**, and **only polyatomic anions decompose.**
- **Hydride stability is explained by H–X BOND ENERGY**, not the cation argument.
