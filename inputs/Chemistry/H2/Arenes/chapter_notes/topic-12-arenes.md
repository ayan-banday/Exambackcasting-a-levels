## UNIT 1 — Introduction to benzene & arenes

### Definition & naming (LO 11.1a)
- **Arene = aromatic hydrocarbon** containing **≥1 benzene ring**. "Aromatic" now means the **extra stability** of benzene and its relatives **due to delocalisation of π electrons** (resonance stabilisation).
- **Benzene = C₆H₆**, drawn as the skeletal hexagon with an inner circle (delocalised π).
- **Mono-substituted naming:** if the substituent group has **≤6 C**, the ring is the **parent** → suffix **-benzene** (e.g. nitrobenzene, chlorobenzene). Some keep trivial names (methylbenzene/toluene, phenol, benzoic acid, aniline). If the alkyl substituent has **>6 C or is too complex**, benzene is the **substituent → prefix "phenyl-"** (C₆H₅–).
- **Multi-substituted naming:** number for the **lowest set of locants**; the **principal functional group is at position 1**, priority order **–CO₂H > –OH > –CH₃ (alkyl) > halogen > –NO₂**; remaining substituents listed **alphabetically**.

### Physical properties
- Benzene = **colourless liquid at rtp, toxic and carcinogenic** (chronic exposure → bone marrow damage, ↓ RBC → anaemia, **leukaemia**). Volatile, flammable.
- **Non-polar molecule → only weak dispersion forces** → **low mp (5.5 °C) and bp (80 °C)**; **insoluble in water** (only forms weak pd–id with water, far weaker than water's H-bonding), **soluble in non-polar solvents** (hexane, CCl₄). Used as a non-polar solvent historically; now mostly an **intermediate to make other chemicals** (styrene, phenol via cumene, cyclohexane → nylon).

### Structure of benzene (LO 11.1b/c/d) ★
- Each C is **sp² hybridised**, bonded to 2 C + 1 H, **trigonal planar, C–C–C bond angle 120°**.
- **6 C–C σ bonds** (head-on sp²–sp² overlap) + **6 C–H σ bonds** (head-on sp²(C)–1s(H)). Each C keeps **one unhybridised 2p orbital perpendicular to the ring plane**.
- **All six parallel 2p orbitals overlap side-on equally → a delocalised π electron cloud above and below the hexagonal plane**, holding **6 delocalised π electrons free to move**.
- **Consequences of delocalisation:**
  - **All six C–C bonds identical**, length & energy **intermediate** between C–C and C=C → **planar regular hexagon**. (Data Booklet: C–C 0.154 nm/350 kJ mol⁻¹; **benzene C–C 0.139 nm/520**; C=C 0.134 nm/610 — quote the *benzene-specific* value.)
  - **Extra stability ("stabilisation/resonance energy").** From ΔH hydrogenation: hypothetical cyclohexa-1,3,5-triene = 3 × (cyclohexene −cyclohexene value) ≈ **−354 kJ mol⁻¹**, but actual benzene = **−205 kJ mol⁻¹** → benzene is **~149 kJ mol⁻¹ more stable** than the hypothetical triene.

### Benzene vs alkene reactivity — the two key differences (LO 11.3e) ★
- Like alkenes, benzene is **electron-rich (π cloud) → attracts electrophiles**. **But:**
  1. **Less reactive towards electrophiles.** The delocalisation gives extra stability, so the π system is **less available**; benzene does **not** decolourise Br₂ readily and needs a **stronger electrophile (e.g. Br⁺, a full +)** rather than the weakly-polarised Br₂ that alkenes react with.
  2. **Undergoes electrophilic *substitution*, not *addition*.** Addition would **destroy the stable delocalised π cloud** (high energy, unfavourable); **substitution preserves aromaticity**, so it's preferred.

## UNIT 2 — Electrophilic substitution of benzene

### General mechanism (LO 11.3m) ★ the named mechanism = mono-bromination of benzene
- **Step 1 (slow, rate-determining):** the **delocalised π electrons attack the electrophile** → **carbocation intermediate** with the **+ charge delocalised over 5 carbons** (2 of the 6 π electrons used for the new bond, 4 left delocalised). Because the **stable 6-π delocalisation is disrupted → high activation energy** → slow/rds. *(Two curly arrows: π cloud → E⁺; and the E–leaving bond breaking.)*
- **Step 2 (fast):** a **Brønsted base removes the H⁺ from the C bearing the electrophile** → **re-forms the delocalised π system, restoring aromaticity** → stable substitution product. *(One curly arrow: C–H bond → ring.)*
- **Energy profile:** two peaks (two steps), **E_a1 > E_a2** (step 1 slow).

### 4.2 Halogenation — Cl₂ or Br₂ (LO 11.4d-i)
- **Reagents/conditions:** **Cl₂(g) + AlCl₃(s)** *(or Br₂(l) + AlBr₃(s))* **as Lewis-acid catalyst, warm.** Alternatives: **FeCl₃/FeBr₃**, or **Fe(s)/Al(s)** (which generate AlX₃/FeX₃ *in situ* — Fe and Al are **not themselves** the catalyst).
- **Observation:** decolourisation of greenish-yellow Cl₂ / reddish-brown Br₂ + **white fumes of HX(g)**.
- **Electrophile generation:** `AlBr₃ + Br₂ → Br⁺ + [AlBr₄]⁻`. The **Lewis acid (Al/Fe is electron-deficient, accepts a lone pair from X₂)** makes the **strong electrophile X⁺ (full +)**. In step 2, **[AlBr₄]⁻ is the Brønsted base** that removes H⁺ and **regenerates AlBr₃**.
- **Why a catalyst is essential:** without it, X₂ is only a **weak electrophile** and benzene (resonance-stabilised) won't react. **Conditions must be anhydrous** — in water, AlCl₃ hydrolyses (`AlCl₃ + 6H₂O → [Al(H₂O)₆]³⁺ + 3Cl⁻`, then partial hydrolysis → acidic pH≈3) and can **no longer accept the lone pair from X₂**.

### 4.3 Nitration — conc HNO₃ (LO 11.4d-ii)
- **Reagent:** **concentrated HNO₃**; **Conditions:** **concentrated H₂SO₄ as Brønsted-Lowry acid catalyst, maintained at 50 °C (for benzene).**
- **Observation:** pale-yellow oily liquid (nitrobenzene) formed.
- **Electrophile = nitronium ion NO₂⁺.** H₂SO₄ is a **stronger Brønsted-Lowry acid → protonates HNO₃**: overall `2H₂SO₄ + HNO₃ → 2HSO₄⁻ + H₃O⁺ + NO₂⁺`. **No reaction without conc H₂SO₄.** Over-vigorous conditions → 1,3-dinitro- / 1,3,5-trinitrobenzene (undesired). Nitration is the **gateway to many syntheses** (→ amines etc.).

### 4.4 Friedel-Crafts alkylation — halogenoalkane (LO 11.4d-iii)
- **Reagent:** chloroalkane (R–Cl); **Conditions:** **AlCl₃ (or AlBr₃) as Lewis-acid catalyst, warm.** **Observation:** white fumes of HCl(g). Electrophile = **R⁺** (`RCl + AlCl₃ → R⁺ + [AlCl₄]⁻`).
- **Only works with *alkyl* halides** (chloro/bromo/iodoalkane) as reagent — **not aryl halides** (e.g. chlorobenzene), because the **C–X bond in aryl halides can't be broken easily** (Topic 15).

## UNIT 3 — Substituent effect on reactivity & position (LO 11.3b, 11.4g) ★★

### Effect on REACTIVITY (Section 5.4)
- **Electron-DONATING groups → ↑ ring electron density → more reactive to electrophiles → "activating".** Need **milder conditions** (lower temp, less/no catalyst, dilute) than benzene.
- **Electron-WITHDRAWING groups → ↓ ring electron density → less reactive → "deactivating".** Need **more vigorous conditions.**
- **Two electronic mechanisms (from Topic 8):**
  - **Inductive effect** (through the **σ bond**, driven by **electronegativity**): –OH, –NH₂, –Cl etc. **inductively withdraw** (O, N, halogen more EN than ring C); **alkyl groups inductively donate** (hyperconjugation, not examined).
  - **Delocalisation** (a **p orbital / lone pair on the atom joined to the ring overlaps the ring π cloud**): groups with a **lone pair on the attached atom** (–OH, –OR, –NH₂, –NR₂, –Cl, –Br, –I) **donate** the lone pair into the ring; groups where the attached atom is **double/triple-bonded to an EN atom** (–CHO, –COR, –CO₂H, –NO₂) **withdraw** density out of the ring.
- **Net effect = the stronger of the two dominates:**
  - **–OH, –NH₂:** inductive withdrawing **but** delocalisation strongly donating (effective **2p–2p overlap** O/N with ring C) → **overall electron-donating → activating.**
  - **Halogens (–Cl/–Br/–I):** inductive strongly withdrawing; delocalisation only **weakly donating** (poor **3p–2p overlap**, size mismatch) → **overall electron-withdrawing → deactivating.**
  - **Alkyl:** weakly donating → **weakly activating.**
  - **–NO₂, –CHO, –CO₂H, –NH₃⁺ etc.:** strongly withdrawing both ways → **strongly deactivating.**

### Effect on POSITION / directing (Section 5.5)
- **The position of the incoming electrophile is set by the substituent ALREADY on the ring — not by the electrophile.**
- **Activating groups (and the halogens) → 2,4-directing (ortho/para).** **Deactivating groups (except halogens) → 3-directing (meta).**
- **Two substituents already present:**
  - Both direct to the **same position** → substitution there.
  - Direct to **different positions** → major product follows the **more strongly activating** group.
  - **Avoid the position *between* two groups in a 1,3-pattern** — **too sterically hindered** (steric effect, LO 11.3b-iii).
  - **Two similar activators (e.g. –CH₃ and –C₂H₅)** → electrophile directed to several positions → **no clear major product** (poor yield).
- **Reason (background, not in syllabus but clarifies):** comes down to **carbocation-intermediate stability**. For activators, the 2-/4- intermediates have a resonance form with **+ on the substituted carbon, stabilised** by the donating group (e.g. tertiary, or lone-pair dispersal) → form faster → major. For deactivators, that same form puts **+ on the carbon bearing the withdrawing group, destabilised** → so 3- is preferred instead.

## UNIT 4 — Reactions of methylbenzene & other substituted benzenes (LO 11.4d/e/f)

**Methylbenzene (toluene) = C₆H₅CH₃** — colourless liquid, insoluble in water, **less toxic than benzene**. Has **two reaction sites: the ring (electrophilic substitution) and the alkyl side-chain (free-radical substitution + oxidation).**

### Ring electrophilic substitutions
- Same three reactions as benzene, but **conditions are milder** (–CH₃ is activating) and **products are 2,4-directed**:
  - **Halogenation:** Cl₂/AlCl₃ (or Br₂/AlBr₃), **rt, absence of UV** *(rt + no UV prevents side-chain free-radical substitution)* → 2-/4-halomethylbenzene.
  - **Nitration:** conc HNO₃ + conc H₂SO₄, **maintained at 30 °C** (vs 50 °C for benzene) → 2-/4-nitromethylbenzene.
  - **Friedel-Crafts alkylation:** chloroalkane/AlCl₃, **rt.**

### 5.6.1 Side-chain free-radical substitution (LO 11.4e-i, 11.4f) ★
- **Reagents/conditions:** **Cl₂(g) or Br₂(l), UV light, room temperature.** The alkyl side-chain **behaves like an alkane** (Topic 10 mechanism: initiation/propagation/termination).
- **Observation:** greenish-yellow Cl₂ / reddish-brown Br₂ **decolourises slowly.**
- Mono-substitution favoured by **limited halogen / excess alkylbenzene**; **excess halogen** → more than one X on the side-chain.
- **Conditions are the decider (LO 11.4f): catalyst (AlX₃) + no UV → ring substitution; UV + no catalyst → side-chain FRS.** Under reflux *with AlCl₃*, **both** can occur (ring ES + side-chain FRS).

### 5.6.2 Side-chain oxidation (LO 11.4e-ii) ★
- **Two routes to benzoic acid:**
  - **Hot acidified KMnO₄** (KMnO₄(aq), dilute H₂SO₄, heat) → **benzoic acid** directly. Observation: **purple KMnO₄ decolourises + white precipitate of benzoic acid.**
  - **Hot alkaline KMnO₄** (KMnO₄(aq), dilute NaOH, heat) → **sodium benzoate** (obs: purple decolourises + **brown ppt MnO₂**); **then add dilute H₂SO₄** → benzoic acid (white ppt).
- **Rule: the side-chain carbon directly attached to the ring must carry ≥1 H atom to be oxidised.** If so, **the whole side-chain is cut down to –CO₂H** (in acid) or **–CO₂⁻** (in alkali) **regardless of side-chain length**; any **further carbons are oxidised off as CO₂**. (e.g. ethylbenzene & propylbenzene both → benzoic acid + CO₂/carboxylic-acid fragments.)
- **No H on the attached C → no oxidation.** **tert-butylbenzene does NOT react** with hot acidified KMnO₄ (the attached C has no H) — KMnO₄ stays purple.
- **Balancing with [O]:** balance **H atoms using H₂O first, then O atoms using [O].**

### 6 Distinguishing tests
| Reagent | Distinguishes | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Br₂ in CCl₄** (or **Br₂(aq)**), dropwise | **alkene vs arene** | alkene **decolourises**; benzene **stays reddish-brown / yellow-orange**. (Use **absence of UV**, or Br₂(aq), to avoid free-radical substitution muddying it.) |
| **Hot acidified KMnO₄** | **terminal alkene vs methylbenzene** | alkene → oxidative cleavage: **CO₂ effervescence + decolourises, NO white ppt**; methylbenzene → side-chain oxidation: **decolourises + white ppt benzoic acid, no effervescence** |
| **Hot acidified KMnO₄** | **methylbenzene vs tert-butylbenzene** | methylbenzene → **decolourises + white ppt**; tert-butylbenzene → **stays purple, no reaction** (no H on attached C) |

## TOP EXAM TRAPS (Topic 12)
- **Benzene C–C bond:** all six identical, **0.139 nm / 520 kJ mol⁻¹**, intermediate between single and double. Quote the **benzene-specific** Data Booklet value, not C–C or C=C.
- **The two alkene-vs-benzene differences:** (i) **less reactive / needs stronger electrophile / doesn't decolourise Br₂**, (ii) **substitution not addition (preserves aromaticity)** — both traced to **delocalisation/extra stability.**
- **Mechanism marks:** step 1 is **slow/rds because the delocalised π system is disrupted → high Ea**; **2 arrows in step 1, 1 arrow in step 2**; step 2 a **Brønsted base removes H⁺ and restores aromaticity.**
- **Always write the electrophile-generating step** and name the catalyst's role: **AlX₃/FeX₃ = Lewis acid** (electron-pair acceptor) for halogenation/Friedel-Crafts; **conc H₂SO₄ = Brønsted-Lowry acid** for nitration (generates **NO₂⁺**).
- **Halogenation must be anhydrous** (water hydrolyses the Lewis acid). **Fe/Al are not the catalyst** — they make AlX₃/FeX₃ in situ.
- **Friedel-Crafts only with alkyl halides, never aryl halides.**
- **–OH/–NH₂ = activating, 2,4-directing** (delocalisation wins); **halogens = deactivating BUT still 2,4-directing** (the exception); **–NO₂/–CHO/–CO₂H = deactivating, 3-directing.** Position is set by the group **already on the ring.**
- **Methylbenzene conditions:** ring halogenation **rt + no UV**; side-chain FRS **UV + rt, no catalyst.** Mixing them up loses the mark (and the product site).
- **Side-chain oxidation:** whole chain → **–CO₂H regardless of length**, but **only if the attached C has ≥1 H**. **tert-butylbenzene doesn't oxidise.**
- Nitration temps: **benzene 50 °C, methylbenzene 30 °C** (activated ring → milder).
