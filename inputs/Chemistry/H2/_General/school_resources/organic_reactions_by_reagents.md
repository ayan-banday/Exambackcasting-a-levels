# Organic Chemistry Reactions by Reagents — HCI reference (VERBATIM source)

> Transcribed verbatim from the Hwa Chong "Organic Chemistry Reactions by Reagents" reference (PDF in this folder). **This is the ground-truth source for reagents, conditions, reaction types and observations for EVERY organic cluster.** Build/refine all organic study sheets against this — reagents and conditions must match word-for-word. Organised by reagent (1–22).

## 1. Halogens, X₂
| Functional group | Reagents & conditions | Type | Product / observation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alkane | **Cl₂(g), UV light** (homolytic fission Cl–Cl → 2Cl•) | Free-radical substitution | Mixture of products (multi-substitution), not for synthesis. Ratio depends on stability of alkyl radicals. Cannot occur in aqueous medium (polar, no homolytic fission). |
| Alkene | **Br₂(l)** (induced dipole δ+Br–Brδ−) | Electrophilic addition | 1,2-dibromo product. **Reddish-brown Br₂ decolourised.** |
| Alkene | **Br₂(aq)** | Electrophilic addition | **Bromohydrin** (–Br + –OH on adjacent C) + some dibromide. **Yellow-orange Br₂(aq) decolourised.** |
| Arene | **Br₂, FeBr₃, warm** | Electrophilic substitution | Bromobenzene. FeBr₃ = Lewis acid catalyst (e⁻-pair acceptor) generates Br⁺; warm to overcome arene's resonance stability. Not aqueous (FeBr₃ dissociates). |
| Arene (methylbenzene) | **Br₂, FeBr₃, no heat** (–CH₃ e⁻-donating/activating via inductive effect) | Electrophilic substitution | 2-bromo + 4-bromomethylbenzene (–CH₃ is 2,4-directing). |
| Phenol | **Br₂(l)** (–OH e⁻-donating via resonance) | Electrophilic substitution | 2-bromo + 4-bromophenol. Ring more e⁻-rich → mono-substitution in organic solvent. –OH is 2,4-directing. |
| Phenol | **Br₂(aq)** (stronger electrophile due to water) | Electrophilic substitution | **2,4,6-tribromophenol, white ppt.** Yellow-orange Br₂(aq) decolourised. |
| Phenylamine | **Br₂(aq)** | Electrophilic substitution | **2,4,6-tribromophenylamine, white ppt.** Yellow-orange Br₂(aq) decolourised. |
| Alcohol with CH₃CH(OH)– structure | **I₂(aq), NaOH(aq), warm** (oxidising agent, [O] by iodine) | Oxidation (tri-iodomethane test) | CH₃COO⁻Na⁺ + **CHI₃ (yellow ppt)**. Temp <60 °C. Step-down (shortens chain by 1 C). |
| Aldehyde/ketone with CH₃CO– structure | **I₂(aq), NaOH(aq), warm** | Oxidation | CH₃COO⁻Na⁺ + CHI₃ (yellow ppt). Carboxylic acid obtained by adding strong acid (H₂SO₄) after. |

## 2. Hydrogen halides, HX
| Functional group | Reagents & conditions | Type | Remarks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alkene | **HX(g)** (X = Cl, Br, I) | Electrophilic addition | Consider **Markovnikov's rule** for major product. |
| Alcohol | **Concentrated HX (X = Cl, Br), heat** | Nucleophilic substitution | HBr generated in situ from NaBr + conc H₂SO₄; **ZnCl₂ catalyst** for 1°/2° alcohols + HCl; **no heating for tertiary** alcohols. |

## 3. Hydrogen cyanide, HCN (⁻:C≡N nucleophile)
| Functional group | Reagents & conditions | Type | Remarks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aldehyde/ketone | **HCN, trace KCN, cold** (HCN = weak acid/proton source; KCN = catalyst/source of :CN⁻; because HCN is volatile & toxic) | Nucleophilic addition | → cyanohydrin. **Step-up** (lengthens chain by 1 C, adds a functional group). |

## 4. Potassium cyanide, KCN (K⁺ ⁻:C≡N nucleophile)
| Functional group | Reagents & conditions | Type | Remarks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Halogenoalkane | **Ethanolic KCN, heat** (ethanol = solvent) | Nucleophilic substitution | → nitrile. **Step-up** (+1 C). Note: **alcohols do NOT react with KCN.** |

## 5. Nitric acid, HNO₃
| Functional group | Reagents & conditions | Type | Remarks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arene | **Conc HNO₃, conc H₂SO₄, 55 °C** | Electrophilic substitution | Conc H₂SO₄ = Brønsted acid catalyst, generates **NO₂⁺** electrophile. → nitrobenzene. |
| Arene (methylbenzene) | **Conc HNO₃, conc H₂SO₄, 30 °C** | Electrophilic substitution | Lower temp because e⁻-donating –CH₃ activates the ring. → 2-nitro + 4-nitromethylbenzene. |
| Phenol | **Conc HNO₃** | Electrophilic substitution | **2,4,6-trinitrophenol** (tri-substitution with conc). Lone pair delocalises into ring → more e⁻-rich. |
| Phenol | **HNO₃(aq)** (dilute) | Electrophilic substitution | 2-nitro + 4-nitrophenol. |

## 6. Concentrated sulfuric acid, H₂SO₄ (catalyst / dehydrating agent / acid)
| Functional group | Reagents & conditions | Type | Remarks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alkene | **1. Conc H₂SO₄ cold, 2. water** (hydration, lab) | Electrophilic addition | → alcohol (Markovnikov). Alternative (industrial): steam, conc H₃PO₄ catalyst, high T & P. |
| Alcohol | **Excess conc H₂SO₄, heat** | Elimination (dehydration) | → alkene + H₂O. **Excess** so it acts as dehydrating agent (else diluted by H₂O). Consider constitutional + cis-trans isomerism. Alternative: Al₂O₃, heat. |
| Alcohol + carboxylic acid | **a few drops conc H₂SO₄, heat** | Condensation (esterification) | → ester + H₂O. Conc H₂SO₄ = catalyst (few drops only, so it doesn't dehydrate the alcohol). |

## 7. Dilute acids — H₂SO₄(aq), HCl(aq), HNO₃(aq)  [dilute = acid(aq)]
| Functional group | Reagents & conditions | Type | Product |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nitrile | **H⁺(aq), heat** | Hydrolysis | → carboxylic acid |
| Ester | **H⁺(aq), heat** (reagent is water) | Hydrolysis | → carboxylic acid + alcohol |
| Amide | **H⁺(aq), heat** | Hydrolysis | → carboxylic acid + NH₄⁺ (or RNH₃⁺) |
| Amine (basic) | **H⁺(aq)** | Acid-base | → alkylammonium salt. Amine regenerated by adding strong base, e.g. NaOH(aq). |

## 8. Sodium hydroxide, NaOH
| Functional group | Reagents & conditions | Type | Product / remarks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nitrile | **NaOH(aq), heat** | Hydrolysis | → carboxylate salt (RCOO⁻Na⁺) |
| Ester | **NaOH(aq), heat** (catalyst; reagent is H₂O) | Hydrolysis | → carboxylate salt + alcohol |
| Amide | **NaOH(aq), heat** | Hydrolysis | → carboxylate salt + NH₃ (or RNH₂). Distinguishing test: **NH₃ turns red litmus blue.** |
| Halogenoalkane | **NaOH(aq), heat** (OH⁻ = nucleophile) | Nucleophilic substitution | → alcohol. If then excess HNO₃(aq) + AgNO₃(aq): **AgCl white / AgBr cream / AgI yellow** ppt. |
| Halogenoalkane | **Ethanolic NaOH, heat** (NaOH = base) | Elimination | → alkene. Consider constitutional + cis-trans isomerism. |
| Phenol | **NaOH(aq)** (phenol = weak acid, emulsion) | Acid-base | → sodium phenoxide + H₂O (clear solution). Phenol regenerated by strong acid, e.g. HCl(aq). |
| Carboxylic acid | **NaOH(aq)** | Acid-base | → carboxylate salt. Regenerated by strong acid. |

## 9. Sodium, Na

|---|---|---|---|
| Alcohol | **Na(s)** | Acid-metal | → alkoxide + **H₂ effervescence** |
| Phenol | **Na(s)** | Acid-metal | → sodium phenoxide + H₂ |
| Carboxylic acid | **Na(s)** | Acid-metal | → carboxylate + H₂ |

## 10. Sodium carbonate, Na₂CO₃
| Functional group | Reagents & conditions | Type | Remarks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carboxylic acid | **Na₂CO₃(aq)** | Acid-carbonate | → carboxylate + **CO₂ effervescence**. Occurs because carboxylic acid is a stronger acid than carbonic acid. **Distinguishing test between alcohol and carboxylic acid** (phenol does NOT react — weaker acid than carbonic acid). |

## 11. Ammonia, NH₃ (weak base)
| Functional group | Reagents & conditions | Type | Remarks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carboxylic acid | **NH₃(aq)** | Acid-base | → ammonium carboxylate. Does **not** form an amide (NH₃ volatile, escapes). |
| Halogenoalkane | **Ethanolic NH₃, heat under high pressure** (NH₃ = nucleophile) | Nucleophilic substitution | → amine. The amine can act as a nucleophile → further substitution with RX. |
| Acyl chloride | **NH₃(l) or NH₃(g)** | Nucleophilic acyl substitution | → amide + HCl. NH₃(aq) can't be used (acyl chloride hydrolysed); ethanolic NH₃ can't (ethanol forms ester); use excess NH₃ (HCl reacts with NH₃). |

## 12. Potassium manganate(VII), KMnO₄ (oxidising agent)
| Functional group | Reagents & conditions | Type | Product / observation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alkene | **KMnO₄, H₂SO₄(aq), heat** | Oxidative cleavage | carbonyls / CO₂. **Purple decolourised** (+ CO₂ effervescence if terminal =CH₂). |
| Alkene | **KMnO₄, NaOH(aq), cold** | Mild oxidation | diol. **Purple → green, brown MnO₂ ppt** (under alkaline conditions). |
| Arene (methylbenzene) | **KMnO₄, H₂SO₄(aq), heat** | Side-chain oxidation | benzoic acid (white ppt). Purple decolourised. |
| Arene (ethylbenzene) | **KMnO₄, H₂SO₄(aq), heat** | Side-chain oxidation | benzoic acid + CO₂. Purple decolourised, CO₂ effervescence. |
| Aldehyde | **KMnO₄, H₂SO₄(aq), heat** | Oxidation | carboxylic acid. Purple decolourised. |
| Primary alcohol | **KMnO₄, H₂SO₄(aq), heat** | Oxidation | carboxylic acid. |
| Secondary alcohol | **KMnO₄, H₂SO₄(aq), heat** | Oxidation | ketone. |

## 22. Acyl chloride, RCOCl (carbonyl C highly e⁻-deficient → prone to nucleophilic attack)

|---|---|---|---|
