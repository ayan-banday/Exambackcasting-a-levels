### 1. 2011 RI H2 P1 (9647 MCQ, 40 Q, 1 hour) — DEEP READ

- **Format:** 40 MCQ, no negative marking, 1 hour. Same length as current 9729 P1 (30 MCQ in 1 hr) but 40 questions — more time pressure per Q.
- **Sample stems:**
  - Q1: Empirical formula from oxide masses (M:O = 7:3) — formula deduction. **[Topic: Stoichiometry, Level 3]**
  - Q3: Bromine trifluoride auto-ionisation (`2BrF3 → BrF2+ + BrF4–`) — shape/lone pair reasoning. **[Topic: VSEPR, Level 4]**
  - Q4: Which property CANNOT be explained by H-bonding — HF2–, alcohol volatility, nitrophenol mp difference, ethanoic acid in benzene. **[Topic: IMF, Level 4 — exemplar]**
  - Q6: Deuterium gas vs unknown X — relative molecular mass from ideal gas equivalence. **[Topic: Gases, Level 3]**
  - Q9: Ellingham diagram for C/CO/CO2 oxidation — assign reactions to lines. **[Topic: Thermo / unfamiliar context, Level 5]**
  - Q10: Radioactive decay first-order kinetics — half-lives M=3d N=2d, given equal amounts at 12d, find ratio at 6d. **[Topic: Kinetics, Level 4]**
- **Reusable for 9729?** 70% reusable — Period 3, IMF, VSEPR, energetics all evergreen. Skip questions framed around outdated mark conventions.

### H3 Chem difficulty markers transferable to H2

**How Cambridge constructs hard Chem questions** (drawn from 2021 RI H3):

1. **Insert-based context wrapping.** Question wraps around a research paper / scientific article excerpt. Student must extract relevant chemistry from text. H2 Free Response sometimes does this — practice handling unfamiliar context.

2. **Quantitative reasoning under partial data.** Given pKa values, predict equilibrium positions in multi-step systems. Requires combining LeChat with acid-base equilibria. H2 P3 Q on ionic eqm uses similar logic.

3. **Cross-topic integration in one question.** Single H3 part-question may demand: structure + bonding + equilibrium + kinetics + mechanism analysis. H2 P3 final parts (8-10m) often demand similar integration.

4. **Mechanism in unfamiliar chemistry.** H3 asks for curly-arrow mechanism on inorganic systems (e.g., silicate condensation). H2 sometimes asks novel mechanism on familiar functional group in unusual context. Same skill — apply curly arrow logic to novel substrate.

5. **Open-ended Bloom Level 5/6 evaluation.** "Suggest one positive and one negative impact" / "Justify the choice of...". H2 P3 high-mark parts ask for similar evaluation. Practice argued, balanced answers, not just lists.

**5 examples of hard-question construction techniques:**

1. **Multi-step synthesis problem with masked product:** Give organic mol formula + clues from 3-4 reactions → deduce 4-5 structures simultaneously. (2011 HCI P3 Q2 rose ketones, 2011 RI P3 Q1 halogen)
2. **Data table → multi-part calculation chain:** Group 2 data table → lattice energy + thermal stability + Ksp + Eo cycle in one Q. (2011 RI P2 Q3)
3. **Unfamiliar context wrapping:** Surfactant micelles / feldspar weathering / Nernst at non-standard cells. Force application of basic principles to new system. (2011 HCI P3 Q1, 2011 RI P3 Q3)
4. **Mechanism + electronic effect explanation:** Ask for mechanism then explain regioselectivity using electronegativity + electronic + delocalisation. (2020 HCI Promo P2 Q2 squalene + ICl)
5. **Cross-topic integration:** Period 3 → ionic eqm → buffer → titration curve sketching → final pH after addition. (2011 RI P3 Q2 histidine)
