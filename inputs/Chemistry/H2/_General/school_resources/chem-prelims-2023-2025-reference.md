## MS conventions (from spot-check of mark schemes in same JC folders)
- Accept variants of synonyms ("greater" / "more" / "higher" all accept for trends).
- Reject answers without state symbols when an equation is asked.
- Mechanism marks: 1 mark per **correctly drawn curly arrow** + 1 for intermediate + 1 for product step.
- "State and explain" carries 2 marks always: 1 for the observation/state, 1 for the explanation tied to it.
- Numerical answers: full marks only with **3 sig fig minimum** and **correct units**.
- For energy cycles: 1 mark for cycle construction, 1 for using correct Hess's Law direction (arrows), remaining for arithmetic.

### MCQ patterns (P1)
- **30 Qs / 30 marks / 1 hour. 1 mark each. No deduction for wrong answer.**
- **A/B/C/D format only.** Always 4 options.
- Topic split typical: ~12 Physical, ~6 Inorganic, ~12 Organic.
- **Common trap types:**
  - Multi-statement (1, 2, 3 — choose which combination is correct) — appears in ~6-8 Qs per paper. Forces evaluation of each statement independently.
  - "Use of the Data Booklet is relevant" flag appears ~5-7 times per paper. Always cross-check ionic radii, E° values, hydration enthalpies.
  - "is **not** correct" / "is **unlikely** to exist" — appears ~2-3 times per paper.
  - "highest yield" / "best route" reagent-order questions — at least 1 per paper.
  - "always" qualifier in stem — testing edge cases, often false.
  - Graph-pattern questions (sketch which graph matches): 1-2 per paper.
  - Isomer-counting Qs (including stereoisomers): 1 per paper usually.
- **Topic guarantees in P1:**
  - Atomic structure with isotopes: Q1-3 usually
  - Born-Haber / lattice energy comparison: 1 Q always
  - At least one mechanism/rate-eq derivation Q
  - At least one Ksp / equilibrium Q
  - At least one Period 3 / Group 2 / Group 17 / Transition metal Q each
  - At least one organic FG-test distinguish Q
  - At least one organic synthesis multi-step Q

### Structured Q patterns (P2)
- **75 marks / 2 hours / 5 questions (variable per-Q marks 10-25).**
- Each Q opens with a topic-anchored context (industrial process, biological molecule, environmental data, novel compound).
- **Mark allocation conventions:**
  - Q1 typically 10-15 marks: Atomic structure / Periodic trends / Bonding. Often opens with IE table, isoelectronic comparison, or VSEPR shape deduction.
  - Q2 typically 13-18 marks: Reaction kinetics OR thermodynamics. Always involves a calculation from data (rate eq derivation, ∆H calc, etc.).
  - Q3 typically 14-17 marks: Organic — either a single FG with mechanism, or multi-FG reaction map (often pyruvic acid-like multi-functional molecule).
  - Q4 typically 14-22 marks: Equilibria / Ksp / electrolysis / data-analysis with PSI/concentration calcs.
  - Q5 typically 15-25 marks: Electrochemistry (Zn-air, mercury cell, fuel cell) OR organic synthesis with mechanism and structure elucidation.
- **Data-analysis conventions:**
  - Energy cycles always require explicit Hess's Law triangle with directional arrows.
  - Rate-eq derivation always presents a table with 3-4 experiments. Always test order in each reagent first, then write rate eq, then determine units of k.
  - Titration curves: identify equivalence pt, indicator choice, pH at half-eq.
  - pH curves: locate buffer region (flat part), endpoint, calculate Ka.
- **Multi-step calculation conventions:**
  - Always carry 4 sig figs through intermediate steps; final to 3 sig figs.
  - Always state units explicitly in final answer.
  - For Faraday calcs: Q=It → n(e)=Q/F → n(product)=n(e)/z → mass=n×Mr.
- **Diagram conventions:**
  - Dot-and-cross diagrams: show lone pairs, central atom labelled.
  - Curly arrows: start from bond OR lone pair; end at atom OR midpoint of new bond. Half-headed arrows for radicals.
  - VSEPR shapes: state bond angle in degrees (e.g., 104.5° for bent water).
  - Orbital diagrams: use ↑↓ notation in boxes.

### Free Response patterns (P3)
- **80 marks / 2 hours / Section A all 3 × 20m + Section B answer 1 of 2 × 20m.**
- **Section A composition (always 3 Qs, 20 marks each):**
  - Q1: Integrated Physical (gas + thermo + kinetics) anchored by an industrial / applied context.
  - Q2: Equilibrium (Kp/Kc/Ksp/buffer) AND/OR Transition Element complex.
  - Q3: Organic synthesis with mechanism AND structure elucidation (multi-step).
- **Section B options (always Q4 OR Q5, 20 marks each):**
  - Q4: Inorganic-heavy (Group 17 / Group 2 / Period 3 / Ksp deep dive).
  - Q5: Organic-heavy (amino acids / buffer chemistry / pharmacological drug / amide chemistry).
- **Organic synthesis pathway conventions:**
  - "Suggest reagents and conditions" — must include: reagent name AND/OR formula, solvent (aqueous/ethanolic/dry), temperature (cold/warm/heat/reflux), catalyst if needed.
  - Multi-step routes always score 1 mark per step including correct intermediate.
  - "Draw structure of product" — skeletal OR displayed; must show all stereochemistry if specified ("3-dimensional structure").
- **Mechanism drawing conventions:**
  - SN1: 3 steps — leaving group departs (curly arrow C-X bond to X), carbocation intermediate with stereochemistry note (planar), nucleophile attack with curly arrow.
  - SN2: 1 step — concurrent nucleophile attack + leaving group departure, transition state with partial bonds, **back-side attack** noted.
  - E1: carbocation formation then proton loss (curly arrow from C-H bond to π bond).
  - E2: concerted — single transition state with 4 partial bonds, antiperiplanar arrangement.
  - Electrophilic addition (alkenes): π bond opens to electrophile, carbocation intermediate, nucleophile attacks (e.g., HBr to alkene — Markovnikov via more stable carbocation).
  - Electrophilic substitution (arenes): electrophile + AlCl3 / FeBr3 catalyst generation, Wheland intermediate (sp3 carbon, ring + above + below), deprotonation to restore aromaticity.
  - Nucleophilic addition (carbonyls): Nu attacks δ+ C of C=O, C-O bond breaks to lone pair on O, protonation step.
  - Nucleophilic acyl substitution (carb. acid derivatives): tetrahedral intermediate then leaving group departs.
- **Reagent/condition specificity required:**
  - Oxidation: "K2Cr2O7, H2SO4, heat" (mild for aldehyde, reflux for carboxylic acid).
  - Reduction: "LiAlH4 in dry ether" (NEVER aqueous) — reduces carboxylic acid, ester, amide, nitrile, carbonyl. "H2, Ni catalyst" for C=C only OR carbonyl with high T/P.
  - Tollens: "Ag(NH3)2+, NH3, warm" — silver mirror with aldehydes.
  - Fehling: "Cu2+/tartrate complex, warm" — brick-red Cu2O ppt with aliphatic aldehydes only (NOT benzaldehyde).
  - 2,4-DNPH: "2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine" — orange ppt with all carbonyls.
  - Iodoform: "I2 in NaOH(aq), warm" — yellow ppt with CH3CO-R or CH3CH(OH)-R structure.
  - Bromine water: "Br2(aq)" — decolourisation with alkenes/alkynes/phenols (white ppt with phenol).
  - Diazotisation: "HNO2 (NaNO2 + HCl), <10°C" then coupling with phenol in NaOH.
  - SN of halogenoalkane to alcohol: "NaOH(aq), warm" (substitution); for elimination: "KOH or NaOH in ethanol, heat" (elimination).
- **Multi-topic integration norms:**
  - Section A Q1 always integrates ≥2 Physical topics (gas + thermo + kinetics typical).
  - Section A Q3 integrates organic mechanism + structure elucidation + reaction prediction.
  - Section B Q5 organic integrates buffer/pH + amino acid + amide chemistry + drug application.
- **Structure elucidation Qs** (often 5-7 marks, near-guaranteed Q3 (e)):
  - "Suggest structures and explain the reactions described" — must give: 1 mark each for structure identification + 1 mark each for explaining a specific reaction observation.
  - Common clues: degree of unsaturation, FG tests (Br2 water, 2,4-DNPH, Tollens, Na2CO3 effervescence, NaOH/I2 yellow ppt).

### Calibration rules for paper generation (most important)

**Paper structure (must match exactly):**
- P1: 30 MCQs / 30 marks / 1h. Topic ratio ~12P / 6I / 12O. Mix multi-statement, Data Booklet, "is not", reagent order, graph-pattern.
- P2: 5 structured Qs / 75 marks total / 2h. Per-Q range 10-25 marks. Sum to 75.
- P3: Section A 3 × 20m + Section B (Q4 OR Q5) × 20m / 80 marks / 2h. Section B presents two options.
- P4: 55 marks / 2.5h. Planning + qualitative + quantitative.

**Topic balance per paper:**
- Each paper must touch all 3 super-clusters: Physical, Inorganic, Organic.
- Each Section B (P3) must offer one Inorganic-heavy AND one Organic-heavy option.
- Cross-topic integration mandatory in P3 Section A Q1.
- Industrial / applied context required for P3 Q1 and P3 Q2.

**Mark allocation conventions:**
- 1 mark per curly arrow in mechanism.
- 2 marks for "state and explain" (1 for state, 1 for explanation tied to it).
- 3-5 marks for energy cycle construction + calculation.
- 5-7 marks for structure elucidation Q.
- 1 mark per correct reagent + condition in synthesis route.
- 2 marks for "describe and explain" trend questions.

**Mechanism drawing standards:**
- Curly arrows must start at electron source (bond or lone pair) and end at electron sink (atom or bond midpoint).
- All intermediates shown explicitly with formal charges.
- Lone pairs shown on atoms where chemically meaningful (e.g., LP on O in carbonyl Nu addition).
- Stereochemistry indicated where relevant (wedge/dash) — especially SN1 vs SN2 product distinction.

**Reagent specificity standards:**
- ALWAYS specify temperature: cold (<10°C) / RT / warm / hot / heat under reflux.
- ALWAYS specify solvent: aqueous (aq) / ethanolic / dry ether / dry / no solvent.
- ALWAYS specify catalyst where applicable: AlCl3 / FeBr3 / Ni / Pt / H2SO4.
- For oxidation: state oxidising agent + conditions (e.g., "K2Cr2O7, dilute H2SO4, reflux" for primary alcohol → carboxylic acid).
- For reduction: "LiAlH4 in dry ether" (never aqueous); "H2 / Ni" for C=C.

**Common error patterns to embed as trap distractors:**
- Confusing E° sign convention (more positive = more easily reduced).
- Forgetting state symbols in equations.
- Using "stronger / weaker" without specifying what attribute.
- Stating "exothermic" without referencing bond breaking vs forming.
- Predicting electrophilic addition for arenes (should be substitution).
- Confusing SN1/SN2: forgetting that tertiary halogenoalkanes prefer SN1 and primary prefer SN2.
- Confusing Fehling (no benzaldehyde) vs Tollens (yes benzaldehyde).
- For Group 2 trends: confusing direction of thermal stability (carbonates/nitrates DECREASE up the group cf. Mg vs Ba decomp temperature).
- For Group 17: confusing reducing power of halides (I- strongest) vs oxidising power of halogens (F2 strongest).
