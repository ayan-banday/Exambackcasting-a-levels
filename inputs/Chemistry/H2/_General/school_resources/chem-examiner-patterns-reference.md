## 2. The 9729 Syllabus Topics

**ORGANIC:**
- Intro to Organic (isomerism, stereo, chirality, E/Z)

- Nitrogen Compounds (amines, amides, amino acids, proteins basic)
- Polymers (addition + condensation; biodegradability)
- Spectroscopy (mass spec, IR, NMR in structure elucidation)

## 4. P3 Section A Standard Architecture

From the 2023-2025 reference (locked-in pattern, corroborated by 2020-2022 and 2017-2019):

- **Q1: Physical integrated** (gas + thermo + kinetics in industrial / applied context). Always synoptic. Often the highest-mark Q in Section A (19-23 marks).
- **Q2: Equilibrium + Transition Element.** Often Kp/Kc/Le Chatelier paired with complex shape/colour/ligand. Always involves a calculation from data.
- **Q3: Organic synthesis + structure elucidation** (multi-step). Mechanism plus deduction of structures from observation table.

**P3 Section B (choice of 1 of 2):**
- **Q4: Inorganic-heavy** (often deduction puzzle on Group 2/17 or Period 3; sometimes paired with Ksp depth)
- **Q5: Organic-heavy** ("Compound deduction puzzle" is the recurring stem; or amino acid + buffer + drug application combo)

**Rule:** Both Section B options must contain organic synthesis OR deduction OR mechanism OR multi-step physical reasoning. One should be "deduce-the-structure-from-clues" style, the other "describe-and-explain-the-mechanism" style.

## 5. Most Recurring Single Questions

From the 2023-2025 reference (recent recurrence, near-certain for 2026):

- **HCl/HBr/HI thermal stability** (appears in 2025 RI P3, 2025 HCI P2, 2025 VJC P3, 2021 RI P3) — near-certain. Mark scheme: bond dissociation energy decreases down Group 17 due to less effective orbital overlap between 1s of H and p of halogen as orbitals become more diffuse. HF > HCl > HBr > HI.
- **Group 2 thermal decomposition of carbonates/nitrates** — describe trend + explain via polarising power of cation. Canonical 4-step mark scheme (see Section 7).
- **Energy cycle for ΔHc of NH3 / hydrocarbon** using ΔHf + ΔHvap + Data Booklet (5-mark cycle, P3 Q1).
- **Transition metal complex colour explanation** (d-d transitions; near-certain Section A Q2).
- **Amino acid pH / electrophoresis / pI calculation** (near-certain Section B Q5).
- **Period 3 oxide/chloride pH** (Na2O basic; Al2O3 amphoteric; SiO2 acidic; MgCl2 pH ~6.5; AlCl3 pH ~3; SiCl4 pH ~2).
- **Hydrolysis ease order: acyl chloride > benzyl chloride > chlorobenzene** — canonical 3-paragraph mark scheme (2013 P3 Q3e / 2022 P3 Q1, verbatim).
- **Amine basicity comparison** (alkyl 3°>2°>1°>NH3; aryl < NH3 due to delocalisation; aryl+EWG < aryl).

## 6. Recurring Exam-Style Stems (from Cambridge actual + prelims)

**Inorganic:**
- "Explain the trend in [thermal stability / solubility / oxidising power] of the Group [2/17] [compounds]."
- "Suggest why [property X of compound A] is [higher/lower] than that of [compound B]."
- "Describe and explain the variation in melting points of the Period 3 oxides."
- "Write equations for the reactions of [compound] with water / NaOH(aq) / H⁺(aq)."
- "State the type of bonding present in… and explain its physical properties."

**Energetics:**
- "Calculate the standard enthalpy change of [reaction/formation/atomisation/lattice energy/hydration]."
- "Construct an energy cycle to determine…"
- "Predict and explain the sign of ΔS for the reaction."
- "Determine whether the reaction is spontaneous at 298 K. Show your working."

**Kinetics:**
- "Determine the order of reaction with respect to [reactant X] using the data."
- "Suggest a rate equation for the reaction."
- "Draw the Boltzmann distribution at T1 and T2 (T2 > T1)."
- "Explain why the reaction occurs faster when temperature is increased."
- "Suggest a possible mechanism that is consistent with the rate equation."

**Equilibria:**
- "Calculate Kc/Kp for the reaction."
- "Predict and explain the effect on the equilibrium position when [pressure/temperature/concentration] is changed."
- "Calculate the pH of [buffer / weak acid / salt solution]."
- "Calculate the solubility of [sparingly soluble salt] in [solvent]."

**Electrochemistry:**
- "Calculate the standard cell potential for…"
- "Determine the feasibility of the reaction. Justify your answer."
- "Calculate the time taken / mass deposited in the electrolysis."
- "Predict the products at the anode and cathode."

**Organic:**
- "Suggest a mechanism for the reaction. Include all relevant lone pairs, partial charges, and curly arrows."
- "Identify compounds [A, B, C…] showing reasoning."
- "Outline how you would synthesise [target] from [starting material] in not more than [n] steps."
- "Explain why [compound X] is more / less [acidic / basic / reactive / stable] than [compound Y]."
- "Suggest a chemical test to distinguish [compound A] from [compound B]. State the reagents, conditions, and observations."
- "Deduce the structure of compound X given the following spectroscopic data..."
- "Write equations for the reactions in steps..."

**Transition metals:**
- "Explain why [compound] is coloured."
- "Account for the variable oxidation states of [TM]."
- "Suggest the shape of the complex and the type of isomerism shown."
- "Write the electronic configuration of [TM ion]."

**Generic:**
- "State and explain..." (2 marks typical)
- "Predict whether..."
- "Suggest..." (AO3 application — typically 2-3 marks)

## 7. Canonical Mark-Scheme Phrases (verbatim Cambridge)

**Period 3 / IE trend:** "Across Period 3, effective nuclear charge increases due to increasing nuclear charge (increasing no. of protons) and the relatively constant shielding effect (due to same no. of filled inner principal quantum shells of electrons). With increasing effective nuclear charge, the electrons are more strongly attracted by the nucleus."

**Group 17 oxidising power:** "Down Group 17, the value of E°(X2/X⁻) becomes less positive and the position of equilibrium of the reduction of X2 to X⁻ lies increasingly to the left. Hence, down the group, X2 has less tendency to be reduced and the oxidising power of X2 decreases."

**Group 2 carbonate thermal stability:** "Down Group 2, cationic radius increases, resulting in a lower charge density and weaker polarising power of the cations. Consequently, there is decreasing extent of distortion of the electron cloud of the CO3²⁻ anion and hence decreasing extent of weakening of covalent bonds within the CO3²⁻ anion. More heat energy is required to break the covalent bonds, causing the decomposition temperature to increase." (Same template applies to Group 2 nitrate and peroxide thermal stability — O2²⁻ instead of CO3²⁻.)

**Transition metal colour (d-d transitions):** "Due to the presence of ligands, the five 3d orbitals are split into two sets of different energy levels. Since the 3d subshell is partially filled, electrons from the lower-energy d orbitals can absorb energy corresponding to certain wavelengths from the visible spectrum and get promoted to the higher-energy d orbitals (d-d transitions). The colour observed is the complement of the colour absorbed."

**Catalyst definition:** "A catalyst is a substance that increases the rate of reaction by providing an alternate reaction pathway of lower activation energy, without itself undergoing any permanent chemical change."

**HCl/HBr/HI thermal stability:** "Thermal stability is related to the H–X bond strength. The more endothermic the bond dissociation energy of the H–X bond, the stronger the H–X bond and the more thermally stable HX. Down Group 17, the valence orbitals of a halogen become more diffuse and this results in less effective orbital overlap between the 1s orbital of H and the p orbital of the halogen." Order: HF > HCl > HBr > HI.

**Hydrolysis order: acyl chloride > benzyl chloride > chlorobenzene** (canonical 3-paragraph mark scheme):
1. Acyl chloride C is bonded to 2 electronegative atoms (Cl and =O), giving the largest +δ on carbonyl C, attracting nucleophiles most.
2. Steric: acyl chloride sp² trigonal planar (less hindered than benzyl sp³ tetrahedral).
3. Aryl chloride: p orbital of Cl overlaps with π electron cloud of benzene → partial double bond character → C–Cl strengthened; electron-rich ring repels nucleophiles.

**Amine basicity (canonical, multi-comparison):**
- "Basicity: CH3NH2 < (CH3)2NH < (CH3)3N. The number of electron-donating methyl groups bonded to N increases, so (CH3)3N has the highest electron density at the N atom and the lone pair is most readily available for coordination to a proton."
- Aryl: "Phenylamine is less basic than ammonia because of the delocalisation of the lone pair of electrons on N into the benzene ring."
- Aryl + EWG: "4-chlorophenylamine is less basic than phenylamine because the electron-withdrawing –Cl group further decreases the electron density on N."

**Amide neutrality:** "The p-orbital on N overlaps with the π-electron cloud of the C=O which allows the lone pair of electrons on N to delocalise into the C=O, reducing the availability of the lone pair on N to form a dative bond with H⁺."

**Ligand definition:** "Ligands act as Lewis bases by donating a lone pair of electrons into the low-lying vacant orbital of the central metal ion, forming a dative covalent / co-ordinate bond."

**Zwitterion:** "A zwitterion is an electrically neutral molecule with oppositely charged ends."

**BP/IMF templates (3 standard):**

A. **Same Mr, different IMFs:** Identify both IMFs → describe nature → compare strength → link to energy to overcome → link to BP.

B. **Same IMF type, different Mr/branching:** "Larger Mr means larger electron cloud, more polarisable, stronger id-id interactions, more energy to overcome, higher BP." Branching: less surface area → weaker id-id → lower BP.

C. **H-bond donor/acceptor comparison:** "[Compound X] has more –OH groups, average more H-bonds per molecule, more extensive H-bonding network, more energy to overcome, higher BP."

**Acidity templates (3 standard):**

A. **Conjugate base stability:** "The more stable the conjugate base, the stronger the acid (lower pKa, larger Ka)."

B. **Electron-withdrawing substituent:** "EWG disperses negative charge on conjugate base, stabilises it, acid is stronger."

C. **Electron-donating substituent:** "EDG intensifies negative charge on conjugate base, destabilises it, acid is weaker."

**Basicity templates (3 standard):**

A. **Alkyl substituent:** "EDG (alkyl) increases electron density on N atom, lone pair more available for coordination to H⁺, more basic. Ordering: 3° > 2° > 1° > NH3 (in gas phase)."

B. **Aryl group:** "Lone pair on N delocalises into benzene ring via p-orbital overlap with π electron cloud → less available → less basic than aliphatic amines."

C. **Aryl + EWG:** "EWG further reduces electron density via inductive (or further resonance withdrawal), even less basic than phenylamine."

## 8. Mechanism Conventions (Cambridge actual)

| Mechanism | Where used | Key conventions |
|---|---|---|
| **SN1** | 3° halide; benzyl/allyl halide | Slow step: C–X bond breaks heterolytically (curly arrow from bond to X); planar carbocation intermediate drawn; fast step: nucleophile lone pair attacks → racemic mixture if chiral. Usually NaOH(aq), heat. |
| **SN2** | 1° halide; methyl halide | Single concerted step; transition state with dashed bonds (5 partial bonds around C); backside attack; inversion of configuration explicitly shown. Usually OH⁻ aq, heat. |
| **E1** | Rare in actual; 3° halide | Slow step forms carbocation (same as SN1); fast step: base removes β-H, π bond forms. |
| **E2** | Halide + ethanolic base | Single concerted step; transition state with 4 partial bonds; antiperiplanar; ethanolic NaOH/KOH, heat under reflux. |
| **Electrophilic addition (EAdd)** | Alkenes + electrophiles | Slow step: π electrons of C=C attack electrophile (curly arrow from C=C to electrophile); carbocation intermediate; fast step: nucleophile attacks. Markovnikov: H adds to C with more H (more stable carbocation). Must justify carbocation stability for major product. |
| **Electrophilic substitution (ESub)** | Arenes | Catalyst generation first (e.g. Cl2 + AlCl3 ⇌ Cl⁺ + [AlCl4]⁻); slow step: 2 of 6 π electrons attack electrophile; cyclohexadienyl cation (Wheland intermediate) drawn with horseshoe arc above 5 sp² carbons + explicit sp³ centre; fast step: loss of H⁺ to restore aromaticity. |
| **Nucleophilic addition (NAdd)** | Carbonyls | Slow step: Nu lone pair attacks +δ carbonyl C; C=O π bond breaks → electrons to O; fast step: protonation of -O⁻. HCN with trace NaCN(aq) cold for cyanohydrin; LiAlH4 in dry ether OR NaBH4 in methanol for reduction. |
| **Nucleophilic acyl substitution (NAcylSub)** | Carb. acid derivatives | Two-step: addition then elimination; tetrahedral intermediate with C-O⁻ then leaving group leaves, C=O reformed. Reactivity: acyl chloride > ester > amide. |
| **Free radical substitution** | Alkane + halogen | Initiation: X2 → 2X• (UV or heat); propagation: X• + RH → R• + HX; R• + X2 → RX + X•; termination: any two radicals combine. Half-headed (fishhook) arrows. |

**Universal mechanism rules (Cambridge actual):**
- 1 mark per **correctly drawn curly arrow** + 1 for intermediate + 1 for product step
- Curly arrows show movement of **electron pairs** (full-headed) — half-headed for radicals
- Tail at electron source (bond or lone pair); head at electron sink (atom or bond midpoint)
- Partial charges (+δ, −δ) must be marked
- Lone pairs must be drawn on nucleophiles
- Transition states in SN2 drawn with dashed bonds + square bracket with ‡
- Carbocations drawn explicitly with + charge on C
- For aromatic mechanisms: cyclohexadienyl with horseshoe arc above sp² carbons + sp³ centre
- Stereochemistry indication required (R/S, cis/trans, E/Z); wedge/dash for chirality
- Carbocation stability flagging required: identify 1°/2°/3°, count electron-donating alkyl groups, dispersal via induction, more alkyl groups = more stable = forms faster = major product

| Alkaline hydrolysis of ester/amide | NaOH(aq), heat under reflux |

**Universal rules:**
- ALWAYS specify temperature: cold (<10°C) / RT / warm / hot / heat under reflux
- ALWAYS specify solvent: aqueous (aq) / ethanolic / dry ether / dry / no solvent
- ALWAYS specify catalyst where applicable: AlCl₃ / FeBr₃ / Ni / Pt / H₂SO₄
- For multi-step: "Step 1: reagents, conditions" / "Step 2: ..."

|---|---|
| "State and explain" | 2 marks (1 state + 1 explain) |
| "State" alone | 1 mark |
| "Curly arrow" in mechanism | 1 mark each |

**Universal rules:**
- Full marks only with 3 sig figs minimum AND correct units

- Reject answers without state symbols when equation asked
- Energy cycle MUST include explicit Hess triangle with directional arrows
- Mark scheme accepts variant synonyms ("greater" / "more" / "higher" all OK for trends)

## 11. P4 Practical Patterns (Ethan's October prep)

**Standard 9729/04 structure (55 marks / 2.5 h, 3-4 tasks):**

**Task 1 — Quantitative (volumetric titration or thermochemistry):** 13-16 marks
- Common: acid-base titration, iodometric back-titration, redox titration (KMnO₄ + reducing agent), Ksp determination, thermochemistry from ΔT data
- Plot data, calculate concentrations, identify systematic errors (heat loss especially), suggest improvements
- Examples: ethanedioate determination (HCI20), Ca(IO3)2 Ksp (RI21), iron(II) oxidation extent (VJC21), basicity of acid via thermometry (HCI22)

**Task 2 — Qualitative analysis (QA):** 14-16 marks
- Identify cations + anions in unknown salts
- Always provides QA notes table at back of paper
- Cation tests with NaOH and NH3 dropwise to excess
- Anion tests: BaCl₂/HCl for SO₄, AgNO₃/HNO₃ for halides, HNO₃/Cu/heat for NO₃
- Organic FG tests: 2,4-DNPH, Tollens, Fehling, NaOH/I₂, NaHCO₃, Br₂(aq), Na metal

**Task 3 — Reaction kinetics (sampling) OR 2nd quantitative:** 14-16 marks
- Iodine clock with IO₃⁻ + sulfur ion; KMnO₄ + reducing agent (glucose, ethanedioate); temperature-dependent rate
- ALWAYS asks for graph: lg(1/t) vs lg(V) for order; lg(1/t) vs 1/T for Ea
- Identify rate-determining step from data

**Task 4 — Planning:** 10-13 marks
- Full experimental design with safety, variable control, measurement protocol, data table, calculation
- Must include: tabulated trials with varying quantities, apparatus specification, heat-loss minimisation (if calorimetry), graph axes labelled with units, calculation steps mapping graph → answer
- Examples: enthalpy of neutralisation via T vs V curve (HCI20), Hneut by extrapolation

**Standard observation wordings (memorise):**
- "Effervescence observed. Colourless, odourless gas relights glowing splint. Hence, oxygen gas is present."
- "Effervescence observed. Colourless, odourless gas turns limewater milky. Hence, carbon dioxide is present."
- "Brown gas evolved (NO₂). Test with damp blue litmus → red."
- "White ppt of [substance] forms; insoluble in excess NaOH(aq); soluble in NH₃(aq) to give colourless solution. Hence, Ag⁺ confirmed."
- "Orange solution decolourises. Hence, alkene present (electrophilic addition with Br₂)."
- "Yellow ppt of CHI₃ with characteristic smell. Hence, CH₃CO- or CH₃CH(OH)- present."

**Precision rules:**
- Burette readings to 2 d.p. (0.05 cm³)
- Temperature readings to 0.2 °C if 0.2 °C thermometer specified
- Always state sig figs in final answer

**Trap zones:**
- Iodometry stoichiometry: 2 Cu²⁺ + 4 I⁻ → 2 CuI + I₂; I₂ + 2 S₂O₃²⁻ → 2 I⁻ + S₄O₆²⁻. Net 2 mol Cu²⁺ ≡ 2 mol S₂O₃²⁻ (1:1)
- QA: white ppt of Mg(OH)₂ insoluble in excess NaOH vs Al(OH)₃ soluble in excess NaOH — must run BOTH NaOH and NH₃ tests
- Anion: SO₃²⁻ gives SO₂ with dilute acid (decolourises KMnO₄); SO₄²⁻ does not
- Planning Q: missing "minimise heat loss" = -1 mark (always include lid + insulating cup + thermometer-through-lid)

| Aspect | 9647 (pre-2017) | 9729 (2017+) |

| Code | 9647 (or 9746 in 2009) | 9729 |

| P4 | Separate national paper | Integrated as 9729/04 (55 m / 2.5 hr); planning moved here from P2 |
| Total weighting | /192 scaled to /100 | Different — see Section 1 |
| Molar volume | 22.4 dm³ at stp | 22.7 dm³ at stp, 24.0 dm³ at rtp |

**Content/style changes:**
- 9647 P3 question-choice allowed students to skip 1 weak topic; **9729 forces full-syllabus coverage**
- 9647 Planning was a distinct skill block (12 marks); 9729 distributes planning across questions
- Organic synthesis multi-step routes (Strecker, Claisen, Williamson) appear in both eras but 9729 expects tighter mechanism with curly arrows + more atom-economy reasoning
- 9647 leaned on "describe what you would observe"; 9729 favours quantitative + data interpretation more
- 9647 has almost no IR/mass-spec interpretation; 9729 expects this
- 9647 has no green chemistry / atom economy framing; 9729 has these explicitly
- Some 9647 papers use older systematic names ("vapourisation," "ferrous") — write modern names

**REMOVED in 9729:**
- Polypeptide secondary/tertiary structure beyond primary sequence (cys disulfide, alpha helix details)
- Some Group 2 hydroxide solubility specifics (e.g. Be(OH)₂ amphoteric depth)

- Polymers section (T22 — addition vs condensation, biodegradability)

## 14. H3 Difficulty Markers (transferable to hard H2 sub-questions)

How Cambridge constructs hard Chem questions (from 2021 RI H3 P1):

1. **Insert-based context wrapping.** Research-paper-style insert with multi-equation context. Student must extract relevant chemistry from text. H2 Free Response sometimes does this.

2. **Quantitative reasoning under partial data.** Given pKa values, predict equilibrium positions in multi-step systems. Combines LeChat with acid-base equilibria. H2 P3 ionic eqm Qs use similar logic.

3. **Cross-topic integration in one question.** Single H3 part-question may demand: structure + bonding + equilibrium + kinetics + mechanism. H2 P3 final parts (8-10m) often demand similar integration.

4. **Mechanism in unfamiliar chemistry.** H3 asks for curly-arrow mechanism on inorganic systems (silicate condensation). H2 sometimes asks novel mechanism on familiar functional group in unusual context. Same skill — apply curly arrow logic to novel substrate.

5. **Open-ended Bloom Level 5/6 evaluation.** "Suggest one positive and one negative impact" / "Justify the choice of...". H2 P3 high-mark parts ask for similar evaluation. Practice argued, balanced answers.

**5 examples of hard-question construction techniques:**
1. Multi-step synthesis problem with masked product (2011 HCI P3 Q2 rose ketones, 2011 RI P3 Q1 halogen)
2. Data table → multi-part calculation chain (2011 RI P2 Q3 Group 2)
3. Unfamiliar context wrapping (2011 HCI P3 Q1 surfactant micelles; 2021 RI H3 feldspar weathering)
4. Mechanism + electronic effect explanation (2020 HCI Promo P2 Q2 squalene + ICl)
5. Cross-topic integration (2011 RI P3 Q2 histidine titration → ionic eqm → buffer → final pH)

## 18. Calibration Rules for Paper Generation (most important section)

**Paper structure (must match exactly):**
- **P1:** 30 MCQs / 30 marks / 1 h. Topic ratio ~12 P / 6 I / 12 O. 4-option A/B/C/D only. NO "1, 2, 3 only" multiple-completion. Mix multi-statement (I-IV), Data Booklet references, "is not", reagent order, graph-pattern.
- **P2:** 4-5 structured Qs / 75 marks / 2 h. Per-Q range 10-25 marks. Sum to 75. No standalone Planning Q.
- **P3:** Section A 3 × ~20m + Section B (Q4 OR Q5) × 20m / 80 marks / 2 h. Section B presents two options (one inorganic-heavy, one organic-heavy).
- **P4:** 55 marks / 2.5 h. Volumetric + QA + kinetics-or-2nd-quant + planning.

**Topic balance per paper:**
- Each paper must touch all 3 super-clusters (Physical, Inorganic, Organic)
- Each Section B (P3) must offer one Inorganic-heavy AND one Organic-heavy option
- Cross-topic integration mandatory in P3 Section A Q1
- Industrial / applied context required for P3 Q1 and P3 Q2
- ~12 Physical + ~6 Inorganic + ~12 Organic in P1
- 6-8 multi-statement traps per P1
- Data Booklet referenced 5-7 times per P1

**P3 Section A architecture:**
- Q1: Physical integrated (gas + thermo + kinetics) in industrial context
- Q2: Equilibrium + Transition Element (Kp/Kc + complex shape/colour)
- Q3: Organic synthesis + structure elucidation (multi-step)

**P3 Section B options:**
- Q4: Inorganic deduction (Group 2/17 or Period 3)
- Q5: Organic compound deduction puzzle (or amino acid + buffer + drug combo)

**Mark allocation conventions:**
- 1 mark per curly arrow in mechanism
- 2 marks for "state and explain" (1 for state, 1 for explanation tied to it)
- 3-5 marks for energy cycle construction + calculation
- 5-7 marks for structure elucidation Q
- 1 mark per correct reagent + condition in synthesis route
- 2 marks for "describe and explain" trend questions

**Mechanism drawing standards:**
- Curly arrows from electron source (bond/lone pair) to electron sink (atom/bond midpoint)
- All intermediates shown explicitly with formal charges
- Lone pairs shown where chemically meaningful
- Stereochemistry indicated where relevant (wedge/dash) — especially SN1 vs SN2 product distinction
- Partial charges (+δ, −δ) marked
- Cyclohexadienyl intermediate with horseshoe arc above 5 sp² C + explicit sp³ centre for arene mechanisms
- Carbocation stability flagging mandatory

**Reagent specificity standards:**
- ALWAYS specify temperature (cold <10°C / RT / warm / hot / heat under reflux)
- ALWAYS specify solvent (aq / ethanolic / dry ether / dry / no solvent)
- ALWAYS specify catalyst where applicable (AlCl₃ / FeBr₃ / Ni / Pt / H₂SO₄)
- For oxidation: state agent + conditions (e.g., "K₂Cr₂O₇, dilute H₂SO₄, reflux" for 1° alcohol → carboxylic acid)
- For reduction: "LiAlH₄ in dry ether" (never aqueous); "H₂ / Ni" for C=C
- No generic "reducing agent" — name the specific reagent

**Use verbatim Cambridge stems** (see Section 6) — avoid generic "consider a reaction"
**Use real apparatus names:** polystyrene cup (not "calorimeter cup"), boiling tube, conical flask, pipette, burette with 2 d.p. precision, thermometer with 0.2 °C divisions
**Always state "Show working clearly"** in calculation Qs worth ≥ 2 marks
**P4 Task structure rigid:** Task 1 always volumetric, Task 2 always QA, Task 3 always graphical kinetics, Task 4 always planning (10-13 m)
**QA Notes table required** for P4 (cations + anions + gas tests + halogen colours)

**Avoid 9647-only formats:**
- 4-of-5 P3 choice (use ALL compulsory format)
- Standalone Planning Q in P2 (move planning to P4 or distribute)
- 40-MCQ P1 (use 30)
- "1, 2, 3 only" multiple-completion MCQs
- Old molar volume 22.4 (use 22.7 stp / 24.0 rtp)
- Older systematic names ("vapourisation", "ferrous")

**Command word weighting** (cross-paper actual counts on RI 2020 P2+P3):
Calculate ~22 / Explain ~15 / Suggest ~12 / State ~10 / Describe ~6 / Construct or Draw ~8 / Deduce ~5 / Predict ~4 / Comment or Evaluate ~3.

**Common error patterns to embed as trap distractors:**
- Confusing E° sign convention (more positive = more easily reduced)
- Forgetting state symbols in equations
- Using "stronger / weaker" without specifying what attribute
- Stating "exothermic" without referencing bond breaking vs forming
- Predicting electrophilic addition for arenes (should be substitution)
- Confusing SN1/SN2: forgetting 3° prefers SN1 and 1° prefers SN2
- Confusing Fehling (no benzaldehyde) vs Tollens (yes benzaldehyde)
- Group 2 trends: thermal stability of carbonates/nitrates INCREASES down the group (cf. Mg vs Ba decomp temperature)
- Group 17: confusing reducing power of halides (I⁻ strongest) vs oxidising power of halogens (F₂ strongest)
- Mistaking salt-bridge function (electrons do NOT pass through it)
- Buffer max capacity at half-equivalence (not equivalence)
- Forgetting Ksp common-ion algebra factor of 2 for MX2 salts
- Electrophoresis: at pH = pI, species is neutral and does not migrate
- Iodoform-positive trap when both starting and product give yellow ppt — must distinguish source
