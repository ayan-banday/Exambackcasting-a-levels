## Content Outline

### Core Idea 1: Matter

#### Atomic Structure

- Sub-atomic particles: proton (charge +1, mass 1), neutron (charge 0, mass 1), electron (charge −1, mass negligible)
- Isotopes: same atomic number, different mass number; relative isotopic mass; relative atomic mass (weighted average)
- Electronic configuration: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d¹⁰ 4s² ...
- **Atomic orbitals:** s (spherical), p (dumbbell), d (complex shapes); Aufbau, Hund, Pauli principles
- **Ionisation energies (IE):** 1st IE trend (across period: increases; down group: decreases); discontinuities reveal sub-shell structure
- Electron affinity, electronegativity

#### Mole Concept and Stoichiometry

- Mole: 6.02 × 10²³ particles; molar mass in g/mol
- Empirical formula from % composition; molecular formula from empirical + Mr
- Stoichiometric calculations: moles = mass/Mr; concentration = moles/volume(dm³)
- Limiting reagent, percentage yield, atom economy
- Back titration, standard solution preparation

#### The Gaseous State

- Ideal gas: pV = nRT (R = 8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹; T in Kelvin; p in Pa; V in m³)
- Dalton's Law of Partial Pressures: pTotal = p₁ + p₂ + ...
- Mole fraction: χ_A = n_A/n_total; p_A = χ_A × p_total
- Real gas deviations: intermolecular forces (lower p) and volume of molecules (higher V) — van der Waals corrections

### Core Idea 2: Structure and Properties

#### Chemical Bonding

**Types:**
- **Ionic:** electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions; NaCl, MgO; lattice structures; high mp
- **Metallic:** delocalised electrons + metal cations; conducts electricity, malleable, high mp
- **Covalent:** shared electron pair; simple molecular (low mp) or giant covalent (very high mp — diamond, graphite, SiO₂)
- **Coordinate (dative) covalent:** both electrons from one atom (e.g., BF₃·NH₃, NH₄⁺, Al₂Cl₆)

**VSEPR theory — shapes:**

| Electron pairs (total) | Bonding | Lone pairs | Shape | Bond angle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 2 | 0 | Linear | 180° |
| 3 | 3 | 0 | Trigonal planar | 120° |
| 3 | 2 | 1 | V-shaped | <120° |
| 4 | 4 | 0 | Tetrahedral | 109.5° |
| 4 | 3 | 1 | Trigonal pyramidal | <109.5° |
| 4 | 2 | 2 | V-shaped | <109.5° |
| 5 | 5 | 0 | Trigonal bipyramidal | 90°, 120° |
| 6 | 6 | 0 | Octahedral | 90° |

**Intermolecular forces:**
- Van der Waals (temporary dipole–induced dipole): increases with M_r / number of electrons
- Permanent dipole–dipole: polar molecules (unequal electronegativity + asymmetric shape)
- Hydrogen bonding: F, O, N — H bonded to F, O, or N; explains anomalous properties of H₂O (high bp, low density of ice)

**Lattice structures:**
- Simple molecular lattice (ICE, CO₂, I₂): low mp, volatile
- Metallic lattice: conduct electricity, malleable
- Ionic lattice: high mp, dissolves in water, conducts when molten/aqueous
- Giant covalent lattice (diamond, SiO₂): extremely high mp, very hard, non-conducting (except graphite)

#### Theories of Acids and Bases

- **Brønsted-Lowry:** proton donor (acid) / proton acceptor (base); conjugate acid-base pairs
- **Lewis:** electron-pair acceptor (acid) / donor (base)
- **Acid strength:** Ka = [H⁺][A⁻]/[HA]; pKa = −log Ka; strong acids fully dissociate
- **pH calculations:**
  - Strong acid: pH = −log[H⁺]
  - Weak acid: pH = ½(pKa − log c)
  - Buffer: pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA]) (Henderson-Hasselbalch)
  - Strong base: pOH = −log[OH⁻]; pH = 14 − pOH
  - Salt hydrolysis: weak acid + strong base → alkaline salt; strong acid + weak base → acidic salt

#### Periodicity

**Across Period 3 (Na to Cl):**
- Atomic radius: decreases (increasing nuclear charge)
- First IE: generally increases (dips at Mg→Al and P→S)
- Electronegativity: increases
- Melting point: rises to Si (giant covalent), drops sharply at P₄, S₈, Cl₂ (simple molecular)
- Electrical conductivity: Na, Mg, Al conduct; Si semiconductor; P, S, Cl non-conductors

**Group 2 (Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba):**
- React with water: M + 2H₂O → M(OH)₂ + H₂ (reactivity increases down group)
- React with dilute acid: M + H₂SO₄ → MSO₄ + H₂
- Thermal decomposition of carbonates: MCO₃ → MO + CO₂ (stability increases down group)
- Solubility: hydroxides increase down group; sulfates decrease down group

**Group 17 (halogens):**
- Physical: F₂ (yellow gas), Cl₂ (yellow-green gas), Br₂ (orange-brown liquid), I₂ (grey solid)
- Halogen displacement: Cl₂ displaces Br⁻ and I⁻; Br₂ displaces I⁻ only
- Halide reactions with AgNO₃: AgCl (white, soluble in NH₃), AgBr (cream, partially), AgI (yellow, insoluble)
- Reactions with H₂SO₄: HF, HCl (no redox); HBr (oxidised to Br₂); HI (oxidised to I₂)
- Disproportionation of Cl₂ in cold NaOH: Cl₂ + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaOCl + H₂O

#### Transition Elements (d-block)

- Definition: elements with incomplete d sub-shell in at least one oxidation state
- Properties: variable oxidation states, coloured ions (d-orbital splitting), catalytic activity, complex ion formation
- **Complex ions:** ligands (lone pair donors), coordination number (2, 4, or 6), monodentate/bidentate/polydentate
  - Ligand substitution: e.g., [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4NH₃ → [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ + 4H₂O (deep blue)
- **Colour:** d-orbital splitting by ligand field → absorption of specific wavelengths → complementary colour observed
- Common oxidation states: Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺, Cu⁺/Cu²⁺, Cr²⁺/Cr³⁺/Cr⁶⁺, Mn²⁺/Mn⁴⁺/Mn⁷⁺

### Core Idea 3: Transformation

#### Chemical Energetics

- **Standard enthalpy changes:** ΔH°_f (formation), ΔH°_c (combustion), ΔH°_hyd (hydration), ΔH°_sol (solution), ΔH°_neut (neutralisation), ΔH°_at (atomisation), ΔH°_bond (bond energy), ΔH°_lat (lattice enthalpy), ΔH°_ea (electron affinity)
- **Hess's Law:** ΔH = Σ(bonds broken) − Σ(bonds formed) OR energy cycle
- **Born-Haber cycle:** for ionic compounds; links lattice enthalpy to other enthalpy changes
- **Entropy (S):** disorder; ΔS > 0 (gas produced, mixing, dissolving)
- **Gibbs free energy:** ΔG = ΔH − TΔS; spontaneous when ΔG < 0

#### Chemical Kinetics

- Rate = Δ[product]/Δt = −Δ[reactant]/Δt
- **Rate equation:** rate = k[A]ⁿ[B]ᵐ (determined experimentally, not from equation)
- **Orders:** zero (rate constant, k = rate), first (half-life constant), second
- **Rate constant k:** units depend on overall order; increases with temperature
- **Arrhenius equation:** k = Ae^(−Ea/RT); ln k = ln A − Ea/RT; gradient of ln k vs 1/T = −Ea/R
- **Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution:** fraction of molecules with energy ≥ Ea
- **Catalysts:** provide alternative pathway with lower Ea; increase k; homogeneous or heterogeneous
- **Mechanisms:** rate-determining step (slowest step); intermediate vs transition state

#### Chemical Equilibrium

- Dynamic equilibrium: forward rate = backward rate; concentrations constant
- **Kc:** Kc = [products]^n / [reactants]^m (mol/dm³ for each species)
- **Kp:** Kp = (partial pressures of products) / (partial pressures of reactants); Kp = Kc(RT)^Δn(gas)
- **Le Chatelier's principle:** system opposes change to restore equilibrium
  - Temperature increase → favours endothermic direction → Kc changes
  - Pressure increase → favours fewer moles of gas → Kc unchanged
  - Concentration change → position shifts, Kc unchanged
  - Catalyst → equilibrium reached faster; Kc unchanged; position unchanged

**Industrial applications:**
- **Haber process:** N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃; ΔH = −92 kJ/mol; 450°C (compromise), 200 atm, Fe catalyst
- **Contact process:** 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃; 450°C, 2 atm, V₂O₅ catalyst

#### Acid-Base Equilibria

- Weak acid calculations: Ka, Henderson-Hasselbalch buffer equation
- **Titration curves:** shape depends on acid strength + base strength; equivalence point pH
- **Indicators:** weak acids — colour change near pKa; choose indicator with pKa ≈ equivalence point pH
- pH curves: strong acid/strong base (sharp jump at pH 7); weak acid/strong base (buffer region, equivalence point pH > 7)

#### Solubility Product (Ksp)

- Ksp = [Mˢ⁺]ˢ[Xᵗ⁻]ᵗ for sparingly soluble salt MₛXₜ
- **Common ion effect:** addition of a common ion reduces solubility
- **Precipitation:** ionic product > Ksp → precipitate forms

#### Electrochemistry

- **Standard electrode potentials (E°):** measured vs standard hydrogen electrode (SHE, E° = 0 V)
- **Electrochemical cell:** E°_cell = E°_cathode − E°_anode; spontaneous if E°_cell > 0
- **Nernst equation:** E = E° − (RT/nF)ln Q
- **Electrolysis:** non-spontaneous; external power source; cathode (reduction), anode (oxidation)
- **Faraday's laws:** charge Q = It; moles of product = Q/(nF) where n = electrons per ion

### Organic Chemistry

#### Introduction

- IUPAC nomenclature: longest carbon chain, substituents alphabetically, lowest locants
- Isomerism: constitutional (structural), stereoisomerism (cis-trans, optical — chiral centres, enantiomers, R/S, plane-polarised light)
- Sigma (σ) bonds: end-on overlap, free rotation; Pi (π) bonds: lateral overlap, no rotation → restricted around C=C

#### Hydrocarbons

**Alkanes:**
- Free radical substitution with Cl₂/UV: initiation, propagation (chain reaction), termination
- Products: mixture of mono/poly substituted; HCl produced

**Alkenes:**
- Electrophilic addition: Br₂ (decolourise bromine water), HBr (Markovnikov's rule — H adds to less substituted C), H₂O/H⁺ (hydration → alcohol)
- **Markovnikov's rule:** more stable carbocation intermediate (tertiary > secondary > primary)
- Oxidation: cold KMnO₄ (diol), hot KMnO₄ (cleaves double bond → carboxylic acid/ketone/CO₂)

**Arenes (Benzene):**
- Delocalised π electrons (3 resonance structures → one real structure with delocalised ring)
- **Electrophilic substitution** (not addition — preserves aromaticity):
  - Halogenation: Cl₂/AlCl₃ → chlorobenzene
  - Nitration: HNO₃/H₂SO₄, 50°C → nitrobenzene (electrophile: NO₂⁺)
  - Friedel-Crafts alkylation: RCl/AlCl₃; acylation: RCOCl/AlCl₃
  - Side chain oxidation: methylbenzene + KMnO₄ → benzoic acid

#### Halogen Derivatives (Haloalkanes)

- **SN1:** tertiary haloalkanes; carbocation intermediate; rate depends only on [R-X]; produces racemic mixture
- **SN2:** primary haloalkanes; backside attack by nucleophile; rate = k[R-X][Nu⁻]; inversion of configuration
- **Elimination:** concentrated KOH/ethanol; H + X removed from adjacent carbons → alkene; secondary/tertiary substrates
- **Nucleophiles used:** OH⁻ (substitution/elimination depending on conditions), CN⁻ (substitution → nitrile), NH₃ (substitution → amine)

#### Hydroxy Compounds

**Alcohols:**
- Oxidation: primary → aldehyde (distil with K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄) → carboxylic acid (reflux)
- Secondary → ketone; tertiary → no oxidation
- Elimination: with concentrated H₂SO₄/170°C → alkene
- Esterification: with carboxylic acid + concentrated H₂SO₄ (catalyst, reversible)
- Reaction with Na: 2ROH + 2Na → 2RONa + H₂ (less vigorous than water)

**Phenols:**
- More acidic than alcohols (pKa ≈ 10 vs 16) — resonance stabilises phenoxide ion
- Electrophilic substitution: OH activates ring → substitution at ortho and para positions
- Reaction with NaOH: sodium phenoxide formed (unlike alcohols — phenol is acidic enough)
- Reaction with Br₂(aq): immediate white precipitate (2,4,6-tribromophenol) — no catalyst needed

#### Carbonyl Compounds

**Aldehydes:**
- **Nucleophilic addition:** HCN + KCN → hydroxynitrile; NaBH₄ (reduction → primary alcohol)
- **Oxidation tests:**
  - Fehling's solution: aldehydes give brick-red precipitate (Cu₂O); ketones — no reaction
  - Tollens' reagent: aldehydes give silver mirror; ketones — no reaction
- **Iodoform test:** CH₃CHO (and CH₃COCH₃, secondary alcohols with CH₃CH(OH)- group) + I₂/NaOH → CHI₃ (yellow precipitate)

**Ketones:**
- Nucleophilic addition: HCN + KCN → hydroxynitrile; NaBH₄ → secondary alcohol
- No oxidation with Fehling's/Tollens'; no iodoform (unless methyl ketone CH₃COR)

#### Carboxylic Acids and Derivatives

- **Acidity:** RCOOH ⇌ RCOO⁻ + H⁺; Ka values
- **Esterification:** RCOOH + R'OH ⇌ RCOOR' + H₂O (reversible, acid catalyst)
- **Acid chlorides (RCOCl):** very reactive; form esters with alcohols, amides with amines, hydrolysis with water
- **Amides (RCONH₂):** hydrolysis with acid or base → carboxylic acid + amine salt

#### Nitrogen Compounds

**Amines (R-NH₂, R₂NH, R₃N):**
- Basicity: alkyl amines > NH₃ > aryl amines (lone pair delocalized into ring)
- Reaction with acids: R-NH₂ + HCl → R-NH₃⁺ Cl⁻
- Nucleophilic substitution with haloalkanes (produces ammonium salt → free amine with NaOH)
- Reaction with acid chlorides → amides

**Amino Acids:**
- Structure: H₂N-CHR-COOH; R = side chain
- **Zwitterion:** in neutral solution, exists as ⁺H₃N-CHR-COO⁻
- **Isoelectric point (pI):** pH at which zwitterion form predominates (no net charge)
- **Peptide bonds:** formed between -COOH of one and -NH₂ of another (condensation); hydrolysis requires acid or base + heat
- **Protein structure:** primary (sequence), secondary (H-bonds → α-helix, β-sheet), tertiary (R-group interactions), quaternary (multiple polypeptides)
