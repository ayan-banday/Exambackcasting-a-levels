**Why it matters:** Carboxylic acids are the **top of the organic oxidation chain** (alkene/alcohol/aldehyde all funnel here) and the **acidity capstone** — they complete the ladder *carboxylic acid > carbonic acid > phenol > water > alcohol* that ties together Topics 14/16. The **acyl chloride / ester derivatives** introduce **nucleophilic acyl substitution** and the reactivity-order reasoning (electronic + leaving-group) that recurs in amides (T19). **Mark-scheme-first核心:** examiners reward (1) the **conjugate-base-stability acidity argument** with the carboxylate's *equal delocalisation over two electronegative O atoms* as the punchline, (2) the **NaHCO₃/Na₂CO₃ effervescence test** as the unique distinguisher of –CO₂H (phenol fails it), (3) the **relative-hydrolysis-of-chlorides** comparison (acyl ≫ alkyl ≫ aryl, with electronic + steric reasons), and (4) **acid vs base ester hydrolysis** (reversible/products are acid+alcohol vs irreversible/products are carboxylate salt+alcohol).

## UNIT 1 — Introduction to carboxylic acids (naming, bp/solubility, prep)

### Structure & naming (LO 11.1a)
- **Carboxyl group –CO₂H** (–COOH): the carbonyl C is **sp² hybridised, trigonal planar, ~120°.**
- **Naming:** replace alkane's **-e with -oic acid**; the **–CO₂H carbon is C1.** Ring-attached –CO₂H → **-carboxylic acid** (cyclohexanecarboxylic acid; two on a ring → **-dicarboxylic acid**, e.g. benzene-1,2-dicarboxylic acid). Aromatic: benzoic acid, 4-nitrobenzoic acid.

### Physical properties — H-bonding + dimerisation
- **bp: carboxylic acid > alcohol of similar Mr** (e.g. propanoic acid 141 > butan-1-ol 118, both Mr 74) for two reasons:
  1. **Dimerise via two H-bonds** (in the pure liquid) → effectively doubles the electron-cloud size → **stronger dispersion forces between dimers.**
  2. **H-bonding between acid molecules is stronger than between alcohols** (the electron-withdrawing C=O makes the O–H more δ+).
- **Solubility:** in **non-polar solvents** (benzene) the acid exists as **H-bonded dimers**, soluble via dispersion with the chain. In **water** the acid does NOT dimerise — it **H-bonds with water**, so **short-chain acids are water-soluble**; **solubility falls as the chain lengthens** (rising dispersion between acid molecules outweighs the H-bonding released). (Benzoic acid: slightly soluble in cold water, dissolves readily in hot.)

### Preparation (LO 11.8a)
- **Oxidative cleavage of alkenes** (T11); **side-chain oxidation of alkylbenzenes** (T12); **acid/alkaline hydrolysis of nitriles** (T15); **oxidation of 1° alcohols** (T16); **oxidation of aldehydes** (T17); hydrolysis of acyl chlorides/esters/amides. The named oxidations use **acidified KMnO₄ or K₂Cr₂O₇, heat under reflux.**

## UNIT 2 — Reactions of carboxylic acids ★★

Three reaction types: **(i) acid–metal / acid–base (O–H breaks → salt); (ii) nucleophilic acyl substitution (–OH replaced); (iii) reduction.** Note: the close C=O and O–H mean acids don't behave like alcohols/aldehydes/ketones — e.g. **no nucleophilic addition, NEGATIVE with 2,4-DNPH and alkaline iodine** (the O–H lone pair delocalises into C=O, making the carbonyl C less electron-deficient).

### Acidity (LO 11.8c) ★★ — the acidity capstone
- Carboxylic acids are **weak Brønsted acids**: O–H breaks → H⁺ + **carboxylate ion RCO₂⁻.** Strength set by **conjugate-base stability** (more dispersed −charge → less likely to re-protonate → more dissociation → larger Ka / smaller pKa → stronger acid).
- **Why stronger than phenol/water/alcohol** (pKa: ethanoic 4.75 < phenol 9.95 < water 15.7 < ethanol 15.9):
  - **Carboxylate: the −charge is delocalised EQUALLY over TWO highly electronegative O atoms → strongly dispersed → very stable** → carboxylic acid is the strongest.
  - **Phenoxide:** −charge delocalised over the **ring** (carbon atoms hold it less well than O) → less stabilised than carboxylate → phenol weaker than carboxylic acid.
  - **Alkoxide:** alkyl group **intensifies** the −charge → destabilised → alcohol weakest.
- **Behaviour ladder (reacts ✓ / no ✗):**
  | | Na | NaOH (strong base) | Na₂CO₃ / NaHCO₃ (weak base) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | alcohol | ✓ | ✗ | ✗ |
  | phenol | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ |
  | **carboxylic acid** | ✓ | ✓ | **✓** |
- **The NaHCO₃/Na₂CO₃ effervescence test (LO 11.8b-i):** only carboxylic acids are strong enough to protonate HCO₃⁻/CO₃²⁻ (acid pKa 4.75 < carbonic acid 6.4) → **CO₂ effervescence** (`CH₃CO₂H + NaHCO₃ → CH₃CO₂⁻Na⁺ + CO₂ + H₂O`; CO₂ turns limewater milky). **This distinguishes a carboxylic acid from a phenol** (phenol pKa 9.95 > carbonic → no reaction). *(Stronger acid protonates the weaker acid's conjugate base — T14.)*

### Substituent effects on acidity (LO 11.8c) ★
- **Electron-donating (alkyl): intensify the −charge → destabilise carboxylate → weaker acid.** (Chain length barely matters — ethanoic ≈ propanoic ≈ butanoic.)
- **Electron-withdrawing (halogen, NO₂, OH, NH₂): disperse the −charge → stabilise carboxylate → stronger acid.** Three modifiers:
  - **More electronegative halogen → stronger EW effect → stronger acid.**
  - **More EW groups → stronger acid.**
  - **Nearer the –CO₂H → stronger acid** (inductive effect falls with distance; negligible beyond ~4 atoms). (e.g. chloroethanoic acid > ethanoic acid.) Same effects for substituted benzoic acids.

### Reactions forming salts/derivatives (LO 11.8b)
- **With metals (redox):** `2CH₃CO₂H + 2Na → 2CH₃CO₂⁻Na⁺ + H₂`. **With alkalis:** → carboxylate salt + H₂O. **With carbonates/hydrogencarbonates:** → salt + **CO₂** + H₂O.
- **Condensation → ester (LO 11.8b-ii):** acid + alcohol (NOT phenol) **+ conc H₂SO₄ catalyst, heat → ester + H₂O** (slow, reversible). The **C–O bond of –CO₂H breaks** (isotopic-labelling evidence — T6). Example: ethyl ethanoate.
- **→ acyl chloride (LO 11.8b-iii):** –OH replaced by –Cl using **PCl₅** (white fumes of HCl), or **PCl₃**, or **SOCl₂ (warm)**. Example: ethanoyl chloride.
- **Reduction → 1° alcohol (LO 11.8b-iv):** **LiAlH₄ in dry ether** (anhydrous). **Carboxylic acids reduce only to the 1° ALCOHOL, not the aldehyde. NaBH₄ and H₂/catalyst are too weak to reduce –CO₂H** → enables selective reduction (e.g. reduce –CO₂H but leave a C=C with NaBH₄... or vice-versa with H₂/Ni).

### Oxidation — special case (LO) ★ — only TWO acids oxidise
- **Only methanoic acid (HCO₂H) and ethanedioic acid (HO₂C–CO₂H) are oxidised by KMnO₄** (they have the special H–C=O / two adjacent –CO₂H structure). **All other carboxylic acids are NOT oxidised.** Observation: **purple MnO₄⁻ → colourless Mn²⁺ + CO₂ effervescence.**

## UNIT 3 — Carboxylic acid derivatives: acyl chlorides & esters

**Derivative = –OH of the acid replaced by –Y** (Y = halogen/–OR/–NH₂...). General reaction = **nucleophilic acyl substitution:** nucleophile attacks the δ+ C=O carbon → tetrahedral intermediate → C=O reforms, **leaving group Y⁻ eliminated.** (H⁻/R⁻ are unstable → ketones/aldehydes don't do this; they add instead.)

### Naming & properties (LO 11.1a)
- **Acyl chloride RCOCl:** replace acid's **-oic acid with -oyl chloride** (ethanoyl chloride, benzoyl chloride). **Ester RCO₂R':** name the **R' on O first**, then parent acid with **-oate** (methyl ethanoate, phenyl ethanoate, methyl benzoate).
- **bp: ester & acyl chloride < carboxylic acid** — they have only **pd–pd** interactions (the C=O is polar) but **cannot H-bond between their own molecules** (no H on an electronegative O); pd–pd is weaker than the acid's H-bonding. (Both bp *higher* than a comparable non-polar molecule, e.g. ethanoyl chloride > 2-chloropropane, because C=O gives a larger dipole.)
- **Solubility:** **esters much less soluble than acids/alcohols** (often immiscible). **Acyl chlorides hydrolyse in water.**
- **Odour:** acyl chlorides = sharp/irritating (from HCl on hydrolysis); **esters = pleasant, sweet/fruity** (used in perfumes/flavourings).

### Relative reactivity (acid derivatives) toward nucleophilic acyl substitution ★
**acyl chloride > ester ≈ acid > amide.** Reasons: **(1) electronic** — Cl withdraws electrons inductively → ↑δ+ on the carbonyl C → more readily attacked; –NH₂/–OR donate by resonance → ↓δ+ → less reactive. **(2) leaving group** — weaker base = better leaving group; **Cl⁻ (weak base) leaves easily; ⁻OH/⁻OR/⁻NH₂ (stronger bases) leave poorly.** → can convert a more reactive derivative into a less reactive one (acyl chloride → ester/amide), not the reverse.

### Acyl chlorides — reactions (LO 11.8d, e, f, g)
- **Hydrolysis (LO 11.8d):** react **violently with water at rt → carboxylic acid + HCl** (white fumes; aliphatic acyl chlorides fume in moist air). `RCOCl + H₂O → RCO₂H + HCl`.
- **Condensation with alcohols/phenols → ester (LO 11.8e,g):** **rt, no catalyst, goes to completion, white fumes of HCl.** Works with **phenols** (unlike carboxylic acids) → **the only route to phenyl esters, e.g. phenyl benzoate.**
- **Condensation with NH₃/amines → amide (LO 11.8e):** with **NH₃ or 1°/2° amines (in excess)** → amide + HCl. **Tertiary amines don't** (no replaceable H on N). Use **excess** amine/NH₃ because some is consumed neutralising the HCl (`NH₃ + HCl → NH₄Cl`). **Carboxylic acids can't be used here** — they just do an acid-base reaction with the amine (→ ammonium carboxylate).
- **Relative ease of hydrolysis: acyl chloride ≫ alkyl chloride > aryl chloride (LO 11.8f)** ★:
  | | acyl chloride (CH₃COCl) | alkyl chloride (CH₃CH₂Cl) | aryl chloride (chlorobenzene) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | conditions | **H₂O, rt** (violent) | aq NaOH, **heat/reflux** | only very high T & P with alkali |
  | electronic | carbonyl C **very** δ+ (Cl **and** O both EW) → cleaves without heating | C δ+ from one Cl only → needs heat | least δ+: **Cl lone pair delocalised into ring → partial double-bond character → C–Cl stronger** |
  | steric | **planar acyl C → little hindrance** | tetrahedral C, more hindered | bulky ring + π repels nucleophile |
  | aq AgNO₃ | **white AgCl ppt immediately** | no ppt cold (needs NaOH+heat) | no ppt even after prolonged boiling |
- **Distinguishing test:** add water → acyl chloride gives **immediate white HCl fumes** (acid doesn't); or **aqueous AgNO₃ → immediate white AgCl ppt** for acyl chloride only.

### Esters — preparation & hydrolysis (LO 11.8g, h)
- **General formula CₙH₂ₙO₂** — ester and carboxylic acid with the same n are **constitutional isomers.**
- **Preparation:** (1) **acid + alcohol + conc H₂SO₄, heat** (condensation, reversible — T16); (2) **acyl chloride + alcohol or phenol** (faster, complete, no catalyst).
- **Acid hydrolysis (LO 11.8h):** **dilute H₂SO₄(aq), heat → carboxylic acid + alcohol.** **REVERSIBLE** (the reverse of esterification); use a **large excess of water** to push equilibrium right.
- **Base hydrolysis / saponification (LO 11.8h):** **dilute NaOH(aq), heat → carboxylate SALT (RCO₂⁻) + alcohol.** **RAPID and IRREVERSIBLE** (the carboxylate has no tendency to be re-attacked → goes to completion). **Add dilute acid afterwards to get the free carboxylic acid.** (Soap = Na salt of long-chain carboxylate from base hydrolysis of triglyceride fats.)

### Comparing acidity of organic compounds in aqueous medium (LO summary)
Always argue from **conjugate-base stability**: rank by how well the −charge is dispersed. Full ladder (strongest→weakest acid): **HCl ≫ carboxylic acid > carbonic acid > phenol > water > alcohol**, modulated by EW/ED substituents and their proximity. Use this to predict whether one species can protonate another's conjugate base.

## TOP EXAM TRAPS (Topic 18)
- **Carboxylate beats phenoxide because the −charge spreads EQUALLY over TWO electronegative O atoms** (vs ring carbons for phenoxide) — say this explicitly.
- **NaHCO₃/Na₂CO₃ effervescence (CO₂) is THE test that separates a carboxylic acid from a phenol** (both react with Na and NaOH; only the acid touches carbonate).
- **Substituent acidity: EWG nearer the –CO₂H = stronger acid** (inductive falls with distance, gone past ~4 atoms); more EWGs / more electronegative halogen = stronger.
- **Carboxylic acids reduce (LiAlH₄, dry ether) only to the 1° ALCOHOL — never stop at the aldehyde.** NaBH₄ / H₂ can't reduce –CO₂H at all (basis for selective reduction).
- **Only methanoic and ethanedioic acid are oxidised by KMnO₄** — all other –CO₂H are not.
- **Acid derivatives reactivity: acyl chloride > ester/acid > amide** (electronic + leaving-group). You can go more→less reactive, not the reverse.
- **Acyl chloride hydrolysis ≫ alkyl ≫ aryl** — give electronic (two EW atoms vs one vs delocalised lone pair) AND steric reasons; aryl chloride won't hydrolyse under ordinary conditions.
- **Phenyl esters & amides need the ACYL CHLORIDE, not the acid** (phenol won't esterify with the acid; amine just acid-base-reacts with the acid). Use **excess** amine to mop up HCl.
- **Acid hydrolysis of ester = reversible (acid + alcohol); base hydrolysis = irreversible (carboxylate SALT + alcohol), then acidify** for the free acid.
- **Esters/acyl chlorides have lower bp than the acid** — pd–pd only, no inter-molecular H-bonding (no H on their O).
