# Chem Topic 11 — Alkenes (H2 9729)

**Why it matters:** The second functional group, and the **highest-yield organic mechanism topic** at A-level. **Electrophilic addition** + **Markovnikov/carbocation-stability** product prediction is examined almost every year (P2/P3 mechanism-draw with curly arrows). The **oxidation reactions** (mild → diol; oxidative cleavage → carbonyls/CO₂) are the standard tools for **locating a C=C** in structure-determination questions, and the **decolourisation tests** (Br₂(aq), KMnO₄) are core distinguishing-test answers. Builds on Topic 8 (mechanisms, curly arrows, electrophile/nucleophile), Topic 9 (cis-trans + enantiomers), Topic 2 (sp² hybridisation, IMF), Topic 10 (the alkane it reduces to).

**Mark-scheme-first核心:** examiners reward (1) **all three reagents/conditions stated** for each reaction, (2) the **two-step mechanism with correctly-placed curly arrows** showing electron movement + the **rate-determining step labelled**, (3) the **"most stable carbocation → major product"** reasoning chain written out, not just asserted.

## UNIT 1 — Nomenclature, electronic structure, cis-trans isomerism, physical properties

### Definition & general formula (LO 11.1a)
- **Alkene = unsaturated hydrocarbon** with **≥1 C=C double bond**; "unsaturated" = fewer than the maximum possible H atoms.
- **General formula CₙH₂ₙ** — but **only** for aliphatic alkenes with **one** C=C. **Not valid** for dienes/polyenes or alicyclic (cyclo) alkenes.

### Naming (same rules as alkanes + these)
1. Longest parent chain **must contain the C=C**; suffix **-ene**.
2. Number from the end **nearer the double bond**; locant precedes the name (but-**1**-ene). If equidistant, start nearer the first branch.
3. Substituents numbered + listed alphabetically.
4. Multiple C=C → **-diene / -triene** with a locant for each (hexa-1,3-diene).
5. **cis-/trans-** descriptor goes at the **front**, before the locants (trans-hexa-1,3-diene).
6. **Cycloalkenes:** number so C=C is between **C1 and C2**, first substituent gets the lowest number (3-methylcyclohexene).

### Electronic structure of ethene (recap Topic 2)
- Each C is **sp² hybridised**, **trigonal planar**, bond angle **120°**.
- C=C = **one σ bond** (head-on overlap of sp² orbitals) **+ one π bond** (side-on overlap of unhybridised p orbitals perpendicular to the sp² plane). The **π-electron cloud lies above and below** the plane.
- C–H bonds are σ (sp²(C)–s(H) head-on overlap).
- **When drawing cis-trans isomers, show the trigonal-planar 120° geometry at each C of the C=C** — examiners penalise non-planar drawings.

### Cis-trans isomerism (recap Topic 9)
- **Two criteria:** (1) **restricted rotation** about the C=C, and (2) **two different groups** on **each** C=C carbon. If either C=C carbon carries **two identical groups → no cis-trans**.
- **cis** = same groups on the **same** side; **trans** = on **opposite** sides.
- Cycloalkenes with **≤7 C** exist **only as cis**; **≥8 C** can be cis or trans.
- **No need to assign cis/trans** when the two C=C carbons have no matching pair of groups (e.g. (Cl)CH=C(CH₃)Br) — just draw both geometric arrangements with correct geometry.

### Physical properties (LO from Unit 1)
- **Bp ↑ with chain length:** more electrons → larger/more polarisable electron cloud → **stronger dispersion forces**. Mp trend irregular (depends on lattice packing).
- **cis vs trans — boiling point: cis is higher.** In a **cis** disubstituted alkene (e.g. cis-1,2-dichloroethene) the polar C–Cl bond dipoles **do not cancel** → net dipole → **permanent dipole–permanent dipole (pd–pd) interactions on top of dispersion** → more energy to separate → higher bp. In **trans**, the dipoles **cancel → zero net dipole → non-polar → dispersion only → lower bp.**
  - *Caveat:* effect is small when substituents are nearly non-polar (cis- vs trans-but-2-ene bp 4 °C vs 1 °C — methyl is barely polar, so cis isn't much more polar than trans).
- **cis vs trans — melting point: trans is higher.** The **trans** isomer is **more symmetrical / straighter → packs better in the solid lattice → dispersion forces act more effectively → more energy to melt.** cis packs poorly.
- **Solubility:** insoluble in water (can only form weak pd–id with water, far weaker than water's H-bonding) — float as an immiscible layer; **soluble in non-polar solvents** (benzene, CCl₄). Less dense than water. Viscosity ↑ with chain length; straight-chain more viscous than branched.

## UNIT 2 — Electrophilic addition (the core mechanism block)

### Why alkenes are reactive towards electrophiles (LO 11.3d)
- Even though **C=C is stronger than C–C**, alkenes are **more reactive than alkanes** because of the **electron-rich C=C**. The **π electron cloud lies exposed on the surface** of the molecule → **attracts electrophiles**. The weaker π bond breaks; two new strong σ bonds form → carbons left joined by a single bond. This reaction type = **electrophilic addition**.

### General two-step mechanism
- **Step 1 (slow, rate-determining):** the **π electrons of C=C attack the electrophile**, the π bond breaks, forming a **positively-charged carbocation intermediate** (the electrophile's bond undergoes **heterolytic fission**). Curly arrow #1 from the C=C π bond to the electrophilic atom; arrow #2 from the breaking bond to the leaving atom.
- **Step 2 (fast):** the **nucleophile** (the anion just generated) attacks the carbocation → **stable product**. Fast because it is attraction between **oppositely-charged species**.

### Carbocation intermediate — structure & stability (the examiner's favourite)
- **Structure:** central C is **+ charged, sp² hybridised, trigonal planar**, with an **empty unhybridised p orbital**. Being electron-deficient, it strongly attracts nucleophiles; being **planar, the nucleophile can attack from either face**.
- **Stability rule: 3° > 2° > 1° > methyl.** **Alkyl groups are electron-donating** → they **disperse the positive charge** → stabilise the cation. More alkyl substituents = more stable. **Electron-withdrawing groups (e.g. halogens) intensify the charge → destabilise.**

### 4.1.3 — Addition of hydrogen halides HX (LO 11.4b-i)
- **Reagents/conditions:** **HCl(g), HBr(g) or HI(g), room temperature.**
- Mechanism (ethene + HBr → bromoethane): polar HBr approaches the π cloud; π electrons attack the δ+ H; H–Br breaks heterolytically → carbocation + Br⁻; Br⁻ then attacks the carbocation → bromoethane.
- *Note:* **HCl(g) (a simple covalent molecule, pd–pd) ≠ hydrochloric acid HCl(aq) (H⁺ + Cl⁻ ions).** Same formula, different species.

### 4.1.3b — Markovnikov / unsymmetrical alkenes (LO 11.4c) ★ highest-yield
- **Markovnikov's rule:** in addition of HX to an alkene, **H adds to the C of the C=C that already holds more H atoms.**
- **The real reason (this is what earns the marks): the electrophile adds so as to generate the MOST STABLE carbocation, which gives the MAJOR product.** I.e. the electrophile (H⁺) adds to the **less-substituted** carbon so the **+ charge sits on the more-substituted (more stable) carbon.**
- *Propene + HX:* H adds to CH₂= end → **2° carbocation** (more stable, two alkyl-bearing neighbours' worth of donation) rather than the 1° → major product is the **2-halopropane**. **(HI + propene → CH₃CHICH₃ — this is the Q13iii erratum answer.)**
- Extends to **any electrophile**, not just H — always "add to give the most stable carbocation."
- **Racemic mixture:** if the carbocation carbon ends up bonded to **3 different groups** (becomes a chiral centre on adding the nucleophile), the **planar** cation is attacked **equally from top and bottom** → **equimolar pair of enantiomers = racemic mixture**.

### 4.1.4 — Addition of halogens X₂ (LO 11.4b-i, 11.3l) ★ the named mechanism
- **Reagents/conditions:** **Cl₂ or Br₂ dissolved in CCl₄ (non-polar solvent), room temperature**, in the **absence of UV/in the dark** (UV would trigger free-radical substitution of the alkyl groups).
- **Observation:** **greenish-yellow Cl₂ / reddish-brown Br₂ decolourised** (distinguishing test).
- **Mechanism (Br₂ + ethene → 1,2-dibromoethane):** non-polar Br₂ approaches the π cloud → the **π cloud induces a dipole in Br₂** (polarises it) → **Br–Br undergoes heterolytic fission** → one Br bonds to the alkene forming a carbocation + Br⁻ (this is the **rds**: breaks π bond + Br–Br bond) → Br⁻ attacks the carbocation (fast, oppositely-charged) → **1,2-dibromoethane.**
- **F₂ reacts violently; I₂ does not react** with most alkenes.
- *Refinement (for-info but worth knowing):* with cyclopentene **only the trans product** forms, not a cis/trans mix → implies a **cyclic bromonium-ion intermediate**, and step 2 is an **Sₙ2 attack by Br⁻ from the opposite face** (→ trans). Sₙ2 detail comes in Topic 15.

### 4.1.5 — Bromine water (LO 11.4b-i) ★ distinguishing test
- **Reagents/conditions:** **Br₂(aq), room temperature.** **Observation: yellow-orange Br₂(aq) decolourised** (distinguishing test for C=C).
- Major product is a **bromoalcohol (bromohydrin)**, NOT the dibromide. Reason: after the carbocation forms, **any nucleophile present can attack it** — both **H₂O and Br⁻** are present, and **water is in large excess → attacks preferentially** → the major product has **–OH and –Br on adjacent carbons.**
- *Exam trap (Ex 4.3):* propene + bromine water contaminated with NaCl(aq) → products are bromohydrin / dibromide / bromochloride (H₂O, Br⁻, Cl⁻ all attack the cation). **The only electrophile present is Br₂** → every product contains **≥1 Br**; you **cannot** get 1,2-dichloropropane (no Cl₂/Cl⁺ electrophile to start the addition).

### 4.1.6 — Addition of steam/water → alcohol (LO 11.4b-i)
- **Industrial (hydration):** **steam H₂O(g), H₃PO₄ catalyst, high temperature & pressure** → alcohol. Follows Markovnikov.
- **Lab (2 steps):** (1) **cold concentrated H₂SO₄**, then (2) **H₂O**. Mechanism (appendix): protonate C=C → carbocation; ⁻OSO₃H attacks → alkyl hydrogensulfate; add cold water → H₂O substitutes –OSO₃H for –OH → alcohol + regenerated H₂SO₄.

## UNIT 3 — Reduction & oxidation

### 4.2 Reduction — catalytic hydrogenation (LO 11.4b-ii)
- **Reagents/conditions: H₂ gas, Ni catalyst** (Pt or Pd also work). **Heterogeneous catalysis** (mechanism = Topic 6: adsorb → react → desorb).
- Alkene + H₂ → **alkane** (C=C → C–C; adds one H to each C). Industrial use: **hardening unsaturated plant oils → margarine.**
- H₂/Ni also reduces other functional groups (later topics).

### 4.3.1 Mild oxidation → diol (LO 11.4b-iii)
- **Reagents/conditions: KMnO₄, dilute NaOH (alkaline), cold.**
- **Observation: purple KMnO₄ decolourised + brown ppt of MnO₂ formed** (distinguishing test).
- Product = the **diol** (–OH added to **each** C of the former C=C). e.g. ethene → ethane-1,2-diol.
- Balance with **[O]:** `CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O + [O] → HOCH₂CH₂OH`.

### 4.3.2 Oxidative cleavage → carbonyls/acids/CO₂ (LO 11.4b-iv) ★ structure determination
- **Reagents/conditions: KMnO₄, dilute H₂SO₄ (acidified), heat** (strong oxidation).
- **Observation: purple KMnO₄ decolourised** (and **effervescence of CO₂** if a terminal =CH₂ is present).
- **The C=C is ruptured.** Product depends on **how many H** are on each C=C carbon — **memorise this table:**

| H atoms on that C=C carbon | That carbon becomes |
|---|---|
| **2** (i.e. =CH₂, terminal) | **CO₂ + H₂O** (the carbon is lost as gas) |
| **1** (=CHR) | **carboxylic acid** (–COOH) |
| **0** (=CR₂) | **ketone** (C=O) |

- **Method:** cut the molecule at the C=C; replace each `=C<` half with its product per the table above. A **diene** cleaves at both C=C (a middle fragment with one H each end → a dicarboxylic acid, etc.).
- **Uses:** (1) **locate the position of the C=C** in a chain/ring (work backwards from the carbonyl/acid fragments); (2) **distinguish terminal vs non-terminal C=C** — terminal (=CH₂) gives **CO₂ effervescence**, non-terminal does not.
- **NOTE: alkenes are NOT oxidised by K₂Cr₂O₇** — only manganate(VII). (Common distractor.)

### Distinguishing-test procedure (practical)
- **Acidified KMnO₄:** 5 drops KMnO₄ + 1 cm³ dilute H₂SO₄; add to 1 cm³ unknown **dropwise**, shake, warm in water bath.
- **Bromine water:** add Br₂(aq) **dropwise** to 1 cm³ unknown, shake.
- **Both reagents added dropwise** because if added in **excess**, the colour change (decolourisation) **cannot be observed** — solution stays purple / yellow-orange.

## TOP EXAM TRAPS (Topic 11)
- **State all reagents AND conditions** for each reaction — losing the "cold/alkaline" or "hot/acidified" half loses the mark, and it's exactly what flips diol vs cleavage.
- **Markovnikov mark scheme = "electrophile adds to give the more stable (more substituted) carbocation → major product."** Don't just quote "H adds to the C with more H" — explain via carbocation stability (alkyl groups electron-donating, disperse +).
- **Halogen addition is in CCl₄, room temp, IN THE DARK** (UV → free-radical substitution instead). Br₂ mechanism (in CCl₄) is the LO-named one — non-polar Br₂ is **polarised/induced-dipole by the π cloud** first.
- **Bromine water → bromohydrin (major), not dibromide** (water in excess attacks the cation). Distinguishing test = **decolourises Br₂(aq)**.
- **Oxidative-cleavage product table** (2H→CO₂; 1H→carboxylic acid; 0H→ketone). Terminal =CH₂ → **CO₂ effervescence** = the test for a terminal C=C.
- **Alkenes NOT oxidised by dichromate(VI)** — only KMnO₄.
- **cis = higher bp** (polar, pd–pd); **trans = higher mp** (packs better). Don't swap them.
- Mild oxidation observation includes the **brown MnO₂ precipitate**, not just decolourisation.
- Reagents added **dropwise** in distinguishing tests so decolourisation is visible (not masked by excess).
- Racemic mixture forms when the carbocation gives a **new chiral centre** (planar cation → attack from both faces).
- Hydration (steam) uses **H₃PO₄ catalyst**, not H₂SO₄ (that's the lab 2-step route).
