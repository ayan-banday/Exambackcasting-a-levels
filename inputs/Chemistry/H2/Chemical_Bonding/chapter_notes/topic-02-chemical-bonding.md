## THE TWO MARK-SCHEME ENGINES (use everywhere)

**Engine A — comparing melting/boiling points (or volatility):**
> identify **structure** of each → identify the **type of attraction broken** on melting/boiling → compare its **strength** (quote the deciding factor) → **"more energy is needed to overcome the stronger ___, hence higher mp/bp."**

The phrase **"more energy is needed to overcome…"** is mark-bearing. An energy comparison is *required* — strength alone doesn't score full marks. ⚠️ For simple molecular substances, **only the intermolecular forces break — NOT the covalent bonds.** Saying "covalent bonds break when ice melts" is a classic 0-mark error.

**Engine B — the strength ladder (which attraction is being broken?):**
ionic / metallic / giant-covalent (all "strong, lots of energy") ≫ hydrogen bonding > pd–pd > id–id (dispersion) (all "weak intermolecular, little energy"). Always name the *specific* force.

## UNIT 1 — Introduction

**All chemical bonds are electrostatic** (attraction between + and − charges). Memorise the three verbatim:
- **Ionic bond:** electrostatic attraction between **oppositely charged ions**.
- **Covalent bond:** electrostatic attraction between the **shared pair of electrons and the (two) positively charged nuclei**.
- **Metallic bond:** electrostatic attraction between a **lattice of positive ions and the sea of delocalised electrons**.

**Electronegativity:** a measure of an atom's **ability to attract the (bonding) electrons in a covalent bond to itself.**
- Factors = same as ionisation energy (nuclear charge, shielding, atomic radius → Z_eff). **Increases across a period** (Z_eff ↑), **decreases down a group** (more shells, larger radius). Don't memorise Pauling values.

**Predicting bond type from electronegativity difference (ΔEN):** small Δ → electron *sharing* → covalent (two non-metals); large Δ → electron *transfer* → ionic (metal + non-metal). Continuum, not binary — most bonds have mixed character. Exceptions exist (AlCl₃ is covalent despite metal+non-metal).

## UNIT 2 — Metallic, Ionic, Covalent bonding

### Metallic
- **Giant metallic lattice:** metal cations + sea of delocalised valence electrons.
- **Strength depends on:** (1) **number of valence electrons** contributed per atom (more → more delocalised e⁻ → stronger); (2) **charge density of cation = charge/radius** (higher charge, smaller radius → stronger).
- **Properties:** high mp/bp (strong bonding); good electrical/thermal conductivity (delocalised e⁻ = mobile charge carriers); malleable & ductile (layers slide, e⁻ sea prevents cation repulsion, bonding intact); hard (Gp 1/2 soft).
- ⚠️ **Boiling point is the better indicator of metallic bond strength** (on melting, cations only move apart; bonding not fully broken).
- *Worked pattern (Mg mp > Na):* Mg gives 2 valence e⁻ vs Na's 1; Mg²⁺ (0.065 nm) smaller + higher charge than Na⁺ (0.095 nm) → higher charge density → stronger metallic bonding → more energy → higher mp.

### Ionic
- **Giant ionic lattice:** lattice of cations and anions; **non-directional**, extends in all directions (vs covalent = directional).
- **Lattice energy (LE):** heat evolved when **1 mole of a pure ionic solid is formed from its constituent gaseous ions.** Larger |LE| → stronger ionic bonding.
- **LE ∝ (q⁺ × q⁻) / (r⁺ + r⁻):** **higher ionic charges and smaller ionic radii → larger |LE| → stronger.** (MgO ≫ NaCl: both ions higher charge & smaller radius.)
- **Properties:** high mp/bp (strong electrostatic attraction); conducts **only when molten/aqueous** (ions become mobile), NOT solid (ions fixed); hard & rigid but **brittle** (shearing pushes like charges together → repulsion → lattice shatters); usually soluble in water (some insoluble, e.g. AgCl).

### Covalent — orbital overlap, σ and π
- Covalent bond = shared electron pair in the region of **orbital overlap** between nuclei (s and p orbitals only at this level).
- **σ (sigma) bond:** head-on overlap (s–s, s–p, p–p). **π (pi) bond:** side-on overlap of p orbitals.
- **π is weaker than σ** (side-on overlap is poorer/smaller than head-on). Single = 1σ; double = 1σ + 1π; triple = 1σ + 2π. Only one σ possible between two atoms; a π needs a σ first.
- **Bond energy:** average energy to **break 1 mole of a covalent bond in the gaseous state to form gaseous atoms.** Larger → stronger.
- **Bond length:** distance between the two nuclei. **Smaller atoms → shorter, stronger bonds** (more effective overlap). **Higher bond order → shorter, stronger.**
- ⚠️ **F–F anomaly:** F–F is *shorter but weaker* than Cl–Cl due to **lone-pair repulsion** between the close F lone pairs.
- **Reactivity (LO 2k):** longer/weaker bond → broken more easily → reacts more readily (HI decomposes on heating, HCl doesn't; N₂ inert due to strong N≡N).
- **Covalent vs van der Waals radius:** bond length/2 = covalent radius (bonded, same molecule); half the distance between non-bonded atoms in adjacent molecules = vdW radius (always larger). Noble-gas radii in Data Booklet are vdW radii.

## UNIT 3 — Dot-and-cross diagrams
- Symbol = nucleus + inner shells; dots/crosses = valence electrons. Use **only dots and crosses** (different symbol per atom). **Octet rule** = tendency to reach ns²np⁶ (empirical, predicts stability, doesn't explain it). H follows a "duet" (1s holds 2).
- **Ionic:** show electron transfer; cation drawn with no valence dots; combine in ratio for neutrality (e.g. Na₂O: each Na loses 1, O gains 2 → 2:1).
- **Exceptions to octet:**
  - **Electron-deficient** (central atom < 8 e⁻): Be (BeCl₂), B (BF₃, BH₃), Al (AlCl₃) — Group 2 & 13.
  - **Expanded octet** (> 8 e⁻): **Period 3+ only** (P, S, Cl, Xe...) using **energetically accessible d orbitals** — PCl₅ (10), SF₆ (12), SF₄ (10). ⚠️ **Period 2 (C, N, O) CANNOT expand** — only 2s + 2p available; 3d too high in energy.
  - **Radicals** (odd electrons, unpaired e⁻ on central Group 15/17 atom): NO, ClO₂, NO₂ — very reactive.
- **Polyatomic ions:** decide central atom (lowest EN, or forms most bonds; H never central). For each (−) add 1 e⁻ to the **more** electronegative atom; for each (+) remove 1 e⁻ from the **less** electronegative atom. Draw **square brackets** + charge superscript.
- ⚠️ If a Period-2 central atom would exceed 8 e⁻ (e.g. NO₂, CO), change a double bond to a **co-ordinate (dative) bond**.

### Co-ordinate (dative) bonding (Unit 3)
- **Both** bonding electrons come from one atom. Needs **donor with a lone pair** + **acceptor with an empty valence orbital**. Once formed, identical to a normal covalent bond. Shown by an **arrow** (donor→acceptor), or two dots/two crosses in dot-cross.
- Standard examples: **NH₄⁺** (N lone pair → H⁺ empty 1s); **NH₃BF₃** (N lone pair → B empty 2p); **Al₂Cl₆** dimer (Cl lone pair → Al empty 3p; bonds break above 800 °C).

## UNIT 4 — Shapes, bond angles, VSEPR, Lewis structures

**VSEPR two principles:** (1) electron pairs/groups around the central atom arrange **as far apart as possible** to minimise repulsion; (2) **repulsion: lone pair–lone pair > lone pair–bond pair > bond pair–bond pair** (a lone pair is held by one nucleus, takes more space, repels more).
- **Count a double/triple bond as ONE electron group.** Shape is named by **atom positions only** (lone pairs ignored in the name but affect angles).

| e⁻ pairs | bp | lp | Shape | Angle | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 2 | 0 | linear | 180° | BeCl₂, CO₂, HCN |
| 3 | 3 | 0 | trigonal planar | 120° | BF₃, NO₃⁻, CO₃²⁻, SO₃ |
| 3 | 2 | 1 | bent | ~118° | SO₂, NO₂⁻, O₃, PbCl₂ |
| 4 | 4 | 0 | tetrahedral | 109.5° | CH₄, CCl₄, NH₄⁺, SO₄²⁻ |
| 4 | 3 | 1 | trigonal pyramidal | 107° | NH₃, NF₃, PCl₃, H₃O⁺ |
| 4 | 2 | 2 | bent | 104.5° | H₂O, H₂S, OF₂, SCl₂ |
| 5 | 5 | 0 | trigonal bipyramidal | 90°,120° | PCl₅, AsF₅ |
| 5 | 4 | 1 | see-saw | — | SF₄ |
| 5 | 3 | 2 | T-shape | — | ClF₃, BrF₃ |
| 5 | 2 | 3 | linear | 180° | XeF₂, I₃⁻ |
| 6 | 6 | 0 | octahedral | 90° | SF₆, PCl₆⁻ |
| 6 | 5 | 1 | square pyramidal | — | IF₅, BrF₅ |
| 6 | 4 | 2 | square planar | 90° | XeF₄, ICl₄⁻, ClF₄⁻ |

**Bond-angle reasoning (the key "explain" mark):**
- CH₄ 109.5° → NH₃ 107° (1 lp) → H₂O 104.5° (2 lp): each lone pair exerts **stronger repulsion**, pushing bond pairs closer → smaller angle.
- **Electronegativity effect:** H₂O (104.5°) > H₂S — O more EN than S → bond pairs held **closer to central atom** → greater bp–bp repulsion → larger angle. Same logic: NH₃ (107°) > NF₃ — H less EN than F pulls bond pair toward N.
- For 5-pair cases, lone pairs go **equatorial** (minimises 90° lp–lp repulsions) → explains T-shape (ClF₃), see-saw (SF₄), linear (XeF₂).
- Multiple central atoms (H₂O₂, C₂H₄, H₂SO₄): state shape **about each central atom**.

**Lewis structures:** lines for bond pairs (arrow for dative); **must show lone pairs on the central atom** when drawing shape (terminal lone pairs may be omitted).

## UNIT 5 — Hybridisation (in-class taught; no MCQ)
- Atomic orbitals (s, p) mix → **hybrid orbitals** that explain molecular geometry. Carbon promotes one 2s e⁻ to 2p, then hybridises.
- **sp³** (1s + 3p → 4 orbitals): tetrahedral, **109.5°**, e.g. C in **ethane**/methane. All four C–H are s–sp³ σ; C–C is sp³–sp³ σ.
- **sp²** (1s + 2p → 3 orbitals + 1 unhybridised p): trigonal planar, **120°**, e.g. C in **ethene**/benzene. C=C = sp²–sp² σ + p–p π (side-on of unhybridised p, perpendicular to plane).
- **sp** (1s + 1p → 2 orbitals + 2 unhybridised p): linear, **180°**, e.g. C in **ethyne**. C≡C = sp–sp σ + two p–p π.
- **% s character → bond strength:** sp (50% s) > sp² (33%) > sp³ (25% s). More s character → **shorter, stronger** bond (s is closer to nucleus; p is elongated). So a C–C single bond made from sp–sp³ overlap (propyne) is stronger/shorter than sp³–sp³ (ethane).

## UNIT 6 — Polarity & intermolecular forces

### Polar bonds & molecules
- **Polar covalent bond:** unequal sharing (different EN) → dipole, δ+ / δ−. Non-polar: equal sharing (H–H, Cl–Cl, O=O; C–H treated as non-polar).
- **Molecule polarity = bond polarity + shape.** Polar bonds whose dipoles **cancel by symmetry** → non-polar molecule (CO₂ linear, BF₃ trigonal planar, CCl₄ tetrahedral). Dipoles that **don't cancel** → polar (H₂O bent, NH₃ pyramidal, CHCl₃, CFCl₃).

### Ionic bonding with covalent character
- Cation polarises (distorts) the anion's electron cloud → covalent character. Depends on:
  - **Polarising power of cation** — high for **small, highly charged** cations (high charge density): Al³⁺ ≫ Cs⁺.
  - **Polarisability of anion** — high for **large** anions (outer e⁻ loosely held).
- CsF: almost purely ionic (low polarising Cs⁺, low polarisability F⁻). Al₂O₃: ionic with significant covalent character (Al³⁺ highly polarising).

### Three intermolecular forces (all electrostatic; weak vs bonds)
1. **id–id (dispersion / instantaneous dipole–induced dipole):** between **all** molecules/atoms; the *only* force between non-polar species. Strength ↑ with **(a) number of electrons** (more polarisable cloud) and **(b) surface area of contact / shape** (elongated > spherical: pentane > 2,2-dimethylpropane).
2. **pd–pd (permanent dipole–permanent dipole):** between polar molecules.
3. **Hydrogen bonding:** requires **H bonded to F/O/N** AND **a lone pair on an F/O/N**. Strongest IMF here.
   - **Strength** ↑ with EN of the atom H is bonded to (HF h-bond stronger than NH₃: larger δ+ on H).
   - **Extent** ↑ with number of (H on F/O/N) and number of lone pairs. **H₂O forms ~2 H-bonds/molecule** (2 lone pairs + 2 δ+ H); NH₃ and HF only ~1 each.

### Effects of hydrogen bonding (high-yield)
- **Anomalously high bp of NH₃, H₂O, HF** vs other hydrides in Groups 15/16/17 and vs CH₄ (same #electrons → similar dispersion, but H-bonding adds extra). **bp order H₂O > HF > NH₃:** H₂O wins on *extent* (2 H-bonds/molecule); HF > NH₃ on *strength* (F more EN → larger δ+ on H).
- **Ice less dense than water:** each H₂O H-bonds to 4 others in an **open tetrahedral lattice** with empty spaces; liquid water's more random arrangement packs closer → ice larger volume per mass → lower density.
- High surface tension; intramolecular H-bonding (2-nitrophenol bp 214 < 4-nitrophenol 290, because intramolecular H-bonds reduce intermolecular ones); carboxylic-acid **dimerisation** in non-polar solvents (ethanoic acid apparent Mr 120).

## UNIT 7 — Physical properties of substances

**Simple molecular (I₂, ice, CO₂, P₄, S₈, Cl₂):** low mp/bp (only weak IMF broken — covalent bonds stay intact); non-conductors (no mobile charges; some react with water to give ions, e.g. HCl); soluble in organic solvents (or water if can H-bond).
- ⚠️ Volatility of Group 17 (F₂ gas → I₂ solid): more electrons down the group → stronger dispersion forces → more energy → lower volatility. (pd–pd would wrongly predict HBr > HI; dispersion from more electrons wins → **HI bp > HBr**.)

**Giant molecular (diamond, graphite, SiO₂):** very high mp/bp (break many strong covalent bonds).
- **Diamond:** each C sp³, bonded to 4 C tetrahedrally → very hard, non-conductor (no mobile e⁻), insoluble.
- **Graphite:** each C sp², bonded to 3 C in hexagonal layers; unhybridised 2p → **delocalised π system** within layers → conducts **along layers**; layers held by weak dispersion forces → soft/slippery lubricant; insoluble.
- **SiO₂/quartz:** each Si–4 O, each O–2 Si (empirical SiO₂); hard, very high mp, non-conductor.

**Metallic / Ionic:** as Unit 2.

### Period 3 trends (LO 5c)
- **Melting point:** Na < Mg < Al (giant **metallic** — more valence e⁻ + higher charge density) → **Si highest (giant molecular**, many strong covalent bonds) → sharp drop to P₄, S₈, Cl₂, Ar (**simple molecular/atomic**, weak dispersion). Among these: **S₈ > P₄ > Cl₂ > Ar** by number of electrons (128 > 60 > 34 > 18).
- **Electrical conductivity:** Na < Mg < Al increasing (more delocalised e⁻); Si semiconductor (metalloid); P/S/Cl/Ar non-conductors (no mobile charges).

### Solubility ("like dissolves like" — energetics)
- Soluble if energy released from **solute–solvent** interactions ≥ energy needed to break **solute–solute** + **solvent–solvent** interactions.
- Identify all three interaction types and compare strengths. Water dissolves: polar/H-bonders (ethanol, propanone) via H-bonding; ionic solids via ion–dipole; fails with non-polar (hexane: weak pd–id can't overcome water's H-bonding). Identify solute–solvent type: ion–dipole, H-bond, pd–pd, pd–id, or id–id.

## TOP EXAM TRAPS (Topic 2)
- mp/bp answers **must** name the structure, the specific attraction broken, AND make an **energy comparison**. Simple molecular → IMF break, **never** the covalent bonds.
- Don't confuse **intramolecular** bonds (covalent, strong) with **intermolecular** forces (weak). "Strength of covalent bond" is irrelevant to a simple-molecular bp question.
- Period 2 atoms **cannot expand the octet**; Period 3+ can (d orbitals). NO₂/CO need a dative bond to keep N/C at ≤8 e⁻.
- VSEPR: count multiple bonds as **one** group; name shape by atoms, not electron-pair geometry.
- Two bond-angle reasons: **lone-pair repulsion** (CH₄>NH₃>H₂O) vs **electronegativity of central/terminal atoms** (H₂O>H₂S, NH₃>NF₃). Pick the right one.
- H-bonding needs **H–(F/O/N) AND a lone pair on (F/O/N)** — both. bp order H₂O>HF>NH₃ uses *extent* vs *strength*, not a single factor.
- "Stronger metallic bond" indicator = **boiling** point, not melting.
- Lattice energy ∝ charge **product** over **sum of radii** — quote charges *and* radii from the Data Booklet.
