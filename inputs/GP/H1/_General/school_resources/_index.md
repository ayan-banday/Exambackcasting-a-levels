## Headline calibration rules (top 5)

1. **Theme balance per P1 paper:** AI + climate + media-democracy + politics + arts + Singapore mandatory; 3-4 SG anchors per paper minimum.
2. **Stem pool restricted to 7 patterns:** 40% quoted-claim+discuss, 15% "to what extent", 15% "how far", 10% "should/is", 10% "consider-view", 5% "examine/assess", 5% binary.
3. **AQ template locked:** 12 marks, directed claim ("How far do you agree that..."), dual-scale (personal + societal), Singapore examples mandatory, at-least-one-passage reference required.
4. **Mark structure locked:** P1 = 30 content + 20 language = 50. P2 = 35 content + 15 language = 50 (passage comprehension ~17 + summary 8 + AQ 12).
5. **Named Singapore examples only:** CHAS, Healthier SG 2023, Smart Nation, Pioneer/Merdeka/Majulah, Green Plan 2030, Pink Dot, Anton Casey, etc. Never invent generic SG references.

## Most-likely 2026 themes (ranked)

1. **AI / generative AI** (HIGHEST) — 9 explicit Qs in 2024-25 prelims; Cambridge has not tested directly as of 2021; ChatGPT 3+ years old by 2026.
2. **Climate "who pays"** — developed vs developing nation burden framing (DHS 2024 Q8, RI 2025 Q7); environment dominates Cambridge actuals (30%).
3. **Media trust / disinformation / democracy** — heavy 2025 cluster (6+ Qs); 2024 global election year.
4. **Geopolitics** — Ukraine, Middle East, China-Taiwan.
5. **Singapore-specific** — locked, 3-4 per paper.
6. **Health / wellbeing** — post-pandemic framing.
7. **Sport as nation / commerce** — 2025 spike.
8. **Arts in digital age**.

## Most significant 17-year evolution

The **Singapore anchor intensification** — ~15% of P1 questions in 2008-2010 → 30%+ in 2024-2025 where every paper has 3-4 SG-anchored questions and every AQ requires SG application. Combined with the **directed-AQ shift** (2008-2014 "How applicable" → 2023+ "How far do you agree that [DIRECTED CLAIM]?"), Cambridge is demanding increasingly specific, evaluative SG-grounded judgment over generic global commentary.
