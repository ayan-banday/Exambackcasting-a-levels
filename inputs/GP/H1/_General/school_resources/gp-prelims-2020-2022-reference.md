### Recurring essay question structures

Top recurring question stems (by frequency across the 3-year span):

1. **"How far do you agree…"** — by far the most common command in P1 and the only AQ stem in P2. Appears in 30%+ of essay questions.
2. **"To what extent…"** — 20-25% of questions. Slightly more analytical than How-far.
3. **"Discuss"** — 15% — quote-led essays demanding balanced argument.
4. **"Comment"** — 10% — demands judgment.
5. **"Do you agree?"** — 10-12%.
6. **"How true is this of your society?"** — Singapore-anchor variant.
7. **"Is this a fair…/fair comment/fair assessment?"** — judgment-style.
8. **"Examine the view that…"** — analytic.
9. **"Assess the view that…"** — judgmental analytic.
10. **"What is your view?"** — open argument.

Quoted-statement openers ("X." Do you agree?) outnumber non-quoted question openers ~3:1. Most JCs use quotation marks around the contested claim, signalling stand-with-or-against demand.

### AQ patterns

**Twin-passage AQs (most common 2020-2022)** — pattern: "How far do you agree or disagree with the views expressed in [these/the/both] two passages? Support your answer with examples drawn from your own experience and that of your society."

**Single-passage AQs** — pattern: "[Author] writes about/argues/discusses/considers [topic]. How far do you agree with [his/her/the author's/these] observations, relating your arguments to your own experience and that of your society?"

Slight variants observed:
- "Illustrate your answer with examples of how you and your society regard X" (NJC, HCI, SAJC, TMJC) — more directive than "Support your answer with examples drawn from..."
- "Relate your arguments to your own experience and that of your society" (CJC 2021)
- "How applicable are his observations to yourself and your society?" (ASRJC 2021 — Bisby) — leaner stem
- "To what extent is X for your society? Develop some of the points made by the author..." (NYJC 2021 — Vincent on solitude) — more open
- "How important is it to be X or Y for you and your society?" (TMJC 2022 — optimism/pessimism)

Trend: 2020-2022 sees twin-passage AQs becoming dominant in the larger JCs (CJC, DHS, EJC, HCI, NJC, RVHS, SAJC, TJC, TMJC, VJC, YIJC). Single-passage AQs remain in ACJC, JPJC, NJC, NYJC, RI selectively. **By 2022, ~60% of JCs ran twin-passage P2.**

- **ACJC 2020-2022**: Single-passage, "Michael Hirschorn / Dan Hancox / Kate Julian" style author bylines. P2 instructions distinctively use "Note that up to 15 marks out of 50 will be awarded for your use of language" exact wording. Topical interest in media, anxiety, modernity.

### Comparison to 2017-2019 era (what shifted, especially COVID influence)

**Shifts visible in 2020-2022 vs the 2017-2019 cohort:**

1. **Crisis-framing dominance.** The 2017-2019 era contained no "crisis" or "shock" prompts at this frequency. From 2020 onwards, JPJC immediately ran two crisis-stem questions in the same paper. By 2022, MI, YIJC and DHS were all using "crises" plural in P1.

2. **Workplace question pivot.** Pre-2020 GP rarely treated "workplace/workspaces/working from home" as a standalone P1 question. 2021 (NYJC Q9 "Do we still have a use for the workplace?") through 2022 (SAJC Q8, TMJC Q8, JPJC Q11) shows the era explicitly absorbing the WFH shift.

3. **Trust-in-science framing.** Vaccine and pandemic-science skepticism produces NYJC 2021 Q6, RI 2021 Q5+Q11, ASRJC 2022 Q5. Pre-2020 science questions tended to be about ethics of research; post-2020 questions add an authority/trust dimension.

4. **Self-sufficiency / globalisation reversal.** 2017-2019 globalisation Qs were typically pro-cooperation framings. 2020-2022 introduces "self-sufficiency" as a contestable virtue (EJC 2021 Q10, SAJC 2022 Q3) — a post-supply-chain-shock framing.

5. **Live arts / physical experience recovery.** RVHS 2022 Q7 ("live performances still relevant in this age of media streaming?") and ACJC 2020 Q12 / TMJC 2020 Q8 / RI 2022 Q3 (museums) tap a clear post-lockdown anxiety about whether physical-presence formats survived. Pre-2020 museum questions existed but were rarer per paper.

6. **AI emergence.** HCI 2022 Q4 ("artificial intelligence is a cause for concern") is one of the earliest explicit AI questions in this prelim corpus. RI 2022 Q5 ("technology completely replace the role of humans") sits adjacent. Pre-2022 AI references were embedded in broader technology Qs.

7. **Mental health front-row.** TMJC 2020 Q1, JPJC 2020 Q12 explicit; RVHS 2022 Q9, TMJC 2022 Q3 maintain the theme. Mental health became a regular standalone Q rather than a sub-theme.

8. **Twin-passage P2 ascension.** By 2022, the majority of JCs ran twin-passage P2 (vs more single-passage configurations in 2017-2019). This raised AQ complexity from "agree-with-one" to "weigh-two-against-each-other."

9. **Cancel culture / online discourse.** New category 2021-2022: TJC 2021 (Wong/Han), JPJC 2021 (Thomas), RI 2022 Q8 ("argue online makes things worse"), ACJC 2022 Q3 (fake news). Pre-2020 was social-media-influence Qs; post-2020 shifts to cancel/discourse mechanics.

10. **Singapore-anchor intensification.** The phrase "your society" appears slightly more often in 2020-2022 P1 questions than in 2017-2019, partly because Cambridge/JCs leaned into local-relevance during pandemic governance and policy.

**What did NOT shift:**

- Bloom's level distribution stayed levels 3-5 dominant.
- Cambridge command-word vocabulary unchanged ("How far / To what extent / Discuss / Comment / Examine / Assess").
- 12-question paper structure unchanged.
- 500-800 word range unchanged.
- Mark-allocation 30 content / 20 language unchanged in P1; 35 content / 15 language unchanged in P2.
- The ban on proper-noun current-event references held (COVID-19 never named directly; framings stay generic).
