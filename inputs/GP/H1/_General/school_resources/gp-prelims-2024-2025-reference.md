## P1 SUGGESTED ANSWERS (representative samples – structure observed across cycles)

### EJC 2025 P1 SAS – framework observed
Format per question: (Terms & Scope) → (Question Requirements) → (Issue & Context) → (Possible Arguments: stand A then stand B, ~5 bulleted args each side) → (Conceptual Understanding: Trends, Implications, Connections, Context) → (Examiner's comments)

Notable verbatim evaluation language used in EJC 2025 SAS:
- "the modern context is critical to addressing the question well"
- "candidates need to demonstrate awareness of the domain of [X]"
- "Examiners noted that candidates were still struggling when the essay questions came with more conditions or qualifiers"
- "Stronger scripts were also able to address the idea of a country 'thriving'"
- "Weaker responses merely listed functions and benefits"
- "Stating the drawbacks of [X] does not mean that it is not essential — after all, something essential can still have negative impacts"
- Examiners flagged students for: strawman counter-arguments, single-sentence balance paragraphs, mechanical "name-dropping" of context, sweeping claims, fractured run-on sentences, invented words ("trustable/untrustable", "irregardless"), "since young" instead of "from a young age"

### RI 2025 P1 SAS – framework observed
Format: (Terms & Scope) → (Band 4 & above vs Band 3 markers in a 2-column table) → (Measures/Arguments side 1: bullet with sub-bullets of named SG examples) → (side 2)

Notable: RI uses explicit band-descriptor side-by-side markers. Band 4+ requires "addressing the issue of [evaluative term]" plus "incorporates Singaporean context/characteristics in determining [stand]." Band 3 = "Lists general benefits and drawbacks" / "Listing of various government efforts." Low Band 3 = "deals solely with matters related to standard of living" (i.e. misses the question's evaluative point).

RI's named SG examples banked in Q1 alone: CHAS subsidies (2019 expansion), Pioneer/Merdeka/Majulah Packages, Healthier SG (2023), reactive→preventive healthcare shift. These are the named examples to use.

### Recurring stand-declaration phrasings (from SAS docs)
- "It is true that [stand] because:"
- "It is not true that [stand] because:"
- "[Subject] is essential because:" / "[Subject] is non-essential because:"
- "Measures have been highly effective:" / "Measures have NOT been effective:"

### Recurring counter-argument / hedging language flagged as good
- "Some might say…"
- "I acknowledge that…"
- "Undeniably…"
- "in general", "on the whole" (hedge sweeping claims)
- "however", "yet" (signposting argument shift)

### Recurring counter-argument / hedging language flagged as BAD (EJC 2025)
- "detractors / critics / dissidents" — wrong register
- "myopic / extreme / failing to consider" used as one-line rebuttals without explanation
- Single-paragraph "both sides" — too thin
- Strawman versions of the opposing view

### Recurring 2024–2025 essay question STRUCTURE patterns

1. **Quote + comment / discuss / how far is this true** (most common ~40% of all questions). E.g. "'Profits without accountability.' To what extent is this accurate of corporations today?"
2. **"To what extent is X true of your society?"** (Singapore-anchored, ~15-20%). Cambridge favourite.
3. **"How far do you agree that…"** with conditional embedded (~15%). E.g. RI 2024 Q7.
4. **Binary frame: X or Y / X vs Y** (~10%). E.g. "Should AI be feared or embraced?" / "Is change to be welcomed or feared?"
5. **Cause-effect / motivator question** (~10%). E.g. "Does technology compromise our freedom?"
6. **Value-judgement question** (~10%). E.g. "Is compromise always desirable?"
7. **Stakeholder / scope question** (~5%). E.g. "Do all countries have equal responsibility…?"

### AQ construction patterns (LOCKED format observed in every school)

Every AQ across 24 schools follows this structure:
- Stem: "The reading passages cover/offer a range of views/perspectives about [TOPIC]."
- Question: "How far do you agree that [DIRECTED CLAIM]?" — directed AQs are now near-universal; only DHS 2024 used the older "How far do you agree or disagree with the views expressed?" form.
- Support requirement: "Support your answer with reference to: (a) the ideas and opinions from at least one of the reading passages; (b) examples drawn from your own experience and that of your society."
- 12 marks (was 10 in 2024 NYJC; 12 became the norm).

### Comprehension question type mix (per ACJC 2024 P2 model, representative)

Across the 8-9 questions before the AQ:
- 1-2 phrase/word meaning Qs (1-2m each)
- 1-2 language analysis / how-language-conveys-meaning Qs (2-3m each)
- 1-2 inference / "what does the author mean" / "what is implied" Qs (2-3m each)
- 1 illustration / "how does the author show" Q (2m)
- 1 contrast / comparison Q across paragraphs (2m)
- 1 rhetorical-device / tone / structure Q (1-2m)
- 1 summary Q (8m, max 120 words, often spanning 1.5 paragraphs)
- 2 cross-passage application Qs (2m each — "find an idea from P3 to support/undermine a P1/P2 claim")
- AQ (12m)
- Plus: 15m language mark (writing quality)
- Total: 50 marks (35 content + 15 language)

### Mark allocation conventions

- Phrase explanation: usually 2m (1m identification + 1m explanation/inference)
- Language analysis: 3m (identification of language device + explanation of effect + sometimes a 3rd contextual mark)
- Inference: 2-3m depending on number of points required
- Summary: 8m (paragraph-bound, exact length cap)
- Cross-passage: 2m each (1m identification + 1m justification)
- AQ: 12m
- Language P2: 15m
- Language P1: 20m (out of 50; content = 30)

## CALIBRATION RULES FOR 2026 PAPER GENERATION (most important — apply to every prompt)

### Rule 1: 8-question P1 paper with mandatory theme balance
Every authentic P1 paper has exactly 8 questions. Distribution per paper (observed average across 24 schools):
- 1-2 arts questions (must include)
- 1-2 SG-anchored questions (3-4 if generous)
- 1 science/tech/AI question (must include — AI heavily favoured for 2026)
- 1-2 politics/governance/globalisation questions
- 1 environment question (must include from 2024 onward)
- 1 society/ethics/values question
- 1 media question (must include)
- Wildcards from: sport, education, health, family, religion, history, gender
- Singapore must be tagged on at least 3 questions ("your society" clause).

### Rule 2: Question STEM phrasing pool
Use only these 7 stem patterns (in this approximate ratio):
- "'[QUOTE]' [Discuss / Comment / What is your view?]" (40%)
- "To what extent is X true of your society / today?" (15%)
- "How far do you agree that [STATEMENT]?" (15%)
- "Should X / Is X [adj]?" (10%)
- "Consider the view that [STATEMENT]." (10%)
- "Examine / Assess / Evaluate [STATEMENT/CLAIM]." (5%)
- "Is X or Y? / Has X gone too far?" (5%)

Never invent new openers (e.g. avoid "Critically appraise", "Synthesise", "Analyse the proposition"). Cambridge does not use these.

### Rule 3: Length, format, marks (LOCKED)
- P1: 1h30, answer ONE of 8 questions, 500–800 words, 50 marks (30 content + 20 language).
- P2: 1h30, 8-10 comprehension Qs + 1 AQ of 12m, 50 marks (35 content + 15 language).
- Each P2 has 3 passages (rarely 2). Passages are 350–900 words each, typically by named or pseudonymous authors. Topic is contested: P1 pro-X, P2 anti-X (or different angle), P3 personal/illustrative.

### Rule 4: AQ construction (LOCKED)
- Use the directed form: "How far do you agree that [DIRECTED CLAIM derived from passage tensions]?"
- Always include both support bullets: "(a) ideas and opinions from at least one of the reading passages; (b) examples drawn from your own experience and that of your society."
- 12 marks.

### Rule 5: Singapore context examples bank (use these named, not invented)
From the SAS docs observed:
- Healthcare: CHAS subsidies (2019 expansion), Pioneer / Merdeka / Majulah Packages, Healthier SG (2023)
- Demographics: aging population, falling birthrates, foreign talent debate
- Politics: ruling party context, public consultation processes, multi-racial / multi-religious framing ("regardless of race, language or religion")
- Economy: "from Third World to First", pragmatism, meritocracy critique, HDB / public housing
- Tech / smart city: Smart Nation initiative, digital ID
- Education: PISA rankings, tuition culture, JC system, exam stress
- Arts: NAC funding, Singapore Biennale, Esplanade
- Climate: Singapore Green Plan 2030, carbon tax, low-lying island vulnerability
- Recent context: post-COVID recovery, presidential election 2023, 4G leadership transition

### Rule 6: Examiner reward conventions (from SAS observations)
A Band 4+ answer (24+ /30 content) requires:
- Direct engagement with the EXACT phrasing of the question (every key word)
- Singapore context woven in (not "name-dropped")
- Trends + Implications + Connections + Context (TICC) on every argument
- Counter-argument as its own paragraph, with detailed rebuttal — NOT one-line dismissal
- Hedged conclusions ("on the whole", "in general", "while X is true, Y") — never sweeping
- 2-3 specific, recent (post-2022) named examples per paragraph

A Band 3 answer (~21 /30) is identified by:
- Listing benefits/drawbacks without evaluating the question's verb (extent, assess, evaluate)
- Generic examples (USA, social media generally)
- Mechanical use of context phrases ("small sleepy fishing village")
- Strawman counter-arguments
- Single-paragraph "both sides" with both views in one paragraph

### Rule 7: Theme balance for an authentic 2026 generated paper

Must include:
- 1 AI / generative-AI question
- 1 climate / environment question
- 1 SG youth or society question
- 1 arts question
- 1 media / democracy question
- 1 politics / geopolitics question
- 1 science or health question (not AI)
- 1 wildcard (sport, family, religion, history, gender, foreign aid, urban life)

### Rule 8: P2 passage construction rules
- 3 passages, contested viewpoint
- Total combined word count 1500–2200
- Passages by named or pseudonymous authors (real names cited in source from Guardian, Economist, NYT, NPR, Atlantic, Medium, plus academic / blogger sources)
- Headers in italics: "Passage 1. [Author/source] [verb describing stance]."
- Passages are line-numbered in increments of 5 for citation

### Rule 9: Mark scheme presentation
- For each comprehension Q, show: From Passage (verbatim lift) | Suggested Answer (full sentence in own words) | Notes / DNA (do-not-accept / accept guidance) | Marks
- For AQ: provide Band Descriptors (Bands 1-5) AND list of 6-8 evaluable passage points each with a corresponding personal-experience rebuttal
- For P1: provide Terms & Scope, Question Requirements, Issue & Context, Possible Arguments (5 each side), Conceptual Understanding (TICC), Examiner's comments

### Rule 10: Avoid these AI / SAS errors that flag B-grade work
Words/phrases to avoid in model essays: "extreme views", "myopic", "delve into", "in today's society we see…", "social media has plagued", "irregardless", "trustable", "since young", "elderlies", "detractors", "this proves that".
