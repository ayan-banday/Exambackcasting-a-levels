## 2023 · ACJC · P2 — Passage: rituals (Karan Johnson, BBC)

**Topic:** The power of daily rituals — social/psychological functions, benefits, dangers
**Source:** Adapted from "The Surprising Power of Daily Rituals," Karan Johnson, BBC News
**Length:** 8 paragraphs, ~990 words
**Author voice:** Pop-science, journalistic, mildly persuasive

**Key examples in passage:** Trobriand Islands fishermen, New Year rituals (Spain, S. America), Rafael Nadal's 19 pre-match rituals, Beyoncé's prayer circle, hazing in military, self-flagellation, end-of-life rituals.

**Comprehension questions (50 marks total, ~17 SAQ + 8 summary + 10 AQ + 15 language):**

1. (Para 1) Why did fishermen perform elaborate rituals in open sea but not in lagoon? Own words. [2] — *Contrast Q*
2. (Para 1) Suggest why effigies are burnt during the New Year in Central and South America. [1] — *Inference Q*
3. (Line 14) Why does the author use 'fateful' to describe the decision to live in bigger groups? Own words. [2] — *Vocab/Literal Q*
4. (Lines 17–18) What does the author mean by 'nimble' and 'whimsical' minds? Own words. [2] — *Vocab Q*
5. (Para 3) How does the example of Rafael Nadal illustrate the importance of rituals? Own words. [2] — *Example Q*
6. (Lines 49–51) Explain the paradox. Own words. [1] — *Paradox Q*
7. (Line 70) Why has the author written 'spiritual practice' in inverted commas? [1] — *Punctuation Q*
8. (Lines 74–82) Explain how two examples illustrate the dangers of rituals. Own words. [2] — *Examples Q*
9. (Para 7) How is modern medicine similar to rituals and how are they different? Own words. [2] — *Compare/Contrast Q*
10. (Lines 91–94) Identify two ways the last two sentences provide an effective ending. [2] — *Stylistics Q*
11. **Summary** — Using para 4–6 only, summarise the benefits of rituals. Opening: "One benefit of rituals is..." Max 120 words. [8]
12. **AQ** — Author discusses benefits and detrimental effects of rituals. How far do you agree or disagree with the author's observations? Use examples from your own experience and that of your society. [10]

**Mark scheme conventions (notable):** Mark schemes tagged with question type. Each row has "from passage" + "suggested answer" + Examiner's Comments. Examiners' Comments include common student mistakes (e.g., "lifted 'conflicts'," "missed paradox," "did not provide context"). Summary scoring band: 14+ pts = 8m, 12-13 pts = 7m, 10-11 pts = 6m, etc.

**AQ marking framework:** Requirement / Explanation / Evaluation / Coherence (4 dimensions). Examples should be specific, not generic ("Chinese New Year" alone insufficient — must name specific ritual like exchanging red packets and elaborate).

## Recurring 2023 essay question structures

1. **Absolute-claim trigger** ("X is dead/inevitable/never/always/only Y"): ~35% of questions. Examples: "Compassion, more than resilience, is what we need" (CJC); "Rest is for the weak" (RVHS); "Peace is an unrealistic dream" (YIJC); "Traditions have no value in a modern world" (TJC).
2. **"To what extent / how far"** triggers: ~30%. Most common at ACJC, ASRJC, EJC. Invite ranged judgment, not binary yes/no.
3. **Singapore-anchored prompt** ("of your society"): ~20%. Frequency rising in 2023 vs earlier years.
4. **Comparative claim** (X vs Y): ~12%. e.g., "Compassion, more than resilience..." (CJC); "Penalties are more effective than incentives..." (VJC); "Corporations, rather than governments..." (RVHS, SAJC).
5. **Open invitation** ("Consider the view of..." / "What is your view?"): ~10%. Lighter trigger, broader scope expected.
6. **Either/or framing**: ~8%. e.g., "Is bigger always better?" (ASRJC); "threat or asset" (JPJC); "threat or opportunity" (DHS).

## AQ patterns

**Recurring AQ stem template (across all 17 JCs):**

> "[Author A] argues [position X], while [Author B] argues [position Y]. How far do you agree with the opinions expressed in these two passages? Support your answer with examples drawn from your own experience and that of your society."

OR (single-passage version):

> "[Author] discusses/argues [topic/claim]. How far do you agree or disagree with the author's observations? Illustrate your arguments with examples drawn from your own experience and that of your society."

**Common variations:**
- "Use examples of how you and your society regard [topic]."
- "Develop some of the points made by the authors..."
- "Relate your arguments to your own experience and that of your society."

**AQ marks:** Universally [10] across all 17 JCs.
**AQ requirements:** Engagement with passage ideas + Singapore application + agreement/disagreement spectrum + specific examples.

**AQ mark scheme conventions:**
- Requirement / Explanation / Evaluation / Coherence (ACJC framework)

- Examiner expects: specific Singapore examples (not generic), proper engagement with passage points (don't just quote and re-explain), evaluative judgment (not just description), cohesive paragraph structure with discourse markers.

## Calibration notes for paper generation

**Authentic question stems to model:**

- "'[Absolute claim].' To what extent do you agree?"
- "'[Provocative quote].' Is this true of your society?"
- "Consider the view that [claim]."
- "How far is [X] still relevant today?"
- "Should [stakeholder] [action]?"
- "To what extent [comparative]?"
- "[Topic] in [your society]. Discuss."

**Mark scheme language conventions:**

- SAQ vocab questions reward paraphrase (specific synonym range listed) with explicit accept/reject notes
- Stylistics questions reward Technique + Context (2-part answer)
- Paradox questions reward Y...yet/but...Z (2-part with connector)
- Summary scoring band: 14+ pts = 8m, top out at 8 marks
- AQ marks 10 with Requirement/Explanation/Evaluation/Coherence framework
- AQ expects specific Singapore examples, not generic phenomena
- Mark schemes increasingly include Examiner's Comments listing common student errors

**Stand-taking conventions in model answers:**

- AQ answers should state stand explicitly, sustain through ranged judgment ("largely agree but reject X"; "agree in part but...")
- Engagement: directly cite passage point, then evaluate against Singapore context
- Examples: NAME specific person/event/policy; vague references penalized
- Counter-balance: at least one paragraph showing opposing view with concession
- Conclusion: synthesise rather than restate; project forward where appropriate

## Five calibration rules for 2026 paper generation

1. **Absolute-claim triggers are the dominant format.** Use them ~35% of the time in any generated paper. Build them as "'X is dead/inevitable/never/only Y' Do you agree?" Provocative absolutes force candidates to engage critically.

2. **Singapore anchor in ~20% of P1 questions.** Use exact phrasings: "...of your society" or "...in your society" — not "in Singapore." This is the canonical examiner phrasing.

3. **Theme weighting: Politics + Ethics + Singapore should cover ~50% of any 12-question paper.** Then 2 each from Arts, Media, Tech, Environment, Economics, Education. 1 each from Family/Health/Sport/Gender.

4. **P2 passages should be 600–1000 words, 5–11 paragraphs, with named author + source attribution.** Twin-passage format is acceptable (and common at top JCs) for compare/contrast topics. Aeon and BBC are realistic source-style models. SAQs total 17 marks, summary 8 marks, AQ 10 marks, language 15 marks = 50 total.

5. **AQ stem must follow canonical template:** "[Author] argues [position]. How far do you agree/disagree with the views? Use examples from your own experience and that of your society." Marks always [10]. Mark scheme always uses Applicable/Less-Applicable to Singapore framing with specific local examples.
