# GP Examiner Patterns — Master Reference

Last updated: 2026-05-30
Scope: Cross-corpus synthesis from 840 source files (17 years of JC prelim papers 2008-2025 + Cambridge actual papers via H1 TYS suggested answers 2011-2021 + J1 promo papers 2022/2025). Use this FIRST when generating any GP practice paper.

## How to use this reference
- Paper-generation tasks: read this file + the relevant era sub-reference (especially the 2024-2025 reference for current style)
- Theme analysis: each section here aggregates evidence across 17 years
- Verbatim question banks live in the sub-references; this file curates the highest-value examples and lays out the calibration rules

## 1. The 8881 H1 GP Format (Ethan's 2026 sitting)

Paper code transition timeline:
- **2008-2010**: 8005 / 8006 / 8806 (pre-syllabus-update lineage)
- **2012**: 8806
- **2013**: 8807 transition begins
- **2014-2020**: 8807 mature mid-syllabus
- **2021+**: 8881 (current syllabus — what Ethan will sit)

### Paper 1 (Essay) — current 8881 format
- Duration: 1 hour 30 minutes
- Choice: answer ONE question from 12 (8807 era) → reducing to **8 in 8881** (confirmed by 2025 NYJC promo; not fully confirmed for prelim/actual but expect 8-12 range)
- Word count: 500-800 words
- Marks: 50 total = 30 Content + 20 Language
- All questions equally weighted

### Paper 2 (Comprehension) — current 8881 format
- Duration: 1 hour 30 minutes
- Format: comprehension passage(s) + Application Question (AQ)
- Marks: 50 total = 35 Content + 15 Language
- Comprehension SAQs ~17 marks
- Summary 8 marks (120-word cap)
- AQ 10-12 marks (standardising at 12 in 2024-2025)
- Twin-passage format dominant at top JCs from ~2015; ~60% of JCs by 2022; standard at most by 2024-2025
- Some 2024-2025 prelims use 3-passage format with cross-passage comparison Qs (ACJC, NJC, RVHS etc.) — Cambridge actuals remain single or twin

## 3. Theme Evolution: What's Rising, What's Declining

### Rising (testable for 2026)

1. **AI / generative AI** — 0 instances 2008-2014. Emerges 2015-16 (HCI Q3 "robotics", ACJC 2016 P2 "Coming Robot Dystopia"). Sparse 2017-2022 (~2-3 per cycle). SURGES 2024-25 (9 explicit AI questions: ASRJC 2024 Q4 + 2025 Q5, CJC 2024 Q4, DHS 2024 Q4, SAJC 2024 Q6, HCI 2022 Q4, TMJC 2025 Q1, etc.). Cambridge has NOT asked AI explicitly yet (as of 2021 TYS); probability for 2026 is very high.

2. **Climate / "who pays" framing** — environment present throughout, but framing shifts. Earlier era: "Is climate change real?" / "Can technology save us?". 2014-16: "individual vs government responsibility" (NJC 2016 Q7). 2020-22: "self-sufficiency" (EJC 2021 Q10). 2024-25: explicit DEVELOPED vs DEVELOPING nations debate (DHS 2024 Q8, RI 2025 Q7, TMJC 2025 Q3, JPJC 2025 Q2 materialism).

3. **Media trust / disinformation / democracy** — earlier era: "media bias", "free press". 2016: post-truth/fake news emerges (MJC 2016 P2 Viner). 2020-22: pandemic misinformation. 2024-25: explicit democracy + media cluster (EJC 2025 Q8, NJC 2025 Q5, RI 2025 Q6, TMJC 2025 Q4, TJC 2025 Q1, JPJC 2025 Q3).

4. **Geopolitics / diplomacy / sovereignty** — emerges sharply 2025 (Ukraine + Middle East + China-Taiwan context). ASRJC 2025 Q1 (diplomacy vs deterrence), HCI 2025 Q1 (warfare tech), HCI 2025 Q5 (Guterres quote), NJC 2025 Q1 ("boundaries no longer matter"), NJC 2025 Q8 (global cooperation feasible).

5. **Singapore anchor density** — rises from ~15% (2008-10) to ~20% (2012-13) to ~30%+ in 2024-25 papers, where every paper has 3-4 SG-anchored questions ("your society" mandatory).

6. **Sport as nation/commerce/pride** — sharply spiked 2025 (ACJC, CJC, ASRJC, SAJC, TMJC all featured sport). Linked to Paris Olympics 2024 + LA 2028 build-up.

7. **Mental health / wellbeing / loneliness** — emerges 2019-20 as standalone topic. RI 2020 Q4 "society done little to address mental health", TMJC 2020 Q1 mental health. By 2024-25 framed as "interconnected but lonelier" (HCI 2025 Q4).

8. **Authority / regulation / individual freedom** — pandemic-era questions (NYJC 2021 Q11 body authority, RI 2021 Q2). 2024-25 surge: ACJC 2025 P2 (authority), DHS 2025 Q4 (change), SAJC 2025 Q4 (youth policy), ACJC 2025 Q5 ("feared or loved").

### Declining (less likely but still possible)

1. **Pure economics** — 2008-09 financial crisis era was heavy on capitalism/poverty/corporations. Less explicit now; merged into ethics + politics framings.

2. **Traditional gender / women-in-workforce** — declining as standalone topic. Now woven into politics (women in decision-making) or sport (women in sport).

3. **War / militarism as standalone** — 2008-2010 heavy (Iraq era), 2012-13 ("nuclear technology"). Now mostly subsumed in geopolitics/diplomacy.

4. **Religion as standalone** — declining. Now framed as politics-religion or science-religion sub-theme.

5. **History as standalone** — declining. When tested, it's via specific framings (DHS 2024 Q3 "study history when we should look to future"; JPJC 2025 Q5 "understanding past").

6. **Family / parenting** — sporadic. Now woven into Singapore society or ethics framings.

### Stable but unchanged in salience

- Arts (always 1-2 per paper; "luxury vs necessity" debate is evergreen)
- Education (always present; framings shift: "exam stress" → "AI in classroom")
- Globalisation (always present; framings shift: borderless world → self-sufficiency)

## 4. P1 Essay Question Stem Patterns (Master)

### Stem types and frequency (aggregated across 17 years)

| Stem | Frequency | Era trend |
|---|---|---|
| "Quote." + Discuss / Do you agree? / Comment | ~40% all eras (most common) | Stable |
| "To what extent…?" | 20-25% | Stable |
| "How far do you agree…?" / "How far is X true…?" | 15-25% (rises post-2020) | Rising |
| "Discuss." (bare) | 10-15% | Stable |
| "Comment." (bare) | 8-12% | Stable |
| "Consider the view that…" | 10% | Stable |
| "Is X…?" / "Should X…?" (binary) | 10-15% | Stable |
| "What is your view?" / "What do you think?" | 8-10% | Stable |
| "Assess / Evaluate / Examine the view/claim" | 5-8% | Stable |
| "How effective / important / relevant is X?" | 5-8% | Stable |
| "How far has X…?" / "How far does X…?" | 5% | Stable |

### Question architecture taxonomy (from 2024-2025 reference — 7 patterns)

From the 2024-2025 agent's pool, these are the patterns to model when generating papers:

1. **Quote + discuss** (~40%): "'X is dead/inevitable/only Y.' Do you agree?"
2. **Your-society anchored** (~15-20%): "...is this true of your society?"
3. **How-far-agree** (~15%): "How far do you agree that…?"
4. **Should / Is** (~10%): binary stand-taking
5. **Consider-view** (~10%): open invitation
6. **Examine / Assess / Evaluate** (~5%): analytic command words
7. **Binary or comparative** (~5%): "X or Y?" / "X more than Y?"

### Question architectures (lens-based, from 2017-2019 reference)

- **Bilateral evaluative** (~50%): X vs not-X debate
- **Extent qualifier** (~25%): To what extent / How far — invites bands of agreement
- **Quoted-claim challenge** (~15%): hyperbolic claim → discuss/comment
- **Comparative trade-off** (~5%): X vs Y (innovation vs status quo, popular vs effective)
- **"In your society" suffix** (~30% appended to above): explicit Singapore frame

### Stand declaration patterns (from SAJC + EJC + RI answer schemes)

- Two-sided stand expected. Pure one-sided answers band-cap at ~16/30 Content.
- Best answers nuance the stand: "Largely agree, with the caveat that..."
- Mark scheme rewards: clarification of terms (what counts as X?) + concrete Singapore example + counterargument + rebuttal
- Standard scheme structure: **Analysis > Clarification of Terms > Stand > Possible Points and Examples** with bilateral Yes/No splits
- EJC 2025 Band 4+: "addressing the issue of [evaluative term]" + "incorporates Singaporean context/characteristics in determining stand"
- Band 3: "Lists general benefits and drawbacks" / "Listing of various government efforts"

## 5. The "Your Society" Anchor

**Singapore-context plant intensifies from ~15% (2008-2010) to 30%+ (2024-2025). Mandatory in AQ across all years.**

### Verbatim phrasings used (model these exactly when generating)

- "...in your society" (most common)
- "...of your society"
- "...your country"
- "...in Singapore" (explicit, less common — Cambridge usually avoids this)
- "How true is this of your society?"
- "Is this a fair description of your society today?"
- "Discuss this with reference to your society."
- "How far would you agree, relating your arguments to your own experience and that of your society?"

### Era patterns

- 2008-2010: ~15-20% of P1 Qs have "your society" plant
- 2012-2013: ~30-40% (sharp uptick)
- 2014-2016: ~20-25% (rising)
- 2017-2019: present in EVERY P1 paper (2-4 questions per paper)
- 2020-2022: present in EVERY P1 paper
- 2023-2025: every paper has 3-4 SG-anchored Qs; AQs universally require SG examples

### Mandatory anchor in AQ across ALL years

Every AQ (P2) requires Singapore application. No AQ allows generic "global" response. Verbatim canonical phrasings:

- "...relating your arguments to your own experience and that of your society"
- "How applicable do you find his/her observations to yourself and your own society?"
- "Illustrate your arguments with examples drawn from your own experience and that of your society"
- "Illustrate your answer with examples of how you and your society regard [X]"

| Era | Single-passage % | Twin-passage % | Notes |

### Cambridge actual papers (TYS 2011-2021)

- 2011-2014: single-passage (parks, music, fame, internet)
- 2015: DUAL passage (sport — Robb vs Benjamin)
- 2016: single (tourism)
- 2017: unknown (image-only PDF, ungrappled)
- 2018: single (voting)
- 2019: DUAL (zoos — Walford vs Morgan)
- 2020: single (apologising)
- 2021: single (fashion)

Cambridge mixes formats. Singles dominate for actuals but duals occur. For 2026 generation, both are authentic.

### Passage characteristics

- Length: 600-1000 words per passage (single); 350-650 per passage (twin); 350-900 (3-passage in 2024-25)
- Paragraphs: 5-11
- Author always named (real or pseudonymous)
- Source attributed: Guardian, Economist, Time, Atlantic, BBC, Aeon, NYT, NPR, Project Syndicate
- Modern academic sources rise post-2015: Steven Pinker, Costica Bradatan, Paul Bloom, Judith Lichtenberg
- Pseudonymous in-house authors common at DHS (Jim Nassium = sport, Justin Thyme = time, Mercedes Karr = transport — DHS uses cryptographic naming as signature)
- Topic types: cultural critique, political analysis, scientific commentary, behavioural psychology (dominant 2023+)
- Singapore authors rare but present: Chong Siow Ann (NJC 2015), Kirsten Han (TJC 2021), Sim Yi Ler (DHS 2020), Lester Wong (TJC 2021/2022)

### AQ stem evolution

| Era | Dominant AQ phrasing |
|---|---|
| 2008-2014 | "How applicable do you find his/her observations to yourself and your society?" |
| 2015-2021 | "[Author A] argues X, [Author B] argues Y. Which writer is more persuasive? Discuss the relevance to you and your society." (twin format) |
| 2022+ | "How far do you agree or disagree with the views expressed in [the/both] passages? Support with examples from your own experience and that of your society." |
| 2023+ | Directed AQ becoming standard: "How far do you agree that [DIRECTED CLAIM]?" |
| 2024-2025 | "How far do you agree that [DIRECTED CLAIM from passage tensions]? Support your answer with reference to: (a) the ideas and opinions from at least one of the reading passages; (b) examples drawn from your own experience and that of your society." |

### AQ marks evolution

- 2008-2010: 8 marks consistent
- 2011-2014: 8 marks (some JCs experimenting with 10m — CJC 2013, VJC 2013, YJC 2013)
- 2014-2016: 10 marks standard at most JCs
- 2017-2021: 10 marks dominant
- 2022-2023: 10 marks standard
- 2024-2025: **12 marks now norm** (was 10 in NYJC 2024)

### Gold-standard AQ model

**SAJC 2014 P2 sample AQ essay** (Emily Speight on internet) — full ~1500-word top-band essay published in the SAJC suggested answers. This is the closest thing to a Cambridge-validated model AQ that exists in the corpus. Worth studying line by line.

Structure of the gold-standard AQ:
1. Intro: contextualise (Singapore 1997 internet launch, household broadband penetration 105%), summarise author's stance, declare own stand
2. Body para per author point: paraphrase author claim → develop SG example (agree) or counter-example (qualify)
3. SG examples woven in throughout: Stomp, TR Emeritus, Mr Brown, MDA, Touch Cyber Wellness, Borders SG closing 2011, NTU/Andrew Duffy research, Amos Yee case
4. Conclusion: returns to overall stand with stat

### Dual-scale evaluation (personal + societal)

Cambridge actual papers increasingly demand TWO scales — personal AND societal. Prelims often only demand one. Modeled by:
- "...you and your society"
- "...yourself and your own society"
- "...your own experience and that of your society"

This is THE Cambridge standard for AQ. Generate accordingly.

### How Cambridge actuals differ from JC prelims

1. **Phrasing is tighter and more elegant** — prelims sometimes add redundant filler
2. **Topics are contemporary but not zeitgeist** — Cambridge avoids questions that date quickly (no specific war/celebrity)
3. **Mark schemes are stingier with marks** but more generous with accepted alternatives
4. **AQs demand dual scales** — personal AND societal
5. **Examiner Reports** (in TYS files like SAJC 2014, VJC 2012) reveal actual marking philosophy — these are the most valuable TYS content

## 9. JC House Styles

### Top-tier philosophy/density (RI, HCI, DHS, NJC, VJC)

- **HCI (Hwa Chong)**: most literary/aphoristic stems, philosophically dense. Bold/provocative absolute claims ("Revolution can only be a good thing", "Wherever power and wealth are at stake, there will be corruption"). Quoted single-clause stems. Strong argumentative passages with strong authorial voice. P2 twin-passage reliably from 2022. AI emergence Q4 2022.
- **RI (Raffles)**: sophisticated phrasings with comparative-claim emphasis ("Consider the value of X"). Clean formatting, real authors (Amy Chua, Susan David). P2 mark schemes with explicit "Singapore Applicable / Less Applicable" templates and named examples (Brownface, Monica Baey, Bukit Brown). Mostly single-passage P2.
- **DHS (Dunman High)**: strictest mark scheme — tabular Lifted/Inferred/Paraphrased columns. PSEUDONYMOUS authors as cryptographic clues (Jim Nassium=sport, Justin Thyme=time, Mercedes Karr=transport). Cover page uniquely shows Content/30 + Language/20. Either-or framing common.
- **NJC**: SH2 designation. Plain question stems without literary embellishment. Heavy use of absolute proverb-style claims. Increasingly twin-passage P2. Bilingual P1 in 2021 (Chinese version released).
- **VJC**: 10-min reading allowance built into 1h30. P2 questions with paragraph-by-paragraph fine-grained 1m/2m fragments. Mark schemes extremely thorough. Most explicitly Singapore-anchored P1 (5+ of 12 Qs use "your society"). VJC EXAM SCHEME UNUSUAL: 13 sub-Qs in P2, 10m AQ in earlier years (early deviator from 8m norm).

### Solid mainstream (ACJC, SAJC, TJC, CJC)

- **ACJC**: heavy two-passage tradition. Mark scheme uses two-column "Text reference | Inferred" format. ACJC mark schemes are GOLD STANDARD for Examiner's Comments — most granular meta-commentary on student errors.
- **SAJC**: uses "Singapore for Singaporeans" type explicit framing. Answer scheme structure: "Analysis / Clarification of Terms / Stand / Points / Examples" with bilateral Yes-No splits. More named-example density (Milgram, Anton Casey).
- **TJC**: heavy "How far do you agree" stems. Locally-authored passages (Lester Wong, Kirsten Han 2021 — woke culture). Detailed answer schemes (longest at 707 lines for 2023).
- **CJC**: plain prose stems, dual-passage P2 trend from 2017+. Provocative "Comment" stems. Metaphorical phrasings ("borrowing from the future").

### Steady mid-tier (JJC, SRJC, TPJC, YJC, PJC, IJC, MJC, NYJC, AJC, RVHS)

- **JJC**: conservative-trad question structures. "Standard Assessment Benchmarks" labelled. Clarification of terms first.
- **NYJC**: formal "What is your view? / Discuss this view" — most directly Cambridge-imitative. Often forward-looking P1 (workplace future, surveillance, solitude).
- **PJC**: 1-passage format with compound AQ phrasing ("develop some of the points... and some account of the experiences..."). Standard format with contemporary themes.
- **RVHS**: International Programme (RVIP) labelling. Heavy environment + politics. P2 twin-passage from 2022. Evocative/poetic P1 framings ("Invisible people living invisible lives", "Rest is for the weak").
- **MJC**: descriptive quotations + Discuss/Comment. Often longer prompts with multiple clauses.
- **IJC**: heavy "to what extent" and "how far". Dual-passage P2 with conformity-style themes.

### Newer / 3-year track JCs

- **MI (Millennia Institute)**: PU3 designation (Pre-University 3, 3-year track). Question style identical to JC papers, slightly more accessible phrasing. Twin-passage P2 standard. Always Singapore-anchored.
- **EJC (Eunoia)**: "To what extent / how far" dominant. P2 twin-passage with global cultural references (Chinese "tangping" in 2023). Mark schemes most detailed in cohort — full "Examiners' Report and Suggested Answer Scheme" with SUBJECT MATTER / KEY TERMS / CONTEXT / ASSUMPTIONS / QUESTION REQUIREMENTS / THOUGHTFUL RESPONSES architecture.
- **ASRJC, JPJC, TMJC, YIJC**: standard 12-question P1 format; twin-passage P2 reliably from 2022.

## 10. COVID-Era Marker (2020-2022)

~22 explicit/near-explicit pandemic-tinged questions catalogued in the 2020-2022 corpus (~5% of all essay questions). Cambridge NEVER named COVID-19 directly. JCs used coded framings:

### 2026 implication

COVID framing now dated. The meta-framing of "global shocks / future crises" continues. Self-sufficiency questions still appearing 2024-2025. WFH/workplace questions tapering. Cambridge convention holds: no proper-noun current-event references.

## 11. Most-Likely 2026 Themes (Ranked)

From 2024-2025 evidence + cross-era trend analysis:

1. **AI / generative AI** (HIGH) — 9 questions across 2024-25, Cambridge hasn't tested explicitly yet, ChatGPT 3+ years old in 2026. Highest probability. Likely stems: "AI as threat to humanity", "AI regulation", "AI replacing human creators", "society too dependent on data".

## 15. Calibration Rules for Paper Generation (the most important section)

These rules govern authentic 8881 paper generation. Every paper-generation task must satisfy these.

### Rule 1: P1 paper structure

- 12 essay questions (8807 era) — pending confirmation if 8881 reduces to 8
- 500-800 word target range
- 1h30 duration, 50 marks (30 Content + 20 Language)
- Every question equally weighted
- **Theme balance per paper:**
  - 1-2 arts questions (mandatory)
  - 1 AI / generative AI question (mandatory for 2026)
  - 1 climate / environment question (mandatory)
  - 1 SG youth or society question (mandatory)
  - 1 media / democracy question (mandatory)
  - 1 politics / geopolitics question (mandatory)
  - 1 science or health question (not AI)
  - 1 wildcard from: sport, family, religion, history, gender, foreign aid, urban life
  - **Singapore anchor tagged on at least 3-4 questions** ("your society" mandatory)

### Rule 2: P1 stem phrasing pool (7 patterns only)

Use ONLY these patterns, in these approximate ratios:

| Pattern | Ratio | Example |
|---|---|---|
| "'[QUOTE].' Discuss / Comment / What is your view?" | 40% | "'Profits without accountability.' Comment." |
| "To what extent is X true of your society / today?" | 15% | "To what extent is your country prepared for future crises?" |
| "How far do you agree that [statement]?" | 15% | "How far do you agree that nothing in the media can be trusted today?" |
| "Should X / Is X [adj]?" | 10% | "Should AI be feared or embraced?" |
| "Consider the view that [statement]." | 10% | "Consider the view that all countries have equal responsibility..." |
| "Examine / Assess / Evaluate [statement/claim]" | 5% | "Assess the view that..." |
| "Is X or Y? / Has X gone too far?" | 5% | "Has society become too dependent on data?" |

NEVER use: "Critically appraise", "Synthesise", "Analyse the proposition", "Discuss the merits and demerits". Cambridge does NOT use these.

### Rule 3: Length, format, marks (LOCKED)

- **P1**: 1h30, answer ONE of 12 (or 8 in 8881), 500-800 words, 50 marks (30C + 20L)
- **P2**: 1h30, 8-10 comprehension Qs + 1 AQ of 10-12m, 50 marks (35C + 15L)
- Twin-passage acceptable (common at top JCs); 3-passage emerging at some 2024-25 prelims
- Single-passage acceptable (Cambridge actuals mix singles and twins)
- Passages 600-1000 words (single); 350-650 (twin); 350-900 (3-passage)
- Named or pseudonymous authors with source attribution

### Rule 4: AQ construction (LOCKED canonical form)

Required template:

> "[Author/Authors] [argues/discusses] [topic/claim]. How far do you agree or disagree with the views expressed in [the passage/these passages]? Support your answer with examples drawn from your own experience and that of your society."

OR (directed AQ, 2023+ standard):

> "How far do you agree that [DIRECTED CLAIM derived from passage tensions]? Support your answer with reference to: (a) the ideas and opinions from at least one of the reading passages; (b) examples drawn from your own experience and that of your society."

- Marks: 10-12 (use 12 for 2026 generation)
- Mark scheme always uses Applicable/Less-Applicable to Singapore framing with specific local examples
- Personal AND societal scale required (dual scale = Cambridge actual standard)

### Rule 5: P2 comprehension Q types (match Cambridge mix)

Per paper, include this mix:
- 1-2 phrase/word meaning Qs (1-2m each)
- 1-2 language analysis Qs (2-3m each)
- 1-2 inference / "what does the author mean" Qs (2-3m each)
- 1 illustration Q ("how does the author show") (2m)
- 1 contrast / comparison Q across paragraphs (2-3m)
- 1 rhetorical-device / tone / structure Q (1-2m)
- 1 summary Q (8m, max 120 words, opener prescribed, paragraph-bound)
- (3-passage format only): 2 cross-passage Qs (2m each)
- AQ (10-12m)

Examiner-rewarded conventions:
- "Use your own words as far as possible" rubric mandatory
- Paragraph-bound summary with prescribed opener
- Inferential answers expected where applicable
- Mark schemes show Lifted | Inferred | Paraphrased columns

### Rule 6: Mark scheme presentation (per the EJC 2025 SAS gold standard)

For each comprehension Q, show:
- From Passage (verbatim lift)
- Suggested Answer (full sentence in own words)
- Notes / DNA (do-not-accept / accept guidance)
- Marks

For AQ, provide:
- Band Descriptors (Bands 1-5)
- List of 6-8 evaluable passage points with corresponding personal-experience rebuttal
- Singapore Applicable / Less Applicable column with specific examples

For P1 essays, provide:
- Terms & Scope
- Question Requirements
- Issue & Context
- Possible Arguments (5 each side)
- Conceptual Understanding (Trends, Implications, Connections, Context = TICC)
- Examiner's comments

### Rule 7: Examiner-flagged GOOD language (verbatim phrasings to use)

- Hedge sweeping claims: "in general", "on the whole", "most", "some", "in many cases"
- Counter-argument intros: "Some might say…", "I acknowledge that…", "Undeniably…"
- Argument shift signposts: "however", "yet", "while X is true, Y"
- Stand declarations: "It is true that…", "It is not true that…", "X is essential because…"
- Strong AQ openers (from VJC examiner reports): "When words fail, music speaks, goes the cliché…" / personal anecdotes / contextual framing of SG ("Singapore 1997 launch of internet…")

### Rule 8: Examiner-flagged BAD language (to AVOID)

NEVER use these in model essays / mark schemes:
- "delve into", "in today's society we see…", "social media has plagued"
- "detractors / critics / dissidents" (wrong register)
- "myopic / extreme / failing to consider" used as one-line rebuttals without explanation
- "irregardless", "trustable / untrustable", "since young" (use "from a young age")
- "elderlies" (use "the elderly"), "youths" (use "young people today")
- "discuss about", "voice out", "emphasise on" (redundant prepositions)
- "I agree to the author's view" (use "agree WITH")
- Mechanistic AQ openers: "In paragraph one, the author writes…"
- Strawman versions of opposing view
- Single-paragraph "both sides" combined — too thin
- Sweeping claims without qualifier
- Fractured run-on sentences
- Invented words

### Rule 9: Singapore examples bank (use these named, not invented)

Refer to Section 14 above. Mandatory: use NAMED specific examples from the bank, not generic "social media" or "Singapore." Examiners explicitly flag "Chinese New Year" alone as insufficient — must name "specific ritual like exchanging red packets and elaborate."

### Rule 10: Examiner reward conventions (Band 4+ criteria)

A Band 4+ answer (24+ /30 content) requires:
- Direct engagement with the EXACT phrasing of the question (every key word)
- Singapore context woven in (not "name-dropped")
- TICC (Trends + Implications + Connections + Context) on every argument
- Counter-argument as its own paragraph, with detailed rebuttal — NOT one-line dismissal
- Hedged conclusions (never sweeping)
- 2-3 specific, recent (post-2022) named examples per paragraph

A Band 3 (~21/30) is identified by:
- Listing benefits/drawbacks without evaluating the question's verb (extent, assess, evaluate)
- Generic examples (USA, social media generally)
- Mechanical use of context phrases ("small sleepy fishing village")
- Strawman counter-arguments
- Single-paragraph "both sides" with both views in one paragraph

### Rule 11: P2 passage construction rules

For 3-passage format (2024-25 prelim style):
- 3 passages, contested viewpoint
- Total combined word count 1500-2200
- Passages by named or pseudonymous authors (real names cited from Guardian, Economist, NYT, NPR, Atlantic, Medium, BBC, Aeon)
- Headers in italics: "Passage 1. [Author/source] [verb describing stance]."
- Passages line-numbered in increments of 5 for citation
- P1: pro-X; P2: anti-X (or different angle); P3: personal/illustrative

For single or twin (Cambridge actual style):
- 600-1000 words per passage (single)
- 500-650 per passage (twin)
- Named author + source attribution
- Line-numbered for citation
- Twin: clearly opposing views

### Rule 12: 2026-specific content guidance

Avoid dated framings:
- Direct COVID-19 references (Cambridge convention: never named)
- Specific celebrities/politicians by name (avoids dating)
- Brexit/Trump-era post-truth without timeless framing

Use forward-compatible framings:
- "Generative AI" / "algorithmic decisions" / "automated systems"
- "Global shocks" / "future crises" / "era of uncertainty"
- "Climate adaptation" / "developed vs developing burden"
- "Information ecosystems" / "democratic legitimacy"
