# GP Prelims 2008-2010 — Examiner Patterns Reference

Last updated: 2026-05-30
Scope: 148 files across 17 JCs and 1 PU centre (Millennia), 2008-2010 prelim cycle. Pre-syllabus-update era (papers coded 8005/8006/8806 — the pre-8881 lineage). The November A-Level prelim cycle that fed into the actual November Cambridge papers.

Paper format (consistent across the era):
- **Paper 1 (Essay):** 1 hour 30 minutes. Answer ONE of 12 questions. 500-800 words. Marked /50 (Content /30 + Language /20). All questions carry equal marks.
- **Paper 2 (Comprehension):** 1 hour 30 minutes. Answer ALL questions. Marked /50 (Content /35 + Language /15). Inserts contain passage(s); answer booklet contains comprehension Qs 1-10ish + AQ (Application Question, Q11 or Q12) worth 8 marks.

JCs covered: ACJC, AJC, CJC (Catholic), HCI, IJC, JJC, MI (Millennia, PU2 + PU3), MJC, NJC, NYJC, PJC, RI/RJC (Raffles), RVHS, SAJC, SRJC, TJC, TPJC, VJC, YJC, DHS.

### AQ (Application Question) — Verbatim Examples

**ACJC 2008 AQ (Q11) — celebrity activism**
"In Passage 1, Drezner argues that celebrities are more successful than politicians in promoting their causes, while in Passage 2, West suggests that celebrity activism can backfire. By referring to their views, discuss whether celebrity activism has been successful in reaching out to young people and whether it will become more effective in bringing about political and social change in the future." [8m]

**AJC 2008 AQ (Q11) — happiness**
"John Ciardi says happiness is the pursuit itself rather than the possession of anything concrete. Erich Fromm says man's happiness is superficial because it is directed by bureaucracy. Which author's view do you subscribe to more and explain why. Discuss the concerns your generation has in its search for happiness. In your answer, develop some of the points made by the authors and give your own views." [8m]

**DHS 2008 AQ — utopia**
"The author argues that attempts to create and drive utopias have been dangerous, enduring and futile. How far do you agree with his view? Support your answer with material from the passage, as well as examples drawn from your knowledge and experience." [8m]

**HCI 2008 AQ — celebrity culture**
"John Schumaker maintains that 'celebrity culture' is severely harming modern society. How far do you agree with his arguments? In your country, what effect is 'celebrity culture' having on your generation in particular? On balance, would you say that this influence is largely beneficial or detrimental? Refer specifically to relevant material from the passage as well as your own experience and opinions in your answer." [8m]

**JJC 2008 AQ — narcissism**
"In Passage A, Trevor Turner describes the manifestations of narcissism in society and discusses the possible reasons for it. In Passage B, Lakshmi Chaudhry examines how new media has fostered narcissism in people's lives. Which author do you find more convincing? ### P2 Comprehension Q-types (consistent across the era)

- **Inference / explain** (1-2m): "What does the word X tell you about…" / "Why does the writer use Y…"
- **Own-words paraphrase** (1-3m): "Explain in your own words as far as possible…"
- **Punctuation/quotation marks** (1m): "Why does the writer put X in inverted commas…"
- **Imagery / metaphor explanation** (2m): "Explain the metaphor…" / "Comment on the appropriateness of the image…"
- **Tone identification** (1-2m): "What is the tone of the writer here and why…"
- **Vocabulary** (5m, fixed slot): 5 single-word/short-phrase definitions from across both passages
- **Summary** (7-8m): "Using material from paragraphs X to Y, summarise WHY/HOW…" with given opening words and word limit (usually 120-130 words)
- **AQ (Application Question)** (8m): variant phrasings — see catalogue above

### AQ marking — Band descriptors (consistent across JCs in this era)

**Band A (6-8m):** systematic reference to both/the text(s), critical and convincing evaluation, apt and effective examples going beyond the texts, high coherence and organisation.
**Band B (3-5m):** examines points from both passages but discussion is superficial, examples clichéd or common, balance lacking, coherence not as sharp.
**Band C (1-2m):** examines only one passage or merely summarises ideas without critical commenting, lacks examples or links to today's world, disorganised.

Strands assessed: **Requirement** (systematic reference to text + question requirements), **Explanation** (depth of argument and examples), **Evaluation** (critical judgement + reasoning), **Coherence** (paragraphing + logical flow).

### Recurring essay question stems (counted across all 49 P1 papers)

| Stem | Approx. count | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| "Do you agree?" / "Do you agree that…?" | ~80 | "Do you agree that the problems of poverty…", "Do you agree with the view that…", "Is this true?" |
| "To what extent…?" | ~70 | "To what extent is patriotism harmful?", "To what extent are…?", "To what extent should…?" |
| "Discuss." / "Discuss this view." | ~60 | "Diplomacy is the key…Discuss.", "Animals matter. Discuss." |
| "How far do you agree?" / "How far is…?" | ~50 | "How far do you agree with this assessment of democracy?" |
| "Comment." / "Comment on…" | ~40 | "Romance novels are only for the foolish. Comment.", "National pride is an outdated notion. Comment." |
| "Is this true?" / "Is this a fair comment?" | ~35 | "It takes very little to be a celebrity these days. Is this true?" |
| "What is your view?" / "What do you think?" | ~25 | "What do you value more: beauty or intelligence?" |
| "Consider the view that…" | ~20 | "Consider the view that education makes people smarter, not better." |
| "Assess…" / "Evaluate…" / "Examine the claim…" | ~15 | "Evaluate this claim.", "Examine the claim that war is the most effective way…" |
| "Should…?" / "Is…?" simple question form | ~20 | "Should sports stars be paid obscene amounts of money?", "Is censorship self-defeating?" |
| Quote + "Comment" / "Discuss" | ~80 (overlaps with above) | Use of named quotes from MM Lee, Mandela, JFK, Wilde, Einstein, Churchill, Gandhi, Camus etc. |

Stem distribution insight: ~60% of questions use a balanced-argument stem ("to what extent / how far do you agree"). Cambridge wants weighed evaluation, not one-sided polemic. The remaining ~40% are "do you agree / is this true / comment" which still expect both sides but allow firmer one-sided conclusion if justified.

### Examiner-cited quotation patterns
Common pattern: anchor a Q with a named-quotation (Mandela, MM Lee, JFK, Wilde, Churchill, Gandhi, Einstein, Camus, Buck, Goethe, Asimov, Burke). These are designed to give students an interpretive anchor — they're not asked to know who said it but to engage with the claim. Example density: ACJC 2010 #10 (Obama "Yes We Can"), CJC 2009 #6 (MM Lee on nation), HCI 2010 #2 (Mandela on children), TJC 2010 #5 (Einstein on science/religion).

### P2 passage source patterns
- Heavy use of: NY Times, The Guardian, The Economist, Time Magazine, Reader's Digest, BBC News features
- Book extracts: John Gray, Shashi Tharoor, Jeffrey Sachs, Paul Collier, Steven Pinker, David Brooks
- Word counts of passages: ~750-950 words per passage. Twin-passage papers: ~600-750 each.
- Era favourites: celebrity culture (4+ papers), narcissism, climate/environment, technology and youth, fear/utopianism, democracy critique

### AQ structural patterns
- **Single-passage AQs (2008-mostly)**: "How far do you agree with the writer's view? Support your answer with material from the passage as well as examples from your knowledge and experience."
- **Twin-passage AQs (2009-2010)**: "Author A argues X, Author B argues Y. Which view do you find more convincing? / How far do you agree with their views? / Discuss with reference to your society/country."
- Almost all AQs ask for Singapore/own society contextualisation
- 8 marks consistently allocated; never split across multiple sub-parts
- The "your generation" framing appears in ~30% of AQs (especially celebrity / youth / new media topics)

### JC house-style observations (qualitative)

- **HCI / RI / NJC / DHS / VJC**: Most demanding questions. Heavier philosophical/quotation-anchored prompts (Camus, Wilde, Asimov, Mandela). More inferential AQs. Two-passage P2s. Setters expect higher-band candidates.
- **ACJC / SAJC / TJC / CJC**: Solidly mainstream Cambridge-style. Mix of one-side and balanced-stand questions. Heavy Singapore engagement.
- **JJC / SRJC / TPJC / YJC / PJC**: Slightly more concrete and approachable phrasings. Heavier use of direct "Do you agree?" and "Discuss" without elaborate quotes. Singapore Qs frequent. Suitable for mid-band candidates.
- **IJC / MJC / NYJC**: Mixed; tend toward more specific or constrained Qs (e.g. "Discuss with reference to one of the following: a, b, c"). MJC tends toward longer prompts with multiple clauses.
- **MI (Millennia, the only PU centre)**: Slightly simpler phrasing, more reliance on common topics (charity, social responsibility, choice, etc.). Targeting H1 PU students, not JC IP/Express.
- **RVHS** (Y6 IP): Quote-heavy (Samaranch, Penn, Wilde, Clinton). Tendency toward poetic/literary framing.

### Cross-year evolution observations

- **2008**: Slightly more single-passage P2s. Celebrity culture, utopianism, privacy/security were "in the air" (post-9/11 era, Web 2.0 emerging).
- **2009**: Two-passage P2s become dominant. Global financial crisis influences questions on capitalism, foreign aid, charity, business ethics.
- **2010**: Two-passage P2s ubiquitous. Internet, social media, ethical consumerism, racism, urbanisation surge. Strong "Noughties retrospective" angle (ACJC 2010 P2). Climate and energy hot.

### Things the era TESTS reliably that are LESS prominent in modern syllabus

- The "Singapore context" + "your country" double-phrasing is constant — modern 8881 Qs still do this.
- Heavy fixation on **celebrity culture** as a teaching topic (4+ P2 papers, several P1 Qs) is somewhat reduced post-2015.
- Less explicit AI/digital-life questioning; the era is "Web 2.0" framed (Facebook, YouTube, blogs, mobile phones) not AI/machine learning.
- The **utopia / civil society / democracy critique** thread is heavier than in recent syllabi.
- Religion appears more often as a discussion topic (peace, extremism, dignity, secularism) than in modern Qs.
