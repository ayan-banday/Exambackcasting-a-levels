## TYS 2011 P2 Suggested Answers · SAJC

### Original Cambridge Paper Reconstruction
- **Passage topic:** "Parks and Green Spaces" by Brendan Thomas (8807 H1 GP Paper 2, 2011)
- **Passage themes:** environment, urban planning, lifestyle, sociology
- **Theme tags:** environment, Singapore (AQ context), education (implicit)

### All Comprehension Questions Verbatim

1. In the first paragraph, how does the author illustrate his description of Nero as 'vain'? **[1]**

2. What is the author implying by using the word 'currently' to describe Nero's reputation (line 1)? **[1]**

3. What is the author implying by using the word 'cocooned' to describe the solo jogger? **[1]**

4. What is the author hinting at by his use of the words 'momentarily at least' (line 21)? Use your own words as far as possible. **[2]**

5. When the author writes that activities in parks are 'impromptu, purposeless, and ungainful' (line 32), what is he implying in each case about the values of the world outside the park? Use your own words as far as possible. **[3]**

6. Explain what is ironic about parks and green spaces being 'sanctuaries of calm' in paragraph 3. **[2]**

7. What is the author's purpose in describing the process whereby trees cool and filter the air as being 'like perspiration from our skin' and 'like lungs' (lines 41-42)? **[2]**

8. In what ways does the entrance policy of High Line (lines 52-53) differ from the general principles of access to parks described in paragraph two? Use your own words as far as possible. **[2]**

9. Explain how the author justifies his use of the word 'illusory' to describe visits to zoos and botanical gardens (lines 68-70). Use your own words as far as possible. **[2]**

10. Suggest one reason for the author ending the passage with the Latin phrase rus in urbe (line 79). **[1]**

11. **Summary [8]:** Using material from paragraphs 2-4 only (lines 12-45), summarise what the author has to say about the benefits that parks and green spaces have for individuals and cities. (Opener: "Parks and green spaces are of benefit because…")

12. **AQ verbatim:** "In this article, Brendan Thomas makes a number of observations about how valuable parks and green spaces are to cities and their inhabitants. How applicable do you find his observations to yourself and your own society?"

### Suggested Answer Analysis

- **AQ structure:** SAJC presents AQ as a 6-row table organised by passage paragraph (Para 2 → 3 → 5 → 6 → 7). Each row has 3 columns: Observations from article | Evaluation | Applicability to Singapore. This is the **2-column→3-column scaffold** of an AQ paragraph: state author's claim → evaluate it generally → apply to Singapore with specific examples.
- **AQ examples used (Singapore):** Gardens by the Bay (paid, profit-driven, possible exclusion of poor), Marina Barrage (free, accessible), park connectors (Siglap, Tampines, Pasir Ris), Pulau Ubin/Bukit Timah/Chek Jawa (truly natural), Night Safari/Singapore Zoo/Botanical Gardens (tourist-priced), community gardening among elderly, Flower Domes/micro-managed habitats.
- **Mark scheme conventions inferred:**
  - 1m questions: single inference expected, must be in context (e.g. "isolated" alone fails Q3; must convey "cut off from the world around him")
  - 2m questions: **two-part answer expected** — Q4 explicitly says "Student MUST note the 2 separate ideas in the phrase for a 2m question [2 or 0]"
  - 3m vocabulary-flip questions: must produce target paraphrase. "Speed-related words" fail for "impromptu" — required word is "planning, preparation"
  - Irony questions need both expectation AND reality stated explicitly
  - "Like X" analogy questions need BOTH the analogy mechanism explained AND the implied significance
- **Summary mark scheme:** 14+ points = 8m, 12-13 = 7m, 10-11 = 6m, 8-9 = 5m… straight conversion table.
- **Verbatim model phrasings worth preserving:**
  - "stage-managed wildness" → "artificially managed/artificially engineered/shaped and controlled by man"
  - "rus in urbe" closing connects to opening Nero/Roman story = thematic closure
  - SAJC summary opener template: "Parks and green spaces are of benefit because they offer a reprieve from overpopulated cities and unite communities…" (121 words, scoring full marks)

## TYS 2012 P2 Suggested Answers · VJC

### Original Cambridge Paper Reconstruction
- **Passage topic:** Music (Michael Gordon)
- **Theme tags:** arts, science (cognition), education, health

### All Comprehension Questions Verbatim

1. What does the quotation from Congreve suggest about the power of music (lines 1-2)? Use your own words as far as possible. **[2]**

2. In the first paragraph, what criticisms does the author make of the music he is referring to? Use your own words as far as possible. **[2]**

3. Who might dismiss the author's views as 'jaundiced' (line 8)? **[1]**

4. What two roles did music perform for members of the societies described in paragraph 2? Use your own words as far as possible. **[2]**

5. How does the story of Orpheus in paragraph 3 demonstrate the power of his music and his 'human frailty' (line 24)? Use your own words as far as possible. **[2]**

6. Why has the author placed the sentence about perfect pitch (lines 55-57) inside brackets? **[1]**

7. Explain what the author means by calling the musical preference on social networking sites 'coded' (line 62). Use your own words as far as possible. **[2]**

8. In paragraph 8, what contrasts does the author make between the fate of Mozart and that of some modern popular musicians? Use your own words as far as possible. **[2]**

9. **Vocabulary [5x1]:** strident, disparate, conducive, readily, enigma

10. **Summary [8]:** Using material from paragraphs 4-6 only, summarise what the author has to say about the benefits that music brings us. (Opener: "Music is of benefit to us because…")

11. **AQ verbatim:** "In this article, Michael Gordon writes about some of the roles and functions music plays in modern life. To what extent do you agree or disagree with his views? Illustrate your answer by referring to the ways in which you and your society regard music."

### Suggested Answer Analysis
- **VJC writes detailed Examiners' Comments** for almost every question — invaluable for understanding what Cambridge actually rewards. Key extracted patterns:
  - For 2m questions, "the question required a careful, point-by-point comparison" (Q8 cross-reference). Cambridge wants **3 explicit comparisons** for "contrast" Qs: wealth, influence, society's response.
  - Misreading is heavily penalised. Many candidates wrote "breast" as "beast" in Q1 — wrong direction entirely.
  - AQ examiner feedback explicitly says: avoid "In paragraph one, Gordon writes that…" mechanistic openings. Use varied discourse markers.
  - Vocabulary mark scheme = 1 word per mark. "shrill" "raucous" "ear-splitting" all accepted for strident.
- **AQ examiner comments highlight key teaching points:**
  - "an apt personal anecdote can add a different dimension to what can so often be an impersonal, unengaged response" — one candidate scored well for grandmother + Alzheimer's + music = remembered grandchildren's names
  - One candidate's killer opener: "When words fail, music speaks, goes the cliché…"
  - One witty SG application: "compared to a job in the financial sector 'music takes a back seat — and maybe it is even in the car boot'"
- **Use of English errors examiners flag:**
  - "I agree to the author's view" should be "I agree with…"
  - "the author emphasises", NOT "emphasises on"
  - "young people today" preferred over "youths" (latter is pejorative)
  - "the elderly", NOT "elderlies"
  - Comma splicing → rambling sentences
- **Summary mark conversion:** 15+ pts = 8m, 13-14 = 7m, 11-12 = 6m… ascending pattern.

The inclusion of these shows the students' lack of selectivity of points related to the reasons for an interest in celebrities." — **selectivity is graded**.
- **AQ note:** "Please check in with Mr Tan regarding the AQ" — VJC's 2013 scheme leaves the AQ model answer unfinished, suggesting it requires teacher-led discussion.
- Summary mark conversion: 14+ = 8m, 13 = 7m, 11-12 = 6m, etc. (slightly different from 2012's 15+ for 8m)

## TYS 2014 P2 Suggested Answers · VJC + SAJC (both versions)

### Original Cambridge Paper Reconstruction
- **Passage topic:** Internet / Digital revolution / Printing press parallel (Emily Speight)
- **Theme tags:** technology, media, education, health

### All Comprehension Questions Verbatim

1. What are the purposes of the author's opening question? **[2]**

2. In lines 2-4, what similarity does the author see between the effects of the invention of the printing press and the development of the internet? Use your own words as far as possible. **[1]**

3. Explain what the author means by describing what has happened to English on the web as "the converse" (line 30) of what happened to Latin. Use your own words as far as possible. **[1]**

4. In paragraph 5, what parallels does the author draw between the consequences for authors of the printing revolution and contributors to today's internet? Use your own words as far as possible. **[3]**

5. In what ways does the author consider the internet "preferable" to television in lines 52-54? Use your own words as far as possible. **[2]**

6. Explain the author's use of the word "very" in line 59. **[1]**

7. In lines 61-66, what does the author suggest are the educational and personal consequences for those who "skim rather than delve"? Use your own words as far as possible. **[2]**

8. In paragraph 8, the author claims that "technological advance is always made at the cost of the previous behaviours or dearly-held principles". How does the remainder of the paragraph illustrate her assertion? Use your own words as far as possible. **[2]**

9. In lines 78-81, how confident is the author about future use of the internet by the young? Use your own words as far as possible. **[2]**

10. How does the quotation from Zhou Enlai (lines 83-84) support the author's argument earlier in this paragraph? Use your own words as far as possible. **[1]**

11. **Summary [8]:** Using material from paragraphs 2-4 only (lines 5-32), summarise what the author has to say about the changes to book production brought about by the invention of the printing press. (Opener: "After the invention of the printing press…")

12. **AQ verbatim:** "Emily Speight generally welcomes the internet but highlights some concerns about its possible effects on individuals and society. How far would you agree with her observations, both for and against the internet, relating your arguments to your own society?" [10]

### Suggested Answer Analysis (combined VJC + SAJC)
- **SAJC's 2014 file is the most complete TYS resource in the corpus** — it includes a full ~1500-word sample AQ essay scoring at the top band. **Worth memorising as a model.**
- **Sample AQ structure (SAJC) — the gold standard:**
  - Intro: contextualise (Singapore 1997 launch of internet, ubiquity), summarise author's stance (welcomes + cautions), declare own stand (deeply applicable to SG)
  - Body para per author point: each para opens by paraphrasing the author's claim, then either agrees with developed SG example or qualifies/disagrees with developed counter-example
  - SG examples woven in: Stomp, TR Emeritus, The Real Singapore, Mr Brown, The Independent, Creative for Causes, #GivingTuesday, MDA, Touch Cyber Wellness (1 in 3 secondary students bullied), Borders SG closing 2011, Page One Vivocity closing 2013, BookActually + Tiong Bahru indie bookshops, Anton Casey, Trend Micro 2014 survey (66% unprotected), Singapore Police Force love scam stats, NTU/Andrew Duffy youth internet research, AGC/Amos Yee case
  - Conclusion: returns to overall stand, mentions household broadband penetration 105%
- **Mark scheme cross-validation (VJC vs SAJC):**
  - Both agree Q4 needs **paired answers** — 1m per pair, not per point. SAJC: "Answers are to be phrased in pairs…"
  - Both require **degree of intensity** in Q2 — "caused problems" / "negative effects" reject (does not capture "seismic")
  - Both flag "the spread of English is being achieved at the expense of Latin" as a wrong reading
- **Specific paraphrasing rejections:**
  - "haste/unhurried" for "impromptu" = 0 (wrong direction — needs planning/preparation)
  - "meaningful, fruitfulness, profitability" for "ungainful" = 0 (needs "quest for tangible benefits")
- **Cambridge examiners' actual report quoted:** "many candidates wrote effectively about the empowering effect the internet was having for 'netizens'… abuses of the net were highlighted: ill-judged blogs, online gambling and internet fraud…" — this is the actual Cambridge feedback letter, GOLD for understanding what marks went where in the real exam.

5. **Summary [8]:** Using material from paragraphs 2-4 only, summarise what the author has to say about the benefits sport brings both to the individual and to society. (Opener: "Firstly, playing sport benefits us…")

**From Passage 2:**
6. What is the author implying about reaction to the survey by the use of the word "resignation" in line 7? **[1]**
7. In paragraph 2, how does the author support his idea that the Faust myth "does not seem outdated" (line 14)? Use your own words as far as possible. **[2]**
8. Why has the author written "performance-enhancing substances" in inverted commas (line 17)? **[1]**
9. What distinction is the author making when he refers to "confessions and revelations of drug taking (lines 19-20)? Use your own words as far as possible. **[2]**
10. In paragraph 4, what differences does the author see between successful athletes in the nineteenth century and those of today? Use your own words as far as possible. **[2]**
11. In the final sentence of paragraph 5, what contrast does the author make between what sport should be and what it often is? Use your own words as far as possible. **[2]**

### Suggested Answer Analysis
- **2015 is a DUAL-PASSAGE paper** — distinct from single-passage 2011-2014. Two passages, separate question groups, then a dual-source AQ.
- **AQ requirements explicit in mark scheme:**
  1. Must make a stand addressing "How important is sport…?"
  2. Apply to given context — "you and your society"
  3. Make reference to key ideas/main points in **both** passages showing clearly which key ideas/main points you agree or disagree with
- **Key idea contrast structure (for dual-passage AQ):**
  - Robb (positive): physical, character, self-esteem, stress outlet, leadership, modesty/resilience, aesthetic pleasure for spectators, patriotism, unity, innovation, prestige, economy
  - Benjamin (negative): drug-taking, financial scandals, match-fixing, winning-is-everything ethos, professionalisation kills joy, bad behaviour celebrated, sport as distraction from real issues, media bias
- **Examiners' comments highlights:**
  - Singapore-specific debate: F1 Grand Prix (supporters vs detractors), Youth Olympics, fast-paced life prevents sport, debate over foreign-born athletes representing Singapore
  - Drug-taking "not an issue in Singapore" due to stringent controls
  - Match-fixing debate
  - Strong answers move beyond description to "insightful and energetic analysis of the purposes and intentions of policies and their short and long term impacts, mediated through the candidates' own experiences"

## TYS 2016 P2 Suggested Answers · VJC

### Original Cambridge Paper Reconstruction
- **Passage topic:** Tourism (Elizabeth Gorey) — focused on Thomas Cook's history + modern tourism
- **Theme tags:** globalisation, economics, environment, ethics, Singapore (tourism destination)

### All Comprehension Questions Verbatim

1. How does the opening sentence demonstrate Thomas Cook's success as a businessman? **[1]**
2. Why would taking trips to Biblical sites be 'dear to Cook's heart' (lines 8-9)? **[1]**
3. In lines 1-14, how does the author illustrate her description of Cook's career as a 'combination of selflessness, personal gain and moral zeal' (line 15)? **[3]**
4. What reasons does the author suggest in lines 17-18 for Cook's tours being popular with women? Use your own words as far as possible. **[2]**
5. In paragraph 3, what distinctions does the author draw between those who travelled in the eighteenth century and some of 'today's gap-year students' (line 27)? Use your own words as far as possible. **[3]**
6. Using your own words as far as possible, explain what the author suggests modern tourists are trying to 'escape from' (line 37) and what they hope to 'escape to' (line 38). **[2]**
7. Explain the author's use of the phrase 'what is even worse' in line 61. Use your own words as far as possible. **[2]**
8. Why do you think the author switches to 'we' in the final paragraph? **[1]**
9. In the light of the final paragraph, why does the author describe Cook's early customers as 'fortunate' (line 86)? Use your own words as far as possible. **[2]**
10. **Summary [8]:** Using material from paragraphs 5-7 only (lines 44-78), summarise what the author has to say about the positive and negative effects of tourism on the destinations visited. (Opener: "One benefit of tourism is…")
11. **AQ verbatim:** "Elizabeth Gorey sees undoubted benefits to tourism but also a growing number of drawbacks. How far do you agree with her observations, relating your arguments to your own experience and that of your society?" **[10]**

### Suggested Answer Analysis
- **Q3 is a 3-trait illustration question** (selflessness + personal gain + moral zeal). One illustration per trait, 1m each. Examiners explicitly note: "weaker answers paraphrased the material without providing a clear link between each of Cook's motivations and the evidence of that motivation."
- **Q5 is a 3-way contrast question** with paired answers. Mark scheme says: "Allow for possible pairing/distinctions, e.g. network with the rich vs helping the underprivileged."
- **Summary mark conversion:** 29 possible points, 15+ = 8m, 13-14 = 7m, 11-12 = 6m… (different scaling because more points available)
- **AQ examples used:** Singapore Tourism Board, Merlion, Jurong Bird Park, Sentosa, medical tourism, YourSingapore branding strategy, Chinatown/Little India/Kampong Glam preservation
- **AQ examiners' comments — key teaching points:**
  - Weaker answers were "formulaic: a quotation from the text — often far too lengthy — was followed by the unnecessary recasting of the quotation into the candidate's own words; then one or two examples of an aspect of tourism were listed; the paragraph then ended with a bald statement of agreement or disagreement with the author's view"
  - Stronger answers: candidates "chose an aspect of the original text that chimed with personal experience and the consequence was writing which was vigorous and engaging"
  - Personal experience of working abroad + cynical view (CV burnishing not altruism) = strong AQ
  - "Parents being constantly anxious about the work they were supposedly having a break from" — strong personal observation

1. In paragraph 1, how does the author demonstrate that Alex Orlyuk is not living a life of 'political apathy'? Use your own words as far as possible. **[3]**
2. How does the author support their assertion that the voting behaviour of Israeli young people is 'all of a pattern with the rest of the rich world' (line 14)? **[1]**

4. Explain the author's use of the word 'presages' in line 22. **[1]**
5. In paragraph 3, how does the author suggest that the attitudes of millennials towards voting may change in the future? Use your own words as far as possible. **[3]**
6. In paragraph 4, what reason does the author suggest as to why 'Millennials do not see voting as a duty' (lines 33-34)? Use your own words as far as possible. **[2]**
7. Explain what the author means by 'a cycle of neglect and alienation' (line 41). **[2]**
8. In paragraph 6, what distinctions does the author draw between the way in which old people and young workers are treated by some policies? **[2]**
9. **Summary [8]:** Using material from paragraphs 7-9 only (lines 53-74), summarise what the author has to say about the positive and negative effects of the strategies used to persuade more young people to vote. (Opener: "One positive effect is…")
10. In paragraph 10, how does the author counter their earlier claim that Alex Orlyuk's attitude is one of 'disaffection' (line 9)? **[1]**
11. **AQ verbatim:** "The author argues that young people 'are more interested in issues and causes than they are given credit for' (line 27). How far do you agree or disagree with this view? ### Suggested Answer Analysis
- **Most-quoted Singapore AQ example: Pink Dot + LGBT rights.** Strong responses contextualised it as "heavily dependent on modern technology" and "against the backdrop of a conservative society."
- **Other strong SG examples:** Ang Zyn Yee anti-plastic-straws campaign, mental health initiatives, treatment of immigrants
- **Lexical handling note:** Some candidates misread "credit" as academic/financial credits rather than "having one's efforts acknowledged, respected and praised" — significant misreading.
- **Mark scheme key idea:** Q5 needs **3 points for 3 marks** about how attitudes may CHANGE: predecessors disproved claims, parents-with-children become more engaged, current gen settling later. Examiners flag the missed implication that "the current generation might act as previous generations did."
- **Use of English errors flagged by examiners:**
  - "ministry of education" / "singapore" — missing capitals on proper nouns
  - "discuss about", "voice out", "emphasise on" — redundant prepositions
  - Good idioms in strong scripts: "plucked out of thin air", "passed with flying colours"
- **Summary scale:** 24 possible points, 15+ = 8m. Adjusts to passage's point density.

# PART 3 — Synthesis: Promos + H1 TYS Patterns

## Promo vs Prelim difficulty calibration

| Dimension | Promo (J1 end-of-year) | Prelim (J2 mid-year) | Cambridge actual (J2 final) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Question count P1 | 8–12 | 12 | 12 (now 8 in 8881) |
| Mark allocation P1 | 50 (30 content + 20 lang) | 50 | 50 |
| AQ marks P2 | 10 | 10 | 10 |
| Language marks P2 | 15/50 (lower) | 20/50 (same as actuals) | 20/50 |
| Phrasing style | Direct, single-clause | Often compound clauses, conditional | Compound, occasional verbatim quotes ("X said Y") |
| Theme range | Predictable core themes | Stretches to niche themes (language, specific art forms) | Mix of safe + curve-ball (e.g. Israeli millennials 2018, fashion industry economics 2021) |
| Stakeholder demand | Implied | Often explicit ("for your society") | Both — and increasingly nuanced ("for you and your society", "for individuals and society") |
| Difficulty distribution | All ~30/50 difficulty | Mix of 30 + 40+ | Skewed harder, especially for 2018+ papers |

**Promos under-prepare for prelims and actuals on three axes:**
1. **Niche themes.** Promos rarely test arts-specific, language-specific, or stakeholder-specific Qs.
2. **AQ stakeholder weighting.** Promo AQs often just say "your society" — actual Cambridge AQs increasingly demand "you AND your society", forcing personal anecdote.
3. **Language mark weight.** Promo P2 lang = 15/50, actual = 20/50. A J1 who passes promo with weak language gets a rude shock at prelim.

### AQ structure conventions in actual Cambridge papers

Common AQ template phrasing across actual papers:
- "How far do you agree/disagree with [author's] observations…"
- "…relating your arguments to your own experience and that of your society?"
- "Illustrate your answer by referring to…"
- Some have explicit author-quotation embedded in the question (2013, 2018) — these require the candidate to engage with that specific quoted phrase
- Dual-passage AQs (2015, 2019) demand engagement with **both** authors and a clear stand between them

Common AQ structural expectations Cambridge rewards (synthesised from VJC examiner comments across years):
1. **Stand declared in introduction.** Not deferred to conclusion.
2. **Engage author's specific claims** — not just quote, but evaluate the claim (agree-with-developed-example or qualify-with-counter-example)
3. **Apply to Singapore society** with specific named examples (people, brands, government bodies, statistics, year-stamped events)
4. **Personal voice** where appropriate — examiner reports repeatedly praise "informed personal voice", warn against "impersonal, unengaged response"
5. **Cohesion** — varied discourse markers between paragraphs. "In paragraph one, the author writes…" is explicitly flagged as weak.
6. **No mechanical recasting.** Lengthy quote → "What the author means is…" → bald agree/disagree statement is the canonical weak pattern.
7. **Nuance over extremes.** "Most" / "some" / "in many cases" instead of "all" / "always".

### How Cambridge actual papers differ from school prelim papers

1. **Cambridge phrasing is tighter and more elegant.** Prelims sometimes lift Cambridge phrasing verbatim (good practice) but often add redundant filler.
2. **Cambridge passage topics are contemporary but not zeitgeist.** Cambridge avoids questions that will date quickly. Prelims sometimes lean too heavily on current events.
3. **Cambridge mark schemes are stingier with marks** but more generous with accepted alternatives. Prelim mark schemes often list one accepted answer; Cambridge lists 2–3 acceptable phrasings.
4. **Cambridge AQs increasingly demand TWO scales** — personal AND societal — while prelims often only demand one.
5. **Cambridge Examiner Reports** (published in TYS files like SAJC 2014, VJC 2012) reveal the actual marking philosophy. **These are the most valuable TYS content**. Prelim "Examiner's Comments" are written by the school teacher, not Cambridge.
