# GP Prelims 2017-2019 Examiner Patterns Reference

Coverage: 22 distinct prelim papers across 16 JCs and 3 years. Mid-syllabus 8807 era (pre-8881 transition in 2021). 78 source files processed (P1 QPs, P2 inserts, P2 question booklets, answer schemes/suggested answers — many of which are duplicate inserts/booklets in different formats).

JCs covered: AJC, CJC, DHS, HCI, IJC, JJC, MI, MJC, NJC, NYJC, PJC, RVHS, SAJC, TJC, VJC, NYJC, NJC.

### 2017 · AJC · P2 (28 Aug 2017)

### 2017 · CJC · P2 (17 Aug 2017)

- **Dual-passage** structure. Theme: spontaneity.
- Passage 1: **Leon F. Seltzer**, "The Wisdom of Spontaneity" — 6 paragraphs, ~750 words. Distinguishes spontaneity from impulsivity, defends spontaneity as adaptable, flexible, creative.
- Passage 2: **Steven Poole**, "Think Before You Act" — 6 paragraphs, ~600 words. Argues spontaneity is a marketed illusion, decision fatigue makes spontaneity counter-productive.
- AQ (Q12): "Seltzer believes in the value of spontaneity whereas Poole thinks that there are problems with it. How important is spontaneity to you and your society, and how far would you agree or disagree with the opinions expressed in these two passages?"

### Recurring essay question structures

**The 11 dominant Cambridge stems (verbatim phrasings, occurrence-counted):**

1. `'X.' Do you agree?` — most common (40+ instances)
2. `'X.' Discuss.` — 25+ instances
3. `To what extent...?` — 30+ instances. Most common variant: `To what extent is/are X...?`
4. `How far do you agree...?` / `How far is this true...?` — 25+ instances
5. `Consider the view that X.` / `Consider the claim that X.` — 12+ instances
6. `'X.' Comment.` — 12+ instances
7. `'X.' What is your view?` / `What do you think?` — 12+ instances
8. `Is X...?` (direct yes/no with qualifier) — 15+ instances
9. `Should X...?` — 20+ instances. Always invites stand declaration.
10. `Assess the view/claim that X.` / `Examine the view that X.` — 8+ instances
11. `How important/effective is X...?` — 8+ instances

**Question architectures (lens through which content is approached):**

- **Bilateral evaluative** (~50%): "X vs not-X" debate — Should X be allowed? Is X true? Most questions.
- **Extent qualifier** (~25%): To what extent / How far — invites bands of agreement, not Yes/No
- **Quoted-claim challenge** (~15%): Quote-mark stem (often hyperbolic) followed by Discuss/Comment/Do you agree. Forces engagement with extreme position.
- **Comparative trade-off** (~5%): X vs Y (innovation vs status quo, self-reliance vs interdependence, popular vs effective)
- **"In your society" application** (~30% as suffix to other types): explicitly Singapore-focused

**Stand declaration patterns (from JJC + SAJC answer schemes):**
- Two-sided stand expected. Pure one-sided answers band-cap at ~16/30 Content.
- Best answers nuance the stand: "Largely agree, with the caveat that..."
- Mark scheme rewards: clarification of terms (what counts as X?) + concrete Singapore example + counterargument + rebuttal
- SAJC scheme structure pattern: "Analysis > Clarification of Terms > Stand / Points / Examples" — formula every JC uses internally

### AQ patterns

**Standard AQ phrasing structures (high frequency):**

1. **Single-passage "observations" format** (most common):
   - "[Author] makes observations about [topic]. How far would you agree with his/her observations, relating your arguments to your own experience and that of your society?"
   - Used by: AJC 2017, DHS 2017, JJC 2017, MJC 2017, NJC 2017, NYJC 2017, RVHS 2017, RVHS 2019, MI 2019

2. **Single-passage "How relevant/applicable" variant**:
   - "How relevant are his arguments to you and your society?" (RVHS 2017)
   - "How applicable do you find their views to yourself and your society?" (PJC 2017)
   - "Support your answer by referring to the ways in which you and your society regard X." (MI 2017)

3. **HCI specific format**: explicit instruction to "Refer to specific material from the passage as well as your own observations of your society"

4. **Dual-passage "with which of the two authors are you more in agreement"**:
   - IJC 2017, CJC 2017, CJC 2018, TJC 2018, VJC 2017, VJC 2018

5. **Quotation-anchored variant** (DHS 2019): pulls a direct phrase from passage (lines X-Y) and asks degree of agreement

**AQ mark allocations:** Typically 8-10 marks for content + share of 15 lang marks. Most papers: 8 marks content. RVHS, NJC, MI typically 10 marks.

**Common AQ instructions across all papers:**
- "your own society" or "your society" — appears in every AQ. Singapore application is mandatory.
- "relating your arguments to" — standard verb phrase
- Either Yes/No engagement OR three-point gradation (largely agree / partially / largely disagree)

### JC house style observations

**AJC**: Globally-focused stems (Q1 small countries, Q2 arts effect change). Heavier focus on "consider the view" / "evaluate the claim" stems. Uses 8-mark AQs.

**CJC**: Question stems use plain prose more than literary quotations. Dual-passage P2 papers in both 2017 and 2018 — house signature.

**DHS**: Uses "For Examiner's Use" boxes with explicit Content/30 + Language/20 breakdown on the cover (more form-style than other JCs). High-philosophical question stems (Q12 2017 "Humour is no laughing matter"). 2017 P2 used Lou Perez (cultural commentary), 2019 P2 used Bernie Greenwood (climate science-cultural). DHS attracts upper-range topical material.

**HCI**: Most literary/aphoristic question phrasings. 2017 Q12 is a poetic couplet. Strong use of quoted single-clause stems followed by "Would you agree" or "What do you think." 2017 P2 passage (Hans Affleck on lies) reflects HCI's preference for sharp argumentative passages with strong authorial voice.

**IJC**: Heavy use of "to what extent" and "how far" — invites bands of agreement. Dual-passage P2 with conformity theme — classic IJC topic preference.

**JJC**: Conservative-trad question structures. Mark scheme labelled "Standard Assessment Benchmarks" with clarification of terms first, then stand-and-points format. Olivia Laing passage (Cambridge-friendly broadsheet psychology) typical of JJC.

**MI**: Note "Preliminary Examination 2 / PU3" wording. 3-year institute. Otherwise question style mirrors JC paper structures exactly. Both years (2017, 2019) use Singapore-applied stems frequently. Passages chosen are slightly more accessible (stories, philanthropy) — accommodation for PU3 audience.

**MJC**: Uses descriptive quotations + Discuss/Comment. Question 2 2017 "Poor governance...main cause of poverty" — typical MJC style. Passage Eric Land on beauty — psychology + cultural angle.

**NJC**: Plain question stems without literary embellishment. Q9 2017 "Can ethics ever be irrelevant to science?" — typical NJC philosophical-direct style. Strong sport/economics combinations (Q1 2017).

**NYJC**: Uses formal "What is your view?" / "Discuss this view" — most directly Cambridge-imitative. Susan B. Kaiser on fashion (2017) reflects NYJC's preference for sociology/identity-focused passages.

**RVHS**: International Programme (RVIP) labelling. Heavy environment + politics. Nick Thorpe (materialism) in 2017, George Monbiot (loneliness) in 2019 — both Guardian-style cultural critics. Q1 2017 RVHS innovation vs status quo is RVHS signature ethics-progress framing.

### Comparison to 2014-2016 era (what shifted in mid-syllabus period)

**Note: 2014-2016 corpus not directly compared in this session — these observations are inferences from the 2017-2019 corpus relative to known earlier patterns from the broader GP knowledge base.**

What's NEW/INTENSIFIED in 2017-2019:

1. **Fake news / post-truth** becomes dominant media theme. Earlier (2014-2016): media bias / mass media restrictions. 2017+: "fake news" as named concept enters question stems (CJC 2018 Singer passage on fake news, DHS 2019 Q8 explicit "fake news"). 2019 sees direct AI questions (MI Q4, MI Q12).

2. **AI as standalone topic** emerges. 2017: AJC Q11 (animal testing in research), HCI Q10 (smart cities), NYJC Q11 (AI advancement), VJC Q6 (afraid of AI). 2018 VJC Q5 "AI will not improve our lives." 2019 MI Q4 "If a job can be replaced by machines." Pre-2017 AI questions were rarer.

3. **Climate change becomes a passage selection signal**. 2017 AJC and DHS 2019 both explicitly choose climate/environment as the P2 passage. The "wanting less is more" sub-theme (DHS 2019) is new.

4. **Loneliness / mental health / individualism crisis** as P2 passage theme. JJC 2017 (Laing) and RVHS 2019 (Monbiot) both target this. Earlier era: technology was framed as enabler, not isolator.

5. **Immigration/borders** intensifies (post-Brexit, post-Trump). CJC 2018 borders question, TJC 2018 immigration P2.

6. **Free speech vs limits debate** sharpens. CJC 2018 P2 (Grayling vs Singer) — explicit debate over fake news limits is a 2018 development.

7. **"In your society" stem ubiquity** — appears in essentially every P1 paper. Possibly a response to Cambridge's emphasis on Singapore application post-mid-2010s.

8. **Cultural pessimism passages dominate P2**. 2017-2019 P2 passages skew strongly toward modern-life-as-pathology (loneliness, lies, materialism, decline of cooking, noise). Earlier era had more balanced or analytical passages.

9. **Reduced sport/family/language questions**. 2017-2019 corpus shows these themes have ~1 question per paper, suggesting they're treated as fallback safety options rather than core focus.

10. **Religion as theme persists** but more political-philosophical (CJC 2017 Q2, DHS 2017 Q5, RVHS 2019 Q9) — framed as governance/secularism debate, not theological.

What's STABLE across both eras: arts as luxury/necessity debate, government surveillance, education's purpose, economic inequality, gender in workplace.
