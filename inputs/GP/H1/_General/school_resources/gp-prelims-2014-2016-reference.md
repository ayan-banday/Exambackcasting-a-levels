# GP Prelims 2014-2016 Reference

Catalogued knowledge base of all GP H1 8807 prelim papers (2014, 2015, 2016) processed from Singapore JC archives. Covers Paper 1 essay banks and Paper 2 comprehension passages, AQ topics, and JC house style observations.

Subject code: 8807 (H1 General Paper). Note: code changes to 8881 in current Cambridge specification but format is identical.

## 2014 · DHS · P2 · Insert

Author: Jim Nassium. Topic: place of sport in the modern world. 8 paragraphs. Passage tracks sport from Olympic motto Citius Altius Fortius, to Mesoamerican ritualistic ball games, British proletariat/bourgeoisie sport during Industrial Revolution, colonial homogenisation, downsides (cheating, citizenship-buying, eating disorders, halo effect), technological revolution moving sport indoors, media celebrity treatment (Bruce Lee vs Jack LaLanne).

AQ: "In this article, Jim Nassium describes some current issues around the subject of sport. How applicable do you find his observations to you and your own society?" [10 marks]

Comprehension Q count: 11 questions including a 120-word summary on paragraphs 4-5 (development and downsides of sport). 8 marks for summary, mark scheme uses point-counting (1-2 pts = 1m, scaling to 14 pts = 8m).

## 2014 · VJC · P2 · Insert

Author: Ilan Stavans. Topic: travel vs tourism. 9 paragraphs. Argues meaningful travel is a search for self-knowledge and meaning, vs tourism which is safe/predetermined/preprogrammed. References Bruce Chatwin nomadism, George Steiner, Bible expulsion, Gilgamesh, Icelandic sagas, Odyssey. Key distinction: travellers vs tourists.

AQ: "Ilan Stavans distinguishes between meaningful, fruitful travel and mere tourism. How far do you agree with his observations that people today are tourists, not travellers in the context of yourself and your society?" [10]

Comprehension: 11 questions. Summary on paragraphs 5, 8, 9 (meaningful travel). VJC mark scheme particularly extensive, with full bandings (Band 1: 8-10, Band 2: 4-7, Band 3: 1-3) and worked examples including Singapore-specific evidence (Chan Brothers, CTC, SA Tours, voluntourism, Couchsurfing).

VJC AQ mark scheme strict on: stand/balanced treatment, distinguishing tourists vs travellers without confusion, requirement to evaluate claims and apply to own society.

## Recurring essay question structures (Cambridge command patterns)

Three dominant question types observed across 2014-2016:

1. **Quotation + verdict prompt:** `'[claim].' To what extent do you agree?` / `Do you agree?` / `Comment.` / `Discuss.`
   Examples: DHS 2014 Q1, DHS 2016 Q1, MJC 2016 Q3. Roughly 40% of all questions.

2. **Direct evaluative question:** `Is [X] [Y]?` / `Should [X] [Y]?` / `How far should [X]?`
   Examples: ACJC 2016 Q1 "Is foreign intervention ever justified?". Roughly 35%.

3. **Society-anchored question:** Always includes "in your society" or "in your country" — forces Singapore application.
   Examples: NJC 2016 Q8 "Is it really possible for your society to achieve inclusiveness?". Roughly 25% of questions, but rising sharply.

Standard prompt frame: "X. To what extent do you agree?" is the most common single template (~25% of all 492 questions).

## AQ patterns

Common AQ structures observed:

1. **Apply observations to your society:** "How applicable do you find his observations to you and your own society?" — DHS standard formula 2014, 2015, 2016
2. **Agreement + relevance:** "How far would you agree with [author]'s observations? Relate your opinions to your society." — most other JCs
3. **Discuss relevance + recommendations:** HCI 2015 (Walker on cities) — slightly more nuanced
4. **Distinguish + apply distinction:** VJC 2014 (tourists vs travellers) — demands clear differentiation of concepts before application

All AQs worth 10 marks. All explicitly demand Singapore context — no AQ allows generic "global" response.

### AQ marking rubric (consistent across JCs)

Band 1 (8-10): systematic reference + balanced treatment + judgement with logical conclusions + apt personal/societal illustration + cogent organisation
Band 2 (4-7): adequate covers but not balanced + superficial evaluation + thin support + recognisable shape
Band 3 (1-3): incomplete/unbalanced + mere summary + thin support + haphazard organisation
0: nothing meets criteria

## Comparison to 2012-2013 era (what shifted)

Based on contextual reading, what changed between 2012-2013 and 2014-2016:

### Theme shifts
- **Technology** rises massively. Social media, smartphones, AI, robotics absent or thin in earlier years; dominant by 2016.
- **Post-truth media** themes emerge (Katharine Viner MJC 2016, Daily Mail David Cameron story; news-as-authority by Alain de Botton SAJC 2016) — directly post-Brexit/Trump era concerns.
- **Migration/globalisation** angle sharpens. 2014 RI Amy Chua piece still about American identity broadly; by 2016 questions specifically address Syrian/African migration to Europe.
- **Singapore-anchored questions** rise from optional ~15% to ~20%+ of P1 banks — exam encouraging applied analysis.
- **AI / Robotics** enters as serious topic only from 2015 onward (HCI 2015 Q3; ACJC 2016 P2; IJC 2016 Q1; PJC 2016 Q1).

### Stylistic shifts
- **Two-passage P2** becomes more common. SAJC 2015 (shame); CJC, IJC, NJC, TJC 2016 all use two-passage format. Pre-2014 was almost universally single-passage.
- **Modern academic sources** (Pinker, Bradatan, Bloom, Lichtenberg) appear more frequently. Earlier years drew more from general broadsheet journalism.
- **AQ rubric standardisation** — three-band system (1-3, 4-7, 8-10) appears identically across JCs by 2015, suggesting Cambridge moderation.

### What stays constant
- 12-question P1 essay format
- 500-800 word limit
- 1h30m timing
- Content/30 + Language/20 = 50 split
- AQ as 10 marks within Paper 2
- Summary question worth 8 marks
- "Use your own words as far as possible" rubric instruction
