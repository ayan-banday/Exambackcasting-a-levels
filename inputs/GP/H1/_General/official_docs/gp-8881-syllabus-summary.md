# General Paper — Syllabus 8881 (H1)

**Level:** H1 (compulsory for all Singapore JC students)
**Year of exam:** 2025

---

## Assessment Objectives

- **AO1 — Critical and Inventive Thinking:** Analyse arguments, evaluate issues of local/regional/global significance, evaluate and synthesise across texts, formulate cogent arguments, observe trends and connections.
- **AO2 — Communication:** Proficient English appropriate to purpose/context/audience. Coherent and succinct ideas. Accepted conventions of spelling/punctuation/grammar. Range of vocabulary.

| Paper | Type | Duration | Marks | Weighting |

## Paper 1 — Essay

- 8 questions (choose 1).
- 500–800 words.
- Questions examine issues of local, regional, and global significance.
- Mark split: **30 content + 20 language.**

### Content marking (30 marks)

| Band | Marks | Descriptor |
|---|---|---|
| A | 25–30 | Argument is coherent, cogent, well-developed with specific evidence. Clear line of reasoning. Nuanced treatment of complexity. |
| B | 19–24 | Argument is sound, mostly developed, relevant evidence. Some complexity handled. |
| C | 13–18 | Argument exists but underdeveloped or partly irrelevant. Limited evidence. |
| D | 7–12 | Argument is weak, largely descriptive, or tangential. |
| E | 1–6 | Minimal relevant content. |

### Language marking (20 marks)

| Band | Marks | Descriptor |
|---|---|---|
| A | 17–20 | Fluent, varied, precise. Grammar and syntax consistently accurate. Wide vocabulary. |
| B | 13–16 | Mostly accurate. Some variety. Vocabulary adequate. |
| C | 9–12 | Some errors but meaning clear. Limited range. |
| D | 5–8 | Frequent errors, some meaning unclear. Basic vocabulary. |
| E | 1–4 | Very frequent errors, meaning obscured. |

### Command words for P1

- **Discuss** — Present multiple perspectives, weigh them, reach a judgment. Always take a clear position.
- **To what extent** — Agree partially, qualify the claim, show nuance. Never "fully agree" without reservation.
- **How far** — Same as "to what extent."
- **Do you agree** — State your position clearly in the thesis. Defend it with counter-argument acknowledgment.

### Thematic areas (not prescribed, recurring)

1. Technology and society (AI, social media, privacy)
2. Environment and sustainability (climate change, green energy, food security)
3. Arts, culture, and values
4. Politics, governance, and democracy
5. Economics, inequality, and globalisation
6. Science, medicine, and ethics
7. Education and youth
8. Singapore-specific issues (multiculturalism, meritocracy, NS, family)

## Paper 2 — Comprehension

- 3 passages (different perspectives on one theme).
- Questions test: interpretation, inference, vocabulary in context, summary, cross-passage comparison.
- Mark split: **35 content + 15 language.**

### Question types

| Type | Marks | What it demands |
|---|---|---|
| Vocabulary in context | 1–2 per word | Explain the word AS USED in the passage. Not dictionary definition. |
| Inference | 2–4 | "What does the writer imply/suggest?" Must go beyond the surface of the text. |
| Explanation/analysis | 3–6 | "Explain how/why..." with reference to specific lines. |
| Summary | 8–10 | Summarise one section in own words. No lifting. |
| Comparison | 5–8 | Compare how two passages treat the same idea. |
| Evaluation | 4–6 | "How effective is the writer's argument?" Must reference specific techniques. |

### Language marking (Paper 2)

15 marks for language quality across all written responses. Consistent errors penalized cumulatively.

## Exam Technique Notes

### P1 Essay structure (Cambridge-preferred)

1. **Introduction (≈80 words):** Define key terms if contentious. State your position (thesis). Preview two or three main arguments.
2. **Body paragraphs (3–4 paragraphs, ≈120–160 words each):** One argument per paragraph. PEEL: Point → Evidence/Example → Explain → Link back to question.
3. **Counter-argument (1 paragraph):** Acknowledge the opposing view, then rebut or qualify it.
4. **Conclusion (≈60–80 words):** Restate thesis with nuance. Do not introduce new arguments.

### Common P1 mistakes to avoid

- Starting every paragraph with "Furthermore" or "In addition" — vary transitions.
- Using examples without explaining their relevance to the argument.
- Arguing "both sides" without committing to a position.
- Exceeding 800 words (language mark penalized for lack of concision).

### P2 comprehension technique

- For vocabulary questions: state what the word means in context, not its dictionary definition. Show you understand connotation.
- For summary: use your own words throughout. Never copy more than 3 consecutive words from the passage.
- For inference: always explain the reasoning chain. "This suggests X because..." not just "X."
- For comparison: use a parallel structure. "While Writer A argues X, Writer B contends Y, demonstrating..."
