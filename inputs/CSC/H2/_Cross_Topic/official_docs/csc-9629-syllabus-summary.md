# China Studies in Chinese — Syllabus 9629 (H2)

**Level:** H2
**Medium:** Chinese (中文)
**Year of exam:** 2025
**Note:** From 2025, CSC uses hybrid e-Written examination — physical question paper, digital submission via SEAB Digital Answer Booklet (DAB).

---

## Assessment Objectives

- **AO1 — Knowledge and Understanding:** Understand and apply knowledge on contemporary China's economy, politics, society, and international relations.
- **AO2 — Analysis and Evaluation:** Examine issues and trends, analyse/evaluate/synthesise perspectives, construct well-reasoned arguments, draw valid inferences and conclusions.
- **AO3 — Interpretation and Evaluation of Sources:** Interpret/analyse/evaluate/synthesise sources from different perspectives; infer outcomes; propose solutions.

---

## Paper Structure

| Paper | Type | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Case Study (案例分析) | 2 hr 45 min | 60 | 40% |
| Paper 2 | Essay / Structured Questions (问答题) | 2 hr 30 min | 90 | 60% |

## Paper 1 — Case Study (案例分析)

Two compulsory case studies.

### Group A (35 marks)
- **Format:** 4 sub-questions using 5 provided sources.
- **Focus:** AO1 + AO2 + AO3. Interpret, analyse, and evaluate sources.
- Source types: text passages, maps, photographs, graphs, data tables, cartoons, posters.
- Sub-questions range from 4–12 marks each.

### Group B (25 marks)
- **Format:** 1 extended question using 4 sources.
- **Focus:** AO1 + AO2. Analyse, evaluate, and propose solutions.
- Requires sustained argument drawing on sources and own knowledge.

### Source skill types (AO3)

| Skill | What to do |
|---|---|
| Comprehension | Identify the main message/argument of a source |
| Inference | Read beyond the surface — what does it imply? |
| Reliability/provenance | Who produced this? What is their purpose/bias? |
| Cross-referencing | Does Source X support or challenge Source Y? |
| Synthesis | Combine sources + own knowledge to build an argument |
| Utility | How useful is this source for understanding the issue? |

### P1 sub-question command words

| Command word | What it demands |
|---|---|
| 根据来源... 解释 | Use the source(s) directly. Quote or paraphrase. |
| 分析 | Break down the issue into components, show relationships |
| 评估 | Weigh evidence on both sides, reach a judgment |
| 提出解决方案 | Propose practical, realistic solutions with justification |
| 比较来源X和Y | Identify similarities and differences in message/tone/purpose |

## Paper 2 — Essays (问答题)

### Structure
- **Group A:** 1 compulsory question (extended essay, ~30 marks)
- **Group B:** Choose 2 out of 3 questions (~30 marks each)
- Total: 3 essays.
- All in continuous prose. No bullet points in final answers.

### Essay marking (levels of response)

| Level | Marks | Descriptor |
|---|---|---|
| L4 | 25–30 | Sustained, well-developed argument. Full use of relevant knowledge. Nuanced analysis. Clear evaluative judgment throughout. |
| L3 | 17–24 | Developed argument with mostly relevant knowledge. Some analysis and evaluation. |
| L2 | 9–16 | Describes more than analyses. Some relevant knowledge. Limited evaluation. |
| L1 | 1–8 | Largely descriptive, limited knowledge, little or no argument structure. |

## The 4 Core Topic Areas

### Topic 1: China's Economic Development and Its Sustainability (经济)

**Key themes:**
- Transition from centrally planned to socialist market economy (1978 reforms)
- Growth drivers: SEZs, FDI, export-led growth, WTO accession (2001)
- Challenges: export-dependence, SOE inefficiency, NPL (non-performing loans), regional disparities (coastal vs inland), energy security, environmental degradation
- Strategies for sustainable growth: domestic consumption (2008 GFC response), SOE reform, technology/services sectors, "dual circulation," green development (carbon neutrality 2060)

**Key concepts:** GDP growth rates, Gini coefficient, household registration (hukou) system, made-in-China 2025, Belt and Road Initiative (BRI)

### Topic 2: Governing China and Its Challenges (政治)

**Key themes:**
- Party-state system: CCP's monopoly on political power
- Ideological and performance-based legitimacy
- Central-local government relations and fiscal federalism
- Challenges: corruption (anti-corruption campaign under Xi), rule of law vs rule by law, bureaucratic capacity, online civic activism, ethnic minority issues (Xinjiang, Tibet)
- Strategies: National Supervision Commission, judicial reform, government-sponsored civic participation, social credit system

**Key concepts:** Democratic centralism, collective leadership, Xi Jinping Thought, National People's Congress, civil society

### Topic 3: Chinese Society and Its Transformation (社会)

**Key themes:**
- Demographic: declining birth rate, ageing population, one-child policy legacy, two-child/three-child policy
- Urbanisation: rural-to-urban migration, megacity growth, urban-rural divide
- Social stratification: rising middle class, income inequality, hukou barriers to mobility
- Changing family structures: declining multigenerational households, rising divorce rates, declining marriage rates
- Civil society: growth of NGOs, social movements, women's rights activism (MeToo in China)
- Values: religious revival (Christianity, Buddhism, traditional beliefs), impact of digital media on traditional values (Confucian family values vs individualism)

**Key concepts:** Hukou reform, silver economy, 内卷 (involution), 躺平 (lying flat), 公民社会 (civil society)

### Topic 4: China's Foreign Policy and Its Implications (外交)

**Key themes:**
- China's rise and its impact on regional/global order
- Foreign policy goals: sovereignty, economic development, great power status
- Key relationships: US-China (trade war, tech competition), ASEAN, Belt and Road Initiative countries, Russia
- South China Sea disputes
- Taiwan issue
- Singapore-China bilateral relations: trade, investment, governance exchanges (Suzhou Industrial Park)

**Key concepts:** 韬光养晦 (hide brightness, nourish obscurity — Deng's foreign policy), "peaceful rise," assertive diplomacy under Xi, multilateralism, 一带一路 (BRI)

## Exam Technique Notes

### Case Study (P1) approach

**For source-comprehension questions (4–6 marks):**
1. Identify the main message of the source.
2. Quote or paraphrase specific evidence.
3. Explain what it shows about the issue.
4. If reliability asked: address provenance (who, when, purpose), potential bias, and limitations.

**For extended case study questions (12–15 marks):**
1. Set up a clear argument in the first sentence.
2. Use 2–3 sources as evidence. Quote briefly, then interpret.
3. Add own knowledge to supplement the sources.
4. Address counter-evidence if present.
5. Reach a conclusion that directly answers the question.

### Essay (P2) approach

**Essay structure:**
1. **Introduction:** Define key terms. State clear argument (stance + two or three main points). Do not write a long preamble.
2. **Body (3–4 paragraphs):** Each paragraph = one argument. Structure: Claim → Evidence (specific fact/data/example) → Analysis (why this supports the claim) → Link to question.
3. **Counter-argument:** One paragraph acknowledging the opposing view, then refuting or qualifying it with evidence.
4. **Conclusion:** Restate main argument with a clear final judgment. No new evidence.

**Common CSC essay mistakes:**
- Writing descriptive lists instead of an argument ("首先... 其次... 再次..." without analysis).
- Using vague examples ("经济发展带来了很多问题") — always be specific (name a policy, year, data point).
- Forgetting to link each point back to the question.
- Writing too much on one topic and ignoring others.
