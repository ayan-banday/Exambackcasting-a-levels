# H2 Biology Prelims 2017-2019 — Examiner Patterns Reference

**Scope:** 301 source files across 2017 (139 from 17 JCs), 2018 (161 from 22 JCs), 2019 (1 consolidated mega-file containing 15 JCs).
**Method:** Deep-read 8 papers from RI, HCI, NYJC/NJC, VJC across 2017+2018, plus 2019 consolidated file with full mark schemes. Skim-scanned 18 additional JC P3 papers for essay-topic coverage and keyword distribution across all extracted text.
**Subject code:** 9744 (current H2 syllabus) throughout all three years. No older 9648 traces.

---

## Paper Structure (Confirmed Constant 2017-2019)

| Paper | Format | Duration | Marks |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | 30 MCQs (4 options A-D, no negative marking) | 1 hour | 30 |
| P2 | 7-10 structured questions, totally compulsory | 2 hours | 100 |
| P3 | Section A (2-3 long structured) + Section B (1 of 2 essays, 25 marks each) | 2 hours | 75 |
| P4 | 3 practical questions (manipulation, observation, planning) | 2h 30min | 55 |

**Quirks:**
- HCI organizes P2/P3 into "booklets" (5-6 booklets per paper). RI uses standard continuous format.
- RI P3 Section B requires answers on separate paper, handed in separately. Most JCs use lined paper at back of QP. 2018 HCI integrated lined paper into QP.
- 2018 HCI introduced 14/11 mark split for essays (vs standard 13/12) — outlier.
- P4 question 3 typically a planning question on foolscap (RI 2017) or separate booklet.

## Topic Distribution Per Paper

### P1 MCQ — Topics Hit (across 4 JCs deep, 2017-2019)
1. **Molecules** — protein structure, enzyme mechanism (allosteric, competitive, induced fit), membrane lipid composition
2. **Cell biology** — organelle identification from EMs, endosymbiosis, stem cell types
3. **Genetics core** — Meselson-Stahl, semi-conservative replication, transcription/translation steps, codon redundancy
4. **Gene regulation** — lac/trp operon, eukaryotic TF function, RNA splicing, eIF2 phosphorylation
5. **Viruses** — influenza vs HIV vs lambda lifecycles, antigenic drift/shift, receptors (haemagglutinin/sialic acid)
6. **Cell signalling** — Ras pathway, RTK cascade, MAP kinase
7. **Mitosis/meiosis** — non-disjunction, ploidy after division stages, polyploidy
8. **Cancer** — BRCA1/2, p53, RAD51, telomerase/Hayflick

### P4 Practical — Recurring Skills

**Standard P4 structure:** 3 questions, 55 marks total.
- Q1 (~22 marks): Manipulation + observation. Common contexts: enzyme activity (catalase from watermelon — HCI 2018; ascorbic acid/Visking tubing — RI 2017).
- Q2 (~14 marks): Microscopy + drawing + measurement. Calculate actual size from magnification. Identify named structures.
- Q3 (~19 marks): Planning question, answered on foolscap/separate paper. Design an experiment with hypothesis, IV/DV/CVs, method, expected results.

**Recurring techniques:**
- Iodine end-point titration (RI 2017 ascorbic acid)
- Alginate bead immobilisation (HCI 2018 catalase)
- Plotting graph from collected data + identify trend
- Calculating rate from gradient
- Drawing apparatus diagram with labels
- Suggesting variables to control + experimental refinements
- Reliability/validity discussion
- Justifying choice of equipment or method

## MCQ Patterns (P1) — Calibration Rules

**Format conventions:**
- 30 questions, 1 hour (avg 2 min per Q)
- 4 options A-D
- No negative marking
- Multi-statement stems ("I, II, III, IV") common: ~30% of paper
- Diagram/graph/EM stems: ~50% of paper
- Pure recall: ~20%; application/data interpretation: ~80%

**Bloom's distribution in MCQ:**
- L1 Remember: ~10% (definitions, single-fact identification)
- L2 Understand: ~25% (explain why mechanism, match structure to function)
- L3 Apply: ~40% (predict outcome from given conditions, calculate ploidy)
- L4 Analyze: ~25% (extract from graph/data, distinguish true from false in list of statements)

**Stem styles to drill:**
- "Which of the following is NOT a likely outcome..."
- "Which statements correctly describe..."
- "How many of the deductions are correct..."
- "Which combination of [X and Y] would result in..."

## Structured Q Patterns (P2) — Calibration Rules

**Mark allocation conventions:**
- Questions range 7-15 marks (mode = 10-12)
- 7-10 questions typically
- Define questions: 1-2 marks
- Describe + explain: 2-5 marks
- Suggest + explain: 2-4 marks (data interpretation)
- Compare/contrast: 2-3 marks
- Outline a process: 3-5 marks

**Command word frequency (deep-read sample):**
1. Explain (most common): ~30%
2. Describe: ~20%
3. Suggest: ~15%
4. State/Name: ~12%
5. With reference to Fig/Table: ~10% (almost every Q has visual stimulus)
6. Compare/distinguish: ~8%
7. Outline: ~5%

**P2 structure conventions:**
- Lined answer spaces in QP (no separate answer booklet typically)
- HCI organizes into 5-6 "booklets" — each booklet is one or two questions
- ~80% of Qs have at least one figure/table reference
- Hot context contexts: disease/clinical scenarios (cancer, HIV, dengue), experimental data tables, comparison of conditions/treatments

## Essay Patterns (P3 Section B) — Calibration Rules

**Format:**
- Choose 1 of 2 essays
- 25 marks total, split 13/12 (HCI 2018 exception: 14/11)
- Each essay has parts (a) and (b) — must answer both
- Continuous prose required, diagrams encouraged
- Answer on lined paper (lined paper provided with QP)

**Essay archetype:**
- Part (a) = often "Describe/Outline/Discuss the role of X" with named examples
- Part (b) = often a "Discuss/Compare" question linking the concept to a new scenario, or applying it (e.g. climate change, cancer therapy, evolution)

**High-yield framings recurring across years:**
- "Discuss the importance of X in living organisms" with named examples → ATP, hydrogen bonds, complementarity, shape, proteins, membranes
- "Describe the process of X and discuss its significance" → variation, mutation, chemiosmosis, antibody diversity, gene regulation
- "Compare and contrast X and Y" → PCR vs Southern, oxidative vs photophosphorylation, lambda vs influenza, B vs T cells

**Mark scheme structure (from 2019 file with answers):**
- Banded into sub-topics (e.g. "Structure & Properties of ATP [max 4]", "Energy currency [max 5]")
- Strict max marks per sub-topic — must spread breadth
- Named examples required (1 mark per valid example)
- "Reject" notes common — must use correct technical terms
- Diagrams give bonus marks if clearly labelled

## Calibration Rules (for generating practice papers)

1. **P1:** 30 MCQs in 1 hour. ~60% have a figure/graph/EM stem. ~30% are multi-statement (I-IV) questions. Include 2-3 "which is NOT" stems. Cover: 4 molecules, 6 genetics/gene reg, 4 cells/division, 3 viruses/immunity, 3 signalling, 3 respiration/photosynth, 3 evolution, 4 mixed application.
2. **P2:** 7-10 questions, 100 marks, 2 hours. EVERY question has a figure or table. Mark allocations cluster at 2-3 (define/state) and 3-5 (explain/describe). Include at least one data-table experimental scenario (e.g. deletion construct or kinetics curve). Hot contexts: enzyme regulation, cancer mechanism, viral lifecycle.
3. **P3 Section A:** 2-3 long structured (15-27 marks each). Use a sustained context across whole question (e.g. Li-Fraumeni cancer pedigree → PCR → multi-step development). Sub-parts must build progressively.
4. **P3 Section B:** 2 essays offered, candidate chooses 1. Each 25 marks, split 13/12. Part (a) describes/outlines a process; part (b) discusses/applies/compares. Mark scheme must band into 3-4 sub-topics with max marks. Demand named examples.
5. **P4:** 3 questions, 55 marks, 2h30. Q1 = enzyme/biological molecule with manipulation. Q2 = microscopy + drawing + calculation. Q3 = planning + hypothesis + variables.
6. **Command words to drill:** Explain (mechanism with cause-effect), Describe (observable features), Discuss (balanced argument with examples), Suggest (apply knowledge to novel scenario), Compare and contrast (similarities + differences in parallel structure), Outline (main steps, no detail).
7. **Bloom's mix:** L3-L5 should be ~70% of marks. L1 only 10% max. L6 (create/design) appears in P4 planning Q.
