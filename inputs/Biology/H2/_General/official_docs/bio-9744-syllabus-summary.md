## Paper 1 — MCQ (30 questions)

- 30 compulsory MCQ, 4 options each.
- Tests breadth across all topics. No negative marking.
- Strategy: eliminate clearly wrong options first. Flag and return to uncertain ones.

---

## Paper 2 — Structured Questions

- Variable number of questions, all compulsory.
- Includes data-based / comprehension questions requiring interpretation of graphs, tables, experimental results.
- Questions integrate across topic areas (e.g., enzyme + gene expression together).
- Short to medium written responses (1–6 marks per sub-question).

---

## Paper 3 — Long Structured + Free Response

### Section A (50 marks)
- 2+ compulsory long structured questions.
- Often includes stimulus material from scientific literature (novel context — apply existing knowledge to unfamiliar scenario).
- Sub-questions: define terms, analyse data, justify decisions, calculate, draw conclusions.

### Section B (25 marks)
- 2 free-response questions — choose 1.
- Extended prose. Scientific argumentation and written communication quality assessed.
- Must construct a sustained argument (not bullet points).

---

## Paper 4 — Practical (55 marks)

Skill areas assessed:
- **P (Planning):** Design investigations, identify variables (IV, DV, control), select techniques.
- **MMO (Manipulation, Measurement, Observation):** Safe apparatus use, accurate recording, units.
- **PDO (Presentation of Data):** Tables (column headers with units), graphs (axes labeled, appropriate scale, best-fit line).
- **ACE (Analysis, Conclusions, Evaluation):** Interpret results, calculate rates/means, draw conclusions, evaluate method, suggest improvements.

## Content Outline

### Core Idea 1: The Cell and Biomolecules of Life

#### Cell Structure
- **Ultrastructure:** ER (rough/smooth), Golgi apparatus, mitochondria, ribosomes (70S/80S), lysosomes, chloroplasts, centrioles, nucleus (nuclear envelope, pores, nucleolus)
- **Prokaryotic vs eukaryotic:** no membrane-bound organelles, 70S ribosomes, no nucleus, circular DNA, plasmids, cell wall (peptidoglycan)
- **Virus:** capsid, nucleic acid (DNA or RNA), envelope (some), no cellular machinery

#### Biomolecules
- **Carbohydrates:** monosaccharides (glucose α/β, fructose), disaccharides (maltose, sucrose, lactose), polysaccharides (starch, glycogen, cellulose — structure and function)
- **Lipids:** triglycerides (ester bonds, saturated/unsaturated), phospholipids (hydrophilic head, hydrophobic tail — membrane structure), steroids (cholesterol)
- **Proteins:** amino acid structure (R groups), peptide bonds, primary/secondary (α-helix, β-sheet)/tertiary/quaternary structure, globular vs fibrous
- **Nucleic acids:** nucleotide structure (phosphate, sugar, base), DNA double helix (anti-parallel, complementary base pairing, H-bonds), RNA types (mRNA, tRNA, rRNA)

#### Membrane Structure
- **Fluid mosaic model:** phospholipid bilayer, integral and peripheral proteins, cholesterol, glycoproteins, glycolipids
- **Functions:** selective permeability, cell signalling, cell recognition
- **Transport:** diffusion, osmosis (water potential ψ = ψs + ψp), facilitated diffusion, active transport, endocytosis/exocytosis

#### Enzymes
- Active site, lock-and-key vs induced fit
- Enzyme kinetics: Vmax, Km, substrate concentration, temperature, pH effects
- **Inhibition:** competitive (increases Km, same Vmax), non-competitive (decreases Vmax, same Km), irreversible
- Cofactors (inorganic) and coenzymes (organic — NAD, FAD, CoA)

#### Stem Cells
- **Types:** totipotent (zygote), pluripotent (embryonic), multipotent (adult), unipotent
- Differentiation: selective gene expression
- Applications: regenerative medicine, disease modelling
- Ethical issues: embryo destruction, source of embryonic stem cells

### Core Idea 2: Genetics and Inheritance

#### DNA Replication
- **Semi-conservative:** each strand as template
- **Enzymes:** helicase (unwind), primase (RNA primer), DNA polymerase III (adds 5'→3', requires primer), DNA polymerase I (removes primer), DNA ligase (seals Okazaki fragments)
- Leading strand (continuous) vs lagging strand (discontinuous/Okazaki fragments)

#### Transcription and Translation
- **Transcription:** DNA → mRNA (in nucleus for eukaryotes)
  - RNA polymerase binds promoter, reads 3'→5' template strand, synthesises 5'→3' mRNA
  - Pre-mRNA processing: 5' cap, poly-A tail, splicing (introns removed, exons joined)
- **Translation:** mRNA → protein (at ribosomes)
  - Codons (mRNA, 5'→3') → anticodons (tRNA, 3'→5') → amino acid sequence
  - Initiation: AUG start codon; elongation: peptide bond formation; termination: UAA, UAG, UGA
  - Post-translational modification (folding, cleavage, glycosylation, phosphorylation)

#### Gene Regulation

**Prokaryotes:**
- **Lac operon** (inducible): structural genes (lacZ, lacY, lacA), operator, promoter, regulatory gene (lacI → repressor)
  - No lactose: repressor binds operator → genes OFF
  - Lactose present: allolactose binds repressor → operator free → genes ON
  - Glucose present + lactose absent: CAP-cAMP binds → enhanced transcription
- **Trp operon** (repressible): tryptophan (co-repressor) binds repressor → operator blocked → genes OFF

**Eukaryotes:**
- Chromatin remodelling: histone acetylation (loosens chromatin → transcription ON), histone deacetylation (tightens → OFF)
- DNA methylation: methylation of cytosine → gene silencing (typically)
- Transcription factors: bind enhancers/silencers (may be far from gene)
- Post-transcriptional: alternative splicing, RNA stability, miRNA silencing
- Post-translational: protein folding, degradation

#### Non-Coding DNA
- Introns (removed during splicing), promoters, enhancers, silencers, centromeres (chromosome segregation), telomeres (protect chromosome ends)

#### Mutations
- **Gene mutations:** substitution (silent, missense, nonsense), insertion/deletion (frameshift)
- **Chromosome mutations:** deletion, duplication, inversion, translocation; non-disjunction (aneuploidy — e.g., trisomy 21)
- Causes: radiation, chemical mutagens, errors in DNA replication

#### Mendelian Genetics
- Monohybrid: dominant/recessive, 3:1 ratio in F2
- Dihybrid: independent assortment, 9:3:3:1 ratio in F2
- Codominance, incomplete dominance
- **Sex-linked inheritance:** X-linked recessive (e.g., haemophilia, colour-blindness) — female carrier, affected males
- **Gene interactions (epistasis):** one gene masks another; modified ratios (12:3:1, 9:3:4, 9:7 etc.)

#### Cancer Biology
- Proto-oncogenes → oncogenes (gain-of-function mutations → uncontrolled growth)
- Tumour suppressor genes (e.g., p53, Rb) — loss-of-function mutations → cell cycle not arrested
- Multi-hit hypothesis: multiple mutations needed for malignancy

### Core Idea 3: Energy and Equilibrium

#### Photosynthesis

**Light-dependent reactions (thylakoid membranes):**
- Photosystems II and I absorb light → excite electrons
- PSII: photolysis of water (2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ + O₂), electrons to ETC
- ETC: electron transport along carriers → pumps H⁺ across thylakoid membrane → H⁺ gradient
- ATP synthesis via photophosphorylation (chemiosmosis through ATP synthase)
- PSI: re-energised electrons reduce NADP⁺ → NADPH

**Calvin Cycle (stroma):**
- CO₂ fixation: CO₂ + RuBP (5C) → 2 × GP (3C) [enzyme: RuBisCO]
- Reduction: GP → G3P using ATP and NADPH
- Regeneration: G3P → RuBP using ATP
- Net output: 1 G3P per 3 CO₂ (net glucose requires 6 turns)

#### Respiration

**Aerobic:**
- Glycolysis (cytoplasm): glucose (6C) → 2 pyruvate (3C); net 2 ATP, 2 NADH
- Link reaction (mitochondrial matrix): pyruvate → acetyl-CoA (2C) + CO₂; NADH produced
- Krebs cycle (matrix): acetyl-CoA + oxaloacetate → 2CO₂; per turn: 3NADH, 1FADH₂, 1ATP
- Oxidative phosphorylation (inner membrane): NADH, FADH₂ donate electrons to ETC → H⁺ pumped → ATP synthase → chemiosmosis → 32–34 ATP
- O₂ is final electron acceptor → H₂O

**Anaerobic:**
- Yeast: pyruvate → ethanol + CO₂ (regenerates NAD⁺)
- Mammals: pyruvate → lactate (regenerates NAD⁺)
- Only 2 ATP net (glycolysis only)

#### Cell Signalling
- Signal molecule binds receptor (surface or intracellular)
- **G-protein coupled receptors:** activate adenylyl cyclase → cAMP → PKA cascade
- **Receptor tyrosine kinases:** autophosphorylation → signal cascade (MAP kinase pathway)
- Amplification: one signal → many second messengers → many effectors

### Core Idea 4: Biological Evolution

- Species concept: reproductive isolation
- **Natural selection:** variation → differential survival/reproduction → heritable traits increase in frequency
- **Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium:** p² + 2pq + q² = 1; conditions: large population, random mating, no mutation, no migration, no selection
- **Speciation:** allopatric (geographic isolation → genetic divergence → reproductive isolation) vs sympatric (e.g., polyploidy in plants)
- Environmental influence on phenotype (norm of reaction)

### Extension Topic A: Infectious Diseases

- **Innate immunity:** physical barriers, phagocytosis (neutrophils, macrophages), complement, inflammatory response
- **Adaptive immunity:**
  - B cells → antibody-mediated (humoral)
  - T cells → cell-mediated; cytotoxic T cells kill infected cells; helper T cells activate B cells
  - Memory cells → faster secondary response
- **Antibody structure:** 4 polypeptides (2 heavy + 2 light), variable region (antigen-binding), constant region
- Vaccination: primary exposure to antigen → memory cells → rapid secondary response on re-exposure
- Antibiotic resistance: natural selection for resistant strains, horizontal gene transfer

### Extension Topic B: Climate Change Impact

- Greenhouse gases (CO₂, CH₄, N₂O) → enhanced greenhouse effect → global temperature rise
- Effects on biodiversity: habitat loss, range shifts, phenological mismatch
- Insects (vectors): Aedes aegypti range expansion → dengue, Zika spread to new regions
- Plants: altered photosynthesis rates, shifted growing seasons, drought stress

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| State | Give a brief, factual answer. No explanation needed. |
| Define | Give the precise meaning. |
| Describe | Give the observable features. What it is, what happens. |

## Exam Technique Notes

### P1 MCQ strategy
- Read all 4 options before choosing.
- Eliminate impossible options first.
- For graph/data questions: identify the trend, then match to the biological explanation.

### P2/P3 structured questions
- 1-mark answers: one correct fact or mechanism.
- 2-mark "explain" questions: must include both cause and effect. "Because X occurs, Y happens."
- Data questions: always reference specific values from the data. "At pH 7, the enzyme activity is 80 units, compared to 20 units at pH 4."
- "Suggest" questions: no single correct answer — credit any reasonable scientific response consistent with the data.

### P3 free-response essays
- Plan before writing: mind map or outline the key points.
- Use scientific terminology correctly throughout.
- Structure: introduction (define key terms, state what the essay will cover) → body (one concept per paragraph with evidence) → conclusion (synthesis statement).
- Do not use bullet points in the final answer.
