What structural features stabilises the DNA double-helix?
a. Extensive hydrogen bonds between base pairs. (Note: each
hydrogen bond is, by itself, weak)
b. Hydrophobic interactions (or ‘stacking forces’) between the stacked
base pairs.
c. Exposure to outside influences of only the sugar-phosphate
backbone.
d. Nitrogenous bases being safely tucked inside the double-helix.
e. Eukaryotes only: DNA double-helix being tightly wound around histones to form a
repeating array of nucleosomes (Fig. 20). The nucleosomes are eventually folded into
higher order structures such as the chromosome, in which the DNA is prevented from
thermal and physical damage.
