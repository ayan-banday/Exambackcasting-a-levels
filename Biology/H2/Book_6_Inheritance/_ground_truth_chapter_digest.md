**2. Book 6 is procedural as much as declarative, and no earlier Book was.** Books 1 to 5 are almost entirely "know this, explain that". Book 6 contains methods: things you execute, in a fixed order, with a fixed layout, to arrive at a numerical or ratio answer. Three of them dominate:

- the **genetic diagram**, a seven-step procedure with a mandated layout,
- **ratio prediction and the reverse inference** from an observed ratio back to a mode of inheritance,
- the **chi-squared test**, a five-step procedure with a mandated conclusion sentence.

Every node in the maps below is tagged **`[METHOD]`** or **`[CONCEPT]`**. A `[METHOD]` node is a procedure with fixed steps and a standard layout, and it is learnt by doing it until the layout is automatic. A `[CONCEPT]` node is a claim you have to be able to state and justify, and it is learnt by explaining it. Mixing the two study modes is the commonest way to waste time on this Book: reading about how to do a genetic diagram produces nothing, and drilling twenty crosses does not teach you what epistasis means.

**3. The layout of a genetic diagram is itself mark-bearing.** This is the single most important structural fact in the Book and it is set out in full in the cross-chapter findings at the end. Marks in this Book are awarded line by line against a fixed set of labelled rows, not for arriving at the right ratio.

### 5. Errors, contradictions and ambiguities

**5.1 The lethal-gene row contradicts itself and contradicts chapter 6.1.** The sheet gives, for Yy × Yy under lethal genes, a **genotypic ratio of 1:2:1** and a **phenotypic ratio of 2:1**. Both cannot describe the same set of surviving offspring. If one quarter of the zygotes (the YY class) die, then among the offspring you can actually count the genotypic ratio is 2 Yy : 1 yy, matching the 2:1 phenotypic ratio. The 1:2:1 is the ratio at fertilisation, before the lethal class is lost. Chapter 6.1 p.21 gives the same cross as phenotypic **2:1** with the note "(¼ offspring dies)" and does not offer a genotypic ratio at all, which is the correct treatment. `[SCHOOL]` on both statements, `[INFERRED]` on the resolution.

**5.2 "AA × aa (test cross)" is a misuse of the term.** The sheet lists AA × aa under "Types of crosses" with the label test cross. A test cross is by definition a cross of an individual of **unknown** genotype showing the dominant phenotype against a homozygous recessive, and chapter 6.1 p.11 says exactly that. If you already know the parent is AA there is nothing to test. The row is describing the outcome you would see **if** the tested parent turned out to be homozygous, which is how 6.1 Fig. 4(a) presents it. Read as printed, the label is wrong.

**5.3 The linkage genotypic ratios use a notation the sheet never defines.** The linkage column writes genotypes as `LG` over `lg`, a two-line fraction meaning the two alleles carried on one chromosome above the two carried on its homologue. That notation is load-bearing (see cross-chapter finding 3) and the sheet uses it without ever saying what the horizontal line means. Chapter 6.1 does the same on pages 33 to 37.

**5.4 Everything else on the sheet checks out.** The 3:1, 1:1, 1:2:1, 2:1, 9:3:3:1, 1:1:1:1 and complete-linkage 3:1 and 1:1 ratios are all correct, and the sex-linkage rules ("Fathers pass X-linked alleles only to daughters", "Sons receive X-linked alleles only from mothers") are correct as stated.

### 6. Load-bearing versus skippable

**Load-bearing.** The whole sheet, used the way it is designed to be used: read a cross, decide which branch it is on, predict the ratio. That is precisely the inference Specimen P1 Q18 demanded. `[OFFICIAL]` Print it and keep it beside every drill.

**Skippable.** Nothing, on grounds of length. But do not memorise the sheet as a table of ratios to recall. LO 2(aa) says outright that "knowledge of the expected ratio for various types of epistasis is not required", and the general principle carries: the ratio is what you **derive** from a diagram, not what you **recall** from a chart. Use the sheet as a check on an answer you have already worked out.

### 5. Errors, contradictions and ambiguities

Chapter 6.1 is, on the biology, one of the cleanest chapters in the corpus. There is no equivalent of Book 3's glucagon-on-skeletal-muscle error, where the notes teach something flatly false about an examined mechanism. What is here is four genuine internal contradictions, one mislabelled figure and a handful of notation slips. Every one is stated below with both conflicting statements quoted.

**5.1 The Case 1 linkage map on page 42 labels the wrong interval, and the text two lines below it says so.** This is the most consequential error in the chapter because it sits inside a worked method.

Breeding Experiment 1 measures COV between body colour and eye colour as 6.02%. Breeding Experiment 2 measures COV between **body colour and wing length** as 18.5%. The page then offers two possible gene orders. The Case 1 diagram draws an arrow labelled **6 cM** from *black body* to *purple eyes*, and a second arrow labelled **18.5 cM** from *purple eyes* to *vestigial wings*. But 18.5% was never measured between eyes and wings; it was measured between **body and wings**. The chapter's own next sentence proves the diagram wrong: "If Case 1 is to be true, the COV for purple eyes and vestigial wings should be about: **18.5 – 6.0 = 12.5%**." You cannot both label that interval 18.5 and then predict it as 12.5. In Case 1 the 18.5 cM arrow should span the whole distance from black body to vestigial wings.

Case 2 is drawn correctly: 6 cM from *purple eyes* to *black body*, 18.5 cM from *black body* to *vestigial wings*, giving eyes-to-wings as 18.5 + 6.0 = 24.5%, which is exactly what the text predicts. And the final answer diagram on p.43 is correct: black body, 6 cM, purple eyes, 12.2 cM, vestigial wings. So the error is isolated to Case 1's arrow placement on p.42. `[SCHOOL]` on the diagram, `[SCHOOL]` on the contradicting sentence, `[INFERRED]` on the correction. Verified by rendering p.42 as an image, so this is not a text-extraction artefact.

**5.2 The Fig. 16 lead-in calls a dominant pedigree a recessive one.** Page 31, section 8.4 X-LINKED DOMINANT INHERITANCE, second bullet: "Fig. 16 illustrates a representative pedigree for a trait that demonstrates **sex-linked recessive** inheritance." The figure caption directly beneath it reads: "Fig. 16: Pedigree of a **sex-linked dominant** condition." The section heading, the caption, the genotypes in the figure and the characteristics list are all consistent with dominant; only the lead-in sentence says recessive. It is a copy-paste from section 8.3, which uses the identical sentence frame. `[SCHOOL]`

**5.3 The X-linked "broad principle" on page 25 is only true in a context the sentence does not state.** Second bullet of the five: "If the mother is homozygous dominant, the daughters will have the normal phenotype but will be carriers of the mutation i.e. they are heterozygous and have the ability to pass the recessive X-linked allele to their offspring even though they do not express the recessive phenotype."

Read on its own, that is wrong. A homozygous dominant mother (X^B X^B) crossed with an **unaffected** father (X^B Y) produces daughters who are X^B X^B and carry nothing. The statement only holds when the father is affected, which is the situation set up by the bullet immediately above it: "An affected father will transmit the recessive X-linked allele to all daughters but not to any sons." Presented as a standalone "broad principle" it invites a candidate to write that a homozygous dominant mother always produces carrier daughters. The rule that actually carries the mark is the one in the first bullet: **an affected father makes all his daughters carriers, and none of his sons anything.** `[SCHOOL]` on the statement, `[INFERRED]` on the correction.

**5.4 The lethal-gene genotypic ratio is inconsistent between chapter 6.0 and chapter 6.1.** Chapter 6.0 gives Yy × Yy as "1:2:1" genotypic and "2:1" phenotypic. Chapter 6.1 p.21 gives the same cross as "**2:1** (¼ offspring dies)" and offers no genotypic ratio. 6.1 is right and 6.0 is wrong, for the reason set out in 6.0 finding 5.1. Use 6.1's version. `[SCHOOL]`

**5.5 The section 5 heading is a category error the chapter never resolves.** The topic outline on p.3 calls section 5 "Modifications of Classical Monohybrid Inheritance". The section header on p.12 reads "5. NON-MENDELIAN INHERITANCE: MODIFICATIONS OF CLASSICAL MONOHYBRID INHERITANCE". But codominance, multiple alleles and lethal alleles all obey Mendel's first law of segregation perfectly; what they modify is the **dominance relationship** between alleles, and hence the phenotypic ratio, not the segregation. The chapter's own p.10 statement of the first law is untouched by any of them. Calling them non-Mendelian is standard textbook shorthand, and no mark will turn on it, but it produces a real misconception if a candidate concludes that segregation does not apply to a codominant gene. Note also that the chapter's own summary sheet 6.0 correctly places codominance, multiple alleles and lethal genes **inside** the "obeys Mendel's Laws" branch, contradicting the chapter heading. `[SCHOOL]` on both, `[INFERRED]` on the resolution.

**5.6 "4 different phenotypes (gametes) are produced" conflates two different things.** Page 38, section 9.3, three times: "4 different phenotypes (gametes) are produced", "Only 2 phenotypes (gametes) are produced", "4 different phenotypes are produced". A gamete is not a phenotype. The equivalence holds only in a test cross, where every offspring phenotype reports the allele combination that came from the heterozygous parent's gamete, and only because the tester contributes nothing but recessives. The chapter never says that. State it explicitly when using the rule, because the rule itself is worth having: **in a test cross, the number and proportion of offspring phenotypes is a direct read-out of the number and proportion of gamete types made by the heterozygote.** `[SCHOOL]` on the wording, `[INFERRED]` on the resolution.

**5.7 The lethal yellow-mouse mechanism names a gene I cannot verify, and the mechanism is outside every outcome anyway.** Page 16: "Extensive deletion of genetic material characterizing the Y allele actually extends into the coding region of an adjacent gene (**Merc**), rendering it non-functional. It is this gene that is critical to embyronic development." I have not been able to confirm that gene name against any source available here and I am not going to assert a substitute. What matters for the exam is unaffected: a dominant allele can be lethal in the homozygote, giving a 2:1 ratio among survivors. The molecular story is unexamined. Flagged only so it is not memorised as fact. `[SCHOOL]`, unverified.

### 5. Errors, contradictions and ambiguities

**5.1 The Fig. 6 caption contradicts its own figure and the Concept Check stem directly above it.** Page 9. This is the clearest factual error in Book 6.

- Concept Check 1 stem: "In mice, epistasis is observed when the homozygous recessive **bb** genotype occurs at a specific locus. **bb** genotype suppresses pigment synthesis... Mice with **bb** genotype are **albino** regardless of genotype at the A locus, mice with **aa** genotype and at least one dominant B allele are **black**."
- The pathway diagram immediately above the caption: "**bb** inhibits the conversion of white to black pigment, hence mouse is white / albino."
- Fig. 6 caption, last sentence: "The presence of genotype **aa** prevents any deposition of pigment and the mouse will be **white**."

By the chapter's own stem, **aa** mice with at least one B are **black**, not white. Only **bb** gives white. The caption is a copy of the Labrador Fig. 4 caption on the facing page, where **ee** does prevent deposition, with the letters swapped incorrectly. The p.10 allele key confirms which reading is right: "a represent the recessive allele for no yellow pigment deposition, **i.e. black fur**." Verified by rendering p.9 as an image. `[SCHOOL]` on all three statements, `[INFERRED]` on the resolution: **bb gives albino, aa with B present gives black, A\_B\_ gives agouti.**

**5.2 The chapter builds a memorisation table that its own learning outcome excludes.** Pages 15 and 16 present the seven modified F2 ratios (9:3:3:1, 9:3:4, 12:3:1, 9:7, 9:6:1, 15:1, 13:3) as a table to be learnt, headed "SUMMARY OF MODIFIED F2 MENDELIAN DIHYDRID RATIOS". LO 2(aa), printed on the chapter's own page 1, says "knowledge of the expected ratio for various types of epistasis is **not** required". The chapter partially acknowledges this by marking the last three rows "Other ratios not covered in lecture (potentially new scenarios)", which concedes that new ratios can appear and therefore that the list cannot be the point. It is not a factual error. It is a study-strategy error, and it is the one that costs the most hours. `[OFFICIAL]` on the exclusion, `[SCHOOL]` on the table.

**5.3 The claim that meiosis and sexual reproduction are "the basis of continuous variation" is an over-claim the chapter does not qualify.** Page 20: "The above three sources arising from meiosis and sexual reproduction accounts for new combination of alleles, which is the basis of **continuous** variation." Four lines later: germline mutations "produce distinct differences between individuals, and frequently form the basis of **discontinuous** variation."

Crossing over, independent assortment and random fertilisation shuffle whichever alleles exist, at whichever loci. For a polygenic trait that shuffling does produce the continuous spread. For a single-gene trait such as the ABO blood group, which the chapter itself gives as its example of **discontinuous** variation on p.18, exactly the same three processes are what generate the variation between siblings. So the sentence is true of polygenic traits and false as a general rule. Chapter 6.1 p.45 lists the same three sources under the neutral heading "ways of introducing genetic variation" without the continuous label, which is the safer version. `[SCHOOL]` on the statement, `[INFERRED]` on the correction.

**5.4 The degrees-of-freedom symbol clashes head-on with the official one, and this is the highest-value finding in the chapter.** Page 23: "**df = n – 1** where **n** is the number of phenotypic classes."

The 9477 syllabus, in the Notes on the Use of Statistics in Biology, prints the χ² equation with "**v = c − 1**" and then defines the symbols: "**n = sample size (number of observations)**", "**c = number of classes**", "**v = degrees of freedom**". `[OFFICIAL]` 9477 Specimen Paper 2 Question 4(d) prints exactly the same thing on the question paper: "v = c – 1, where v = degrees of freedom, c = number of classes." `[OFFICIAL]`

So in the official symbol set, **n is the sample size and c is the number of classes**, and the school's notes use **n for the number of classes**. Anyone who has learnt "n − 1" from these notes and then meets a stem where n is defined as the sample size has a live collision. In the maize question on the specimen, n would be 380 and c would be 4. Subtracting one from the wrong quantity gives v = 379 instead of v = 3, and the critical value is then unreadable. The corpus records this failure mode in an examiner's own words: candidates "cited an incorrect χ²crit value from the table due to the wrong degrees of freedom, or gave contradictory statements, and lost credit". `[SCHOOL]`

The school's underlying explanation is correct, and worth keeping: "The degree of freedom... is always one less than the number of **classes of data**." Only the letter is wrong. **Write v = c − 1 and say classes out loud.**

**5.5 Worked Example 6 quotes two slightly different critical values for the same test.** Page 26, step 4(c) of both columns: "χ²crit, df=2, α=0.05 = **5.99**". Step 5 of both columns: "χ²crit (= **5.991**)". The table on p.23 prints 5.99. Trivial, but note the p.27 Concept Check reprints a **different** table (from N02) whose df=3, p=0.05 entry is 7.82 where the p.23 table gives 7.81. Real papers print their own tables and the values differ in the last digit; always read the table on the paper in front of you rather than the one you memorised.

**5.6 Small notation and wording slips.** Page 14: the F2 genotypic ratio is printed "9 C\_P : 7 (3 C\_pp + 3 ccP\_ + 1 ccpp)", missing the underscore after P in the first class; it should read 9 C\_P\_. Page 12, Fig. 7 caption: "enzyme E1 (meaning: ww, the inhibitor W for the enzyme is non-functional)" describes a non-functional inhibitor where the mechanism the chapter teaches everywhere else is that **ww produces no inhibitor at all**, which is a different thing. Page 15 heading: "DIHYDRID" for dihybrid. Page 21 topic-outline entry: the superscript 2 in "Chi–squared (χ2)" has broken away from the χ in the text layer of the contents page.

**5.7 Beyond these, the chapter is sound and the arithmetic all checks out.** The three epistasis worked examples give the ratios they claim (9:3:4, 12:3:1, 9:7) and every Punnett square is correctly filled. Worked Example 5's χ² of 4.0 is right (1.0 + 3.0), the df of 1 is right, and 4.0 > 3.84 does mean reject H0. Worked Example 6's 7.6 and 0.15 are both right, and the probability commentary in the FYI boxes (about 2.5% and above 90%) is right for df = 2. The five-step procedure and both conclusion sentences match every mark scheme in the corpus word for word.

### 6. Load-bearing versus skippable

**Load-bearing:**

- **Page 5, the dihybrid-versus-gene-interaction distinction.** One gene to one character against many genes to one character. This is the concept that makes epistasis make sense, and it is the thing an epistasis question is really testing.
- **Page 7, the four definitions.** Epistasis, epistatic gene, hypostatic gene, and the contrast with complete dominance (different locus, not same locus). LO 2(aa) says "**describe** the interaction between loci", and this is the description.
- **The three worked examples as procedures, pages 8, 11 and 14.** Not for their ratios but for the move each one makes: identify which genotype at the epistatic locus does the suppressing, work out which phenotypic classes therefore merge, and only then read off the ratio. That is what "focus of this section is on problem solving" means.
- **The biochemical basis figures, Figs. 4, 6, 7 and 8.** Every epistasis answer that earns full marks is an enzyme-pathway answer: which allele makes which functional enzyme, which step is blocked, which product fails to appear. The corpus schemes reward exactly this and penalise answers that stop at "the gene masks the other gene".
- **Section 3.1 and 3.2, pages 18 to 19, all six rows.** LO 2(cc) is a whole outcome on this one comparison, and it names the two causes in its own words: "many, additive genes" against "one or a few genes". The other five rows are the marks.
- **Section 3.3, page 20.** The three meiotic and fertilisation sources, and that only mutation makes new alleles. Bridges to Book 3 LO 2(t) and Core Idea 4.
- **Page 21, the three assumptions.** Random fertilisation, equal survival, large sample. This is the answer to "suggest why the observed results differ from the expected", which the corpus asks repeatedly.
- **Pages 22 to 24, the five-step chi-squared procedure, in full and in order.** LO 2(dd). The two conclusion sentences on p.24 must be reproducible verbatim.
- **Worked Example 6, page 26.** Two hypotheses tested against one data set, with the better fit chosen. This is the highest-order thing the chi-squared test can be asked to do and the specimen's maize question is a simpler version of it.

**Skippable or heavily discounted:**

- **The ratio table on pages 15 and 16, as a memorisation target.** LO 2(aa) excludes it in writing. Read it once to see that different suppression patterns merge different classes; do not learn the seven numbers.
- **The Greek etymology of epistasis** and the names "duplicate interaction", "duplicate dominant epistasis" and "dominant and recessive epistasis" for the three uncovered ratios. Zero syllabus hits for any of them.
- **The Learning Experiences block, page 2, and the References list.**
- **Nothing else.** At 27 pages this is the shortest chapter in the Book and, once the ratio table is discounted, close to all of it is examinable.

### 3. The school's genetic-diagram layout, verbatim, because the layout is what is marked

| 3 | "List down the **parental gametes**" | "**\*Gametes should always be circled**" and "Each gamete contains only one allele for the stem-length gene. In this case, every gamete produced by one parent has the same allele." |

**Six conventions inside that layout, each of which a scheme in the corpus polices:** `[SCHOOL]`

1. **Gametes are drawn inside circles.** The school states it as a rule with an asterisk: "\*Gametes should always be circled". Prelim schemes award the gamete mark for "correct gametes **and circling of the gametes**" as one condition, so an uncircled gamete row can score nothing even when the alleles are right.
2. **Phenotype and genotype are separate, labelled rows, and every genotype sits under its phenotype.** Step 7's note, "It is important to show correlation between genotype & phenotype", is the whole point. Corpus schemes reject a bare ratio: one prints "compulsory to see phenotypes, not just the numbers", another rejects "1:1:1:1 with no phenotypes".
3. **The cross symbol is a lower-case x between the two parents,** and the two parents sit side by side on the same line as their label.
4. **"Random fertilisation" is written as a line of its own** immediately before the Punnett square. It is not decoration; it names the assumption the square encodes.
5. **The Punnett square is labelled by parent and by sex.** The header row reads "F1 Male gametes" and the left column reads "F1 Female gametes", with the gametes circled inside those header cells. Where sex matters, as in every sex-linkage question, the labels are load-bearing and a scheme in the corpus rewards "parental genotypes matching parental phenotypes **and gender**".
6. **Step 1 is skipped only when the question has already defined the alleles.** The school says so. When it has not, the "Let ... represent ..." line is itself a mark, and schemes reject wrong or invented symbols: "Reject: different symbols used", "penalised if the correct symbols are not used", "Reject: if symbols for blood groups are wrong / if colourblindness is not represented as sex-linkage".

**Three variants of the layout that the chapters introduce without flagging them as variants:**

- **Sex linkage** replaces plain letters with superscripts on chromosome symbols: "Designate X-linked alleles as superscripts on top of X or Y chromosomes in genetic representation" (6.1 p.23). So the parental genotype row reads X^H X^h × X^H Y, and the phenotype row must state **sex as well as trait**: "1 normal female : 1 normal male : 1 normal female (carrier) : 1 haemophiliac male" (6.1 p.24).
- **Linkage** replaces the plain pair with a two-line fraction, the alleles on one chromosome written above the alleles on its homologue, as in LG over lg (6.1 pp.33 to 37). A prelim scheme in the corpus makes this explicit and expensive: "**penalise under MP2 & MP3 if linkage notation not used**". The chapters never define the notation in words; they just start using it on p.33.
- **Codominance and multiple alleles** use a shared capital with distinguishing superscripts, C^R and C^W for shorthorn coat, L^M and L^N for MN blood group, I^A, I^B and i for ABO (6.1 pp.12 to 14). The lower-case i for the recessive ABO allele is the exception that proves the notation rule, and it is the one candidates lose marks on.

**How the marks are actually split.** A corpus scheme for a four-mark linked-gene diagram lists them as: "1. Correct parental phenotypes; 2. Correct parental genotypes; 3. Correct offspring genotypes (for all 4); penalise under MP2 & MP3 if linkage notation not used; 4. Correct offspring phenotypes (for all 4)". Another prints the tolerance directly on the paper: "Correct F2 genotypes: all = 1m, **≥2 mistakes: 0m**. Correct F2 phenotype ratio: all = 1m, **≥2 mistakes: 0m**." `[SCHOOL]` One mistake is survivable; two in the same row is not.
