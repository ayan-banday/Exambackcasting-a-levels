Question 1 [11 marks]

(a) The fruit fly, Drosophila melanogaster, has autosomal genes for body colour and wing shape. A fly with normal features is called a wild type; it has a striped body and its wings are longer than its abdomen. There are mutant variations such as an ebony-coloured body or vestigial wings. Wild type features are coded for by dominant alleles, A for wild type body and B for wild type wings.

State what is meant by the term autosomal in this context. [1]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (a)

(a) State what is meant by the term autosomal in this context (Drosophila, genes A/a and B/b). [1]

1. The genes for body colour and wing shape, or A/a and B/b, are not located on a sex chromosome.

2. Examiner comment: this part was fairly well done with a sizable proportion scoring full credit; students who failed to do so often lost marks for using back the root word "autosome" from the question stem in their answers rather than using their own words to illustrate the meaning.

(b) In an investigation on the tomato genome, pollen grains from the anthers of a pure-bred plant with green leaves and hairy fruit were transferred by a scientist to the stigmas of a pure-bred plant with mottled (green and yellow) leaves and smooth-surfaced fruit. All the F1 generation had green leaves and smooth fruit.

Define what is meant by a pure-bred plant. [1]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (b)

(b) Define what is meant by a pure-bred plant. [1]

1. A plant that is homozygous at both gene loci coding for colour of leaves and texture of fruit.

2. Examiner comment: this question was poorly done. Most students correctly mentioned 'homozygous' but did not define it in the context of the question. Many also incorrectly referred to 'homozygous alleles' instead of genes or genotype.

(c) Define the terms gene and allele and explain how they differ. [4]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (c)

(c) Define the terms gene and allele and explain how they differ. [4]

1. Gene is a heritable factor or unit of inheritance.

2. Gene is composed of DNA.

3. Gene controls a specific characteristic, or codes for a polypeptide or protein. Maximum 2 marks for the gene half.

4. Allele is a form of a gene.

5. Alleles of a gene occupy the same gene locus, that is, the same position on the chromosome.

6. Alleles occur in pairs in a diploid cell.

7. Alleles cause slightly different phenotypes for the same characteristic.

8. Alleles differ from each other by one or a small number of bases or base pairs. Maximum 2 marks for the allele half.

(d) Define the following term: recessive allele. [2]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (d)

(d) Define the following term: recessive allele. [2]

1. A recessive allele is an allele that is masked in expression, or masked in the phenotype, by a dominant allele in a heterozygous individual.

2. It is an allele that is only expressed in the phenotype in homozygous recessive individuals.

(e) The Labrador is a breed of domestic dog. Its fur coat can be chocolate, black and yellow. In Labradors, TYRP1 is a gene that codes for the fur colour. In this gene locus, it has two alleles, B and b. The dominant allele, B, codes for an enzyme that will result in the production of melanin, leading to black fur. The recessive allele, b, will code for another enzyme that results in the production of a brown form of melanin, leading to chocolate fur.

Explain the terms locus and dominant allele. [2]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (e)

(e) Explain the terms locus and dominant allele. [2]

1. Locus refers to the position occupied by the gene on the chromosome.

2. Dominant refers to an allele whose characteristic is expressed in the phenotype even in the presence of an alternative allele.

(f) The genes for eye colour and coat colour in mouse-deer are found on separate chromosomes. One gene is found on chromosome 3, while the other gene is found on the X chromosome. Female mouse-deer are XX and male mouse-deer are XY. To investigate the inheritance of eye colour and coat colour in mouse-deer, scientists performed a reciprocal cross using purebred individuals.

Explain what is meant by 'reciprocal cross'. [1]

---

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (f)

(f) Explain what is meant by 'reciprocal cross'. [1]

1. The same characteristics or traits are used or investigated, but the genders or sexes are reversed or switched.

Question 2 [10 marks]

(a) Explain how genotype is linked to phenotype. [8]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (a)

(a) Explain how genotype is linked to phenotype. [8] One mark each.

1. A genotype of an organism is determined by the alleles of specific genes. Accept genetic make-up with respect to the alleles.

2. The phenotype of an organism is the physical manifestation or trait resulting from a specific genotype.

3. And its interaction with the environment.

4. Reference to when different alleles are transcribed to form mRNA.

5. Reference to mRNA being translated into polypeptide chains or proteins.

6. Different alleles will result in the production of different polypeptide chains or proteins.

7. Different gene products or proteins may affect different cellular pathways, different parts of a pathway, or different functions.

8. Resulting in different phenotypes.

9. The alleles present for each particular gene may be dominant or recessive.

10. Dominant alleles mask the effect or influence of recessive alleles.

11. Hence only the dominant allele will be expressed in the phenotypes of homozygous dominant and heterozygous organisms.

12. Phenotypes of homozygous recessive individuals will only be expressed when both alleles are recessive.

(b) Explain why the offspring produced by the same parents are different in appearance.

Restrict your answer here to the single point about where a phenotype that neither parent carried can come from at all. [2]

---

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (b)

(b) Where a phenotype that neither parent carried can come from at all. [2]

1. Gene mutation gives rise to new alleles which code for new phenotype, characteristic or trait.

2. [INFERRED] The three meiotic sources, crossing over, independent assortment and random fertilisation, only produce new combinations of alleles that already exist, so none of them can be the answer here; mutation is the only source of a new allele.

Source: ACJC Prelim 2014 P2 Q9(b), reprinted as ACJC Prelim 2018 P2 Q7(a), stem and full key verbatim including the one-mark-each instruction (a); RI Prelim 2024 P3 Q4(a), a 15-mark essay whose marking point 3 is quoted verbatim, with the stem narrowed here to that single point so the question does not become an essay (b). Archetype: the genotype-to-phenotype chain, which is LO 2(w) entire and is reused whole in Cluster 2, where every full-mark epistasis answer is this chain run twice with one run broken. Trap: stopping at "the dominant allele masks the recessive one" without ever reaching transcription, translation and a protein, which is the half of the answer that carries most of the eight marks; leaving out the environment clause from the definition of phenotype, which is LO 2(bb) sitting inside LO 2(u); answering (b) with crossing over or independent assortment, neither of which makes a new allele. LOs: 2(w) how genotype produces phenotype through a polypeptide; 2(u) genotype and phenotype defined; reach-back to Book 2 gene mutation as the origin of new alleles. Concepts fused: (1) genotype as allelic composition; (2) phenotype as genotype plus environment; (3) transcription; (4) translation; (5) a different allele giving a different polypeptide; (6) a different protein acting at a different step of a pathway; (7) dominance as masking within one locus; (8) the homozygous recessive as the only genotype that expresses a recessive phenotype; (9) mutation as the sole source of a new allele.

Question 3 [7 marks]

(a) Briefly explain how another factor, besides crossing over, can bring about genetic variation in the offspring produced by a sexually reproducing organism. Exclude mutation from your answer. [1]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (a)

(a) A factor besides crossing over that brings about genetic variation, excluding mutation. [1]

1. Independent assortment of homologous chromosomes during meiosis I results in different combinations of alleles in the gametes, giving 2^n possible combinations where n is the number of homologous pairs. Reject independent assortment without any sufficient elaboration.

2. Or, random fertilisation, that is, the random fusion of gametes, results in different genotypes or combinations of alleles in the offspring, because each gamete carries a different set of chromosomes.

(b) Explain how the random fusion of gametes leads to the expression of rare, recessive alleles. [2]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (b)

(b) Explain how the random fusion of gametes leads to the expression of rare, recessive alleles. [2]

1. By chance, both gametes may carry the recessive allele.

2. The zygote or offspring then has a pair of recessive alleles, that is, it is homozygous for the recessive allele, so the recessive phenotype is expressed.

(c) A couple already have two children, neither of whom suffers from glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency, a sex-linked recessive condition.

Explain why the probability of the third child having G6PD deficiency is independent of whether the first and second child suffers from the disease. [2]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (c)

(c) Why the probability for the third child is independent of the first two. [2]

1. Gametes are produced from separate meiosis, where the alleles of the G6PD gene undergo random segregation.

2. Each fertilisation results from random fertilisation.

(d) In a particular variety of tomato plant, the allele for red fruit colour (A) is dominant over the allele for orange fruit (a) and the allele for green base when ripe (B) is dominant over the allele for no green base when ripe (b). Two students, Eugene and Cornelius, each crossed a plant with red fruit and green base when ripe with a pure bred plant with orange fruit and no green base when ripe.

Describe briefly how a plant breeder ensures that the offspring produced are only from the desired cross. [2]

---

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (d)

(d) How a plant breeder ensures the offspring are only from the desired cross. [2]

1. Anthers are removed before maturity, to produce male sterility.

2. Pollen is transferred by hand.

3. Plants are isolated, or flowers are bagged, before and after pollination. Any two.

Question 4 [8 marks]

Chronic Granulomatous Disease (CGD) is characterised by defects in the enzyme NADPH oxidase, causing phagocytes (for example, neutrophils) to improperly clear invading pathogens. X-CGD is the most common type of CGD and primarily affects males, with less females getting the disease. It is caused by a mutation in a gene on the sex chromosome.

(a) With reference to the information provided above, state the mode of inheritance of CGD. [1]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (a)

(a) State the mode of inheritance of CGD. [1]

1. X-linked recessive.

(b) Explain your answer for (a). [2]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (b)

(b) Explain your answer for (a). [2]

1. If the gene that causes the disease were on the Y chromosome, females would not get the disease as they have no Y chromosome, but females do have a chance of getting the disease in the question context.

2. Males only have one X chromosome, and there are no corresponding alleles on the Y chromosome to mask the effects of the recessive allele, that is, there is no other X chromosome to mask the effect of the recessive allele.

3. Females have 2 copies of the X chromosome, so if one copy has the recessive allele the other copy of the X chromosome might still be able to mask the effect.

4. If the disease were caused by a dominant allele, more females would be expected to get the disease as females have two copies of the X chromosome and would show symptoms once they inherit a dominant allele on an X chromosome.

(c) A woman not affected by CGD marries a man not affected with CGD. Their daughters are all not affected, while they have some sons who are affected, and some sons who are not.

Using the symbols A/a, construct a genetic cross diagram to show the outcomes. [4]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (c)

(c) Using the symbols A/a, construct a genetic cross diagram to show the outcomes. [4]

1. Key. Let X^A represent the X chromosome with the allele A for normal NADPH oxidase; let X^a represent the X chromosome with the mutated allele a for NADPH oxidase; let Y represent the Y chromosome. The allele for normal NADPH oxidase (A) is dominant to the allele for mutant NADPH oxidase (a).

2. Parental phenotypes: mother not affected × father not affected. Parental genotypes: X^A X^a × X^A Y. The mark is awarded for parental genotypes matching parental phenotypes and gender, plus parental gametes circled.

3. Parental gametes, circled: X^A and X^a from the mother; X^A and Y from the father.

4. F1 genotypes: X^A X^A, X^A X^a, X^A Y, X^a Y.

5. F1 phenotypes: not affected female, not affected female carrier, not affected male, affected male. F1 phenotypic ratio 2 : 1 : 1. The mark is awarded for genotypes corresponding to phenotypes with gender, plus the F1 phenotypic ratio.

6. The four marks are: the key, the parental row with circled gametes, the F1 genotypes, and the F1 phenotypes with the ratio.

(d) Use the genetic cross diagram in (c) to find the probability of the couple getting a son with CGD. [1]

---

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (d)

(d) Probability of the couple getting a son with CGD. [1]

1. The probability of getting a son with CGD is 1 out of 2, that is, 0.50 or 50% or one half.

2. [INFERRED] The denominator is the sons only, because the stem asks for the probability among sons; a candidate who quotes one quarter has answered a different question, namely the probability among all offspring.

Question 5 [9 marks]

(a) In chickens, feather colour is either white, black or speckled. The alleles for black feathers and white feathers are denoted by C^B and C^W respectively. A cross between a white chicken and a black chicken gave rise to a speckled chicken.

Using the symbols provided, draw a genetic diagram to show the results of a sibling mating between two speckled chickens. [3]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (a)

(a) Genetic diagram for a sibling mating between two speckled chickens. [3]

1. F1 phenotype: speckled chicken × speckled chicken. F1 genotype: C^B C^W × C^B C^W.

2. F1 gametes: C^B and C^W from one parent, C^B and C^W from the other. One mark for the gametes.

3. F2 genotypes: C^B C^B, C^B C^W, C^B C^W, C^W C^W. One mark for the genotypes.

4. F2 phenotypes: black chicken, speckled chicken, speckled chicken, white chicken, in the phenotypic ratio 1 : 2 : 1. One mark for the phenotypes together with the phenotypic ratio.

5. There is no error carried forward for wrong F1 genotypes, and one mark is penalised if the correct symbols are not used.

6. [INFERRED] Speckled is codominance, not incomplete dominance: the heterozygote shows both parental colours distinctly, as separate black and white feathers, rather than a blended grey. A the papers scheme rejects incomplete dominance outright on this cross.

(b) The ABO blood group gene has three alleles. The O allele differs from the A allele by the deletion of a single nucleotide G in exon 6. The A and B alleles differ from each other by 7 nucleotides, producing 4 amino acid differences between the two enzymes they encode.

Explain why the allele O is recessive while alleles A and B are co-dominant to each other. [4]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (b)

(b) Why the allele O is recessive while alleles A and B are co-dominant to each other. [4] Maximum 4 marks.

1. The deletion of a single nucleotide G in exon 6 of the O allele.

2. Resulted in a frameshift mutation and the early introduction of a stop codon, that is, a nonsense mutation.

3. Leading to the synthesis of a truncated protein, that is non-functional.

4. Despite alleles A and B differing by 7 nucleotides and 4 amino acids, they both give rise to functional proteins with different functions, adding different substrates to the H antigen, or words to that effect.

5. Presence of both antigens A and B on the red blood cells.

(c) Colour pattern phenotype in the tortoise beetle involves alleles that show codominance. There is also an order of dominance of alleles (dominance hierarchy). Four alleles exist: L^T, L^R, L^V and L^r. The heterozygote L^T L^R has the distinct dfm-b phenotype, in which both parental patterns appear together on the same beetle.

Use this information to identify the codominant alleles and list the dominance hierarchy with alleles from the most dominant to the least dominant. [2]

---

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (c)

(c) Identify the codominant alleles and list the dominance hierarchy. [2]

1. The codominant alleles are L^T and L^R.

2. The dominance hierarchy is L^T and L^R > L^V > L^r, that is, L^T and L^R together, then L^V, then L^r.

Question 6 [6 marks]

(a) Nail patella syndrome is a rare genetic syndrome characterised by unusual changes in one's nails, kneecaps and bones. It is caused by mutations in the LMX1B gene that encodes for a transcription factor that plays a vital role in early embryonic development.

Fig. 8.1 shows the transmission of the nail patella syndrome along with ABO blood type. Individuals suffering from the nail patella syndrome are shaded, and the blood type of each individual is also stated.

With reference to Fig. 8.1, justify why nail patella syndrome is an autosomal dominant disease. [4]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (a)

(a) Justify why nail patella syndrome is an autosomal dominant disease. [4]

1. Genetic disorder is rare, so individuals from outside the family, for example II-3 and II-5, are unlikely to carry the allele.

2. Offspring III-3, III-4, III-6 and III-7 would have inherited a mutant allele from the affected parent, and a normal allele from the other parent. Identify the corresponding parents based on whichever offspring are raised as the example.

3. Heterozygous III-3, III-4, III-6 and III-7 are affected by nail patella syndrome, so the mutant allele is dominant.

4. Affected father II-4 produced an unaffected daughter III-5, which is not possible if the mutant allele is sex-linked dominant. Accept if other individuals are quoted.

(b) Severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID) caused by adenosine deaminase (ADA) deficiency is a type of autosomal recessive disorder.

Explain what is meant by the term recessive in this context. [2]

---

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (b)

(b) Explain what is meant by the term recessive in this context. [2]

1. Reference to the idea of two copies of the mutant allele being present before the phenotype is expressed.

2. A scheme on a parallel pedigree question rejects "individual must be homozygous recessive" on its own, because recessive is not explained. The expected shape names the conclusion and then the reason: recessive, because it requires both alleles to be defective before the child is phenotypically affected.

Answer - part (b)

(b) Explain your answer, and what changes if two heterozygous pairs are linked. [2] [INFERRED: no official key beyond the letter]

1. Only heterozygous gene pairs generate two kinds of gamete; homozygous pairs generate one. In PpqqRrSsTTUu the heterozygous pairs are Pp, Rr, Ss and Uu, which is four, so the number of gamete types is 2^4 = 16. qq and TT contribute one allele each and multiply the count by 1.

3. If two of the heterozygous pairs sat on the same chromosome with no crossing over between them, they would be inherited as a single unit, so the two pairs would contribute 2 gamete types between them rather than 4, and the total would fall from 16 to 8. This is why linked genes fail to produce the expected ratios.

(b) Explain your answer, taking each option in turn. [3] [INFERRED: reasoning not in the official key]

1. The most informative cross is C c × C^h c, because each parent shows the phenotype of its more dominant allele while hiding a recessive one, and albino c is the most recessive of the four alleles, so it can hide behind either.

Answer - part (d)

(d) Why no fixed phenotypic ratio can be quoted for a multiple-allele cross. [2] [INFERRED]

1. If a gene controlling a characteristic has three or more alleles, the alleles are called multiple alleles, but only two of them can be present in any one diploid individual, so which two the parents happen to carry decides which phenotypes are even available in the offspring.

2. There is therefore no fixed ratio attached to multiple-allele crosses; the ratio has to be derived from the particular pair of alleles each parent carries, which is why the question asks for a maximum number of colours rather than a ratio.

(e) Wild-type freshwater snails, Physa heterostropha, have pigmented shells. When two pure-breeding albino snails were crossed and their F1 selfed, the F2 generation consisted of 48 pigmented snails and 35 albino snails.

Answer - part (e)

(e) What the pigmented F1 rules out, and what it points to. [2] [INFERRED: this record's own key covers the later parts of the question only]

1. Two pure-breeding albino parents are each homozygous, and their F1 is pigmented, so the two parents cannot be homozygous for the same gene; if they were, every offspring would be albino as well.
