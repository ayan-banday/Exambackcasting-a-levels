## Questions

### Question 1  [10 marks]

In a species of plant, round seed shape is dominant to wrinkled.

**(a)** A plant with round seeds is crossed with a plant with wrinkled seeds. Some offspring have
wrinkled seeds.

Using a full genetic diagram, determine the genotype of the round-seeded parent and give the expected
phenotypic ratio of the offspring. **[6]**

**(b)** Name the type of cross carried out in (a), and explain what makes it able to reveal the
unknown genotype. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why a heterozygous parent produces two types of gamete in equal numbers, naming the
stage of meiosis responsible. **[2]**

### Question 2  [8 marks]

In cattle, coat colour is controlled by a single gene with two codominant alleles. **C^R** gives red
coat, **C^W** gives white coat, and the heterozygote is roan.

**(a)** Draw a full genetic diagram for a cross between two roan cattle, giving the offspring
phenotypic ratio. **[5]**

**(b)** Explain why codominance gives a 1:2:1 phenotypic ratio where complete dominance gives
3:1. **[2]**

**(c)** State what is meant by multiple alleles, and why a gene with multiple alleles has no single
fixed ratio. **[1]**

### Question 3  [10 marks]

Haemophilia is caused by a recessive allele carried on the X chromosome.

**(a)** Draw a full genetic diagram for a cross between a carrier female and an unaffected male,
labelling the Punnett square by parent and by sex, and give the expected ratio of offspring by
phenotype and sex. **[6]**

**(b)** Explain why a male is described as hemizygous for a sex-linked gene, and what follows from
that. **[2]**

**(c)** State **two** features of a pedigree that would suggest a condition is X-linked recessive
rather than autosomal recessive. **[2]**

### Question 4  [8 marks]

In a plant, tall (**T**) is dominant to dwarf (**t**) and purple flower (**P**) is dominant to white
(**p**). The two genes are on different chromosomes.

**(a)** A plant heterozygous for both genes is test crossed. State the genotype of the other parent,
the gametes produced by each parent, and the expected phenotypic ratio of the offspring. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain, in terms of meiosis, why a double heterozygote produces four types of gamete in equal
numbers. **[2]**

**(c)** A student writes that gene interaction and dihybrid inheritance are the same thing. Explain why
this is wrong. **[2]**

### Question 5  [8 marks]

Two genes are located on the same chromosome.

**(a)** Write the genotype of an individual heterozygous at both loci in the correct notation for
linked genes, and explain why writing it as **AaBb** would lose marks. **[3]**

**(b)** A test cross of this individual produces four phenotypic classes: 42, 8, 9 and 41.

State what these results show about the two genes, identify which classes are the recombinants, and

**(c)** State what result would have been obtained had the two genes been completely linked. **[1]**

### Question 6  [8 marks]

In one species, flower colour depends on two genes. Gene **A** codes for an enzyme that converts a
colourless precursor into a colourless intermediate. Gene **B** codes for an enzyme that converts the
intermediate into a purple pigment. The recessive alleles of both genes code for non-functional enzymes.

**(a)** Explain why an individual of genotype **aaBB** is white, using the pathway. **[3]**

**(b)** Two individuals of genotype **AaBb** are crossed. Derive the expected phenotypic ratio of the
offspring, showing your reasoning. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why epistasis is not the same as dominance. **[2]**

### Question 7  [8 marks]

A dihybrid cross produced 320 offspring in four phenotypic classes: 170, 62, 66 and 22. The expected
ratio was 9:3:3:1.

**(a)** State the null hypothesis for a chi-squared test on these data. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the expected number in each class, and state the degrees of freedom, showing how you
obtained it. **[3]**

**(c)** The calculated value of χ² is 2.41. The critical value at p = 0.05 is 7.82. State the conclusion
in full. **[2]**

**(d)** A second investigation measured the height of the same plants and obtained a continuous range of

# Mark Schemes

### Question 1

**(a)** Six marks, one per row. **The layout is the mark scheme.**

**Let T represent the allele for round seed and t the allele for wrinkled seed.**

**Parental phenotypes: round seed × wrinkled seed.**

**Parental genotypes: Tt × tt.**

**Gametes: (T) (t) × (t) (t) — the gametes must be CIRCLED.**

**Random fertilisation, shown as a Punnett square labelled by parent.**

**Offspring genotypes Tt : tt, offspring phenotypes round : wrinkled, ratio 1 : 1. Every genotype must sit under its phenotype.**

The round-seeded parent must be **Tt**, because a wrinkled offspring is **tt** and must have received a **t** from each parent.

Gap check. **Uncircled gametes score nothing for that row**, and **a bare ratio with no phenotypes under the genotypes is rejected.** Two mistakes in the same row scores zero for that row.

**(b)** Both.

**A test cross: the individual of unknown genotype is crossed with the homozygous recessive.**

**The homozygous recessive contributes only recessive alleles, so the offspring phenotypes reveal the alleles contributed by the unknown parent; any recessive offspring proves it was heterozygous.**

**(c)** Both.

**The two alleles at a locus are on homologous chromosomes.**

**These separate at anaphase I of meiosis, so half the gametes receive one allele and half the other.**

**Source:** LOs 2(u), 2(v), 2(x) and 2(y). Row structure and the circling and phenotype rules verbatim from the School (HCI) Prelim 2024 P2 Q7 and NJC Prelim 2015 P2 Q10 schemes.

### Question 2

**(a)** Five marks.

**Let C^R represent the allele for red coat and C^W the allele for white coat; the alleles are codominant.**

**Parental phenotypes: roan × roan. Parental genotypes: C^R C^W × C^R C^W.**

**Gametes: (C^R) (C^W) × (C^R) (C^W), circled.**

**Random fertilisation, Punnett square labelled by parent.**

**Offspring genotypes C^R C^R : C^R C^W : C^W C^W, phenotypes red : roan : white, ratio 1 : 2 : 1.**

**(b)** Both.

**In codominance both alleles are expressed in the heterozygote, so the heterozygote has its own distinct phenotype rather than resembling the homozygous dominant.**

**The heterozygote therefore forms a third visible class, so the 3:1 of complete dominance separates into 1:2:1.**

**(c)** One.

**Multiple alleles means more than two alleles exist for the same gene in the population, although any one diploid individual still carries only two; because the possible genotype combinations vary with which alleles the parents carry, there is no single fixed ratio.**

**Source:** LO 2(x), codominance and multiple alleles. Notation and marking points from ACJC Prelim 2011 P2 Q4(a)(i) and VJC Prelim 2017 P2 Q4.

### Question 3

**(a)** Six marks.

**Let X^H represent the X chromosome carrying the normal allele and X^h the X chromosome carrying the haemophilia allele; the Y carries no allele for this gene.**

**Parental phenotypes: carrier female × unaffected male. Parental genotypes: X^H X^h × X^H Y.**

**Gametes: (X^H) (X^h) × (X^H) (Y), circled.**

**Random fertilisation, with the Punnett square labelled by parent AND by sex.**

**Offspring genotypes X^H X^H : X^H X^h : X^H Y : X^h Y.**

**Phenotypes: normal female : carrier female : normal male : haemophiliac male, ratio 1 : 1 : 1 : 1.**

**(b)** Both.

**A male has only one X chromosome and the Y carries no allele for this gene, so he has only one allele at the locus rather than two.**

**He therefore expresses that allele whether it is dominant or recessive, which is why far more males than females are affected by an X-linked recessive condition.**

**(c)** Any two.

**Far more affected males than affected females.**

**An affected male never passes the condition to his sons, since he gives them his Y.**

**An affected female must have an affected father, and all her sons will be affected.**

**Reciprocal crosses give non-identical results.**

**Source:** LO 2(x), sex linkage. Marking points from MJC Prelim 2013 P2 Q5, RVHS Prelim 2014 P2 Q4(b) and IJC Prelim 2015 P2 Q4.

### Question 4

**(a)** Four.

**The other parent is the homozygous recessive, ttpp.**

**The double heterozygote TtPp produces four gamete types: (TP) (Tp) (tP) (tp), circled.**

**The homozygous recessive produces one gamete type: (tp).**

**Offspring phenotypic ratio: tall purple : tall white : dwarf purple : dwarf white = 1 : 1 : 1 : 1.**

**(b)** Both.

**The two genes are on different chromosomes, so their alleles assort independently.**

**The bivalents line up independently of one another at the metaphase plate in metaphase I, so all four combinations are equally likely.**

**(c)** Both.

**Dihybrid inheritance concerns two different characters, each controlled by its own gene.**

**Gene interaction concerns a single character controlled by two or more genes acting together.**

**Source:** LOs 2(x) and 2(y). Marking points from the 9477 Specimen P2 Q4(a) and (b) and ASRJC Prelim 2023 P2 Q6(b).

### Question 5

**(a)** Three.

**The genotype is written in the two-line stick form, with one chromosome's alleles above the line and its homologue's below: A B over a b.**

**Writing AaBb does not show that the two loci are on the same chromosome.**

**It therefore loses the marking point for the genotype and the marking point for the gametes, because the gametes that can be produced depend on which alleles are on which chromosome.**

**(b)** Four.

**The four classes in a large majority and a small minority show that the two genes are linked but not completely: this is incomplete linkage.**

**The two large classes, 42 and 41, are the parental types; the two small classes, 8 and 9, are the recombinants.**

**The recombinants arose by crossing over between non-sister chromatids at a chiasma during prophase I of meiosis.**

**Crossing over occurred between the two loci, exchanging alleles between the homologous chromosomes, so new combinations were produced in a minority of gametes.**

**(c)** One.

**Only the two parental classes would have appeared, in a 1:1 ratio, with no recombinants at all.**

**Source:** LO 2(z). The two-mark penalty rule is verbatim from the School (HCI) Prelim 2024 scheme: *penalise under MP2 and MP3 if linkage not shown.*

### Question 6

**(a)** Three.

**The individual is homozygous recessive at gene A, so it produces no functional enzyme for the first step.**

**The colourless precursor is therefore not converted into the colourless intermediate.**

**Even though functional enzyme B is present, it has no substrate to act on, so no purple pigment is formed and the flower is white.**

Gap check. **Naming the enzyme that fails and the product that never forms is what earns the marks**, not the word "epistasis".

**(b)** Three.

**A dihybrid cross of AaBb × AaBb gives 9 A_B_ : 3 A_bb : 3 aaB_ : 1 aabb.**

**Only A_B_ has both functional enzymes and is purple. A_bb has the intermediate but cannot convert it, so it is white; both aa classes lack the first enzyme, so they are white.**

**The 3, 3 and 1 classes therefore merge into a single white class, giving 9 purple : 7 white.**

Derive it as above. **The outcome states in its own brackets that the ratios are not required, so never recall one, and never volunteer a subtype label** — schemes print *reject recessive epistasis*.

**(c)** Both.

**Dominance is an interaction between two alleles at the same locus, where one masks the other.**

**Epistasis is an interaction between alleles at two different loci, where one gene suppresses the expression of a different gene.**

**Source:** LO 2(aa). Marking points from NYJC Prelim 2022 P2 Q7(a) and VJC Prelim 2023 P2 Q7(b).

### Question 7

**(a)** One.

**There is no significant difference between the observed numbers and the expected numbers based on a 9:3:3:1 ratio, and any difference is due to chance.**

**(b)** Three.

**Expected numbers: 320 × 9/16 = 180; 320 × 3/16 = 60; 320 × 3/16 = 60; 320 × 1/16 = 20.**

**Degrees of freedom v = c − 1, where c is the number of phenotypic classes.**

**There are 4 phenotypic classes, so v = 4 − 1 = 3.**

Gap check. **Degrees of freedom counts CLASSES, not offspring.** Your notes write **df = n − 1** using n for classes, which collides with the key's symbol list where n is sample size. **Write v = c − 1** and the collision disappears.

**(c)** Both.

**χ² of 2.41 is less than the critical value of 7.82 at p = 0.05, so the difference is not significant and we do not reject the null hypothesis.**

**The results are therefore consistent with a 9:3:3:1 ratio, so the two genes are unlinked and assorting independently.**

**Say "do not reject H₀", never "accept H₀".** Both halves are needed: the statistical verdict, then the biological conclusion.

**(d)** Both.

**A t-test.**

**Height is continuous variation, giving measurements and means rather than counts in discrete classes, and chi-squared can only be used on frequencies in categories.**

**Source:** LO 2(dd) and 2(cc). Marking points and both wording rules from the Book 6 Cluster 3 schemes, including the critical-value conventions.
