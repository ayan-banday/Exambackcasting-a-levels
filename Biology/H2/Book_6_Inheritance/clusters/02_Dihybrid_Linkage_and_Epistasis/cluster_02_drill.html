Question 2 [6 marks]

(a) In a dihybrid inheritance, gene B/b codes for flower colour while gene H/h codes for leaf shape of a plant.

Explain how different characteristics can be inherited independently in dihybrid inheritance. [2]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (a)

(a) Explain how different characteristics can be inherited independently in dihybrid inheritance. [2]

1. The 2 genes, encoding for flower colour and leaf shape, are found on different pairs of homologous chromosomes, that is, they are unlinked genes.

2. Reference to independent assortment occurring. Reject "random assortment". The full version is that it allows for random arrangement of the alleles of one gene pair at the metaphase plate during metaphase I and II, independent of the alleles of the other gene pair.

3. Subsequent segregation of alleles of one gene pair is independent of the alleles of the other gene pair during anaphase I and II, which allows the production of gametes with different combinations of alleles.

(b) Mendelian inheritance refers to an inheritance pattern that follows Mendel's First Law of Segregation and Mendel's Second Law of Independent Assortment.

State Mendel's Second Law of Independent Assortment. [1]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (b)

(b) State Mendel's Second Law of Independent Assortment. [1]

1. Reference to independent segregation of different allele pairs.

2. [INFERRED] Note for revision, not for the answer: the 9477 syllabus never names Mendel and never uses the words segregation or independent assortment. The biology is required by LOs 2(v) and 2(z); the attribution is not. School papers still name him, so answer the question as asked and do not spend time on the attribution.

(c) Plants are a good choice of experimental organisms for carrying out such crosses and for performing statistical tests. Compared to plants, humans are less ideal and it is usually more difficult to arrive at reliable conclusions for observations involving humans.

Suggest why. [2]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (c)

(c) Suggest why humans are less ideal experimental organisms than plants. [2]

1. Humans produce limited offspring, which makes statistical tests difficult.

2. Humans have a long life span and some traits only appear at a later stage in life.

(d) Drosophila melanogaster, commonly known as the fruit fly, has been a cornerstone of genetic research for over a century.

Suggest why fruit flies are good experimental organisms for carrying out crosses in genetic research. [1]

---

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (d)

(d) Suggest why fruit flies are good experimental organisms for carrying out crosses. [1] Any one of the following.

1. Fruit flies have a short life cycle, allowing researchers to study multiple generations in a short period.

2. Fruit flies can produce a large number of offspring from a single cross, providing sufficient sample sizes for statistical analysis.

3. Fruit flies are easy to handle or maintain, are small and require minimal space and resources, can be cultured in large numbers in simple lab vials with standard media, and are easily anaesthetised, for example by chilling, for examination and manipulation under a microscope.

4. Fruit flies show distinct phenotypes which make it straightforward to observe and track the inheritance of specific genes through crosses.

5. Fruit flies have a relatively small number of chromosomes, only four pairs, three autosomes and one pair of sex chromosomes, simplifying analysis.

(a) Predict the expected phenotypic ratio in the F2 progeny of the test cross. [1]

2. Reject 1:1:1:1 with no phenotypes.

(b) In Capsella sp., heart-shaped fruit is dominant to narrow fruit at each of two gene loci.

Describe how you could identify Capsella sp. plants with heart-shaped fruits, which are homozygous dominant in at least one gene locus. [2]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (b)

(b) How to identify plants that are homozygous dominant in at least one gene locus. [2]

1. Perform a test cross.

2. If the plants with heart-shaped fruits are homozygous dominant in at least one gene locus, all offspring produced will only have heart-shaped fruits.

3. [INFERRED] The instrument works because a homozygous recessive partner contributes only recessive alleles and therefore masks nothing, so the number and proportion of offspring phenotypes is a direct read-out of the number and proportion of gamete types made by the tested parent.

Answer - part (d)

(d) Explain your answers to (c). [2] Any two.

1. There are greater numbers of parental phenotypes and fewer numbers of recombinants.

2. The genes A/a and B/b are linked, located on the same chromosome, autosomal, and show incomplete linkage.

Question 5 [8 marks]

(a) Wing pattern in the butterfly species Heliconius melpomene is controlled by genes on autosomal chromosomes. The gene for banding pattern in the upper wing has two alleles: a dominant allele F coding for a full band, and a recessive allele f coding for a broken band. The gene for ray pattern in the lower wing has two alleles: a dominant allele R coding for rays, and a recessive allele r coding for no rays. The inheritance of these genes is an example of autosomal linkage.

Explain the meaning of the term autosomal linkage. [2]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (a)

(a) Explain the meaning of the term autosomal linkage. [2]

1. Linkage: genes that control different characters are located on the same chromosome, that is, the alleles are inherited together as a unit.

2. Autosomal: the genes are not located on a sex chromosome.

3. The the reject lines, verbatim: reject "alleles found on the same chromosome"; reject just saying that the two loci are linked; reference must be made to the genes being on the same chromosome, and to that chromosome not being a sex chromosome. A separate scheme rejects "sex-linked" where autosomal linkage was wanted.

4. Parallel keys worth learning: autosomal linkage is where two or more genes are found in different gene loci on the same autosomal chromosome, and the alleles of the genes do not assort independently and tend to be inherited together.

(b) State precisely the type of genetic inheritance observed. [1]

1. Autosomal linked genes, or autosomal linkage. Reject: sex-linked.

(c) Explain your answer in (b). [2]

(d) Some of the germline cells in the parental female fruit fly have been discovered to have a mutation at the centromere of chromosome 5.

Answer - part (d)

(d) Suggest how a mutation at the centromere of chromosome 5 could affect the gametes formed. [3] Any three.

1. The mutated centromere has a different 3D conformation, so the kinetochore cannot bind.

2. Spindle fibres cannot attach.
