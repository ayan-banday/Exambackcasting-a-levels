# Cluster 02 Drill: Dihybrid Crosses, Linkage and Epistasis
### Biology H2 (9477) · Book 6 · attempt on Loom/Wispr Flow, reasoning aloud, BEFORE reading answers

**Eleven questions, 76 marks, 34 parts.** The mark-a-minute rule under-counts this drill badly. **Six of
these questions ask you to lay out a full genetic diagram**, and a four-mark diagram written properly,
with the key, the two phenotype rows, the two genotype rows, the circled gametes, the random
fertilisation line, the labelled square and the two ratios, takes eight to ten minutes and not four. Set
aside **an hour and three quarters**, or run it in two sittings.

**This is the load-bearing cluster of Book 6.** LO 2(x) is the outcome the whole Book is built around and
it says **dihybrid crosses**. Everything Cluster 1 did with one gene arrives here with two. Genetic
diagram construction, ratio prediction and reverse inference all live here.

**Every question below is an actual exam paper question.** The stem is the paper's own wording. Where a
stem has been edited it is only to let it stand without the parts of the original that came before it,
and the edit is recorded in the Sources block at the foot of that question.

**Question 1 is the anchor.** It is 9477 Specimen Paper 2 Question 4, parts (a) and (b), the only
extended Book 6 question that exists on an official paper. Four of its eleven marks are for the diagram
alone, and the stem hands you the ratio before you start. You are told the answer and paid to lay out
the working.

**Four real figures, three questions figure-led.** Book 6 figures are not micrographs; they are
**counts**. Offspring numbers in a table, a pedigree chart, a Southern blot, a chromosome ideogram. The
skill is arithmetic and inference on those counts.

**Two MCQs, both real.** Question 6 is 9477 Specimen Paper 1 Question 18, the only clear Book 6 MCQ in
existence and the single most exam-representative question in this drill.

**Cumulative over Cluster 1 and Books 2 and 3.** Q2 needs independent assortment placed at metaphase I
from Book 3. Q5(d) needs non-disjunction and aneuploidy from Books 2 and 3. Q7 needs sex linkage and the
allele key from Cluster 1, on a species where the male has no Y at all. Q8 and Q11 need the
genotype-to-phenotype chain from Cluster 1 run twice, once per locus, with one run broken.

**One scope ruling you must hold on to, because it saves hours.** LO 2(aa) excludes the modified
epistasis ratios **in its own brackets**: *"knowledge of the expected ratio for various types of
epistasis is not required; focus of this section is on problem solving."* Your notes tabulate seven of
them across two pages. **No question in this drill turns on recalling one.** Every epistatic ratio here
is derived from the interaction the stem describes, and two of them, in Q7 and Q11, appear in no table
anywhere. Chromosome mapping is gone too: linkage maps, map units, centimorgans, crossover value as a
computed number and gene ordering all are not in the learning outcomes. **Recognising linkage from data stays.
Converting it to a distance goes.**

**What the marks reward.** Use the symbols the question supplies and no others. Write linked genotypes in
the **two-line stick form**, one chromosome's alleles above its homologue's, because a scheme
**penalises under MP2 and MP3 if linkage notation is not used**, so one habit costs two marks. Circle
your gametes and **underline them to show linkage**. Label which offspring classes are **parental** and
which are **recombinant**. Never write a ratio without its phenotypes. In an epistasis answer, name the
**enzyme** and the **pathway step it blocks**, because a scheme prints *penalise once if no reference to
enzyme*, and never volunteer a subtype name, because *reject recessive epistasis* and *reject
recessive/dominant epistasis* both appear in this the papers.

**How to run it.** One part at a time, aloud, answers untouched. Rate your confidence out loud before
every reveal.

### Question 2

**(a)** *Explain how different characteristics can be inherited independently in dihybrid inheritance.* **[2]**

**1.** The **2 genes, encoding for flower colour and leaf shape, are found on different pairs of homologous chromosomes**, that is, they are **unlinked genes**.

**2.** Reference to **independent assortment occurring**. **Reject "random assortment".** The full version is that it **allows for random arrangement of the alleles of one gene pair at the metaphase plate during metaphase I and II, independent of the alleles of the other gene pair**.

**3.** **Subsequent segregation of alleles of one gene pair is independent of the alleles of the other gene pair during anaphase I and II**, which **allows the production of gametes with different combinations of alleles**.

**(b)** *State Mendel's Second Law of Independent Assortment.* **[1]**

**1.** Reference to **independent segregation of different allele pairs**.

**2.** [INFERRED] Note for revision, not for the answer: the 9477 syllabus **never names Mendel and never uses the words segregation or independent assortment**. The biology is required by LOs 2(v) and 2(z); the attribution is not. School papers still name him, so answer the question as asked and do not spend time on the attribution.

**(c)** *Suggest why humans are less ideal experimental organisms than plants.* **[2]**

**1.** **Humans produce limited offspring**, which makes statistical tests difficult.

**2.** **Humans have a long life span and some traits only appear at a later stage in life.**

**(d)** *Suggest why fruit flies are good experimental organisms for carrying out crosses.* **[1]** Any one of the following.

**1.** **Fruit flies have a short life cycle, allowing researchers to study multiple generations in a short period.**

**2.** **Fruit flies can produce a large number of offspring from a single cross, providing sufficient sample sizes for statistical analysis.**

**3.** **Fruit flies are easy to handle or maintain, are small and require minimal space and resources, can be cultured in large numbers in simple lab vials with standard media, and are easily anaesthetised, for example by chilling, for examination and manipulation under a microscope.**

**4.** **Fruit flies show distinct phenotypes which make it straightforward to observe and track the inheritance of specific genes through crosses.**

**5.** **Fruit flies have a relatively small number of chromosomes, only four pairs, three autosomes and one pair of sex chromosomes, simplifying analysis.**

**Trap:** writing **random assortment**, which a scheme rejects by name; giving independent assortment without naming the stage, which is where the second mark sits; saying "the genes are on different chromosomes" and stopping, without the assortment step that follows from it; forgetting that segregation at anaphase and assortment at metaphase are two different events.
**LOs:** 2(x) dihybrid inheritance; 2(z) the structural reason linkage breaks independent assortment; reach-back to Book 3 for metaphase I and anaphase I.
