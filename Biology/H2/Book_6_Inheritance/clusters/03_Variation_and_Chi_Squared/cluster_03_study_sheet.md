## A. The environment modifies the phenotype a genotype produces

**A1. The claim, which is the whole of LO 2(bb) in two sentences.** **The genotype of an organism is
not always directly expressed in the phenotype.** **Gene expression and the resultant phenotype are
often modified through the interaction between an individual's particular genotype and the
environment.** This is the same clause that sits inside the definition of phenotype in Cluster 1, so
the two outcomes reinforce each other.

**A2. Diet in honeybees is the example the syllabus names, so it is the one to have ready.** A female
larva can develop into either caste, and **what it is fed decides which**. A larva fed **royal jelly
throughout development differentiates into a queen**; one fed it only at the start
**differentiates into a worker**. The genotype does not change between the two, only the diet does.
The outcome says **using examples including**, so this one is compulsory and the others are optional.

**A3. Three alternates the chapter gives, each with a different environmental factor.** **Elevation**
changes the growth of **yarrow plants** grown from the same stock at different heights. **Soil
acidity** changes **hydrangea flower colour** from blue-violet to pink, by changing the **availability
of aluminium**. **Light** produces **freckling in humans** and drives **chlorophyll synthesis in
plants**. The chapter's fourth, **late-onset diabetes**, is legitimate but is not the named one.

**A4. This is the only essay-shaped material in the Book, and one rehearsed bridge covers all of it.**
You cannot write an essay about a Punnett square, and the specimen set no Book 6 essay at all. Every
one of the nine essay-scale stems in the corpus asks a version of the same thing: **how variation
arises**, or **how phenotype is linked to genotype and the environment**. The bridge to rehearse runs
**meiosis and fertilisation shuffle existing alleles**, **mutation makes new ones**, and **the
environment modifies how they are expressed**, with an example attached at each step. Section C
supplies the first two links.

## B. Continuous and discontinuous variation, which is LO 2(cc) entire

**B1. Variation defined, and the split.** **Variation describes the recognisable differences in
characteristics between organisms of the same natural population or species.** The chapter splits it
by the shape of what you observe: **discontinuous variation is variation in characters of type**, and
**continuous variation is variation in characters of degree**.

**B2. The six axes the notes compare them on, all of which are marks.**

| Axis | **Discontinuous** | **Continuous** |
|---|---|---|
| Observable phenotype | **definite and clear cut**, divisible into **distinct groups**, so **discrete phenotypic classes** are observed and **intermediates are not observed** | **not clear cut and cannot be divided into distinct contrasting groups**, so a **range of phenotypes** is observed and **intermediates are observed** |
| Number of genes | **one or a few genes** | **many, additive genes** |
| Effect of environment | **little or no environmental effect on the phenotypic expression of the gene** | phenotypes are **modified by the cumulative effect of varying environmental factors acting on the different genotype** |
| Mode of measurement | measurements are represented on **bar graphs** | measurements form a **normal distribution curve** with **a continuous range of intermediate forms between two extremes**, represented on **histograms** |
| Type of analysis | **qualitative**, analysed by **making counts and ratios** | **quantitative**, giving estimates of population parameters such as the **mean and standard deviation** |
| Statistical test | **chi-squared test** | **t-test** |

**B3. The causes come straight out of the outcome's own brackets.** LO 2(cc) supplies them without the
usual vocabulary: **many, additive genes control a characteristic** against **one or a few genes
control a characteristic**. The word **polygenic** returns **zero syllabus hits** while the definition
is printed in the outcome, so learn the mechanism and expect the stem to spell it out rather than name
it.

**B4. Polygenic inheritance, and the two marking points its phrasing is split across.** The
definition: **variations controlled by the combined effect of multiple genes**, in which **genes act on
phenotype in an additive manner**, so that **effects of single genes are too slight to be detected**
while **the combined effect can produce individuals of infinite phenotypic varieties**. A scheme
accepts **combined effect of multiple genes or polygenes** and separately awards **they act on the
phenotype in an additive manner**, and it **rejects multiple additive genes** as a compressed version
of the two. **Additive belongs to the action, not to the genes.**

**B5. Name the type of variation, not the shape of the graph.** A scheme accepts **continuous** and
**rejects normal distribution** and **stabilising selection**, because describing the curve is not
naming the phenomenon. A separate scheme **rejects combinations of genes** where **combinations of
alleles** was wanted, the same gene-for-allele swap Cluster 1 flagged.

## C. Where variation comes from, and the one line that bridges to Core Idea 4

**C1. Three sources come out of meiosis and fertilisation.** **Crossing over** at prophase I,
**independent assortment** at metaphase I, and **random fertilisation**. The first two are Book 3
content being reused, and the third is the assumption written into every genetic diagram.

**C2. None of the three makes a new allele, and that sentence is repeatedly tested.** **All genetic
variation arising from meiosis and sexual reproduction results in new combinations of existing
alleles. It does not give rise to new alleles.** Shuffling cannot invent a sequence that was not
already in the population.

**C3. Mutation is the only source of new alleles, and only some mutations are inherited.** **Gene
mutation is an inheritable change in the nucleotide sequence of DNA, which occurs at a single gene
locus on a chromosome, resulting in formation of new alleles.** **Chromosomal mutation** is instead **a
change in the structure of a chromosome, involving several gene loci, or a change in the number of
chromosomes**. The inheritance filter: **most mutations occur in somatic cells and are not passed from
one generation to the next**, and **only mutations occurring during the formation of gametes, that is
germline mutations, can be inherited**.

**C4. Asexual reproduction gives almost no genetic variation**, because offspring are produced by
**mitosis from one parent**, so differences between them are environmental rather than genetic.

## D. The three assumptions behind every predicted ratio

**D1. A genetic diagram predicts, it does not report.** The chapter states it plainly: **a genetic
diagram, whether of monohybrid or dihybrid inheritance, is only a prediction of the likely outcome in
the offspring generation**. It shows the probable results on three assumptions: **fertilisation is
random**, **there is equal opportunity of survival among offspring**, and **a large number of offspring
are produced**, meaning a **large sample size**.

**D2. These three are the answer to a question the corpus asks constantly.** When a stem says *suggest
why the observed results differ from the expected results*, the expected answer is that one or more of
the three has failed: fertilisation was not random, some genotypes died before they could be counted,
or the sample was too small for chance deviation to average out. It is also the conceptual reason the
chi-squared test exists at all, so treat D and E as one thought.

---

## E. What the chi-squared test does, and when it is the right test

**E1. It tests discrete counts against a predicted ratio.** **The chi-squared test is a statistical
test for the significance of data that consists of discontinuous or discrete variables.** What it
measures is **goodness of fit**: **how well the observed experimental results fit the expected
results**.

**E2. The logic is a statement about chance, in both directions.** **The larger the difference between
observed and expected results, the more likely that the hypothesis is incorrect and hence is not due to
chance alone.** **The smaller the difference between observed and expected results, the more likely
that the hypothesis is correct and hence is due to chance alone.** Every part of the conclusion in F5
is one of those two sentences made precise.

**E3. The data type decides the test, and this is the cleanest pairing in the Book.** **Counts in
discrete classes** are **discontinuous** data and go to the **chi-squared test**. **Measurements on a
continuous scale**, giving a **mean and standard deviation**, are **continuous** data and go to the
**t-test**. 9477 Specimen Paper 4 Question 2(e) asked candidates to **state a statistical test** for a
difference between two means, and the answer was a t-test, so the pairing is examined in both
directions.

**E4. Four facts the syllabus settles, and each one changes what you drill.** **Candidates are not
expected to remember the following equations or what the symbols stand for**, and **candidates will be
given access to the equations, the meaning of the symbols, a t-table and a chi-squared table.** So
nothing here is a memory test; what is tested is judgement. **Chi-squared tests will only be expected
on one row of data**, which rules out contingency tables entirely, so every test you will ever be set
is **a single list of phenotypic classes against a single expected ratio**. **Questions involving the
use of a chi-squared test may be set on Papers 1, 2 and 3.** On **Paper 4** only **an understanding of
the use of the tests** is examinable and **detailed computation will not be required**.

## F. The five-step procedure, in the school's order

**F1. Step 1, state both hypotheses.** **The null hypothesis states that there is no significant
difference between the observed and expected results**, and **it assumes that any differences are due
to chance**. **The alternative hypothesis states that there is significant difference between the
observed and expected results**, and **it assumes that differences are not due to chance**. The
template the chapter gives, with the ratio filled in: **H0: there is no difference from the expected
ratio**, **HA: there is difference from the expected ratio**. Note that **null hypothesis** returns
**zero syllabus hits** and the specimen asked for a conclusion **without ever using the words**, so H0
language is a safe scaffold rather than a requirement.

**F2. Step 2, calculate the expected numbers from the ratio, and calculate them properly.** **The
expected values for each phenotypic class are obtained by calculating the proportion of the total
number of offspring that are expected to fall within that phenotypic class.** For a 9 : 3 : 3 : 1
ratio on 380 grains, the first expected value is **9/16 × 380**, worked out, not estimated at about
216. This is one of the three marks in Specimen Paper 2 Question 4(c), and it is the step candidates
skip. **Every downstream cell inherits the error if it is wrong.**

**F3. Step 3, calculate the statistic from the formula as it is printed.** The official form, which is
what appears on the paper:

> **χ² = Σ (O − E)² / E**

where **Σ is the sum of**, **O is the observed value** and **E is the expected value**. Your notes add
an intermediate symbol **d for the deviation**, writing **Σ d²/E**, which the official sources do not
use. **Use the official form and drop d.** Work class by class, one row per phenotypic class, then sum.

**F4. Step 4, find the critical value in the table.** Three things fix which number you take: **the row
is determined by the degrees of freedom**, **the column is determined by the level of significance**,
and the default is fixed by the school and matched by practice: **for Biology, unless stated otherwise,
we take the level of significance to be 0.05**. State what you have taken in the form the schemes
print: **χ²crit, v = 3, α = 0.05 = 7.81**. Section G is the whole of getting the row right.

**F5. Step 5, write the conclusion, which is marked in separate components rather than as one
sentence.** Three of them are statistical: **the critical value quoted with its degrees of freedom**,
**the comparison made explicitly** as greater than or less than, and **the verdict in terms of
probability and chance**. Both branches are fixed wording.

**When χ²calc is greater than χ²crit:** **the probability that chance alone is the reason for the
difference between observed and expected results is less than 5%**; **the deviation is significant**;
**hence, reject H0 in favour of HA**.

**When χ²calc is less than χ²crit:** **the probability that chance alone is the reason for the
difference between observed and expected results is more than 5%**; **the deviation is not
significant**; **hence, do not reject H0**.

**Then finish with the biology, which is a separate mark from the statistics.** A scheme's version:
**therefore the observed results do not fit the expected 1 : 1 : 1 : 1 test cross phenotypic ratio, or
do not follow Mendelian inheritance**, or **the genes are linked, that is, not on different
chromosomes**. Stopping at *not significant* leaves that mark on the table.

**Three reject lines govern the wording.** **Say do not reject H0, never accept H0**, because every
scheme in the corpus words the negative branch that way. **Due to chance on its own is explicitly
rejected**, in two schemes: **reject due to chance alone**, and **reject due to chance on its own**.
The accepted form is the full probability sentence above. And **quote the critical value with its
degrees of freedom attached**, not as a bare number.

## G. Degrees of freedom, where two symbol sets collide

**G1. This is a notation collision, not a concept you have misunderstood.** Three sources describe the
identical piece of biology with different letters, and one of the letters means something else in the
official set.

| Source | What it prints | Its symbol for the number of classes |
|---|---|---|
| **9477 syllabus**, Mathematical Requirements | **v = c − 1**, with **v = degrees of freedom**, **c = number of classes**, and separately **n = sample size (number of observations)** | **c** |
| **9477 Specimen Paper 2 Q4(d)**, printed on the paper | **v = c − 1**, where **v = degrees of freedom, c = number of classes** | **c** |
| **Your notes, chapter 6.2 page 23** | **df = n − 1**, where **n is the number of phenotypic classes** | **n** |

**G2. The collision is on the letter n and it is head-on.** In the official set, **n is the sample
size**, the number of observations. In your notes, **n is the number of classes**. Both are printed as
the thing you subtract one from. So a candidate carrying the school's habit into a stem that defines n
officially will subtract one from the wrong quantity.

**G3. What that costs, on the specimen's own numbers.** The maize question has **four phenotypic
classes** and **380 grains**. Officially, **c = 4**, so **v = c − 1 = 3**, and the critical value at
α = 0.05 is **7.81**. Substituting the sample size instead gives **380 − 1 = 379**, which is not a row
on any printed table, so the critical value becomes unreadable and the conclusion mark goes with it. An
examiner comment names exactly this: candidates **cited an incorrect critical value from the table due
to the wrong degrees of freedom, or gave contradictory statements, and lost credit**.

**G4. Your notes' underlying explanation is correct, and worth keeping.** They say the degrees of
freedom **measures the number of classes of data that are independent of each other** and **is always
one less than the number of classes of data**, and they give the reason: **when the total is known and
the number in every class but one has been determined, the number in the last class is fixed**, so one
is subtracted. **The reasoning is right. Only the letter is wrong.** Write **v = c − 1** and say
**classes** out loud as you write it.

**G5. Two tables are in circulation and they disagree in the last digit.** Read the one printed on the
paper in front of you.

| **v** (degrees of freedom) | **9477 Specimen Paper 2 table** | **Your notes, 6.2 p.23** | **Concept Check 3 table** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **3.84** | 3.84 | 3.84 |
| **2** | **5.99** | 5.99 | 5.99 |
| **3** | **7.81** | 7.81 | **7.82** |
| **4** | **9.49** | 9.49 | 9.49 |

**The 7.82 is not a typing error**, it is a different printed table adapted from an older paper. Your
notes also print **5.99** in one step of Worked Example 6 and **5.991** two lines later. Neither changes
a verdict, and the habit that protects you is to **quote the value from the table you were given**.

## I. Errors in your notes, and what this cluster cuts

1. **Chapter 6.2 page 20 over-claims what the three meiotic sources produce.** It says they **account
   for new combination of alleles, which is the basis of continuous variation**, then four lines later
   says germline mutations **frequently form the basis of discontinuous variation**. Crossing over,
   independent assortment and random fertilisation shuffle **whichever alleles exist, at whichever
   loci**. For a polygenic trait that does give the continuous spread, but for a single-gene trait such
   as the **ABO blood group**, the chapter's own example of **discontinuous** variation two pages
   earlier, the same three processes generate the variation between siblings. Chapter 6.1 page 45 files
   the identical three under the neutral heading **ways of introducing genetic variation**, which is the
   safer version. **The statement is true of polygenic traits and false as a general rule.**
2. **The degrees-of-freedom symbol in chapter 6.2 page 23 collides with the official one.** Section G
   is that finding in full, and it is the most expensive thing in this cluster to get wrong.
3. **Two critical values in your notes disagree with the specimen's table by one digit**, at **7.82
   against 7.81** for v = 3, and **5.991 against 5.99** for v = 2 within one worked example. See G5.
4. **The specimen paper has its own discrepancy.** Its Table 4.1 gives two of the four observed counts
   as **65 and 21** while Table 4.2, the working table you are told to complete, gives **55 and 31**.
   Both routes land below 7.81, so the verdict is unchanged. **Complete the table you are told to
   complete.**
