(f) State two reasons why the chi-squared test was used. [2]

Question 4 [7 marks]

(a) In a cross investigating the R and D genes in a plant, three phenotypic classes were counted and the calculated value of χ² was 0.38. The critical value at 2 degrees of freedom is 5.99 at p = 0.05 and 4.61 at p = 0.10.

Using the calculated value of χ² and the table, explain what conclusion can be drawn from the data. [2]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (a)

(a) Conclusion from χ² = 0.38 on three classes. [2]

1. df = 3 − 1 = 2, so p > 0.10; since p > 0.05, at a level of significance of 5% we do not reject the null hypothesis, which states that the expected results and observed results are not significantly different.

2. Thus there is no significant difference between the expected and observed results, and the biological conclusion, which the scheme marks as compulsory, is that the R and D genes are completely linked.

(b) In a separate cross in dogs, the observed numbers of the four F2 phenotypes were 30 : 24 : 7 : 3 against an expected ratio of 9 : 3 : 3 : 1, and the calculated chi-squared value was 15.33. The critical chi-squared value for these results at a probability of p = 0.05 is 7.81.

State what can be concluded about the inheritance from the chi-squared value. [3]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (b)

(b) What can be concluded from χ² = 15.33. [3]

1. The value of χ² = 15.33 is larger than the critical value of 7.81 at the 5% significance level.

2. That is, the probability that the deviation from the expected ratio is due to chance is less than 5%, that is, p < 0.05.

3. Contextualising this, the deviation of observed numbers of 30 : 24 : 7 : 3 from the expected ratio of 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 is significant and is not due to chance.

4. Alternatively, the observed numbers of 30 : 24 : 7 : 3 do not conform to the expected ratio of 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 and this is not due to chance.

(c) A student recorded the density of stomata for shade and sun leaves of a plant species and carried out a statistical test to determine whether there was a significant difference between the mean stomatal density for these two types of leaves.

State a statistical test that could have been used to determine whether the difference in mean stomatal density between the sun and shade leaves is significant. [1]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (c)

(c) State a statistical test for a difference between two means. [1] [INFERRED: no official key]

1. The t-test, because two sets of continuous, normally distributed measurements are being compared to see whether their means differ significantly.

2. The pairing is the cleanest thing in this cluster. Counts in discrete classes are discontinuous data and go to the chi-squared test. Measurements on a continuous scale, giving a mean and standard deviation, are continuous data and go to the t-test. The syllabus says candidates will be given a t-table and a chi-squared table, and Paper 4 examines an understanding of the use of the tests without detailed computation, which is exactly what this part is.

(d) In any genetic cross, the observed results are usually different from the expected results. Suggest one reason why such a difference may occur, referring only to events after meiosis. [1]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (d)

(d) One reason the observed results differ from the expected, after meiosis only. [1] Any one.

1. Random fusion of gametes.

2. Differential survival of sperm or ova with particular genotypes.

3. Differential survival rate of zygotes with particular genotypes.

4. These are two of the three assumptions behind every predicted ratio: that fertilisation is random, that there is equal opportunity of survival among offspring, and that a large number of offspring are produced. When a stem asks why observed differs from expected, one of the three has failed, and the stem's phrase "after meiosis" rules out the third source, which is the shuffling meiosis itself does.

Answer - part (b)

(b) Explain your answer, taking each row in turn. [3]

1. The calculated chi-square value is 8.81 and df is 3. This means 0.02 < p < 0.05. At the 5% significance level, p < 0.05. Therefore the difference between observed and expected results is significant and the difference is due to other factors, that is, linked genes in this context, and not chance alone.

5. D is wrong for the mirror reason to A: p > 0.05 and "significant" contradict each other.

6. Note that only B and C are internally consistent, so the real work is deciding which side of 0.05 the calculated value falls, and that is settled by the class sizes, not by the letters.

Question 7 [10 marks]

Describe and explain the difference between continuous and discontinuous genetic variation. [10]

---

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer

Describe and explain the difference between continuous and discontinuous genetic variation. [10]

The paper's own answer is a table of nine axes, with the explanation for each written beside it.

1. Number of genes. Continuous variation is usually the combined effect of many genes or polygenes controlling the characteristic. Discontinuous variation involves one or a few genes controlling the characteristic.

2. Alleles. Many alleles against few alleles.

3. Additive effect. There is an additive effect of two or more, usually many, genes on a single phenotypic character, explained by many genes or alleles, each of which has a small effect on phenotype. In discontinuous variation there is no additive effect, explained by few genes or alleles, each of which has a large effect on phenotype.

4. Effect of alleles. Different alleles have a small effect on phenotype against different alleles have a large effect on phenotype.

5. Type of curve. Continuous variation results in a bell-shaped or normal distribution curve, explained by the additive effect, the range of phenotypes, the quantitative nature of the data, and intermediate phenotypes being more common than extreme phenotypes. Discontinuous variation results in a bar graph, explained by no additive effect, discrete phenotypes, qualitative data and no intermediates.

6. Nature of character. Quantitative against qualitative.

7. Phenotypes. Continuous variation consists of an unbroken range of phenotypes with no discrete phenotypes, explained by phenotypes being affected by environmental factors, the additive effect, and many genes or alleles. Discontinuous variation consists of discrete phenotypes, explained by phenotypes not being affected by environmental factors, no additive effect, and few genes or alleles.

8. Intermediate phenotypes. Intermediate phenotypes are more common than extreme phenotypes against there are no intermediates.

9. Effect of environment. Phenotypes are affected by environmental factors against the environment has little or no effect on phenotype. Note that a separate scheme rejects "no effect" on its own, so write little or no.

10. How to write it. The command is describe and explain, so every axis needs the difference stated for both sides and then the reason attached. State both sides positively; do not write "continuous is many genes, discontinuous is not". The word polygenic is not in the learning outcomes while its definition is printed inside LO 2(cc), so learn the mechanism and expect a stem to spell it out rather than name it.

Question 11 [12 marks]

In the human population, different combinations of phenotypes are observed in each individual, except identical twins.

Explain how the different combination of phenotypes in each individual could have arisen. [12]

---

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer

Explain how the different combination of phenotypes in each individual could have arisen. [12]

1. The phenotype of an organism is the physical or chemical characteristic that is expressed.

2. Phenotypes are determined by the genotype or genes of the organism, which refers to the genetic constitution or allele combination the organism has for a particular characteristic.

3. Depending on the nucleotide sequence, there can be more than one allele for a particular gene, with each different allele coding for a slightly different phenotype.

4. Each individual will inherit two copies of a gene, one from each parent. The resulting phenotype is dependent on which allele is expressed.

5. Allele expression can be influenced by the combinations that come via meiosis, Mendelian interaction, co-dominance, incomplete dominance, linkage and epistasis.

6. Meiosis. During prophase I of meiosis, crossing over of non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes may occur, which results in exchange of alleles.

7. During metaphase I, alleles of different characteristics on different genes assort independently from each other.

8. Thirdly, during anaphase I, homologous chromosomes segregate independently from each other.

9. These processes all allow for different combinations of dominant and recessive alleles for each characteristic to be found in gametes, which would thus give organisms a large amount of phenotypes.

10. Mendelian inheritance. Genes that follow Mendelian inheritance would have two alleles that can code for a particular characteristic. The allele that is expressed when the individual is heterozygous for the gene is known as the dominant allele. The recessive phenotype is expressed only if the individual is homozygous for the recessive allele.

11. Co-dominance and incomplete dominance. Co-dominant alleles would both be equally expressed in a heterozygote, with the phenotype showing both versions of the characteristic. Incomplete dominance would see the heterozygote expressing a phenotype that is an intermediate of the phenotype of an individual that is homozygous for either allele.

12. Linkage. Genes found on the same chromosome would be inherited together and would not independently assort. This would influence the phenotypes that arise after the organism has developed, as certain traits tend to be inherited together, for example hair colour and eye colour.

13. Sex linkage. Genes that are located on sex chromosomes would be linked to the gender of the offspring, and phenotypes arising from these genes would show a preference to a particular gender. Sex-linked diseases that are caused by recessive alleles tend to be more common amongst males, as they would only carry a single copy of the gene compared to females.

14. Epistasis. Epistasis is where two genes are required for a characteristic to be expressed, and the allele of one gene masks the phenotypic expression of a second gene at an entirely different locus.

15. Two things this essay needs that the key does not spell out. First, the three meiotic processes shuffle whichever alleles already exist and none of them creates a new one; the only source of a new allele is gene mutation, and only germline mutations are inherited. Second, random fertilisation is a fourth source in its own right, sitting after meiosis, and it is the assumption written into the bottom of every genetic diagram you have drawn.
