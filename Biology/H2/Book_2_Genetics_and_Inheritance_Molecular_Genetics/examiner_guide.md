# Topic Examiner's Guide — Biology H2 (9477) > Book 2: Genetics and Inheritance (Molecular Genetics)

> Specialises the subject guide to Book 2. Drives the three-cluster breakdown and question
> generation. **Read `../subject_examiner_guide.md` first** — in particular the finding that exam

## 0. Scope boundary — read this before anything else

Core Idea 2 runs **(a) → (dd): 30 learning outcomes**, and they are split across **four** school
books. Book 2 teaches **10 of them**. `[OFFICIAL]`

| Book 2 teaches | Book 3 teaches | Book 4 teaches | Book 6 teaches |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) (b) (c) **(d) partial** (h) (i) (j) (k) (l) (m) | (n) (o) (p) (q) (r) (s) (t) | **(d) partial** (e) (f) (g) | (u)–(dd) |

The school's own Book 2 cover page lists a, b, c, d, h, i, j, k, l, m. That list is correct.

### 0.1 The one scope fact that matters most — LO 2(d) is a third covered

LO 2(d) demands **"the structure and organisation of viral, prokaryotic and eukaryotic genomes
(including DNA/RNA, single-/double-stranded, number of nucleotides, packing of DNA,
linearity/circularity and presence/absence of introns)"**. `[OFFICIAL]`

Chapter 2.3 is titled *Organisation of the **Eukaryotic** Genome* and delivers the eukaryotic third
only. Its own overview map defers the rest explicitly — the branch labelled *"Prokaryotic or Viral
Genomes"* carries the note *"(KIV: Genetics of Viruses and Bacteria)"* and stops there. The identical
LO is re-claimed verbatim on the front of **both** Book 4 chapters. `[SCHOOL]`

**Consequence.** LO 2(d) appears on the Book 2 cover page, so a Book-2-only pass looks complete while
leaving two-thirds of the outcome unanswered. *"Compare the organisation of viral, prokaryotic and
eukaryotic genomes"* — a question the syllabus wording practically invites — is unanswerable from
Book 2 alone. What Book 2 gives on the non-eukaryotic side is five scattered "Note:" boxes: enough
for a comparison-table row, not enough for genome sizes, nucleotide counts, or viral genome typing.

**How the clusters handle it.** Cluster 1 teaches the eukaryotic third properly and builds the
comparison table with the prokaryotic/viral columns **marked as owed to Book 4**. It does not fake
the missing two-thirds, and it does not quietly drop them either.

### 0.2 What Book 2 foreshadows but never teaches

Reading Book 2 end to end gives a false sense of having covered these. It has not. `[SCHOOL]`

- **Cancer.** 2.3 §5.1.5 covers telomerase reactivation, cellular immortality, evasion of apoptosis
  and labels a p53 mutation in a figure — all tagged "(KIV: Molecular Basis of Cancer)". LOs (p)(q)(r)
  are **Book 3**.
- **The classic DNA experiments.** 2.1 §1 names Griffith (1928), Avery (1944) and Hershey–Chase
  (1952), then defers them: *"Study the above experiments through the Case Study"*, "(KIV: Genetics of
  Bacteria / Viruses)". Named in Book 2, taught in **Book 4**.
- **Mitosis, meiosis, the whole inheritance block.** Books 3 and 6.

## 1. Content Node Map (coverage checklist)

**127 nodes** across the three clusters, of which **31 carry no LO** and are cut or demoted per §0.3.
LO letters are `[OFFICIAL]`; node grouping is `[SCHOOL]` from the chapter structure.

### Cluster 01 — DNA Structure and Replication + Genome Organisation (2.1, 2.3) — 32 nodes

- [ ] **DNA and RNA structure (a)** — nucleotide components, phosphodiester backbone, antiparallel
      strands, complementary base pairing (2 H-bonds A–T, 3 G–C), major/minor groove. The three RNAs:
      **mRNA, tRNA, rRNA** — structure tied to role for each. *(mitochondrial and chloroplast DNA are
      explicitly NOT required.)*
- [ ] **Why DNA suits heredity** — information storage with chemical stability, accurate replication,
      capacity for variation.
- [ ] **Semi-conservative replication (b)** — Meselson–Stahl reasoning, and the full enzyme roster
      with each enzyme's exact job: helicase, single-strand binding proteins, topoisomerase/gyrase,
      primase, DNA polymerase III, DNA polymerase I, ligase.
- [ ] **Leading vs lagging strand (b)** — 5'→3' synthesis only, Okazaki fragments, why the asymmetry
      is forced rather than chosen.
- [ ] **The end replication problem (b)** — why linear chromosomes shorten, and why circular ones do
      not. **Required.** Telomerase as the cell's answer is background, not examinable mechanism.
- [ ] **DNA packing (d)(h)** — DNA → nucleosome (histone octamer + H1) → 30 nm fibre → looped domains
      → condensed chromosome. Euchromatin vs heterochromatin, and the link to accessibility.
- [ ] **Non-coding DNA (h)** — introns, centromeres, telomeres, promoters, enhancers, silencers:
      structure and function of each. *(Transposons, satellite DNA, pseudogenes, segment duplication
      NOT required.)*
- [ ] **Eukaryotic genome organisation (d, eukaryotic third)** — gene density, repetitive sequences,
      multigene families, linear chromosomes.
- [ ] **Genome comparison table (d)** — eukaryote column complete here; **prokaryote and virus
      columns owed to Book 4** and flagged as such, never invented.

### Cluster 02 — Eukaryotic Gene Expression + Control of Gene Expression (2.2, 2.4) — 49 nodes

- [ ] **The central dogma and the gene (a)(c)** — template vs coding strand, the genetic code as
      triplet, degenerate, non-overlapping and near-universal; start and stop codons.
- [ ] **Transcription (c)** — initiation (promoter, TATA box, transcription factors, RNA polymerase
      II), elongation (5'→3', template read 3'→5'), termination. Every step is a mark.
- [ ] **RNA processing (c)** — 5' methylguanosine cap, 3' poly(A) tail, splicing by spliceosome and
      snRNPs; introns out, exons joined. **Alternative splicing** — one gene, several polypeptides.
- [ ] **Translation (c)** — amino acid activation and aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase, initiation,
      elongation, translocation, termination; ribosome A/P/E sites; codon–anticodon pairing.
- [ ] **Post-translational modification (c)** — folding, cleavage, addition of groups; links back to
      Book 1 protein structure.
- [ ] **Differential gene expression (i)** — the idea that every cell has the same genome and differs
      by which genes are expressed, spatially and temporally.
- [ ] **Control level 1 — chromatin (i·i)** — histone modification (acetylation loosens, deacetylation
      and methylation tighten) and DNA methylation.
- [ ] **Control level 2 — transcriptional (i·ii)** — control elements: promoters, **enhancers**,
      **silencers**; proteins: transcription factors, **activators** and **repressors**.
- [ ] **Control level 3 — post-transcriptional (i·iii)** — splicing (incl. alternative),
      polyadenylation, 5' capping as regulatory points.
- [ ] **Control level 4 — translational (i·iv)** — **half-life of RNA** and **initiation of
      translation**. These two only.
- [ ] **Control level 5 — post-translational (i·v)** — **biochemical modification** and **protein
      degradation**.

> All five levels are named in the LO. A drill that stops at three has covered 60% of the outcome.

### Cluster 03 — Mutations + Molecular Techniques + Bioethics (2.5, 2.6, 2.7) — 46 nodes

- [ ] **Gene mutation (k)** — substitution, addition, deletion; and how each changes the amino acid
      sequence, including **frameshift**. Silent, missense, nonsense outcomes.
- [ ] **Chromosomal aberration (k)** — **numerical**, including aneuploidy and **trisomy 21**;
      **structural**, including translocation, duplication, inversion, deletion.
- [ ] **Mutation → disease (l)** — **sickle cell anaemia** named in the LO: the single base
      substitution, Glu→Val, and the chain from primary structure to haemoglobin polymerisation to
      cell shape to symptoms. The archetypal cross-book chain back to Book 1.
- [ ] **Mutagens and mutation rate** — spontaneous vs induced.
- [ ] **PCR (j·i)** — denaturation, annealing, extension; Taq polymerase, primers, thermal cycling;
      **advantages and limitations explicitly required by the LO**.
- [ ] **Gel electrophoresis (j·ii)** — charge and size separation, why DNA migrates to the anode,
      reading a band pattern.
- [ ] **Southern blotting and nucleic acid hybridisation (j·iii)** — transfer, probe design,
      detection; what a band means.
- [ ] **Bioethics of maternal genetic screening (m)** — **trisomy-21 named in the LO**. Both sides
      plus a judgement, not a list.

## 3. Mark Scheme Logic (this topic specifically)

**Ordered steps earn ordered marks.** In a replication or translation chain, a scheme awards per
correct step in the correct sequence. Naming the enzyme without its job, or the job without the
enzyme, is a half-answer that scores nothing. Write *"helicase unwinds the double helix by breaking
the hydrogen bonds between complementary bases"*, not *"helicase unwinds DNA"*. `[SCHOOL]`

**Direction and polarity are marks in their own right.** 5'→3' synthesis, the template read 3'→5',
antiparallel strands, DNA migrating to the anode. Reversing a direction is the most common way to
lose a mark that the candidate otherwise understood. `[SCHOOL]`

**Named specifics beat generic nouns.** *DNA polymerase III* where the scheme wants III rather than
"DNA polymerase"; *spliceosome* not "an enzyme"; *Taq polymerase* not "a heat-stable polymerase";
*trisomy 21* not "an extra chromosome". `[SCHOOL]`

**Command-word discipline.** *Describe* wants observable features or ordered steps; *explain* wants
the mechanism with cause and effect; *suggest* signals there is no recall answer and the examiner
wants reasoning from the given data — the second most common command in this Book, so this matters
more here than anywhere else; *discuss* requires both sides and a judgement; *compare* requires both
sides on the same axis.

**LO (j) hides two requirements in a bracket.** *"polymerase chain reaction (including its advantages
and limitations)"* — the advantages and limitations are part of the outcome, not garnish. `[OFFICIAL]`

**LO (m) is a discuss, not a list.** Bioethics of maternal genetic screening wants arguments on both
sides and a reasoned position. A list of considerations with no judgement caps. `[SCHOOL]`

## 4. Trap Model (this topic specifically)

Every generated distractor must encode one of these. No invented distractors.

| Trap | The misconception | The wrong answer it produces |
|---|---|---|
| **Template ↔ coding strand swap** | Cannot say which strand is read | Transcribes the coding strand; gives mRNA identical to the template |
| **Direction reversal** | Memorised "5 to 3" without meaning | Polymerase reading 5'→3'; lagging strand synthesised continuously |
| **Semi-conservative ↔ conservative** | Misreads Meselson–Stahl | Predicts two fully-old and two fully-new molecules after round one |
| **Intron ↔ exon inversion** | Swaps which is removed | "Exons are spliced out"; counts introns as coding |
| **Splicing placed after translation** | Loses the order of processing | Splices the polypeptide, or processes mRNA in the cytoplasm before export |
| **Transcription and translation coupled in eukaryotes** | Imports the prokaryotic picture | Ribosomes translating the transcript inside the nucleus |
| **Frameshift under-claim** | Treats every substitution as catastrophic and every indel as minor | Says a single substitution shifts the reading frame; misses that a 3-base deletion does not |
| **Silent mutation ignored** | Forgets the code is degenerate | Insists every base change alters the protein |
| **Sickle cell over-claim** | Recalls the disease, not the mechanism | "The gene is faulty" with no Glu→Val, no polymerisation, no shape change |
| **Histone acetylation direction** | Inverts the effect | Says acetylation condenses chromatin and silences the gene |
| **Enhancer must be adjacent** | Assumes control elements sit next to the promoter | Denies distant enhancers or looping |
| **Gel migration reversed** | Forgets DNA is negatively charged | Small fragments nearest the well; migration to the cathode |
| **PCR needs a cell** | Confuses PCR with cloning | Describes plasmid transformation for an in-vitro amplification |
| **Restriction-enzyme import** | Carries in dropped syllabus content | Answers a technique question with sticky ends and ligation — **not in 9477 at all** |

## 5. High-Yield Flags

Nodes that earn heavy marks or fail often. These get **15–20 drill questions**, not the standard 10.

1. **The five levels of control (i)** — the largest single LO in the Book, five named sub-levels, and
   the bank's heaviest topic at 234 questions with 95 at hardest tier. A drill covering three levels
   has covered 60% of an outcome.
2. **Translation as an ordered chain (c)** — long, sequential, every step a mark, and the place
   direction errors cost most.
3. **Sickle cell anaemia (l)** — named in the LO, and the cleanest cross-book chain in the syllabus:
   base substitution → amino acid → primary structure → haemoglobin quaternary structure → cell shape
   → symptom. Reaches straight back into Book 1.
4. **PCR advantages and limitations (j·i)** — the bracketed half of the LO, and the half that gets
   skipped.
5. **The end replication problem (b)** — required by the LO, easy to mistake for the cut telomerase
   content and drop by accident.
6. **LO (m) bioethics** — low frequency, high mark value, `discuss` command, and it sits in a
   three-page chapter that reads like a footnote.
7. **LO (d)'s missing two-thirds** — flag at every revision pass until Book 4 is studied. The most
   likely way this Book produces a nasty surprise.
