### Verbatim 9477 Learning Outcomes for Book 2

Reproduced word for word from `inputs/Biology/H2/official_docs/Biology_H2_syllabus_2026.pdf`,
pp.14-16. Candidates should be able to: `[OFFICIAL]`

**(a)** describe the structure and roles of DNA and RNA (tRNA, rRNA and mRNA) (knowledge of the
structure and roles of mitochondrial DNA and chloroplast DNA is not required)

**(b)** describe the process of DNA replication and how the end replication problem arises

**(c)** describe how the information on DNA is used to synthesise polypeptides (description of the
processes of transcription, formation of mRNA from pre-mRNA in eukaryotes and translation is required)

**(d)** describe the structure and organisation of viral, prokaryotic and eukaryotic genomes
(including DNA/RNA, single-/double-stranded, number of nucleotides, packing of DNA,
linearity/circularity and presence/absence of introns)

**(h)** describe the structure and function of non-coding DNA in eukaryotes (i.e. portions that do not
encode protein or RNA, including introns, centromeres, telomeres, promoters, enhancers and silencers)
(knowledge of transposons, satellite DNA, pseudo-genes and duplication of segments is not required)

**(i)** explain how differential (i.e. spatial and temporal) gene expression in eukaryotes can be
regulated at different levels:
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**i** chromatin level (histone modification and DNA methylation)
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**ii** transcriptional level (control elements, such as promoters, silencers and enhancers, and proteins, such as transcription factors, including activators and repressors)
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**iii** post-transcriptional level (processing of pre-mRNA in terms of splicing, polyadenylation and 5ʹ capping)
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**iv** translational level (half-life of RNA and initiation of translation)
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**v** post-translational level (biochemical modification and protein degradation)

**(j)** describe the principles and procedures of these molecular techniques:
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**i** polymerase chain reaction (including its advantages and limitations)
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**ii** gel electrophoresis
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**iii** Southern blotting and nucleic acid hybridisation

**(k)** explain what is meant by the terms gene mutation and chromosomal aberration. For gene
mutation, knowledge of how substitution, addition and deletion could change the amino acid sequence
(including frameshift) is required. For chromosomal aberration, knowledge of numerical aberration
(including aneuploidy, as in the case of trisomy 21, i.e. Down syndrome) and structural aberration
(including translocation, duplication, inversion and deletion) is required

**(l)** explain how gene mutations can result in diseases (including sickle cell anaemia)

**(m)** discuss the bioethics of genetic maternal screening for mutations, including trisomy-21

Core Idea 2 closes with the standing instruction: *"Use the knowledge gained in this section in new
situations or to solve related problems."* `[OFFICIAL]`

### The four exclusions the syllabus states outright

These are written into the LO text and they are the cheapest marks in the Book to lose by
over-answering. `[OFFICIAL]`

1. **Mitochondrial and chloroplast DNA structure and roles are not required** (LO a). Note the trap:
   Specimen Paper 3 Q1 is built entirely on mtDNA, but every part asks about genome organisation,
   meiosis, PCR or phylogeny. mtDNA is the *stimulus*, never the *content*.
2. **Transposons, satellite DNA, pseudo-genes and duplication of segments are not required** (LO h).
3. LO (i) is **eukaryotes only**. The word "operon" does not appear anywhere in the 9477 syllabus. See
   §5 for how much corpus material this invalidates.
4. LO (j) names **exactly three techniques**. Recombinant DNA technology, gene cloning, ligation,
   vectors, DNA sequencing, DNA profiling, CRISPR, microarrays, Northern and Western blots are **not
   named**. Restriction enzymes survive only as the digestion step that feeds a Southern blot.

## 1. Content Node Map (coverage checklist)

Every node the exam can test in Book 2, grouped by the cluster that will teach it. Convergence
requires no UNTOUCHED node. LO letters are `[OFFICIAL]`.

### Cluster 01, DNA structure, replication and the central dogma (LOs a, b, c)

- [ ] **DNA structure (a)**: nucleotide components, phosphodiester backbone, complementary base
      pairing, antiparallel strands, 5ʹ and 3ʹ directionality, the double helix, and why the stability
      is *cumulative* weak hydrogen bonds plus base stacking rather than any one strong bond.
- [ ] **RNA structure and the three RNAs (a)**: mRNA, tRNA (clover leaf, anticodon, amino-acid
      attachment site), rRNA. For each, structure paired to role. DNA versus RNA on every axis.
- [ ] **Chargaff's rules (a)**: %A = %T, %G = %C, and the arithmetic that follows.
      **`[type override → quantitative]`**
- [ ] **Semi-conservative replication (b)**: the Meselson-Stahl experiment and how to read a density
      gradient after one, two and three generations.
- [ ] **The replication fork (b)**: helicase, single-strand binding proteins, topoisomerase, primase,
      DNA polymerase III, DNA polymerase I, ligase. For each, the reaction it catalyses and the
      direction it works in. Leading versus lagging strand, Okazaki fragments, why a primer is needed.
- [ ] **The end-replication problem (b)**: why linear chromosomes lose terminal sequence, what
      telomeres and telomerase do, and why circular prokaryotic genomes never face it.
- [ ] **Transcription (c)**: RNA polymerase, promoter, template strand, initiation, elongation,
      termination, and the products.
- [ ] **Pre-mRNA processing (c)**: 5ʹ capping, polyadenylation, splicing by the spliceosome,
      alternative splicing. This node is examined under both LO (c) and LO (i)(iii).
- [ ] **The genetic code (c)**: triplet, degenerate, non-overlapping, universal, with the exam
      consequence of each property.
- [ ] **Translation (c)**: amino acid activation by aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase, ribosome A, P and E
      sites, initiation, elongation, translocation, termination, polysomes.
- [ ] **Replication versus transcription versus translation compared**: a recurring question in its
      own right, not just a summary.

### Cluster 02, genome organisation and the control of gene expression (LOs d, h, i)

- [ ] **Genome comparison (d)**: viral, prokaryotic and eukaryotic genomes across the six axes the LO
      names, which are DNA or RNA, single or double stranded, number of nucleotides, packing of DNA,
      linear or circular, and presence or absence of introns. The LO gives you the comparison table
      and the mark scheme expects it point for point.
- [ ] **DNA packing (d)**: DNA plus histone octamer to nucleosome, then the 10 nm beads-on-a-string,
      then the 30 nm chromatin fibre, then looped domains on a protein scaffold, then the condensed
      metaphase chromosome. Every level is a separate mark.
- [ ] **Euchromatin versus heterochromatin**: transcriptional accessibility, and the exact word the
      scheme wants for an active region.
- [ ] **Non-coding DNA (h)**: introns, centromeres, telomeres, promoters, enhancers, silencers.
      Structure paired to function for each. Nothing beyond these six.
- [ ] **Chromatin-level control (i)(i)**: histone acetylation and deacetylation, the ionic-bond
      argument, DNA methylation, and which direction each pushes expression.
- [ ] **Transcriptional control (i)(ii)**: general versus specific transcription factors, the TATA box
      and the initiation complex, activators binding enhancers, repressors binding silencers, DNA
      looping and mediator proteins.
- [ ] **Post-transcriptional control (i)(iii)**: alternative splicing, polyadenylation, 5ʹ capping,
      and how one gene yields several proteins.
- [ ] **Translational control (i)(iv)**: mRNA half-life, initiation factors, translational repressors.
- [ ] **Post-translational control (i)(v)**: biochemical modification (phosphorylation, glycosylation,
      cleavage of a precursor) and protein degradation by ubiquitin and the proteasome.
- [ ] **Identifying which level is operating** from a described observation. This is the applied form
      of LO (i) and it is the level at which the exam actually tests it.
- [ ] **Spatial versus temporal differential expression**: the LO's own framing, easy to skip.

### Cluster 03, mutations, molecular techniques and bioethics (LOs k, l, j, m)

- [ ] **Gene mutation types (k)**: substitution (silent, missense, nonsense), addition, deletion, and
      the reading-frame consequence of each. Indels in multiples of three do not shift the frame.
- [ ] **From mutated gene to altered polypeptide (k, l)**: the full causal chain, which is altered base
      sequence to altered codon to altered amino acid to altered bonding to altered tertiary
      conformation to altered active or binding site to loss of function.
- [ ] **Sickle cell anaemia (l)**: the named example the LO requires. GAG to GTG, glutamate to valine,
      the hydrophobic patch, polymerisation of deoxygenated HbS, sickling.
- [ ] **Chromosomal aberration (k)**: numerical (aneuploidy, non-disjunction, trisomy 21) and
      structural (translocation, duplication, inversion, deletion). Both categories, named.
- [ ] **Somatic versus germ-line mutation**: only germ-line changes are inherited.
- [ ] **PCR (j)(i)**: denaturation, annealing, extension, the temperatures, the role of primers and
      Taq, exponential amplification, and the LO's explicit demand for **advantages and limitations**.
- [ ] **Gel electrophoresis (j)(ii)**: charge on DNA, direction of migration, agarose as a sieve, the
      relationship between fragment size and distance migrated, loading dye and its two jobs, and
      visualisation. Reading a banding pattern is a distinct skill from describing the method.
      **`[type override → quantitative]`** for size estimation from a ladder.
- [ ] **Southern blotting and nucleic acid hybridisation (j)(iii)**: restriction digestion,
      electrophoresis, denaturation, transfer, labelled probe, hybridisation, autoradiography. Every
      step needs its purpose attached.
- [ ] **Bioethics (m)**: the four principles from the school's framework, which are respect for
      persons and autonomy, maximising benefits and minimising harms, justice, and care. Plus the
      decision-making sequence, which is ethical question, facts, stakeholders, ethical principles,
      alternate options and rebuttals, decision and justification. Applied specifically to maternal
      screening for trisomy 21. This is a reasoning scaffold, not content.

### What the specimen actually asked (the useful detail)

**Paper 1, the five Book 2 stems.** `[OFFICIAL]`

| Q | Node tested | Stem type |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | Template strand plus tRNA anticodons from a given non-template sequence | "Which row" 2x2 grid, both cells must be right |
| 10 | Gene length versus mRNA length in *E. coli* and *Drosophila*, introns and splicing | Data table plus **negative stem** ("does **not** help to account for") |
| 13 | Definition of a control element | Four long-prose definitions, one correct |
| 14 | PCR flowchart with one box missing | Four full-sentence completions |
| 15 | Classic versus mosaic Down syndrome | Applied aneuploidy, mitosis versus meiosis timing |

Two structural facts follow. **Not one of the five is plain recall.** Q9 and Q15 need a mechanism run
forwards on unfamiliar data. Q10, Q13 and Q14 use **long-prose distractors** where each of A to D is a
full sentence or longer, and at two minutes per question that reading load is a real timing risk. And
**Q13 and Q14 are both "pick the correct full statement" items**, which punish half-knowledge harder
than any row-matching stem does, because three of the four options are individually plausible.

**Paper 2 Q3, the Book 2 anchor question.** Twelve marks, five parts, opening on two aligned DNA
sequences and their encoded ras peptides.

| Part | Ask | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | **Explain** how the mutation may change the structure and function of the ras protein | 3 |
| (b) | **Describe** the differences between gain-of-function and loss-of-function mutations | 2 |
| (c) | **State** two factors that can cause mutations | 2 |
| (d) | **Describe** how the c-myc translocation differs from the H-ras substitution | 2 |
| (e) | With reference to Fig 3.3, **describe** differences between three tumour-incidence curves | 3 |

Part (d) is the single most Book-2 part on the whole specimen. It hands you a chromosomal
translocation and a base substitution side by side and asks you to distinguish them. That is LO (k)
tested exactly as written. Part (a) is the mutation-to-disease causal chain from LO (l), worth three
marks, which tells you the expected chain is three links long and not two.

**Paper 2 Q6(b), "Outline the roles of centromeres" [3].** This sits inside a mitosis question, so it
looks like Book 3, but centromeres are named in LO (h) and taught in Book 2.3. **Book 2 content gets
harvested from inside other Books' questions.** A Book 2 revision pass that only looks at questions
titled "DNA" or "gene expression" will miss marks like this one.

**Paper 3 Q1, the 32-mark anchor.** Fourteen separately-marked parts across one mtDNA research
narrative, running genome organisation, endosymbiosis, respiration, nature of science, meiosis, PCR,
molecular phylogeny, the species concept and continuous variation. Book 2 supplies three parts:

- (a)(i) **State one way** a typical prokaryotic and a typical eukaryotic genome are **similar** [1]
- (a)(ii) **List three ways** they are **different** [3]
- (c)(i) **Describe and explain** how PCR increases the quantity of DNA for analysis [3]

Two things to take from that. First, the genome comparison was asked as a **similarity first, then
differences**, which is the exact shape most candidates get wrong by producing only differences.
Second, PCR appeared as a **service technique inside someone else's investigation**, applied to
degraded Neanderthal bone DNA, not as a stand-alone "describe PCR" question.

**Paper 3 Section B.** The two essay options were Q4 on the roles of proteins and Q5 on the entry of
molecules into cells. **Both Book 1. A candidate who had studied only Book 2 had no essay to write.**
This is the mirror image of the Book 1 guide's headline finding, and it cuts the other way here.

**Paper 4.** Zero Book 2 content across all three questions, which were photosynthesis, stomatal
density microscopy, and a photosynthesis planning question. Note that the syllabus does name an
**agarose gel electrophoresis cell, TAE/TBE buffer and agarose powder** in the P4 apparatus list
`[OFFICIAL]` (syllabus p.23), so a gel practical is permitted and simply did not appear this time.

**The pattern is unmissable.** Book 2 essays are almost never "describe transcription". They are
**property-led**: pick a property (stability, accuracy, specificity, complementarity, diversity of
roles) and make the candidate assemble evidence for it from across the whole molecular-genetics
machinery. Which means the essay preparation is not chapter revision. It is building three or four
property-themed answer spines that reach across 2.1, 2.2, 2.3 and 2.4 at once.

**The honest tension.** The corpus says Book 2 owns a quarter of all essays. The official specimen
gave Book 2 no essay at all. Both are true. The specimen is one paper and picked two Book 1 options.
Do not conclude from one specimen that Book 2 essays are dead, and do not conclude from the corpus
that one is guaranteed.

## 3. Mark Scheme Logic (this topic specifically)

> **Hard cap on this whole section.** SEAB publishes no 9477 mark scheme, and the 9477 specimen papers
> in the corpus arrive without one. Every marking claim below is `[SCHOOL]`, distilled from JC prelim
> schemes reproduced verbatim in the question bank. They are expected to be broadly representative of
> Cambridge house style, not identical to it. Quoted reject and accept lines are verbatim from those
> schemes.

**1. Comparison marks need point-to-point pairing, and schemes say so explicitly.**
The prokaryote versus eukaryote genome scheme lists sixteen paired points and then states:
*"Reject differences if there is no point to point reference."* Writing "prokaryotic genomes are small"
and separately "eukaryotic genomes have introns" scores nothing, because neither sentence compares
anything. Each mark is one axis with both organisms on it. `[SCHOOL]`

**2. Packing levels are marked as an ordered sequence with the right verb at each step.**
The chromatin-packing scheme reads: nucleosomes with H1 to give the 10 nm beads-on-a-string, which
**coils (R: fold)** to form the 30 nm chromatin fibre, which **folds (R: coil)** into looped domains on
a scaffold to form the 300 nm fibre, which is further coiled into the condensed chromosome. The verbs
coil and fold are **not interchangeable**, and each is a separate reject. This is the strictest lexical
requirement in the Book. `[SCHOOL]`

**3. Every technique step must carry its purpose. Procedure alone scores nothing.**
The gel-visualisation scheme accepts *"staining the gel with ethidium bromide and visualised with UV
light to observe bands"* and accepts methylene blue or a DNA-binding dye, but rejects *"vague reference
to blue dye, dye and loading dye."* On the probe route it accepts *"radioactively labelled probes that
are complementary to both mutant and normal allele, visualised using autoradiography"* and rejects the
answer *"when probe is only complementary to one type of allele."* The pattern is consistent across
PCR, gel and Southern blotting: name the reagent, name what it does, name what you observe. `[SCHOOL]`

**4. The mutation-to-disease chain is marked link by link and the first link must be the gene.**
A KRAS scheme runs seven marking points capped at four, and carries the instruction:
*"Reject marking points 1 and 2 if the answer just states 'at the 12th amino acid position' without the
idea that it occurred in the DNA / gene."* Starting the chain at the protein forfeits the opening
marks even when the rest of the chain is correct. Start at the base sequence, every time. `[SCHOOL]`

**5. Named mutation categories must be distinguished by their defining feature, not by a side effect.**
A missense versus nonsense scheme awards *"a missense mutation will contain an amino acid different
from the original because only one codon is changed"* against *"a nonsense mutation would result in a
premature stop codon, leading to a shortened / truncated polypeptide"*, and then adds
*"Reject references to degeneracy of genetic code."* Degeneracy is the reason a silent mutation exists;
it is not a distinguishing feature between missense and nonsense, and the scheme treats it as noise.
`[SCHOOL]`

**6. Electrophoresis of proteins is separated by charge, and the scheme punishes the size answer.**
For the HbA versus HbS gel: *"At alkaline pH, HbA and HbS are negatively charged and migrate towards
the positive electrode. One less negatively charged amino acid (glutamic acid) in HbS results in slower
migration"*, with *"Reject: size, as HbA and HbS are of the same molecular size."* DNA gels separate by
size, protein gels here separate by charge, and importing the DNA logic loses the mark. `[SCHOOL]`

**7. Replication is not simultaneous and saying so is an explicit reject.**
The trombone-model scheme awards *"two DNA polymerases are grouped into a complex to synthesise in the
same direction, with looping of one of the template strands, to increase efficiency"* and then states
*"Reject: simultaneous synthesis of leading and lagging strands."* The leading and lagging strands are
made in the same *net* direction by looping, not at the same time in the same way. `[SCHOOL]`

**8. Precision nouns are enforced throughout Book 2.** From the reject lists in the corpus:
*deoxyribose* not "sugar, ribose or pentose"; *phosphate group* not "phosphate head"; *cytosine* not
"nitrogenous base" or "pyrimidine"; *anticodon* singular where one is meant, with plural rejected;
*proteasome hydrolyses peptide bonds into small peptides* with "amino acids" rejected; *allele* not
"gene" in a screening context; *the snRNPs of the spliceosome*, not the spliceosome as a whole, first
recognise the splice sites. `[SCHOOL]`

**9. Directionality is load-bearing and gets its own marks.** Fifty-seven distinct scheme lines in the
Book 2 corpus turn on the 3ʹ end: *"can only add deoxyribonucleotide to free 3ʹ-OH end of an existing
strand"*, *"needs 3ʹ OH to initiate"*, *"primers only add at 3ʹ"*. On sequence questions, missing the
prime symbol has been penalised half a mark on its own (TJC 2025 P2 Q5b:
*"−1/2 if apostrophe is missing"*). `[SCHOOL]`

**10. Command-word discipline, as it bites in this Book specifically.** *Describe* the process wants
the ordered steps and nothing about why. *Explain* wants the causal chain with each link separate.
*Outline* implies brevity and is the syllabus's own word for restricting to essentials, so a long
answer to an Outline is wasted time rather than extra marks. *Suggest* signals there is no recall
answer, which in Book 2 usually means an unfamiliar gene, an unfamiliar technique variant, or a novel
organism. `[OFFICIAL]` glossary meanings, `[SCHOOL]` marking behaviour.

**11. Essays are marked for breadth across the molecular machinery, then depth.** The examiner-stated
failure modes in the corpus name *"misinterpreting question scope, going too deep on one element
instead of breadth"* and *"including irrelevant content (describing DNA replication when the question
asked about RNA structure)"*. A Book 2 essay on the roles of RNA that spends half its length on
transcription has already capped itself. `[SCHOOL]`

## 5. High-Yield and Low-Yield Flags

### High yield: these get 15 to 20 drill questions, not the standard 10

1. **The five levels of gene expression control, tested as "which level is operating here"**
   (LO i). Chapter 2.4 is the largest examined chapter in the Book at 836 corpus questions, it is 35%
   figure-led, and it supplies more Section B essays than any other Book 2 file at 19. It also
   supplied a specimen MCQ (Q13) and an applied specimen MCQ in a stem-cell wrapper (Q8). The skill
   that pays is not listing the five levels. It is reading an observation and naming which level
   explains it, with the other four available to rule out.

2. **Sequence manipulation with correct directionality** (LOs a, c). Thirty-eight percent of chapter
   2.2's corpus questions, plus Specimen P1 Q9 as a two-cell row-match. Converting between
   non-template strand, template strand, mRNA codon and tRNA anticodon, with 5ʹ and 3ʹ labelled
   correctly, has to be mechanical. Half a mark has been deducted for a missing prime symbol.

3. **The prokaryote versus eukaryote versus virus genome comparison** (LO d). Fifty-nine of the 158
   main-bank Genome Organisation records, and it opened Specimen P3 Q1 as a 4-mark similarity-plus-
   differences pair. The LO itself hands you the six comparison axes. Drill it as a table you can
   reproduce point for point, and drill the **similarity** direction too, which is the half most
   candidates skip.

4. **PCR, gel electrophoresis and Southern blotting, each step with its purpose** (LO j). The largest
   corpus file at 1187 questions, 51% of them procedure-plus-purpose. PCR appeared on the specimen
   twice, as P1 Q14 (complete the flowchart) and as P3 Q1(c)(i) applied to degraded fossil DNA. The LO
   also explicitly demands the **advantages and limitations** of PCR, which is a separate node that
   procedural drilling misses.

5. **The mutation-to-disease causal chain, starting at the gene** (LOs k, l). Specimen P2 Q3(a) was a
   3-mark version of it, which fixes the expected chain length. Sickle cell is the LO's named example
   and has to be available cold. The gene-mutation versus chromosomal-aberration distinction is
   Specimen P2 Q3(d) tested word for word off LO (k).

6. **DNA packing, all five levels, with the right verb at each step** (LO d). The tightest lexical
   requirement anywhere in the Book, it appears in Section B essays about DNA condensation, and it
   feeds the euchromatin and heterochromatin argument that chapter 2.4 then needs.

7. **Property-led essay spines.** Given that Book 2 owns 25% of the corpus essay stems and that those
   stems are almost all property-led rather than process-led, the highest-return essay preparation is
   four cross-chapter spines: **stability and accuracy** (DNA structure, replication fidelity, repair,
   telomeres), **specificity and complementarity** (base pairing, active sites, TF-to-control-element
   binding, codon-to-anticodon, probe-to-target), **diversity of roles** (the three RNAs, plus
   alternative splicing yielding several proteins from one gene), and **regulation** (the five levels).

### Low yield, and the honest finding of this build

**LO (m), the bioethics of genetic maternal screening, is almost untested in the entire corpus.**
This is the same finding the Book 1 guide reported for Bioethics, and here it is sharper because
LO (m) is the only bioethics Learning Outcome in the whole of Core Idea 2, and grep confirms it is the
only occurrence of the word "bioethics" in the 9477 syllabus. Measured:

- The `Bioethics` bank holds **199 questions**. Of those, **1 (0.5%)** touches maternal or prenatal
  screening, and even that one is a PCR-and-gel technique question, not an ethics discussion.
- Stem-cell and cloning ethics account for 17% of the Bioethics file, and Human Genome Project ethics
  for 19%. **Neither has a 9477 Core Idea 2 Learning Outcome.** Stem-cell ethics belongs to the Core
  Idea 1 stem-cell LOs taught in Book 3, and the Human Genome Project has no LO at all.
- The `Gene_and_Chromosomal_Mutations` bank carries a concept heading literally called *"Bioethics of
  genetic screening for mutations"* with **zero questions filed under it**.
- A corpus-wide regex across all 27 topic files for amniocentesis, prenatal, chorionic villus, NIPT,
  maternal screening and screening-for-trisomy returned **16 records**, of which **15 are not about
  maternal screening at all**. Seven are the "globin gene family" MCQ, where "prenatal" is only a
  developmental-stage label on a graph axis. Four are Human Genome Project ethics, two are RFLP
  disease detection, one is HIV, and one is mutation timing in the cell cycle.
- **All four 9477 specimen papers contain zero bioethics questions.**
- The school's own chapter 2.7 is **three pages long** and contains no biology. It is a generic
  framework of four principles and a six-step decision sequence, borrowed from an external ethics
  primer.

**Conclusion.** Spend one short session on LO (m), no more. Learn the four principles (respect for
persons and autonomy, maximising benefits and minimising harms, justice, care) and the six-step
sequence, then rehearse them once against maternal screening for trisomy 21 with both sides and a
judgement. Do not build a cluster around it, do not drill it with ten questions, and do not use the
existing Bioethics bank as preparation for it, because 48 of its 53 main-bank records are stem-cell
ethics and only one record in the whole 199 is about screening. The correct posture is a rehearsed
4 to 6 mark answer held in reserve, not a studied topic.

**Two further low-yield flags, and these cost time rather than marks:**

- **Operons.** 255 of the 836 Control of Gene Expression corpus questions (**31%**) test the lac or trp
  operon, and the cross-corpus digest ranks it a top-recurrence topic at 7 of 15 deep-read papers.
  Under 9744 that was correct. Under 9477 it has **no Learning Outcome**, the word appears nowhere in
  the syllabus, and the school's own chapter 2.4 mentions operons exactly once, in a sentence saying
  eukaryotic genes are **not** organised into them. Skip these questions when drilling. They are the
  single largest time sink in the Book.
- **Recombinant DNA, cloning and sequencing.** 256 of 1187 Molecular Techniques questions (**22%**)
  are about vectors, plasmids, ligation and gene cloning, and a further 4% about sequencing and
  profiling. LO (j) names PCR, gel electrophoresis, and Southern blotting with nucleic acid
  hybridisation, and nothing else. Restriction enzymes and RFLP (20% of the file) survive only as the
  digestion step feeding a Southern blot, so keep those and drop the rest.

Taken together, roughly **a third of the raw Book 2 question corpus tests content that 9477 does not
examine.** Filtering the bank before drilling is worth more than adding questions to it.

**One node that is low frequency but should not be dropped:** the **advantages and limitations of
PCR** are written into LO (j)(i) as a required element, and the corpus files only 13 questions under
"PCR, what it can and cannot show" against 54 on the procedure. Low frequency, explicitly examinable,
cheap to learn.
