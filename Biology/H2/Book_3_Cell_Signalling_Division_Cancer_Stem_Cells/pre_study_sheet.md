# Pre-Study Sheet: Cell Signalling, Division, Cancer and Stem Cells

Layer 1 = main arguments. Layer 2 = sub-arguments. No detail, no examples, anchor points only.

Exam weighting: roughly 13 to 16% of the whole A-level on the 9477 specimen, and it arrives as figures. Data and figure interpretation is 33.5% of its question corpus, and 35.9% of its questions are figure-led.

---

## PART A: CELL SIGNALLING

### 1. Signalling exists to coordinate cells that never touch, and every answer runs three stages in order

- 1.1 The three stages: **ligand-receptor interaction**, then **signal transduction**, then **cellular response**, which the syllabus defines as a **change in gene expression**.
- 1.2 Reception is where **specificity** lives: a **ligand** binds a **complementary** binding site, forming a **ligand-receptor complex** and changing the receptor's **3D conformation**.
- 1.3 Two receptor types are examined, deliberately paired: the **G-protein linked receptor (GPLR)** and the **receptor tyrosine kinase (RTK)**.

### 2. Transduction is a phosphorylation cascade, and kinases with phosphatases make the switch

- 2.1 The signal passes along **relay proteins**, each activated by phosphorylation.
- 2.2 **Protein kinases** add phosphate and **protein phosphatases** remove it, so the pair works as a **molecular switch**.
- 2.3 A **second messenger** is a small, water-soluble, non-protein relay inside the cell. **Cyclic AMP** is the named one, and its **nature** carries marks as well as its **role**.
- 2.4 **Signal amplification** is why a few signal molecules give an enormous response, and it is what kinases and phosphatases are for.
- 2.5 **Amplification is not activation**, and telling the two apart on a pathway diagram is a real question, not a pedantic one.

### 3. Termination and specificity are separate outcomes, each with its own named answer

- 3.1 **Termination** returns every component to its inactive state, run by three named enzymes: **protein phosphatase**, the **intrinsic GTPase** and **phosphodiesterase**.
- 3.2 **Specificity of response** explains why two cells given the same signal do different things: the answer is the **combination of receiving, transducing and responding proteins** each cell carries.

### 4. Insulin and glucagon are the one application the syllabus wants

- 4.1 They are **antagonistic hormones** setting **blood glucose concentration**, and each works through a different receptor type:
    - **Insulin** acts through a **receptor tyrosine kinase**.
    - **Glucagon** acts through a **G-protein linked receptor**, and its target is the **liver** only, because muscle carries no glucagon receptor.

## PART B: CELL AND NUCLEAR DIVISION

### 1. The cell cycle is the frame, and mitosis and meiosis are compared on chromosome behaviour

- 1.1 The cycle is **interphase** (**G1, S, G2**), then **M phase**, then division of the cytoplasm, with **G0** the exit for cells that stop dividing.
- 1.2 **Mitosis** gives two nuclei genetically identical to each other and to the parent; **meiosis** halves the chromosome number and gives cells that are not.
- 1.3 The comparison always runs on **behaviour of chromosomes**, never on daughter-cell counts.

### 2. Four structures have to be described, and revision usually covers only the first

- 2.1 The four: **chromosomes**, **nuclear envelope**, **cell surface membrane** and **centrioles**.
- 2.2 Stages are **prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase**, once in mitosis and twice across **meiosis I** and **meiosis II**.
- 2.3 The **cell surface membrane** half is division of the cytoplasm, which splits by organism: a **cleavage furrow** in animals, a **cell plate** in plants.
- 2.4 The terminology block is where marks are lost: **chromatin** against **chromosome**, **homologous chromosomes** against **sister chromatids**, **centromere** against **kinetochore**, **centriole** against **centrosome**, and the three microtubule types, **astral**, **kinetochore** and **polar**.

### 3. Meiosis I is the reduction division, and one fact separates it from meiosis II

- 3.1 **Meiosis I reduces** the chromosome number; **meiosis II is equational**.
- 3.2 The separating fact: **centromeres do not divide at anaphase I**.
- 3.3 **Crossing over** happens between **non-sister chromatids** of **homologous chromosomes**, and **non-sister** is the word carrying the mark.
- 3.4 **Independent assortment** is the random orientation of each homologous pair at **metaphase I**, independent of every other pair.

### 4. Division matters for what it produces, and for what happens when it is not regulated

- 4.1 Meiosis gives **three** sources of variation, and the third is the forgotten one: **crossing over**, **independent assortment** and **random fertilisation**.
- 4.2 Mitosis gives **growth, repair and asexual reproduction**.
- 4.3 The cycle must be **tightly regulated**, because **dysregulation of checkpoints** gives **uncontrolled cell division and cancer**.

## PART C: MOLECULAR BASIS OF CANCER

### 1. Cancer is uncontrolled division, and "uncontrolled" has a precise meaning

- 1.1 The **rate of cell division exceeds the rate of cell death**. It does not mean the cells divide faster.
- 1.2 Every cancer answer is a **causal chain run forwards from the mutation**. Describing what the normal protein does instead is penalised as reverse framing.

### 2. Two classes of gene do the work, each with its own mutation type and named example

- 2.1 A **tumour suppressor gene** codes for a protein that **inhibits** uncontrolled division; a **proto-oncogene** codes for one that **stimulates** normal division.
- 2.2 **Loss of function** of a tumour suppressor needs **both copies** mutated; **gain in function** of a proto-oncogene needs only **one**.
- 2.3 The named examples are **p53** for loss of function and **ras** for gain in function, and both must be available cold.
- 2.4 **ras** sits immediately downstream of a **receptor tyrosine kinase**, so a mutated ras is a signalling fault as much as a genetic one.

### 3. Cancer is a multi-step process with exactly three named components

- 3.1 **Accumulation of mutations** in a **single cell lineage** within one lifetime, so what is inherited is the **predisposition**, never the cancer.
- 3.2 **Angiogenesis** is the growth of new blood vessels the tumour induces to supply itself.
- 3.3 **Metastasis** makes a tumour **malignant** rather than **benign**, and it counts only once there are **secondary tumours at distant sites**. Spread into neighbouring tissue alone is **invasion**.
- 3.4 Four causative factors, named exactly: **genetic**, **chemical carcinogens**, **ionising radiation** and **loss of immunity**.

## PART D: STEM CELLS

### 1. A stem cell is defined by two features, and the wording of the first is strict

- 1.1 **Long-term self-renewal** by mitosis while staying **unspecialised**. "Divides indefinitely" is rejected.
- 1.2 The capacity to **differentiate** into specialised cell types.
- 1.3 Differentiation is **differential gene expression**, which is why a stem-cell question often turns out to have a gene-regulation answer.
- 1.4 It is directed by **internal signals**, meaning the cell's own gene expression, and by **external signals** from neighbouring cells and the **microenvironment**.

### 2. There are three levels of potency, and the distinction between the first two is one clause

- 2.1 **Totipotent** (**zygotic stem cells**) make every cell of the body **and** the **extra-embryonic membranes**.
- 2.2 **Pluripotent** (**embryonic stem cells**) make all **three germ layers** but **not** the membranes.
- 2.3 **Multipotent** (**lymphoid and myeloid stem cells**) means many cell lines within one tissue type, which is why a blood stem cell is multipotent and not pluripotent.

### 3. The normal functions in a living organism are the half revision skips

- 3.1 **Embryonic** stem cells build the organism; **blood** stem cells replace short-lived blood cells.
- 3.2 The **haematopoietic** hierarchy splits into the **lymphoid** and **myeloid** lineages, and a myeloid progenitor cannot make lymphocytes. An answer must name the **precise lineage**, because "blood stem cell" is rejected as vague.
- 3.3 **Symmetric and asymmetric division** explains why the population never exhausts itself while still sending out differentiating cells, and the **stem-cell niche** decides which one happens.

## One-line mental map

**Cell signalling:** reception carries the specificity, a phosphorylation cascade with second messengers transduces and amplifies it, phosphatases and GTPases terminate it, the response is a change in gene expression, and insulin against glucagon is the worked case.

**Cell and nuclear division:** one cycle, four structures to describe, mitosis identical against meiosis halved, and meiosis supplies crossing over, independent assortment and random fertilisation as the three sources of variation.

**Cancer:** division outruns death because tumour suppressors lose function and proto-oncogenes gain it, and the disease builds by accumulated mutations, then angiogenesis, then metastasis.

**Stem cells:** self-renewal plus differentiation driven by differential gene expression, graded totipotent to pluripotent to multipotent, and sustained by symmetric and asymmetric division inside a niche.
