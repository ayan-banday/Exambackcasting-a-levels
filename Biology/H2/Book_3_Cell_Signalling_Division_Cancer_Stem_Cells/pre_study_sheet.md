## 3.1 Cell Signalling

1. **Cell signalling** is how cells communicate, and it exists because a multicellular organism must coordinate cells that never touch.
2. Every answer runs the outcome's three stages in order: **ligand-receptor interaction**, **signal transduction**, then **cellular response**, which the outcome defines as a **change in gene expression**.
3. Reception is where **specificity** lives: a **ligand** binds a **receptor**'s **binding site** to form a **ligand-receptor complex**, and the mark-bearing words are **complementary** and **3D conformation**.
4. The syllabus examines two receptor types and deliberately pairs them: the **G-protein linked receptor (GPLR)** and the **receptor tyrosine kinase (RTK)**.
5. Transduction is a **phosphorylation cascade** passed along **relay proteins**, and the enzymes that run it and reverse it, **protein kinases** and **protein phosphatases**, make a **molecular switch**.
6. A **second messenger** is a small, water-soluble, non-protein relay inside the cell, **cyclic AMP** is the named one, and LO 3(n) asks for **roles and nature**, so its properties carry marks too.
7. **Signal amplification** is why a handful of signal molecules produces an enormous response, and LO 3(o) frames it as what **kinases and phosphatases** are for.
8. **Amplification is not activation**, and telling the two apart on a pathway diagram is a real question, not a pedantic one.
9. **Signal termination** returns every component to its inactive state, and three named enzymes do it: **protein phosphatase**, the **intrinsic GTPase** and **phosphodiesterase**.
10. **Specificity of response** explains why two cells given the same signal do different things, and the answer is the **combination of receiving, transducing and responding proteins** each carries.
11. LO 3(p) is the only application the syllabus wants: **insulin** works through an **RTK** and **glucagon** through a **GPLR**, and as **antagonistic hormones** they set **blood glucose concentration**.

## 3.2 Cell and Nuclear Division

1. The **cell cycle** is **interphase** (**G1**, **S**, **G2**), then **M phase**, then division of the cytoplasm, with **G0** as the exit for cells that stop dividing.
2. **Mitosis** makes two nuclei genetically identical to each other and to the parent, **meiosis** halves the chromosome number and makes cells that are not, and the two are compared on **behaviour of chromosomes**, never daughter-cell counts.
3. LOs 2(n) and 2(s) each name **four** structures whose behaviour must be described, and revision usually covers only the first: **chromosomes**, **nuclear envelope**, **cell surface membrane** and **centrioles**.
4. The stages are **prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase**, once for mitosis and twice for **meiosis I** and **meiosis II**, and stage names are expected but the **sub-divisions of prophase are not**.
5. The terminology block is where most marks are lost: **chromatin** against **chromosome**, **homologous chromosomes** against **sister chromatids**, **centromere** against **kinetochore**, **centriole** against **centrosome**, and the three microtubule types, **astral**, **kinetochore** and **polar**.
6. The **cell surface membrane** half of the outcome is division of the cytoplasm, which splits on organism: a **cleavage furrow** in animals against a **cell plate** in plants.
7. **Meiosis I is the reduction division** and **meiosis II is the equational division**, and the one fact separating them is that **centromeres do not divide at anaphase I**.
8. **Crossing over** happens between **non-sister chromatids** of **homologous chromosomes**, and the word carrying the mark is **non-sister**.
9. **Independent assortment** is the random orientation of each homologous pair at **metaphase I**, independent of every other pair.
10. LO 2(t) names **three** sources of variation and the third is the one that gets forgotten: **crossing over**, **independent assortment** and **random fertilisation**.
11. LO 2(o) gives the significance of mitosis in its own words, **growth, repair and asexual reproduction**, and adds that the cycle must be **tightly regulated** because **dysregulation of checkpoints** gives **uncontrolled cell division and cancer**.

## 3.3 Molecular Basis of Cancer

1. Cancer is **uncontrolled cell division**, and uncontrolled means the **rate of cell division exceeds the rate of cell death**, not that the cells divide faster.
2. Two classes of gene do the work in LO 2(q): a **tumour suppressor gene** codes for a protein that **inhibits** uncontrolled division, a **proto-oncogene** for one that **stimulates** normal division.
3. The two mutation types come in the syllabus's own words: **loss of function** of a tumour suppressor, needing **both copies** mutated, against **gain in function** of a proto-oncogene, needing only **one**.
4. The outcome names **p53** and **ras**, so both must be available cold as the worked example of each mutation type.
5. Every cancer answer is a **causal chain run forwards from the mutation**, and describing what the normal protein does instead is penalised as reverse framing.
6. LO 2(r) makes cancer a **multi-step process** and names exactly three components: **accumulation of mutations**, **angiogenesis** and **metastasis**.
7. Those mutations accumulate in a **single cell lineage** inside one lifetime, so what gets inherited is the **predisposition**, never the cancer.
8. **Angiogenesis** is the growth of new blood vessels a tumour induces to supply itself.
9. **Metastasis** makes a tumour **malignant** rather than **benign**, and it only counts once **secondary tumours at distant sites** are named, since spread into neighbouring tissue alone is **invasion**.
10. LO 2(p) names exactly four causative factors: **genetic**, **chemical carcinogens**, **ionising radiation** and **loss of immunity**.
11. The bridge back to chapter 3.1 is that **ras** sits immediately downstream of a **receptor tyrosine kinase**, making a mutated ras a signalling fault as much as a genetic one.

## 3.4 Stem Cells

1. A **stem cell** is defined by two features and the outcome asks for exactly those two: **long-term self-renewal** by mitosis while staying unspecialised, which is the required phrase since "divides indefinitely" is rejected, and the capacity to **differentiate** into specialised cell types.
2. Differentiation is **differential gene expression**, which sits under everything else in the chapter and is why a stem-cell question can turn out to have a gene-regulation answer.
3. It is directed by **internal signals**, meaning the cell's own gene expression, and by **external signals** from neighbouring cells and from the **microenvironment**.
4. LO 1(t) names three levels of potency, each with its own example: **totipotency** (**zygotic stem cells**), **pluripotency** (**embryonic stem cells**) and **multipotency** (**lymphoid and myeloid stem cells**).
5. The distinction between the first two is one clause: **totipotent** cells make every cell of the body **and** the **extra-embryonic membranes**, **pluripotent** cells make all **three germ layers** but **not** the membranes.
6. **Multipotent** means many cell lines within one tissue type, which is why a blood stem cell is multipotent and not pluripotent.
7. The **haematopoietic** hierarchy splits into the two lineages the outcome names, **lymphoid** and **myeloid**, and an answer must give the **precise lineage**, since "blood stem cell" is rejected as too vague.
8. LO 1(u) asks for the **normal functions in a living organism**, the half revision skips because therapy is more interesting: **embryonic** stem cells build the organism, **blood** stem cells replace short-lived blood cells.
9. **Symmetric and asymmetric division** answers why the population never exhausts itself while still producing a stream of differentiating cells, and the **stem-cell niche** decides which happens.

## Scope corrections: what your notes teach that 9477 does not examine

All checked by literal search of the syllabus PDF, so these are safe to act on.

**Zero hits, and for these that settles it.** **Apoptosis**, **cyclin**, **CDK**, **telomerase**, **karyotype**, **induced pluripotent stem cells**, **therapeutic cloning**, **homeostasis**, **feedback** and **diabetes** appear nowhere in 9477. Your notes teach all ten.

**Zero hits as words, but still examined under other wording.** **Kinetochore**, **cytokinesis**, **bivalent**, **chiasma** and **independent assortment** are absent as named terms, but the biology behind them is not. Cytokinesis is examined as the **cell surface membrane** in LOs 2(n) and 2(s), independent assortment through LO 2(t), the rest through LO 2(s). Learn the biology, and expect not to be handed the word.

**Five specific rulings.**

1. **LO 2(o) says** knowledge that dysregulation of checkpoints causes uncontrolled cell division and cancer is required, but **details of the mechanism are not required**. That cuts chapter 3.3's cyclin, CDK and MPF material and the checkpoint boxes. Keep the fact, drop the machinery. Largest single cut in the Book.
2. **But LO 2(q) survives that cut** and names them explicitly: explain how the **loss of function** mutation of **tumour suppressor genes, including p53**, and **gain in function** mutation of **proto-oncogenes, including ras**, results in uncontrolled cell division. Cancer genetics is fully examinable. Do not over-cut.
3. **LO 2(s) says** the names of the main stages of meiosis are expected, **but not the sub-divisions of prophase**.
4. **Telomerase is cut separately.** LO 2(r) names only accumulation of mutations, angiogenesis and metastasis, so the fourth step your notes teach has no outcome.
5. **Bioethics is dropped entirely.** Do not read any of it in this Book.

**Why your notes overshoot, measured.** In the stem-cell question bank, **34.8%** of questions test iPSCs, SCNT, cloning and ethics with no syllabus backing. In the cell-signalling bank, **22.8%** test homeostasis, diabetes or excluded receptors. Filter before you drill.
