# Mark Schemes

### Question 1

**(a)** **During anaphase I and II, even though homologous chromosomes and sister chromatids are moving towards the poles respectively.**

**The genetic material still remains within the same cell, the nucleus is not formed yet and the cell has not divided into 2 individual cells.**

**So DNA content and chromosome number will remain unchanged / will remain constant.**

Note on the counting convention, since the figure plots both quantities per nucleus. The step in each trace is read at telophase, once the nuclei have re-formed, which is why the anaphases look flat. If you were instead counting centromeres, chromosome number would double at anaphase II the moment the centromeres divide and return to the daughter value at cytokinesis. Both conventions are accepted. Halving chromosome number and DNA content at the same stage is accepted under neither.

**(b)** **Peak at about 4 a.u. represents cells at G1 phase / after telophase.**

**Peak at about 8 a.u. represents cells at synthesis phase / interphase / after semi-conservative replication.**

**(c)** **Separation of homologous chromosomes during meiosis I / anaphase I results in nuclei having DNA content at peak of about 4 a.u. (from peak of about 8 a.u.).**

**Separation of sister chromatids during meiosis II / anaphase II results in nuclei having DNA content at peak of about 2 a.u.**

**(d)** **96 telomeres.**

**There are 11 tetrads / bivalents and 1 pair of unpaired chromosomes (accept any suitable number based on clarity of the diagram). Each chromosome consists of two sister chromatids, hence the total number of sister chromatids is 48. Both ends of each sister chromatid are flanked by telomeres, hence the total number of telomeres is 96.**

Gap check for this question. **B3-15** is the whole of it, run in three different currencies so the counters cannot be fused. Part (a) tests why neither counter moves at the anaphases, (b) and (c) test where the DNA counter does move and by how much, and (d) tests the chromosome-to-chromatid step directly, since 24 chromosomes give 48 chromatids and only then 96 telomeres. If you reach for the ploidy level anywhere in this question instead of counting centromeres and then chromatids, the gap stays ACTIVE.

### Question 2

The key is **A**.

Statement **1** is **correct**. **Peak X sits at 2C, the unreplicated diploid DNA content, so the cells in X are in G1, and X is by far the tallest peak, so most cells are in G1.** The reason it is the tallest is the general one: **on a snapshot of an asynchronous population the number of cells caught in a phase is proportional to how long that phase lasts, and G1 is the longest phase of the cycle.**

Statement **2** is **wrong**. **Area Z holds cells at 4C, which have completed replication and are in G2 or M, but there is no guarantee that every one of them completes nuclear division: a cell that fails a checkpoint is held there, and a cell may leave the cycle instead.** Reading a peak as a promise about what happens next is the error.

Statement **3** is **wrong**. **Area Y holds cells with a DNA content between 2C and 4C, so these are cells caught partway through replication, which is S phase, not anaphase.** A cell in anaphase has already finished replicating and therefore sits in the 4C peak with Z. Note also that the stem says the distribution is of cells **during interphase**, and anaphase is not interphase.

Gap check for this question. **B3-16** is statement 1, on a histogram you have not seen before, with the phase not named for you. **B3-15** is statement 3, because the only way to place a cell in anaphase on this axis is to remember that anaphase changes chromosome number and not DNA content, so anaphase cells still read 4C.

**Source:** VJC Prelim 2024 P1 Q11, stem, figure, all three statements and all four options exactly as the paper prints them, with the figure extracted from the question paper. The VJC answer table gives the letter **A** only, so the statement-by-statement reasoning is `[INFERRED]` and the marks were raised from the paper's 1 to 5 to force a justification of all three statements. This is the only multiple choice part in the drill, and it is here because gap B3-16 is about reading a histogram and so has to use one.
**Concepts fused:** peak height against phase duration, DNA content against chromosome number, 2C and 4C notation, G1, S and G2 read off a profile, checkpoint arrest and G0 as reasons a cell may not divide, why anaphase cells are not in the trough.

### Question 3

Six differences, point by point, mitosis against meiosis I.

| Basis of comparison | Mitosis | Meiosis I |

| Synapsis | **During prophase, no synapsis / no pairing of homologous chromosomes.** | **During prophase I, synapsis occurs / homologous chromosomes pair up to form bivalents.** |

The question as a whole is the decay check on the mitosis-against-meiosis comparison, which you produced well last time.

### Question 8

**(a)** **Change in sequence of centromeric DNA.**

**Shape of the mutated centromeric sequence is no longer complementary to the kinetochore protein complex, hence they cannot bind.**

**Microtubules / spindle fibres are unable to attach to the centromere via the kinetochore.**

**(b)** **Spindle fibres may not have formed during prophase I of meiosis.**

**Cytokinesis may not have occurred during meiosis, and therefore the number of chromosomes remains in the diploid state.**

Gap check for this question. Both parts are **B3-15** approached from the failure side, which is the hardest place to hold the two counters apart. If you find yourself reasoning in ploidy levels alone here, go back to the table in the mend doc.
