### Question 8

**(a)** Both.

**Totipotent stem cells, such as the zygote or zygotic stem cells,**

**have the potential to become any cell type to form an entire organism, including the extra-embryonic membranes.**

The paper **rejects "extra-embryonic cells"**. The word is **membranes**.

**(b)** Both.

**Similarity: both types of cell are able to proliferate indefinitely.**

**Difference: cancer cells do not have the ability to differentiate into almost all the different cell types, whereas embryonic stem cells are pluripotent cells that have the ability to differentiate into almost all types of cell except cells of the extra-embryonic membranes.**

**(c)** Maximum 2 from agreement and maximum 2 from disagreement, total 3.

Agreement. **Tumour cells contain different mutations, and variation in the population of tumour cells allows natural selection to operate. Tumour cells show characteristics which confer a selective advantage, at least one of: no longer needing normal growth inhibition signals, no longer requiring an external signal to divide, not undergoing apoptosis, or dividing continuously. This enables them to survive longer or divide more frequently than normal cells, and to pass mutated alleles of critical genes to progeny cells.**

Disagreement. **A population is at the organism level and not the cellular level. Only mutations in germline cells could pass mutated alleles down to offspring. There is a lack of a specific selection pressure in this context.**

Gap check. **B3-29** is parts (a) and (b), testing the same clause from opposite sides: totipotent **includes** the extra-embryonic membranes, pluripotent **excludes** them. That clause is the whole distinction and it is the one you dropped twice. The three germ layers, since you named them wrongly on the first pass, are **ectoderm, mesoderm, endoderm**; the blastocyst is the structure the inner cell mass sits in, not a germ layer.

**B3-26** is part (c). You gave good resistance mechanisms and then said *"I have no idea, I'm just being smart."* The frame is the answer: a tumour is **genetically heterogeneous**, so there is **variation**; a drug is a **selection pressure**; the resistant cells survive and take over. Note that the third disagreement point, **lack of a specific selection pressure**, is exactly what a chemotherapy drug supplies, which is why drug resistance is the clean case.

**B3-28**, since it does not have its own question: **myeloid gives erythrocytes, neutrophils, monocytes and platelets. Lymphoid gives B lymphocytes, T lymphocytes and natural killer cells.** You put neutrophils and monocytes in the lymphoid line and caught it a minute later. A progenitor restricted to one line is **multipotent**, and that restriction is what makes it multipotent rather than pluripotent.

**B3-25 and B3-27**, also without their own question, because the marks would not fit in an hour. **Cancer is multi-step and needs several mutations to accumulate in one cell lineage**, which is what the tumour-free survival graph was showing and what you left unresolved. Among those accumulated mutations are the ones that cause **loss of contact inhibition** (a normal cell stops dividing when it touches its neighbours) and **loss of anchorage dependence** (a normal cell will not divide unless attached to a surface). They are two different properties, and losing anchorage dependence is the one that lets a cell survive loose in the bloodstream.
