# Mark Schemes

### Question 1

Similarities, any of the following.

**Both receptor and enzyme have sites with specific 3D conformation which only molecules of complementary shape will fit into**, or put the other way, **both hormone and substrate bind to specific sites, the receptor site and the active site, on receptor and enzyme respectively.**

**Both show high specificity in their interactions.**

**Both receptor and enzyme can be reused, do not change 3D conformation after the interaction, and remain unchanged at the end of the interaction.**

**Both interactions require molecules to be chemically compatible.**

**Temporary weak bonds such as ionic bonds, hydrogen bonds and hydrophobic interactions (any 2) are formed between hormone and receptor and between enzyme and substrate during the interactions.**

Differences.

| feature | hormone and cell surface receptor interaction | substrate and enzyme interaction |
|---|---|---|
| enable reactions to proceed faster by lowering activation energy | **no** | **yes** |
| site for binding | **binding site** | **active site** |
| conformational changes in site for binding | **receptor changes shape upon binding of hormones** | **active site does not change shape upon binding of substrates (lock and key hypothesis)** |
| induced fit | **no** | **yes, as the substrate enters and binds to the active site, it induces a conformational change in the shape of the enzyme such that the substrate can fit even more snugly into the active site** |
| cofactors | **cofactors not required** | **cofactors may be required** |
| complex formed | **hormone-receptor complex** | **enzyme-substrate complex** |
| formation of product | **no changes to hormones after binding; hormones dissociate from receptor after activation of other proteins** | **changes to substrate after binding, products formed; substrate is broken down / converted to product after reaction** |
| function | **cell signalling** | **catalysing biological reactions** |

QWC: **clearly expressed using the correct terminology and communicated without ambiguity, with at least 1 similarity and 1 difference addressed**.

Gap check for this question. **B3-2 temporary weak bonds**, **B3-3 reused and unchanged**, **B3-4 binding site against active site**, **B3-5 lowering activation energy**, **B3-6 hormone unchanged where the substrate becomes product**. If any of the five is still missing, that gap stays ACTIVE.

### Question 2

**(a)** The key is **A**.

Row **A** is **correct**. **Amplification at 1 and 3 only, cellular response at 5 only.**

Row **B** is **wrong**. **Amplification is right at 1 and 3, but calling every stage a cellular response is not: stages 1 to 4 are transduction inside the rod cell, and stage 6 happens in the brain, not in this cell.**

Row **C** is **wrong**. **Stage 2 is not amplification, because 500 activated G proteins activate 500 phosphodiesterase molecules and the number of molecules does not increase. Stage 4 is not amplification either, because 10⁵ hydrolysed cyclic GMP molecules close only 250 channels, so the number falls. Stage 5 is the cellular response, not amplification, and stage 6 is outside the cell.**

Row **D** is **wrong**. **Stage 2 does not amplify, for the reason given above, and stage 1 does amplify, since one activated rhodopsin activates 500 G proteins. Stage 6 is the signal reaching the brain, not a response of this cell.**

The rule being applied. **Amplification occurs only where the number of molecules increases at that step.** **Activation is one molecule switching one molecule on, and the count holds.**
