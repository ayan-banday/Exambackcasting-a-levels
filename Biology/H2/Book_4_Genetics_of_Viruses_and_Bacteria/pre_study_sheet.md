## 4.1 The Genetics of Viruses

1. A virus is an **obligate intracellular parasite**, and the whole chapter turns on the difference between the inactive **virion** outside a host cell and the active virus inside one.
2. LO 1(f) asks you to **discuss** how viruses challenge the **cell theory**, so the answer is organised against the theory's **three statements** rather than written as a list of things viruses do.
3. Every virus has a **genome** and a **capsid** made of **capsomeres**, only **enveloped viruses** add an **envelope** carrying **viral glycoproteins**, and LO 1(e) asks you both to describe that split and to **interpret drawings and photographs** of it.
4. Viral genomes are the most varied in biology, and LO 2(d) names the axes you describe any of them on: **DNA or RNA**, **single or double stranded**, **linear or circular**, **number of nucleotides**, **packing**, and **introns present or absent**.
5. An RNA genome is either **positive sense** or **negative sense**, and that single label is what decides which **polymerase** the virus has to carry inside itself.
6. LO 2(e) names three reproductive cycles and one worked example of each: **bacteriophages** running the **lytic** and **lysogenic** cycles with **lambda phage**, **enveloped viruses** with **influenza**, and **retroviruses** with **HIV**.
7. All three cycles are described against the same five-slot frame, **adsorption**, **penetration**, **synthesis and replication**, **assembly** and **release**, so what changes between viruses is what fills the slots.
8. The **lytic** and **lysogenic** cycles are two options open to the same kind of virus, and the words that separate them are **virulent phage** against **temperate phage**, plus the **prophage** on the lysogenic side.
9. **HIV** is the syllabus's named **retrovirus**, and the terms that define it are **reverse transcriptase**, **reverse transcription** and the integrated **provirus**.
10. **Influenza** is the syllabus's named enveloped virus, and its two surface proteins, **haemagglutinin** and **neuraminidase**, are also the proteins LO 2(f) then makes vary.
11. LO 2(f) names exactly two mechanisms of viral variation, **antigenic shift**, which is **reassortment** between two or more strains, and **antigenic drift**, which is **accumulated mutation** within one strain, and both exist for **immune evasion**.

## 4.2 The Genetics of Bacteria

1. LO 1(d) fixes what a **typical bacterial cell** is and lists the five features itself: **small and unicellular**, **peptidoglycan cell wall**, **circular DNA**, **70S ribosomes**, and **lack of membrane-bound organelles**.
2. The bacterial genome sits in a **nucleoid** on a single **circular chromosome**, with **plasmids** alongside it, and LO 2(d) tests all of that as a comparison against the **eukaryotic** genome.
3. LO 2(d) includes **packing of DNA** in its own words, which is why **supercoiling** and **histone-like proteins** are examinable even though neither term appears anywhere in the syllabus.
4. **Binary fission** is the whole of prokaryotic **asexual reproduction**, and LO 2(g) asks only that you **outline the mechanism**, which sets the expected length.
5. The defining limitation of binary fission is that it makes **genetically identical** daughter cells and therefore **cannot generate variation**, and the rest of the outcome exists to answer that.
6. Variation comes instead from **horizontal gene transfer**, and LO 2(g) names exactly three routes: **transformation**, **transduction** and **conjugation**.
7. **Transformation** is the uptake of **naked DNA** from the surroundings by a **competent** cell.
8. **Transduction** is transfer carried by a **bacteriophage**, and it comes in two named forms, **generalised** and **specialised**, which is the point where this chapter joins the lytic and lysogenic cycles from 4.1.
9. **Conjugation** is transfer through direct contact between an **F+** and an **F-** cell, and LO 2(g) restricts it to the role of the **F plasmid** and cuts **Hfr** by name.
10. Transferred DNA only becomes heritable through **homologous recombination**, which transformation and transduction both need and conjugation does not.
11. The point of all three is the single thing LO 2(g) says they do, **give rise to variation in prokaryotic genomes**, and **antibiotic resistance** is the case the exam keeps using to test it.

## Errors in your notes

1. **Chapter 4.1 page 40 says antigenic shift can happen without any genetic change.** It cannot. The same chapter defines shift on page 38 as **reassortment** and its table on page 43 says **two or more viral strains are involved**. Page 40's modes 2 and 3 are cross-species transmission of an unchanged strain. This sits directly on the distinction LO 2(f) exists to test.
2. **Chapter 4.1 page 27 says the endocytic vesicle fuses with a lysosome.** The endosome acidifies, and the chapter's own page 26 entry on **M2** gives the right account.
3. **Chapter 4.1 page 36 blames the high RNA-virus mutation rate on the host cell.** It is the **viral polymerase** that has no proofreading, which is what the schemes credit.
4. **Chapter 4.1 page 21 gives lambda a base plate**, copied from the T4 step, when its own structure list names only a head and a single non-contractile tail fibre.
5. **Chapter 4.1 pages 28 and 34 say influenza has capsomeres.** Page 26 correctly says **nucleoprotein** and **nucleocapsid**.
6. **Chapter 4.2 page 23 names two different genes in one explanation**, with the text tracking the **bio** gene and a figure label saying **gal**.
7. **Chapter 4.2 page 25 defines the F factor as a plasmid or as a segment integrated into the chromosome.** That second half is Hfr, which the syllabus cuts by name.
8. **Neither chapter mentions the rolling circle mechanism or the origin of transfer**, and at least eight prelim schemes award marks for them in conjugation answers. That is a missing mark rather than a wrong one, so supply it.
