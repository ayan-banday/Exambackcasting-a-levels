## A. Influenza is the enveloped case, and its genome explains everything else about it

**A1. The genome is eight separate pieces of negative-sense single-stranded RNA.** The human influenza
virus is an enveloped virus in which the **viral negative sense single-stranded RNA is present in the
virion in eight separate pieces**. Because the strand is negative sense, it **must be used as a template
for the synthesis of positive-sense single-stranded RNA, which serves as mRNA for translation**. Two
consequences run through the rest of this sheet: the virus **has to carry its own polymerase**, and
because the genome is **segmented**, it can **reassort**, which is section G.

**A2. Influenza has a nucleocapsid, not a shell of capsomeres.** **Nucleoprotein (NP) associates with
the viral nucleic acid to form the nucleocapsid.** Say nucleoprotein and nucleocapsid for influenza.
The chapter itself slips twice, on pages 28 and 34, and says capsomeres, which belong to **helical and
icosahedral capsids** rather than to a ribonucleoprotein.

**A3. The envelope carries two named glycoproteins, and each does two jobs.** The envelope is a
**phospholipid bilayer obtained from the host upon budding**. **Haemagglutinin (HA) binds to sialic
acid containing receptors** and so **attaches the virus to the receptor on the host cell membrane**.
**Neuraminidase (NA) hydrolyses mucus, allowing the virus to enter cells of the respiratory tract**, and
it **facilitates budding by cleaving sialic acid containing receptors**. Write both names out in full.
A scheme awards **Haemagglutinin** and **Neuraminidase** and then explicitly rejects the abbreviations,
which is awkward because the chapter uses HA and NA everywhere after introducing them once.

**A4. The virion carries its own replicase, and the reason is a stock explain mark.** **PB1, PB2 and PA
form the RNA-dependent RNA polymerase, the replicase, responsible for replication and transcription**,
and it is **already included as part of the virion**. It has to be, because **animal cells cannot make
RNA using an RNA template**. No host enzyme can read an RNA template, so a negative-sense RNA virus that
arrived without its own polymerase could not even begin. Beyond that, **M2 acts as an ion channel to
lower or maintain the pH of the endosome in the host cell**; the rest of the protein inventory has no
outcome behind it.

## B. The influenza reproductive cycle, filled into the five slots

**B1. Adsorption.** **Haemagglutinin molecules on the viral membrane bind to sialic acid containing
receptors on the membrane of the host cell.**

**B2. Penetration is endocytosis followed by an acid-triggered fusion.** **The virus is taken in by
receptor-mediated endocytosis, forming an endocytic vesicle within the host cell called an endosome.**
**The endosome then becomes acidified**, which **triggers conformational changes in the HA protein**,
which **causes the viral envelope and the endosome membrane to fuse**, **releasing the eight viral
segments directly into the host cell cytoplasm**. **The viral RNAs are then transported into the
nucleus.** Chapter 4.1 page 27 attributes the pH drop to fusion with a lysosome; that is wrong and it
contradicts the chapter's own M2 entry, so write acidification of the endosome and not lysosomal fusion.

**B3. Synthesis and replication, where the positive-sense copy does two separate jobs.** **Viral
replicase copies the negative-sense RNA template into complementary positive-sense RNAs.** Those
positive-sense strands are then used twice over, and both uses are marked:

- **As templates for synthesis of full-length negative-sense viral RNAs by viral replicase**, which
  are **packaged into new viral particles as their nucleic acid**.
- **As mRNA, translated in the cytoplasm by host protein synthesis machinery** to give the viral
  proteins.

The division of labour by ribosome type is worth a mark on its own. **Free ribosomes synthesise enzymes
and matrix proteins**, while the **surface glycoproteins are synthesised by rER-bound ribosomes**,
**transported to the Golgi apparatus for glycosylation**, and then **incorporated into the host cell
membrane** via vesicles which fuse with it.

**B4. Assembly.** Assembly is complete when the **eight negative-sense viral RNAs associated with NP,
together with enzymes such as the viral replicase, are packaged**. **Acquisition of the glycoprotein
studded membrane envelope occurs during release**, not before it, which is why the envelope is always
the host's.

**B5. Release is budding, and neuraminidase is what finishes it.** The virus is **released from the host
cell by budding, acquiring the host cell's lipid bilayer as its envelope**, and the host membrane
carrying HA, NA and M2 buds off with the virion components. Then a problem: HA on the new envelope and
sialic-acid containing receptors on the host membrane bring the two together, so **the new viral
particle remains attached to the host cell**. **Neuraminidase releases the virus by cleaving sialic acid
residues on the cellular receptor** that bind the newly formed virions to the cell. The exit word is
**budding**, and a scheme explicitly rejects exocytosis for an enveloped virus that takes the cell
surface membrane on its way out.

## C. HIV is the retrovirus, and the definition carries the marks

**C1. A retrovirus is defined by its route, not by having RNA.** **Retroviruses are enveloped RNA
viruses which replicate by means of a DNA intermediate synthesised by the enzyme reverse
transcriptase.** One scheme in the corpus rejects the answer that HIV simply has RNA and demands the
strict version: **single-stranded positive-sense RNA, reverse transcribed to double-stranded DNA,
integrated as a provirus**. Write the strict version, because it scores under the lenient scheme as
well.

**C2. The structure is two copies of the genome, three enzymes and two glycoproteins.** The genome is
**two identical single-stranded RNA molecules**, surrounded by a **capsid**, with **matrix protein
forming a second layer enclosing the capsid**, inside a **phospholipid bilayer obtained from the host
upon budding**. The surface glycoproteins are **gp120**, which **binds to CD4 receptors on white blood
cells** such as **macrophages and T helper cells**, and **gp41**, which **aids in the fusion of the HIV
envelope and the host cell membrane**. The enzymes are **reverse transcriptase**, two molecules each
associated with one RNA, **to reverse transcribe viral RNA into DNA**; **integrase**, which
**facilitates incorporation of double-stranded DNA into the host cell's genome**; and **protease**,
which **cleaves viral polypeptide into functional proteins during viral maturation**. Each is carried in
the virion because no host enzyme performs its job.

## D. The HIV reproductive cycle, filled into the same five slots

**D1. Adsorption.** **Glycoprotein gp120 on the surface of HIV binds to the CD4 receptor**, a
cell-surface receptor **found on T helper cells and macrophages** of the host immune system.

**D2. Penetration needs a second receptor before fusion can happen.** On binding to CD4, **gp120
undergoes a conformational change**, allowing it to bind a **co-receptor, CXCR4 on T helper cells or
CCR5 on macrophages**. **gp41 pulls the virus closer to the host cell**, and the co-receptor
**facilitates entry of the gp120-CD4 complex** through the host cell membrane. **The HIV envelope fuses
with the host cell membrane, releasing the viral nucleic acid and enzymes into the host cell.** Note the
difference from influenza: HIV fuses **at the cell surface membrane**, influenza fuses **with the
endosome membrane** after being taken in.

**D3. Reverse transcription runs in two passes with an RNA breakdown between them.** **Reverse
transcriptase first reverse transcribes the viral RNA into a complementary DNA strand.** The **RNA
strand of the DNA-RNA hybrid is broken down by the ribonuclease H component** of the reverse
transcriptase. The **newly synthesised DNA strand is then used as a template for synthesis of the other
complementary DNA strand**, forming a **double-stranded DNA molecule**. Say template and synthesis,
never conversion: a scheme prints an explicit reject for the idea that RNA is turned into or converted
into DNA.

**D4. Integration creates the provirus, and the provirus is what latency means.** The double-stranded
DNA **passes through the nuclear pore into the host nucleus**, where **integrase catalyses the
integration of the viral DNA into the genetic material of the host**. This **newly integrated viral DNA
is called a provirus**, which **may remain in a latent, inactive state for several years**, producing
few or no copies of HIV. The **latent phase ends when the host cell is stimulated in an immune
response**.

**D5. Transcription supplies both products, then protease finishes the job after release.** When the
host cell receives a signal, the **proviral DNA is transcribed by the host RNA polymerase into RNA which
serves as nucleic acid for new virions**, and it is also **transcribed into viral mRNA, translated to
produce a single long chain of HIV proteins which is later cleaved**. Assembly occurs when the **two
single-stranded RNAs, with reverse transcriptase, integrase and protease, are surrounded by assembled
capsid** near the host cell membrane. The **immature HIV buds off from the host cell, surrounded by host
membrane**, and then **maturation occurs when HIV protease cleaves the long chains into smaller
functional proteins**. Maturation happens **after** budding, which is why an immature particle exists at
all.

## E. HIV against influenza, held side by side

This is the most efficient revision object in the Book. Specimen Paper 1 Question 11 is exactly this
comparison, and the same table answers every state-two-differences part in the bank. Compare **point to
point on the same feature**, because a scheme rejects yes-and-no comparisons such as fusion against no
fusion.

| Feature | **Influenza** | **HIV** |
|---|---|---|
| Genome | **eight segments of negative-sense single-stranded RNA** | **two identical single-stranded RNA molecules**, positive sense |
| Coat around the genome | **nucleoprotein forming a nucleocapsid** | **capsid**, with a **matrix** layer outside it |
| Attachment | **HA to sialic acid containing receptors** | **gp120 to CD4 receptors** |
| Second binding step | none | **co-receptor CXCR4 or CCR5** after a **conformational change in gp120** |
| Entry | **receptor-mediated endocytosis**, then **fusion with the endosome membrane** after acidification | **fusion of the envelope with the host cell membrane** at the surface |
| Enzyme carried in | **RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (replicase)** | **reverse transcriptase**, plus **integrase** and **protease** |
| DNA stage | **none at any point** | **double-stranded DNA made by reverse transcription** |
| Integration into host genome | **never** | **provirus, integrated by integrase** |
| Where the genome is copied | **nucleus**, by viral replicase | **host genome**, transcribed by **host RNA polymerase** |
| Latency | none | **latent for several years** |
| Release | **budding**, then **NA cleaves sialic acid** to free the virion | **budding**, then **protease cleaves polypeptides** to mature the virion |

**The two shared slots are penetration and assembly.** Everything else on that list differs, which is
why generalising a DNA stage, integration or reverse transcription from HIV to influenza is the trap
the specimen question is built on.

## F. Variation in viral genomes arises three ways

**F1. Mutation, and the real reason viral genomes mutate fast.** **Eukaryotic genomes undergo rigorous
proofreading**, giving very low error rates, and viruses do not. The cause is a property of the enzyme:
**viral RNA-dependent RNA polymerases and reverse transcriptases have no proofreading exonuclease
activity of their own**, so **errors are introduced at every replication cycle and are not corrected**.
The chapter states this correctly in one line, that **viral polymerases are also prone to errors**, and
incorrectly in two others, which section L records. Retroviruses such as HIV encode on average **one
point mutation for every replication cycle**, because the reverse transcriptase is **unable to correct
nucleotide misincorporation errors**. Mutation is what **antigenic drift** is made of.

**F2. Recombination is named by the chapter and then dropped, so supply it yourself.** Viruses may
**undergo recombination with the genome of another strain**, so that the genomes **exchange genetic
information** and give **genomes with new combinations of alleles**, and the **progeny contain some
genes from both original strains**. It appears once on page 36, never returns, and has no row in the
page 43 table.

**F3. Reassortment needs two strains in one cell and a segmented genome.** A host cell may be **infected
with two viral strains which introduce two sets of genetic material** into it. During formation of the
progeny, **different segments of the viral genome may be packaged into the progeny virus**. That gives a
**sudden and drastic change in the viral genome**, which is **antigenic shift**.

## G. Antigenic shift is reassortment, and two or more strains are not optional

**G1. The definition.** **Antigenic shift is a sudden change in the antigenicity of a virus owing to
reassortment of the segmented virus genome with another genome of a different antigenic type.**
**Antigenicity** itself is **the capacity of an antigen to induce an immune response in a host**. In
influenza, shift produces **a new haemagglutinin, or a new haemagglutinin and a new neuraminidase, in
the viruses that infect humans**.

**G2. Influenza is susceptible to shift specifically because its genome is in eight pieces.** **The
influenza virus, which contains eight single-stranded RNAs, is susceptible to reassortment since it can
easily reshuffle its genome during packaging of the new virion.** A virus with a single unsegmented
genome cannot reassort at all, so this is the mechanical precondition, not a detail.

**G3. The mixing vessel is where the two strains meet.** Different viruses of different origins may
**infect a pig, which provides the opportunity for the viruses to reassort**, forming a new virus which
contains **different surface antigens from both the avian and human influenza strains**. The output is
**viruses with new combinations of haemagglutinin and neuraminidase**. Because the new strain has not
been circulating in the population, **there is unlikely to be any immunity against it**, so it is
**easily transmissible**.

**G4. Your chapter contradicts itself here, and this is the error to be most careful about.** Page 40
lists three modes of inter-species transmission and calls all three antigenic shift, but modes 2 and 3
say the bird strain jumps to humans **without undergoing genetic change**. Under the chapter's own
page 38 definition, and its own page 43 table, which states that **two or more viral strains are
involved**, a jump with no genetic change is not shift at all. It is **direct cross-species transmission
of an unchanged strain**. **Only mode 1, where avian and human strains co-infect an intermediate host
and reassort, is antigenic shift.** Answer shift as reassortment every time, and treat modes 2 and 3 as
routes of transmission rather than as shift.

## H. Antigenic drift is mutation accumulation inside one strain

**H1. The definition.** **Antigenic drift is the gradual accumulation of minor mutations in the genes of
influenza viruses that results in altered antigenicity.** It **happens continually over time as the
virus replicates**, and it **produces viruses that are closely related to each other and usually share
the same antigenic properties**.

**H2. The mutations are in the two surface protein genes.** Drift arises when viruses **undergo
continuous, subtle antigenic changes due to accumulation of mutations to the haemagglutinin and
neuraminidase genes**. Those are the same two proteins shift reshuffles, which is why the two mechanisms
look similar in their effect and are entirely different in their cause.

**H3. Call it antigenic drift, never genetic drift.** A scheme prints an explicit reject for genetic
drift, which is a population-genetics term from a different Core Idea and a live confusion under exam
pressure.

## I. Shift against drift, on the six axes the school's own table uses

| | **Antigenic shift** | **Antigenic drift** |
|---|---|---|
| **Number of viruses** | **two or more viral strains are involved** | **only one viral strain is involved** |
| **Mechanism for change** | **reshuffling of genome between different strains**, giving a **dramatic alteration of the type of haemagglutinin or neuraminidase on progeny virus** | **accumulation of point mutations in the gene of the surface antigen**, giving a **minor alteration of the 3D conformation of haemagglutinin or neuraminidase** |
| **Nature of change** | **abrupt and major change in the genome of the virus** | **gradual accumulation of minor point mutations in the genome of the virus** |
| **Rate of occurrence** | **occasionally occurs, giving rise to pandemics** | **regularly occurring, giving rise to seasonal epidemics** |
| **Effect of host immunity** | **population has no immunity to the novel combination of surface proteins**, and **no drugs or vaccines are present to treat the virus** | **a proportion of the population may still have pre-existing immunity to the modified surface proteins**, and **antiviral drugs and seasonal vaccines are available** |
| **Cross-species transmission** | **may result in a progeny virus which can infect a new species** | **virus only infects individuals of the same species** |

**The mark-bearing distinction is the top two rows, not the magnitude.** Two or more strains against
one strain, and reassortment against point mutation. Big change against small change is a consequence of
those, and defining the pair that way round scores nothing.

---

## J. Why either mechanism matters, as one causal chain

**J1. The chain from base sequence to immune escape.** A change in the genome gives **surface proteins
with different 3D conformations**, so **antibodies are no longer complementary to them** and **will not
recognise and bind to them**. That lets the virus **evade detection by the host immune system**.
**Vaccines become ineffective, since the antibodies generated would not be able to bind specifically to
the new surface antigen owing to its new and different 3D conformation.** Run it as a chain and it is
worth several marks; state only the endpoint and it is worth one.

**J2. Magnitude decides pandemic against seasonal epidemic.** The **major change in surface proteins**
during a shift leaves **a large number of individuals susceptible to the new virus**, giving an epidemic
which could spread across the globe, a **pandemic**. The **relatively gradual and minor change** during
drift allows **some people to be unaffected due to pre-existing immunity**, so it results in only a
**seasonal epidemic**.

**J3. Genetic change alone does not cause an outbreak.** An examiner comment on exactly this point is
blunt that **antigenic shift and antigenic drift alone do not lead to outbreaks**. An outbreak answer
needs the genetics **plus a contributing factor**: **environmental change such as deforestation**,
**human behaviour and movement such as travel and urbanisation**, **economic and trade practices such as
wildlife trading**, or **public health capacity**. Antibodies, vaccines and immunity themselves belong
to Extension Topic A, so use them as the consequence and do not build the answer out of them here.

## L. Errors in your notes. Do not encode these.

1. **Page 40 says antigenic shift can occur without genetic change. It cannot.** Modes 2 and 3 describe a
   bird strain jumping to humans **without undergoing genetic change**, and call it shift. The same
   chapter defines shift on page 38 as **reassortment** and states on page 43 that **two or more viral
   strains are involved**. This is the most dangerous line in Book 4 because it sits on the exact
   distinction LO 2(f) tests. **Shift is reassortment, always, and always needs two or more strains
   co-infecting one host.**
2. **Page 27 says the endocytic vesicle fuses with an acidic lysosome.** The **endosome becomes
   acidified**; fusion with a lysosome would deliver the virion to hydrolases and destroy it. The
   chapter's own page 26 entry, that **M2 acts as an ion channel to lower or maintain the pH of the
   endosome**, is the account to keep.
3. **Page 36 blames the high RNA-virus mutation rate on the host cell**, saying there are no
   proofreading mechanisms in the host. Page 41 half-corrects it by blaming the lack of a complementary
   strand. Neither is right. **The viral polymerase has no proofreading exonuclease activity of its
   own**, which is what the schemes credit. Page 41 also says DNA replication where influenza replicates
   RNA.
4. **Pages 28 and 34 give influenza and HIV capsomere proteins.** Both sentences are boilerplate reused
   from the general template on page 16. **Influenza has nucleoprotein and a nucleocapsid**, as page 26
   states correctly.
5. **The chapter says negative-sense RNA is converted to positive sense, and converted back again.**
   Nothing is converted. **A complementary strand is synthesised and the original strand survives.**
   Chapter 4.2's version of the same paragraph gets it right, and a scheme rejects the conversion verb on
   the HIV side.
6. **Recombination is introduced as one of three mechanisms and then never used again.** No definition
   in operational terms, no row in the comparison table. Section F2 above supplies it.
7. **Page 38 prints a garbled definition sentence**, reading new haemagglutinin and/or new haemagglutinin
   and neuraminidase, where the intended sense is **a new haemagglutinin, or a new haemagglutinin and a
   new neuraminidase**.
8. **Both spellings of haemagglutinin appear**, twice as haemagglutinin and nine times as hemagglutinin.
   Use the British spelling in full, and never abbreviate to HA or NA on a question that asks you to
   state or name.
