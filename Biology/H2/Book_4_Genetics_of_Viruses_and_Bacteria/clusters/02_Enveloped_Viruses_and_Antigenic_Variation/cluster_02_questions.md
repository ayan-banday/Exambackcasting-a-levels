**Cumulative over Cluster 1 and Books 1, 2 and 3.** Question 4(c) needs the phage exit route from

**The one line in your own notes that will cost you marks.** Chapter 4.1 page 40 says antigenic shift
can occur *without undergoing genetic change*. It cannot. The same chapter defines shift on page 38 as
**reassortment** and states on page 43 that **two or more viral strains are involved**. Questions 6(b),
6(d) and 6(e) are built on exactly that distinction, and the mark schemes attached to them state the
correction. A bird strain jumping straight to humans with no genetic change is **direct cross-species
transmission**, not antigenic shift.

**What else the marks reward.** Write **haemagglutinin** and **neuraminidase** in full, because a
scheme awards the full names and prints a reject for HA and NA. Say **budding**, not exocytosis, for
influenza and HIV. Say **template and synthesis**, never conversion. Write influenza's coat as
**nucleoprotein forming a nucleocapsid**, not capsomeres. Say **antigenic drift**, never genetic drift.
Give the shift-against-drift distinction as **two or more strains against one strain** and
**reassortment against point mutation**, not as big change against small change.

### Question 2

**(a)** *Describe the reproductive cycle of the influenza virus and explain how new strains of the virus may arise as a result of mutation.* **[13]**

**Adsorption**

**1.** **The haemagglutinin glycoproteins on the viral envelope recognise and bind to sialic-acid containing receptors on the host cell plasma membrane.**

**Penetration and uncoating**

**2.** **The influenza virus enters the host cell by receptor-mediated endocytosis,**

**3.** **whereby the host cell membrane invaginates and pinches off, engulfing the virus in an endocytic vesicle.**

**4.** **Acidification causes the viral envelope to fuse with the endocytic vesicle membrane,**

**5.** **releasing the nucleocapsid into the cytoplasm of the cell.**

**6.** **The capsid is then degraded by cellular enzymes, releasing the viral nucleic acid.**

**Replication**

**7.** **The viral genome functions as the template for synthesis of a complementary RNA strand,**

**8.** **using the viral RNA-dependent RNA polymerase and host RNA nucleotides in the nucleus.**

**9.** **This complementary RNA acts as a template for the synthesis of new copies of viral RNA, that is the genome,**

**10.** **and functions as mRNA which undergoes translation to produce viral proteins, for example capsid proteins and glycoproteins for the viral envelope.**

**Maturation**

**11.** **The glycoproteins synthesised by ribosomes on the ER are transported to the host cell's plasma membrane via vesicles and incorporated into the plasma membrane.**

**12.** **The capsid is assembled around the viral RNA genome and the RNA-dependent RNA polymerase.**

**Release**

**13.** **The new influenza viruses bud off from the host cell's plasma membrane, so the new viruses acquire their lipid bilayer from the plasma membrane of the host cell, with viral proteins and glycoproteins embedded.**

**14.** **Neuraminidase catalyses the cleavage of sialic acid residues from haemagglutinin, facilitating the release of the newly replicated viruses for the next round of infection.**

**15.** Maximum 11 marks for points 1 to 14.

**Antigenic drift**

**16.** **Point mutations in the haemagglutinin gene in influenza virus occur during the replication of viral RNA.**

**17.** **Accumulation of mutations results in slight changes to the shape of haemagglutinin,**

**18.** **leading to antigenic drift.**

**19.** **The shape of the new haemagglutinin is now complementary to other membrane receptors found on new types of host cells.**

**20.** Quality of written communication: **good spread of knowledge communicated without ambiguity, to include at least 1 marking point from each of the 2 sections, the reproductive cycle of the virus in points 1 to 14 and antigenic drift in points 16 to 19.**

### Question 4

**(a)** *How many of these features also occur in the reproductive cycle of the influenza virus?* **[1]**

**1.** The key is **A, two**.

**(b)** *Explain your answer, saying for each of the five listed features whether it also occurs in the reproductive cycle of the influenza virus.* **[4]** [INFERRED: the 9477 specimen papers ship without a mark scheme; the per-feature reasoning is cross-checked against NYJC Prelim 2022 P2 Q5(d) and MI Prelim 2018 P2 Q2(b), whose keys contrast the two cycles on the same axes]

**1.** **Penetration occurs in both.** Both viruses enter a host cell, though by different routes: **influenza by receptor-mediated endocytosis followed by fusion with the endosome membrane, HIV by fusion of its envelope with the host cell surface membrane at the surface.**

**2.** **Integration with host DNA does not occur in influenza.** **Influenza never makes DNA and its genome is never integrated**; integration is done by **HIV integrase** and produces the provirus.

**3.** **Formation of cDNA does not occur in influenza.** **Influenza carries no reverse transcriptase**, so **no DNA intermediate is made at any point in its cycle.**

**4.** **Transcription of viral DNA does not occur in influenza.** There is **no viral DNA in an influenza-infected cell to transcribe**; the influenza genome is copied by the **viral RNA-dependent RNA polymerase**, and it is the **HIV provirus that is transcribed by host RNA polymerase.**

**5.** **Assembly of new virus particles occurs in both.** Both viruses **package their genome with their associated proteins and enzymes before leaving the cell.**

**6.** So **the two shared features are penetration and assembly**, which is why the key is A.

**(c)** *Explain why enveloped viruses such as HIV will not exit their host cells the same way as the T4 bacteriophage.* **[2]**

**1.** **Enveloped viruses exit host cells through budding.** **Reject: exocytosis.**

**2.** **So as to obtain their viral envelope from the host cell surface membrane.**

**3.** **T4 bacteriophages are not enveloped viruses, hence they exit the host cell through lysis.**

**Source:** 9477 Specimen Paper 1 Question 11 in full, verbatim from the official question paper (a); the follow-on wording in (b) is the drill's, with the per-feature reasoning cross-checked against the NYJC Prelim 2022 P2 Q5(d) and MI Prelim 2018 P2 Q2(b) keys, which contrast the two cycles feature by feature; NYJC Prelim 2025 P2 Q8(c), stem and key verbatim including the reject, (c).

This is the single most efficient revision target in Book 4: build the HIV-against-influenza comparison once and it answers a stem shaped like this, a compare question and half of an essay.
**Trap:** generalising a DNA stage, reverse transcription or integration from HIV to influenza, which is exactly what the specimen is built to catch; giving exocytosis for HIV, which a scheme rejects by name; comparing yes against no rather than point to point, which schemes reject outright, so say where each virus fuses rather than that one fuses and the other does not.
**LOs:** 2(e)ii and 2(e)iii held together, the reproductive cycles of enveloped viruses and of retroviruses, in (a) and (b); 2(e) exit route by virus, in (c). Reach-back to Cluster 1 for phage lysis in (c).
**Concepts fused:** (1) penetration as a shared slot with different mechanisms; (2) integration as retrovirus-only; (3) reverse transcription as retrovirus-only; (4) transcription of viral DNA as retrovirus-only; (5) assembly as a shared slot; (6) budding as the enveloped exit; (7) where the envelope comes from; (8) lysis as the naked-phage exit; (9) why the exit route follows from whether the virus has an envelope.

### Question 5

**(a)** *Explain how viral enzyme U helps with the release of new influenza viruses.* **[1]**

**1.** **Viral enzyme U is neuraminidase and it cleaves sialic acid receptors away from the surface of the new influenza particles.**

**(b)** *Suggest and explain how these drugs act to prevent viruses leaving cells.* **[3]**

**1.** **Drugs can be inhibitors of neuraminidase.** A **competitive inhibitor enters and binds to the active site and competes with the substrate for binding at the enzyme's active site.** Alternatively a **non-competitive inhibitor binds to the allosteric site and the enzyme's 3D conformation is changed such that the conformation of its active site is altered and the substrate can no longer bind to the enzyme's active site.**

**2.** As a separate point, that is another role of the drugs rather than the inhibitor role, **drugs may act by breaking down or hydrolysing neuraminidase, or by disrupting the bonds within the secondary and tertiary structures of neuraminidase, resulting in loss of the specific 3D conformation of the enzyme and its active site, so neuraminidase is denatured and the substrate can no longer bind to the enzyme's active site.**

**3.** **No or fewer enzyme-substrate complexes are formed.**

**4.** The **host cell receptor is still complementary to haemagglutinin and haemagglutinin remains attached to the receptor, so the newly formed virus does not leave the cells.**

**(c)** *Describe how EBOV acquires its viral envelope.* **[3]**
