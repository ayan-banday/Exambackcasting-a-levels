**4.** Similarity: **both have RNA.**

**2.** **Genome can be circular or linear, and there can be more than one copy of the genome.**

**4.** The capsid is **made from identical protein subunits known as capsomeres**.

**5.** The **capsid protects the viral genome and determines the specific shape of the virus.**

**6.** **Viral envelopes are phospholipid bilayer membrane derived from the host cell membrane.**

**7.** The envelope is **embedded with glycoprotein and surrounds the capsid**.

**8.** **Glycoproteins are essential for the attachment of virus to host cell receptor.**

**9.** The envelope **protects the virus from enzymes, chemicals and a harsh environment.**

### Question 3

**(a)** *The influenza virus carries its own viral RNA polymerase in its virions but not SARS-CoV-2. Suggest why.* **[2]**

**1.** The **influenza virus genome is negative sense RNA, and there is no available enzyme in host cells to use negative sense RNA as a template to make positive sense RNA.**

**2.** Whereas **SARS-CoV-2 has a positive sense RNA genome, which can be used directly as a template for translation using host ribosomes to make the viral RNA-dependent RNA polymerase.**

**3.** So the point is not that a host RNA-dependent RNA polymerase exists for one virus and not the other. Neither virus has one available. The difference is that **a positive-sense genome can make its own polymerase after entry, and a negative-sense genome cannot be read at all until a polymerase it brought with it has copied it.**

**(b)** *Suggest how the positive sense RNA strand of rubella virus is utilised in making progeny virus.* **[2]**

**1.** **Viral polymerase converts positive sense RNA to negative sense RNA to use as a template for synthesis of more copies of positive sense RNA.**

**2.** **Positive sense RNA is the template for translation of viral proteins.**

**3.** Marker's note: unlike influenza, rubella has a **positive sense RNA genome, so it can directly be used for translation; reverse transcriptase is not involved.**

**(c)** *Describe one similarity and two differences between RNA-dependent RNA polymerase and reverse transcriptase.* **[3]**

**1.** Similarity: **both are viral proteins, that is they are only coded for by viral genes and not the host genes.**

**2.** Similarity: **both are dependent on RNA as a template.**

**3.** Difference in **template used**: RNA-dependent RNA polymerase **uses RNA, both positive and negative strand**, whereas reverse transcriptase **uses an RNA template first and then the single-stranded cDNA**.

**4.** Difference in **products**: RNA-dependent RNA polymerase **only forms RNA, that is positive-sense RNA for translation when using negative RNA as a template, and negative RNA when using positive RNA as a template to create more copies of the viral genome, giving single-stranded RNA**; reverse transcriptase **forms a cDNA-RNA hybrid using RNA as template, then double-stranded cDNA when using cDNA as a template**.

**5.** Difference in **catalytic activity**: RNA-dependent RNA polymerase **has RNA polymerase activity, that is extension of RNA using host free ribonucleotides**, whereas reverse transcriptase **has reverse transcription activity, that is extension of cDNA, ribonuclease or RNase activity, that is degrading RNA in the cDNA-RNA hybrid, and DNA polymerase activity, that is formation of complementary DNA to form double-stranded cDNA**.

**6.** Difference in **monomers**: **ribonucleotides against deoxyribonucleotides.**

**(d)** *Explain, with an example, how the Central Dogma may not always hold true.* **[2]**

**1.** **There can be reverse flow of genetic information from RNA to DNA.** For example, **in HIV, reverse transcriptase produces DNA from viral RNA, that is reverse transcription occurs in HIV.**

**2.** **RNA instead of DNA can store genetic information.** For example, **the influenza virus has several segments of linear negative single-stranded RNA genome.**

**3.** **RNA can undergo replication.** For example, **influenza virus contains RNA-dependent RNA polymerase which synthesises positive-sense RNA using negative-sense RNA as the template via complementary base pairing.**

**4.** Any one point plus its example earns one mark, and two such pairs earn both marks.

### Question 4

**(a)** *Which features of viruses provide evidence that they are non-living?* **[1]** [INFERRED: the 9477 specimen papers ship without a mark scheme; cross-checked against YIJC Prelim 2025 P1 Q16, which sets the same content with six numbered statements and whose key audits each one]

**1.** The key is **C, features 2, 3 and 4**.

**(b)** *Explain your answer, stating for each of the four numbered features whether it is evidence.* **[3]** [INFERRED: reasoning taken from the YIJC Prelim 2025 P1 Q16 key, which audits the same statements]

**1.** **Feature 1 is not evidence.** **Being cut by bacterial restriction enzymes is about the susceptibility of DNA and RNA, not about whether the virus is alive**, and **DNA and RNA from living organisms can equally be cut by restriction enzymes**.

**2.** **Feature 2 is evidence.** Every living cell contains **both DNA and RNA**, whereas a virion carries **only one type of nucleic acid**, so it **lacks the full genetic information needed for independent life**.

**3.** **Feature 3 is evidence.** A virus can be **crystallised like a pure chemical**, which no living cell can.

**4.** **Feature 4 is evidence.** Viruses have **no metabolism of their own and cannot generate ATP**, so they must **use the host cell's energy supply**.

**5.** Note for the related YIJC version of this question: **lacking cellular structure alone does not directly explain obligate parasitism; the key is the absence of metabolic and energy-generating systems.**

**(c)** *Explain why viruses are considered obligate parasites.* **[2]**

**1.** Viruses are **acellular, that is they lack cellular or biosynthetic machinery or organelles required for survival or for metabolic reactions.**

**2.** **Viruses need to use the host cell's resources for synthesis of viral proteins and nucleic acids to complete the reproductive cycle.**
