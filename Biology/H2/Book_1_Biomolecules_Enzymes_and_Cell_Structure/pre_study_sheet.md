## 1.0 Water and Macromolecules

1. Water is **polar** because oxygen is more **electronegative** than hydrogen, putting a partial negative charge on O and partial positives on the two H.
2. Polar water molecules attract each other and form **hydrogen bonds** — weak individually, decisive in aggregate.
3. Water dissolves polar and charged solutes by forming **hydration spheres** around them, which is why it is the universal solvent.
4. Molecules are **hydrophilic**, **hydrophobic**, or **amphipathic** (both on one molecule) depending on whether they can hydrogen-bond with water.
5. **Condensation** joins two monomers by removing one **H₂O**; **hydrolysis** breaks the bond by adding one **H₂O** back.
6. The four families are **carbohydrates, lipids, proteins and nucleic acids** — three are polymers, and **lipids are not**.

---

## 1.1 Lipids

1. Lipids are **non-polar and hydrophobic**: insoluble in water, soluble in organic solvents.
2. A **triglyceride** is **glycerol + 3 fatty acids** joined by **ester bonds**, formed by condensation with the loss of **3 H₂O**.
3. **Saturated** fatty acids have no C=C and pack closely; **unsaturated** ones have C=C **kinks** that prevent close packing, weakening **hydrophobic interactions** and lowering the melting point.
4. Triglycerides are efficient energy stores because they are **highly reduced** — more energy per gram than carbohydrate — and being hydrophobic they carry **no water of hydration**, so they add no mass and don't affect **water potential**.
5. A **phospholipid** is glycerol + **2 fatty acids** + a **phosphate group**, giving a hydrophilic head and hydrophobic tails: it is **amphipathic**.
6. Amphipathic phospholipids self-assemble in water into a **bilayer** (also **micelles** and **liposomes**), which is the basis of every membrane.
7. **Cholesterol** is a four-ring lipid that sits between phospholipids and regulates **membrane fluidity**.

---

## 1.2 Carbohydrates

1. Two monosaccharides join by **condensation**, losing one **H₂O**, to form a **glycosidic bond**; **hydrolysis** reverses it by adding H₂O.
2. Glucose exists as **α-glucose** and **β-glucose**, differing only in the orientation of the **-OH on the anomeric carbon** — **α below the ring, β above**.
3. **α-glucose** joined by **α(1,4)** bonds gives unbranched **amylose**; adding **α(1,6)** bonds creates branches, giving **amylopectin** and **glycogen**. All three are **storage**.
4. **β-glucose** joined by **β(1,4)** bonds forces **alternate monomers to invert**, producing the long straight chains of **cellulose**. It is **structural**.
5. Storage polysaccharides work because they are **large and insoluble** (so they don't affect **water potential**), **compact**, and **easily hydrolysed** back to glucose when needed.
6. Cellulose gets its **tensile strength** from **hydrogen bonds between the -OH groups of parallel chains**, which bundle into **microfibrils** and then **macrofibrils**.
7. A sugar is **reducing** if it has a **free carbonyl group**; **sucrose** is **non-reducing** because both carbonyls are locked into the glycosidic bond.

---

## 1.3 Proteins

1. Amino acids join by **condensation** to form **peptide bonds**, losing H₂O.
2. Every amino acid has an **amine group**, a **carboxyl group**, a hydrogen and a variable **R group** — and the R group determines where that residue ends up in the folded protein.
3. **Primary structure** is the sequence of amino acids held by **peptide bonds**, and it determines every level above it.
4. **Secondary structure** is the **α-helix** and **β-pleated sheet**, held by **hydrogen bonds between the C=O and N-H of the backbone** — **not** the R groups.
5. **Tertiary structure** is the overall 3D fold, held by four bonds between **R groups**: **hydrogen, ionic, disulfide and hydrophobic interactions**.
6. **Quaternary structure** is two or more polypeptides associating, using those same four bond types.
7. **Haemoglobin** is **globular** and transports oxygen: hydrophilic residues outside make it **soluble**, a **haem group containing Fe²⁺** binds O₂, and its four subunits give **cooperative binding**.
8. **Collagen** is **fibrous** and structural: a repeating **Gly-X-Y** sequence puts the small **glycine** every third residue so three chains pack tightly into a **triple helix**, and **covalent cross-links** give **tensile strength**.
9. **Denaturation** breaks the **hydrogen, ionic and hydrophobic bonds** holding the 3D shape, so the shape and function are lost — but the **peptide bonds and primary structure survive**.

## 1.4 Enzymes

1. Enzymes are **globular proteins** that speed up reactions by **lowering activation energy**; they do **not** change how favourable the reaction is.
2. The **active site** is formed by folding, from residues that are far apart in the primary sequence — which is why losing the fold destroys catalysis.
3. Substrate binds the active site to form an **enzyme-substrate complex**; the product no longer fits, so it is released and the enzyme is reused.
4. **Lock-and-key** says the active site is rigid and exactly complementary; **induced fit** says it is flexible and moulds around the substrate.
5. Raising temperature raises kinetic energy and the frequency of **effective collisions** up to the **optimum**; past it, **denaturation** destroys the active site, **irreversibly**.
6. Changing pH alters the **ionisation of the R groups**, disrupting **ionic and hydrogen bonds** and distorting the active site — usually **reversibly**.
7. Rate rises with **substrate concentration** until the active sites are **saturated**, and with **enzyme concentration** until substrate becomes the limiting factor.
8. **Vmax** is the maximum rate; **Km** is the substrate concentration at **½Vmax** and measures affinity — a **low Km means high affinity**.
9. **Competitive inhibitors** resemble the substrate and bind the **active site**, so **Vmax is unchanged but Km increases** — adding more substrate overcomes them.
10. **Non-competitive inhibitors** bind **elsewhere** and distort the active site, so **Vmax falls but Km is unchanged** — more substrate does not help.
11. Whole pathways are regulated by **end-product inhibition**, where the final product switches off an earlier enzyme.

## 1.5 Eukaryotic Cell Structure and Function

1. **Cell theory**: the cell is the fundamental unit of life, all living things are made of cells, and all cells come from **pre-existing cells**.
2. Cell size has an upper limit set by the **surface area to volume ratio** — so organisms grow by having **more cells, not bigger cells**.
3. **Prokaryotes** have **no nucleus and no membrane-bound organelles**; they have a **peptidoglycan cell wall**, **circular DNA** and **70S ribosomes**.
4. **Eukaryotes** are **compartmentalised**, which lets incompatible reactions run simultaneously and **increases membrane surface area** for embedded enzymes.
5. A secreted protein follows the **secretory pathway**: **ribosome on rough ER → ER lumen for folding → transport vesicle → Golgi for modification → secretory vesicle → exocytosis**.
6. Which organelles a cell has in abundance reports its job — lots of **rough ER** for protein secretion, **smooth ER** for lipid synthesis and detoxification, **mitochondria** for high metabolic activity.
7. **Mitochondria** and **chloroplasts** both have a **double membrane**, their own **circular DNA** and **70S ribosomes**, and inner membranes that are **impermeable to protons** so a gradient can be built.
8. **Magnification** is how much larger the image is; **resolution** is the ability to distinguish two close points as separate. The electron microscope wins on **resolution**, because electrons have a much **shorter wavelength** than light.

## 1.6 Cell Membrane and Transport

1. The **fluid mosaic model**: a fluid **phospholipid bilayer** with a mosaic of **proteins** embedded in and on it.
2. Phospholipids are held together only by weak **hydrophobic interactions**, so they move **laterally** with ease but **flip transversely** only rarely.
3. **Cholesterol** acts as a temperature buffer: it **decreases** fluidity at high temperature and **increases** it at low temperature.
4. **Integral proteins** are embedded in the bilayer and are **amphipathic**; **peripheral proteins** sit on the surface and are hydrophilic.
5. **Glycoproteins and glycolipids** on the outer surface are the markers for **cell-to-cell recognition**.
6. **Simple diffusion** moves **small or hydrophobic** molecules **down** the concentration gradient, with **no protein and no ATP**.
7. **Facilitated diffusion** moves **large or hydrophilic** molecules **down** the gradient through a **channel or carrier protein**, still with **no ATP**.
8. **Active transport** moves substances **against** the gradient through a **carrier protein**, and requires **ATP**.
9. **Osmosis** is the net movement of water from a **less negative to a more negative water potential** across a partially permeable membrane.
10. **Endocytosis** brings material in by **invagination** of the membrane; **exocytosis** releases it by **vesicle fusion**. Both require **ATP**.
