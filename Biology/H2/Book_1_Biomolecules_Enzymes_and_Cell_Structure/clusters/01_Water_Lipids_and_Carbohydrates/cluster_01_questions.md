### Question 1

**(a)** *Which types of glycosidic bond result in the biologically important characteristics of
glycogen?* Key: **B** **[1]**

[INFERRED — extension, no official key] Taking the five columns of row B in turn. **Branching chains
require α(1,6) bonds**, because a branch can only start where **carbon 6 of one residue is joined to
the anomeric carbon of another**, and α(1,4) linkage simply extends the existing chain. **Coiled chains
require α(1,4) bonds**, because the **α(1,4) linkage angles successive residues at about 109°**, which
**winds the chain into a helix**. **Compact shape requires both**, since the **helix from α(1,4)
packs the chain tightly** and the **branching from α(1,6) folds those helices in on one another**, so
that **more glucose is stored per unit volume**. **A large osmotically neutral molecule also requires
both**, because it takes both bond types to lock **hundreds to thousands of glucose residues into a
single molecule**, which means **very few solute particles**, so the store has **almost no effect on
the water potential of the cell**. **Rapid hydrolysis requires α(1,6)**, because it is the **α(1,6)
bonds that create the branch points**, and **many free branch ends allow a larger number of enzyme
molecules to act at the same time**.

Row **A** fails at the first column: it makes **branching α(1,4)**, which is the linear bond. Row **C**
fails at the second column: it makes **coiling α(1,6)**, when the helix comes from **α(1,4)**. Row
**D** fails at the first column too: it makes branching **"1,6 and 1,4"**, but **α(1,4) never produces
a branch** on its own. **[3]**

**(b)** *Explain the relationship between the composition of starch and the rate of hydrolysis by
amylases, using Table 1.1.*

**As amylose content increases from 18.7% to 35.8%, the percentage of starch hydrolysed after 4 hours

**(c)** *Explain what is common to the monolayer and the bilayer and why the number of layers differs.*
