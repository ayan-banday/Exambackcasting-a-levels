# Topic Examiner's Guide — Biology H2 (9477) > Book 1: Biomolecules, Enzymes and Cell Structure

> Specialises the subject guide to Book 1. Drives the three-cluster breakdown and question
> generation. **Read `../subject_examiner_guide.md` §3.0 first** — it explains why the school's
> Book 1 is not the same set as the syllabus's Core Idea 1.

## 0. Scope boundary (get this right before anything else)

Book 1 covers **9477 Core Idea 1, Learning Outcomes (a) through (s)** — that is Core Idea 1
**minus stem cells**. `[OFFICIAL]`

| In Book 1 | Not in Book 1, but examined under Core Idea 1 |
|---|---|
| LOs (a)–(d), (g)–(s) — cell theory, organelles, bacterial cell, monomers, bonds, biomolecules, membranes, transport, protein structure, named proteins, enzymes, inhibition | **LO (t)–(u) stem cells** → taught in **Book 3** |
| | **LO (e)–(f) virus structure + "how viruses challenge the cell theory"** → the *structure* is examined here alongside the bacterial cell; the *genetics* is **Book 4** |

**Practical consequence.** A Book 1 revision pass that stops at the Book's last page leaves LO (e),
(f), (t) and (u) untouched. LO (f) in particular — *discuss how viruses challenge the cell theory and
concepts of what is considered living* — is the distinctive H2 AO3 hook of this Core Idea and is a
standing free-response candidate. Flag it, do not silently drop it.

**Weighting.** Cell + Biomolecules runs **15–20% of total marks**, worth **15–25 marks in Paper 2**
and **5–15 in Paper 3**, and the corpus shows it **constant across every era** — it never has a quiet
year. `[SCHOOL]` (examiner-patterns-reference L64). It also carries the **highest quantitative load**
of any non-genetics topic (enzyme kinetics, microscopy/magnification). `[SCHOOL]`

## 1. Content Node Map (coverage checklist)

Every node the exam can test in Book 1, grouped by the cluster that will teach it. Convergence
requires no UNTOUCHED node. LO letters are `[OFFICIAL]` (syllabus pp.12–13).

### Cluster 01 — Water, Lipids and Carbohydrates
- [ ] **Water** — polarity and hydrogen bonding; the properties that follow (solvent, high specific
      heat capacity, cohesion/surface tension, density of ice, transparency) and the biological
      consequence of each. *Note: water is taught as Book 1 chapter 1.0 but has no dedicated 9477 LO —
      it is examined as supporting reasoning inside (i), (j) and (l) questions.* `[SCHOOL]`
- [ ] **Monomers (g)** — α-glucose and β-glucose: structure, the difference, and why it matters.
      Glycerol and fatty acids. *(Chemical formulae of specific R-groups NOT required.)*
- [ ] **Bond formation and breakage (h)** — glycosidic bond, ester bond, by condensation; hydrolysis
      as the reverse. Which -OH groups react, and what leaves.
- [ ] **Polysaccharide structure → function (i)** — starch (amylose AND amylopectin), cellulose,
      glycogen. For each: monomer, bond type and position (1,4 vs 1,6), chain shape, and the paired
      functional consequence.
- [ ] **Lipid structure → function (i)** — triglyceride (ester bonds, energy storage) and
      phospholipid (amphipathic, bilayer). Saturated vs unsaturated and the effect on packing/fluidity.
- [ ] **Reducing vs non-reducing sugars** and the biochemical tests, where the notes cover them. `[SCHOOL]`

### Cluster 02 — Proteins and Enzymes
- [ ] **Amino acids (g)** — general structure, the four groups, how R-groups differ.
- [ ] **Peptide bond (h)** — formation by condensation, breakage by hydrolysis.
- [ ] **Four levels of protein structure (m)** — primary, secondary (α-helix, β-pleated sheet),
      tertiary, quaternary, AND the exact bond at each level: hydrogen, ionic, disulfide,
      hydrophobic interactions.
- [ ] **Temperature and pH on protein structure (n)** — which bonds break, in which order, and what
      happens to conformation. Peptide bonds do **not** break in denaturation.
- [ ] **Haemoglobin (o)** — globular, transport. Structure → function, quaternary, haem groups.
- [ ] **Collagen (o)** — fibrous, structural. Triple helix, glycine every third residue, staggered
      cross-links. *(Number of amino acids / count of secondary structures NOT required.)*
- [ ] **Haemoglobin vs collagen contrast** — the paired comparison is itself a recurring question.
- [ ] **Enzyme mode of action (p)** — active site, enzyme–substrate complex, **lowering of activation
      energy**, specificity; lock-and-key vs induced-fit as competing hypotheses.
- [ ] **Factors affecting rate (q)** — temperature, pH, enzyme concentration, substrate
      concentration. Each curve's shape and the explanation for **every region** of it.
- [ ] **Measuring rate (q)** — rate of product formation (catalase/gas) or substrate disappearance
      (amylase/starch/iodine). `[OFFICIAL]` — an experimental-design hook that also feeds P4.
- [ ] **Inhibitor structure (r)** — competitive vs non-competitive, described by **where they bind**.
- [ ] **Inhibitor effects (s)** — on rate, and on Vmax and Km specifically, including allosteric
      inhibitors. **`[type override → quantitative]`**

### Cluster 03 — Cell Structure and Membrane Transport
- [ ] **Cell theory (a)** — the three tenets, verbatim.
- [ ] **Organelle identification (b)** — from drawings, photomicrographs AND electron micrographs:
      rough and smooth ER, Golgi body, mitochondria, ribosomes, lysosomes, chloroplasts, cell surface
      membrane, nuclear envelope, centrioles, nucleus, nucleolus, cytosol, cellulose cell wall.
- [ ] **Organelle functions (c)** — for every structure listed in (b).
- [ ] **The secretory pathway** — the ordered route a secreted protein takes. Not a separate LO but
      the single most-asked application of (b)+(c). `[SCHOOL]`
- [ ] **Microscopy** — magnification vs resolution; light vs electron microscope; eyepiece graticule
      and stage micrometer; magnification and scale-bar calculation. **`[type override → quantitative]`**
- [ ] **Bacterial cell (d)** — small, unicellular, peptidoglycan cell wall, circular DNA, 70S
      ribosomes, no membrane-bound organelles. Prokaryote vs eukaryote comparison.
- [ ] **Virus structure (e)** — including enveloped viruses and bacteriophages; interpret drawings
      and photographs. *(Taught with Book 4 material — flagged here as examinable Core Idea 1 scope.)*
- [ ] **Viruses vs the cell theory (f)** — `discuss`-command territory: both sides plus a judgement.
- [ ] **Fluid mosaic model (j)** — and the role of **each** constituent: phospholipids, proteins,
      glycolipids, glycoproteins, cholesterol. Cholesterol's dual effect at high vs low temperature.
- [ ] **Membrane functions (k)** — at the cell surface and within the cell.
- [ ] **Transport (l)** — simple diffusion, osmosis, facilitated diffusion, active transport,
      endocytosis, exocytosis. For each: energy required or not, protein required or not, direction
      relative to the gradient.
- [ ] **Water potential** — ψ, solute vs pressure potential, sign convention, direction of net water
      movement; isotonic/hypertonic/hypotonic; plasmolysis and turgor.
      **`[type override → quantitative]`**

## 3. Mark Scheme Logic (this topic specifically)

**The paired structure-function rule.** In this topic more than any other, marks come in pairs. A
mark scheme line reads "coils/helical **AND** compact for storage" — the structural feature alone
earns nothing, the function alone earns nothing. Ethan's answers must always land both halves in the
same sentence. `[SCHOOL]` (HCI 2013 P2 S/F table)

**Markers reject approximate phrasing — verified.** `[SCHOOL]` (H3 2022 EJC + YIJC 2025)

| Earns the mark | Does NOT earn it |
|---|---|
| "**tight** triple helix" | "compact triple helix" |
| "glycine is **the smallest** amino acid" | "one of the smallest amino acids" |
| "**procollagen**" | "collagen" (where the precursor is meant) |
| "**bud off**" | "pinch off" |

The lesson generalises: this topic's mark schemes are lexically strict. Encode the notes' exact words
from first exposure (rules §5b), not a paraphrase that means the same thing.

**Command-word discipline.** *Describe* wants observable features; *explain* wants the mechanism with
cause and effect; *compare* requires both sides on the same axis (a list of differences with no
similarity, or vice versa, caps the mark); *suggest* signals there is no recall answer and the
examiner wants reasoning from the given data. `[SCHOOL]`

**Essay breadth.** Paper 3 Section B punishes over-depth on one molecule. A "polymers" essay that
covers only carbohydrates is capped regardless of quality. Both parts of these essays typically ask
for "named examples" or "various roles" — breadth first, then depth. `[SCHOOL]` (L250, L515)

**Named specifics beat generic nouns.** "A protein" where *haemoglobin*, *collagen*, *catalase* or
*rubisco* is available loses the mark. `[SCHOOL]` (L525)

## 4. Trap Model (this topic specifically)

Every generated distractor must encode one of these real, file-traceable traps. No invented distractors.

| Trap | The misconception | The wrong answer it produces | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Structure without function** | Thinks describing the molecule answers the question | "1,4 glycosidic bonds make it coil" with no "→ compact → storage" | HCI 2013 P2 `[SCHOOL]` |
| **Cellulose ↔ amylose monomer swap** | Loses which polymer uses which glucose isomer | "cellulose is made of α-glucose"; H-bonds pointing inward | NJC 2013 P2 `[SCHOOL]` |
| **Collagen as a single α-helix** | Conflates the triple helix with secondary-structure α-helix (and with the amylose helix) | "collagen is a long α-helix" | `[INFERRED]` |
| **Denaturation over-claim** | Thinks denaturation breaks peptide bonds | "high temperature breaks the peptide bonds" instead of hydrogen/ionic bonds and conformation | LO(n) + NJC enzyme scheme `[OFFICIAL]+[SCHOOL]` |
| **Denaturation under-claim** | States the effect, skips the mechanism | "the enzyme stops working" with no bond disruption → active-site shape change | as above |
| **Centriole "enclosed by a single membrane"** | Assumes every organelle is membrane-bound | **Only 28% answered correctly** — the single hardest Book 1 MCQ trap in the corpus | YIJC 2025 `[SCHOOL]` |
| **Endocytosis vs exocytosis membrane balance** | Cannot say which process gains and which loses membrane phospholipid | **45% correct**; 38% picked the reverse | YIJC 2025 `[SCHOOL]` |
| **Reversed gradient direction** | The universal MCQ distractor | water "enters" where it leaves; "up" the gradient where it is down | L127, L321 `[SCHOOL]` |
| **Prokaryote ↔ eukaryote over-generalisation** | Takes a eukaryote fact as universal | "bacteria have 80S ribosomes"; "bacterial DNA is in a nucleus" | L130–131 `[SCHOOL]` |
| **Km / Vmax swap under inhibition** | Memorised the table without the mechanism | says competitive lowers Vmax, or non-competitive raises Km | 2008 HCI H2 Q3–4 `[SCHOOL]` |
| **Magnification ↔ resolution** | Uses the words interchangeably | "the electron microscope has higher magnification" where *resolution* is the point | `[SCHOOL]` |

## 5. High-Yield Flags

Nodes that earn heavy marks or fail often. These get **15–20 drill questions**, not the standard 10.

1. **Enzyme inhibition with Vmax/Km reasoning** — heaviest quantitative load in the topic, an evergreen
   MCQ (2008 HCI Q3–4 still reused), and a standing P2 data question. Must be understood
   mechanistically, never as a memorised table.
2. **Polysaccharide comparison (cellulose vs starch vs glycogen)** — a **10-mark** question in its own
   right (2021 VJC P2 Q4b) and the archetypal paired structure-function set.
3. **Membrane structure and transport** — 6 of 30 banked essays, plus the two worst-performing MCQ
   traps in the corpus (centriole 28%, endo/exocytosis 45%).
4. **Protein structure levels with the correct bond at each level** — feeds denaturation, enzymes,
   haemoglobin, collagen and membrane proteins. A weak node here poisons the whole Book.
5. **The secretory pathway** — the standard way (b)+(c) get examined in application form rather than
   as recall.
6. **LO (f) viruses vs the cell theory** — low frequency, high mark value, `discuss` command,
   and easy to skip because it sits outside the printed Book.
