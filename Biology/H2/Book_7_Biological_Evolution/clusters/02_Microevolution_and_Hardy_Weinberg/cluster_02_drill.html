Question 1 [9 marks]

(a) Explain why the population is the smallest unit that can evolve. [4]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (a)

(a) Explain why the population is the smallest unit that can evolve. [4]

1. Individual organisms cannot evolve.

2. An individual has the same genome its entire life, except for mutations acquired during its lifetime.

3. Natural selection acts on individuals in a population, but only populations evolve.

4. Evolution is a process that takes place over extended periods of time as alleles coding for adaptations are passed from one generation to another.

5. Note on the school's own text. Chapter 7.4 p.71 opens by calling the species the smallest independently evolving unit. That contradicts LO 4(d) word for word, and contradicts the whole of chapter 7.3 section 3.2. The species is the smallest unit of the classification system, which is a different claim and a correct one. Write population every time.

(b) Describe how micro-evolution is related to macro-evolution. [2]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (b)

(b) Describe how micro-evolution is related to macro-evolution. [2]

1. Changes in allele frequency in the gene pool of a population over generations, which is micro-evolution.

2. May lead to reproductive isolating mechanisms forming between populations.

3. Preventing interbreeding / gene flow.

4. Leading to speciation / forming of new species.

(c) Two subspecies of reindeer, Rangifer tarandus, live in North America. The Woodland subspecies is large-bodied and lives in warmer southern woodland; the Barren Ground subspecies is small-bodied and lives on the colder northern tundra. Assess the relative importance of natural selection and genetic drift in producing the different body sizes of the two subspecies of reindeer. [2]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (c)

(c) Assess the relative importance of natural selection and genetic drift in producing the different body sizes of the two subspecies of reindeer. [2]

1. Genetic drift, not natural selection, is more important.

2. And any one from the following. Small size in the north is not adaptive / beneficial.

3. Small animals lose heat faster / find it harder to keep warm.

4. Smaller fat reserves are not good with an unstable food supply.

5. Less food in the north reduces growth / size.

6. Any other valid point.

7. Marker's note. Endothermic animals such as mammals at higher latitudes should be larger, not smaller, with a smaller surface area to volume ratio to minimise heat loss, so the size difference is the opposite of what adaptation would predict. The alternative explanation is that the phenotypic difference is not genetic but results from food shortage in the tundra and a plentiful food supply in the south.

(b) The difference between the allele frequencies in populations 2 and 3 occurred over a short period of time, despite both living on beaches. Suggest an explanation for the change in the allele frequencies. [2]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (b)

(b) Suggest an explanation for the change in the allele frequencies between populations 2 and 3. [2]

1. Reference to genetic drift.

2. Occurrence of a chance event. Accept a named event, for example natural disaster, disease outbreak, predators.

3. Reference to drastic reduction of the size of mice population over a short period of time. Accept bottleneck.

4. Reference to small populations of mice.

5. Any valid point, for example reference to gene flow.

6. Note: maximum 1 if no reference to a particular population, or to either population being affected.

7. Examiner's comment. The question states that both populations 2 and 3 live on the beach, so the environment is the same and the allele frequencies should be the same. There is a significant difference and it surfaced after a short period. For such a scenario, natural selection is unlikely to be the cause. Genetic drift, where a catastrophic chance event occurs to either population and causes a drastic reduction in numbers, results in an immediate change in allele frequency where some alleles are under-represented, over-represented or eliminated. Allele frequency change is more pronounced in small populations. Another common error is that there is a mutation and allele C or R was created. This question clearly takes into account that alleles C and R already existed in the gene pool. Mutation is not the answer.

Question 3 [11 marks]

(a) In the Southern Ocean there are three distinct types of orca, Type B, Type C and Type D. Type B and Type C are mainly seen near the coastline of Antarctica; Type D is mainly seen offshore, further from the coastline. Phenotypic differences between Type D orca and the other types could have resulted from the process of genetic drift, a process when small, isolated groups expand into new environments and carry only a subset of the original gene pool. Compare evolution by genetic drift and evolution by natural selection. [3]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (a)

(a) Compare evolution by genetic drift and evolution by natural selection. [3]

1. Similarity. Both are changes in allelic frequency over time.

2. Difference. Genetic drift is random whereas natural selection is non-random, acting on selective advantage, plus elaboration.

3. Difference. Genetic drift may increase or decrease fitness, but natural selection always increases fitness.

4. Difference. The effect of genetic drift is stronger in a small population, whereas natural selection affects both big and small populations in the same way.

(b) Regressive evolution is a change in a population over time that involves the loss of certain phenotypic characteristics. It is thought to be caused by either genetic drift or natural selection. An example of regressive evolution is the loss of eyes in one form of the Mexican cavefish, Astyanax mexicanus. These eyeless cavefish live in caves that are in total darkness. There are three theories to explain how the loss of eyes in the cavefish has occurred.

Theory A: There is no advantage to having eyes in a cave that is in total darkness, where energy sources are scarce. Having eyes is a disadvantage as there may be an energy cost.

Theory B: A mutation has occurred in a single gene. This mutation has two effects, a lack of eye development, and an increase in the number of chemoreceptors on the skin, for better detection of molecules released by other organisms in the surrounding.

Theory C: Various mutations occurred in the genes responsible for eye development over a period of time. Randomly, these mutations increased in frequency in small isolated populations. Eventually this produced a population of eyeless cavefish.

State and explain which of the above theories are based on natural selection as the cause of loss of eyes. [5]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (b)

(b) State and explain which of the theories are based on natural selection as the cause of loss of eyes. [5]

1. The theories based on natural selection are A and B.

2. In A, low availability of energy / food to cave-dwelling fishes acts as a selection pressure.

3. Less energy used, for example to synthesise proteins for eye development, means a lower need for food, which could be a selective advantage.

4. In B, there is a selective advantage of having more chemoreceptors.

5. More chemoreceptors allow for better detection of food / prey / mate in caves.

6. Theory C is not natural selection. It is drift: the mutations increased in frequency randomly, in small isolated populations, with no reference to any advantage conferred.

(c) Three pairs of hedgehogs were introduced to the island of Alderney. A survey taken in 2017 showed that 60% of the hedgehogs on Alderney had blonde spines. Suggest and explain reasons why the proportion of hedgehogs with blonde spines was so high. [3]

---

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (c)

(c) Suggest and explain reasons why the proportion of hedgehogs with blonde spines was so high. [3]

1. Reason 1. Since only 3 pairs of hedgehogs were introduced into the island, genetic drift / founder effect occurred.

2. Alleles coding for blonde spines were over-represented in this founding population.

3. When breeding occurred within the small founding population, the frequency of alleles coding for blonde spines increased.

4. Reason 2. Hedgehogs with blonde spines may have had a selective advantage / were selected for as they were camouflaged against the light coloured sand in the island and hence were not easily detected by predators.

5. Hence hedgehogs with blonde spines, the favourable trait, survived and reproduced and passed the favourable alleles coding for blonde spines to the offspring.

6. Thus, over time, the frequency of blonde spines alleles increased in the population.

7. Directional selection had occurred as blonde spines were favoured.

Question 5 [15 marks]

Section B free-response essay. In nature, many mechanisms preserve and restore variation in a population. Describe the various mechanisms that help to preserve variation in a population. [15]

---

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer

Describe the various mechanisms that help to preserve variation in a population. [15]

1. Diploidy. An organism has 2 sets of chromosomes / is diploid and genetic variation is maintained and hidden from selection in the form of recessive alleles.

2. Recessive alleles that are less favourable than their dominant counterparts, or even harmful in the current environment, can persist because they are propagated in heterozygous individuals.

3. When a heterozygote containing the recessive allele is able to survive and reproduce, the recessive allele is passed down to the offspring, and is preserved in the population.

4. Heterozygote protection maintains a huge pool of alleles that might not be favoured under present conditions but some could bring new benefits when the environment changes.

5. Balancing selection. Balancing selection occurs when natural selection maintains stable frequencies of two or more phenotypic forms in a population.

6. It includes heterozygous advantage and frequency-dependent selection.

7. Heterozygote advantage. Individuals who are heterozygous at a particular locus have greater fitness than those who are homozygous at that locus.

8. Natural selection will tend to maintain two or more alleles at that locus.

9. For the sickle-cell allele, individuals homozygous for HbS (genotype HbSHbS) suffer from sickle cell anaemia and HbAHbA are individuals who are normal.

10. Individuals who are heterozygous (genotype HbAHbS) suffer from the sickle cell trait. HbA and HbS alleles are codominant with respect to each other so heterozygous individuals produce both normal and abnormal haemoglobins.

11. Malaria parasite acts as a selection pressure in places with malaria.

12. Individuals without the HbS allele / individuals of HbAHbA genotype are at a selective disadvantage, with decreased fitness due to mortality from malaria.

13. Heterozygotes, who show the sickle cell trait and carry a HbS / mutated / recessive allele, are at a selective advantage as compared to individuals of HbAHbA genotype, as malaria parasites inside sickle-shaped red blood cells are removed by the immune system.

(e) Cepaea nemoralis banding: calculate the allele frequencies, the heterozygote frequency and the number of homozygous dominant snails. [5]

This part is constructed, not lifted. [INFERRED] No real paper in the papers asks for the three outputs LO 4(n) names, frequencies of alleles, genotypes and phenotypes, in one question.
