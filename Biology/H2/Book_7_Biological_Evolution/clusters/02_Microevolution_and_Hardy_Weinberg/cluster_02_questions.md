### Question 1

**(a)** *Explain why the population is the smallest unit that can evolve.* **[4]**

**1.** **Individual organisms cannot evolve.**

**2.** **An individual has the same genome its entire life, except for mutations acquired during its lifetime.**

**3.** **Natural selection acts on individuals in a population, but only populations evolve.**

**4.** **Evolution is a process that takes place over extended periods of time as alleles coding for adaptations are passed from one generation to another.**

**(b)** *Describe how micro-evolution is related to macro-evolution.* **[2]**

**1.** **Changes in allele frequency in the gene pool of a population over generations, which is micro-evolution.**

**2.** **May lead to reproductive isolating mechanisms forming between populations.**

**3.** **Preventing interbreeding / gene flow.**

**4.** **Leading to speciation / forming of new species.**

**(c)** *Assess the relative importance of natural selection and genetic drift in producing the different body sizes of the two subspecies of reindeer.* **[2]**

**1.** **Genetic drift, not natural selection, is more important.**

**2.** And any one from the following. **Small size in the north is not adaptive / beneficial.**

**3.** **Small animals lose heat faster / find it harder to keep warm.**

**4.** **Smaller fat reserves are not good with an unstable food supply.**

**5.** **Less food in the north reduces growth / size.**

**6.** Any other valid point.

### Question 3

**(a)** *Compare evolution by genetic drift and evolution by natural selection.* **[3]**

**1.** Similarity. **Both are changes in allelic frequency over time.**

**2.** Difference. **Genetic drift is random whereas natural selection is non-random, acting on selective advantage**, plus elaboration.

**3.** Difference. **Genetic drift may increase or decrease fitness, but natural selection always increases fitness.**

**4.** Difference. **The effect of genetic drift is stronger in a small population, whereas natural selection affects both big and small populations in the same way.**

**(b)** *State and explain which of the theories are based on natural selection as the cause of loss of eyes.* **[5]**

**1.** The theories based on natural selection are **A and B**.

**2.** **In A, low availability of energy / food to cave-dwelling fishes acts as a selection pressure.**

**3.** **Less energy used, for example to synthesise proteins for eye development, means a lower need for food, which could be a selective advantage.**

**4.** **In B, there is a selective advantage of having more chemoreceptors.**

**5.** **More chemoreceptors allow for better detection of food / prey / mate in caves.**

**6.** Theory C is **not** natural selection. It is drift: the mutations **increased in frequency randomly, in small isolated populations**, with no reference to any advantage conferred.

**(c)** *Suggest and explain reasons why the proportion of hedgehogs with blonde spines was so high.* **[3]**

**1.** Reason 1. **Since only 3 pairs of hedgehogs were introduced into the island, genetic drift / founder effect occurred.**

**2.** **Alleles coding for blonde spines were over-represented in this founding population.**

**3.** **When breeding occurred within the small founding population, the frequency of alleles coding for blonde spines increased.**

**4.** Reason 2. **Hedgehogs with blonde spines may have had a selective advantage / were selected for as they were camouflaged against the light coloured sand in the island and hence were not easily detected by predators.**

**5.** **Hence hedgehogs with blonde spines, the favourable trait, survived and reproduced and passed the favourable alleles coding for blonde spines to the offspring.**

**6.** **Thus, over time, the frequency of blonde spines alleles increased in the population.**

**7.** **Directional selection had occurred as blonde spines were favoured.**

RI Prelim 2025 P2 Q10(b), stem and both full reasons verbatim (c), with the three founding pairs carried in from the paper's own Q10(a) preamble.
**Archetype:** compare and distinguish, then evaluate. Compare is, but drift against selection is the discrimination this outcome exists to test, and part (b) is the evaluate form of it: three rival explanations, decide which two are selection and say why.
**Trap:** calling drift adaptive. Chapter 7.3 pp.58 and 66 are explicit that drift changes frequencies **at random** and that **alleles fixed by drift are as likely to be harmful as beneficial**; only natural selection consistently produces adaptive evolution. In (c) the scheme wants **both** reasons, so an answer that gives only the founder effect or only camouflage is half an answer. In (a), giving three differences and no similarity misses the first marking point.
**LOs:** 4(d) throughout, genetic drift and the founder effect, and the contrast with 4(c) natural selection. Reach-back to Book 2 in (b), where Theory B turns on a single gene mutation with two phenotypic effects.
**Concepts fused:** (1) drift and selection share the outcome, an allele frequency change; (2) random against non-random; (3) fitness may fall under drift but not under selection; (4) population size dependence; (5) an energy cost as a selection pressure; (6) a pleiotropic mutation conferring a second, advantageous effect; (7) random fixation in small isolated populations as the drift alternative; (8) the founder effect over-representing an allele; (9) camouflage against a light substrate as a second, selective explanation of the same observation; (10) directional selection named from the direction of the shift.

### Question 4

**(a)** *Explain how the small size of the Florida population resulted in a high frequency of these normally rare variant forms.* **[3]**

**1.** **The Florida puma population has gone through a genetic bottleneck, which drastically reduced its size.**

**2.** **As a result, genetic diversity was reduced and some alleles were lost from the gene pool, leaving behind the over-representation of recessive alleles that code for the normally rare variant forms.**

**3.** **The rare and recessive alleles coding for the rare variant forms, that are usually masked in heterozygotes, were more likely to be inherited in homozygous form, allowing their effects such as bent tails or undescended testes to be expressed.**

**4.** There is also reference to **inbreeding: within the isolated puma population, inbreeding became more likely, leading to increased homozygosity and reduced heterozygosity.**

**(b)** *Suggest why the introduced females were taken from Texas and not from points A or B.* **[1]**

**1.** Any one of the following. **Texas is closer to Florida, unlike points A or B which are more distant and far away.**

**2.** **Given their close proximity, Texas and Florida pumas are more closely related / more genetically similar / share a more recent common ancestor.**

**3.** **Texas and Florida have similar climates / habitats / environment.**

**4.** Reference to **availability: Texas has many pumas**.

**(c)** *Suggest why New Zealand robins do not have the red breast trait even though it is present in their Australian robin ancestors.* **[1]**

**1.** **Australian robins might have gained the red breast by a mutation which occurred in the Australian population, after the colonisation of New Zealand by forms which did not possess it**, which is a reference to the **founder effect**.

**2.** **Red-breasted forms might have reached New Zealand where the characteristic was at a selective disadvantage**, or the reverse argument.

**3.** **The frequency of the red breast allele decreased and eventually disappeared from the gene pool.**

**4.** Reference to **genetic drift**, such that red-breast individuals accidentally die.

**(d)** *Suggest how the Maui Creeper appears in Maui-Nui.* **[2]**

**1.** **It emerged in Oahu and when Maui-Nui formed later, it colonised Maui-Nui.**

**2.** **This may be due to honeycreepers having reduced competition for resources in Maui-Nui / better resources or environment available in Maui-Nui.**

**3.** **Leads to founder effect, which has reduced genetic variation compared to the original population.**

**(e)** *Suggest why some iron tolerant plants can be found 50 m or more away from the mine area.* **[1]**
