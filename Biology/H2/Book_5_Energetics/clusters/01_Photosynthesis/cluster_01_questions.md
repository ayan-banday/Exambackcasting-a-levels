paper, year and question number sit in the Sources block at the foot of each one. The stem is the
paper's own wording. Where a stem has been edited it is only so that it stands without the parts of
the original that came before it, and the edit is recorded in the Sources block.

**Book 5 is the highest-value Book in the syllabus.** It measured 23% to 25% of the whole A-level on
the 9477 specimen. This cluster owns learning outcomes 3(a) chloroplast, 3(b), 3(c), 3(d), 3(e) and
the thylakoid half of 3(l), which is six of the Book's twelve outcomes.

**Explain is the commonest command word in this Book at 27.7%, nearly double Describe.** Photosynthesis
is not a describe-the-stages topic in the examiner's hands. Nine of the ten questions below ask you to
account for something rather than to recite it.

**Four real figures.** Every one is the actual image lifted from the paper the question came from, not
a redrawing. Where a figure could not be recovered from the source PDF the paper's own prose
description of it is printed instead and the question is answerable from that description.

**Two MCQs, both from the 9477 specimen paper.** Question 7(a) is Specimen Paper 1 Question 19 and
Question 8(a) is Specimen Paper 1 Question 20, both word for word. The paper awards one mark for the
letter. The drill then asks you to justify all four options, which is where the real work is.

**Cumulative over Books 1 to 4.** Question 5(c) needs the hydrophobic core of a phospholipid bilayer
from Book 1. Question 5(d) needs membrane fluidity and temperature from Book 1. Question 7 needs
active sites and competitive inhibition from Book 1 enzymes. Question 1 needs 70S ribosomes and

**What the marks reward.** Write **photolysis**, never hydrolysis, because a scheme prints "Reject:
hydrolysis" against it. Write **ATP is synthesised**, never "energy is produced", because two schemes
reject that outright. Say **stroma**, not chloroplast, when a stem prints the word precisely. Name
both nouns correctly: the **chlorophyll a is photoactivated** and the **electron is photoexcited**,

a stem says name the structure, because a scheme prints "R! Grana". Quote figures with units off any
graph you are told to refer to.

**Do not quote a number the outcome did not ask for.** Total ATP yields, proton-to-ATP ratios and the
names of the electron transport chain carriers are all excluded in writing by the syllabus. Give the

*With reference to the structure and function of the chloroplast, explain how the efficiency of photosynthesis is maximised in plants.* **[13]**

**Light harvesting.**

**1.** **In the light dependent reaction, harvesting of light energy is maximised with the use of different pigments, for example carotenoids, chlorophyll a and chlorophyll b, which utilise different wavelengths of light.**

**2.** **Energy captured is channelled to photosystems so that efficiency of light energy harvested is maximised.**

**3.** **Photosynthetic pigments involved in photophosphorylation are arranged on the thylakoid membrane at 90 degrees / perpendicular to the direction of sunlight.**

**4.** **This increases the efficiency of physical capture of light energy.**

**5.** **Folding of the thylakoid membrane allows for an increase in surface area.**

**6.** **Which allows for more pigments to be packed on the thylakoid membrane and efficiency maximised.**

**Replenishment of electrons to the photosystems.**

**7.** **The arrangement of cytochromes involved in photophosphorylation allows for a reduction in energy from PSII to the ETC and finally to PSI.**

**8.** **This allows for the electron to be of an energy low enough so it can be accepted by PSI and replenish PSI in the process.**

**9.** **Structural coupling of PSII with photolysis of water allows for the electron replenishment of PSII with the splitting of water and evolving of oxygen as a by-product.**

**ATP synthesis.**

**10.** **Cytochromes in the electron transport chain are coupled with proton pumps.**

**11.** **Free energy that is lost due to the transfer of electrons from one cytochrome to the next is used by proton pumps which actively pump protons from the stroma to the thylakoid space.**

**12.** **Folding of the thylakoid membrane increases the surface area of the membrane.**

**13.** **This allows for a greater number of stalked particles each containing ATP synthase which allows for maximising of ATP synthesis.**

**14.** **Structural positioning of photosynthetic pigments that allow for cyclic photophosphorylation to take place.**

**15.** **Cyclic photophosphorylation in turn facilitates the generation of additional ATP needed so that the light independent reaction can continue.**

**Light independent reaction.**

**16.** **Structurally the thylakoid membrane and the inner chloroplast membrane see to the compartmentalisation of processes, creating a common region, the stroma.**

**17.** **Within which multiple copies of the enzymes involved in the Calvin cycle are held in common, which sees to the regeneration of ribulose bisphosphate (RuBP) in a faster time along with the synthesis of glucose.**

**18.** Every point on this list is a **structure paired with the efficiency it buys**. An answer that describes photosynthesis without naming the structural feature each time answers a different question.

**Source:** CJC Prelim 2021 P3 Q4a, stem and the whole key verbatim, section headings included.
**Archetype:** structure-to-function essay. One of the recurring Paper 3 shapes in the papers, alongside "Describe how plants harness light energy" [15] and "Outline and contrast chemiosmosis in photosynthesis and respiration" [15].
**Trap:** writing the light-dependent reaction as a process narrative and never naming a structure, which loses every mark on this list; organising by stage rather than by feature, which an examiner comment on a related 15-mark essay names as the reason almost nobody earned the quality-of-written-communication mark.
**LOs:** 3(a) chloroplast structure; 3(b) pigments; 3(c) the light-dependent reactions with reference to chloroplast structure; 3(d) the Calvin cycle; 3(l) chemiosmosis.
**Concepts fused:** (1) several pigments widening the absorbed spectrum; (2) funnelling to the reaction centre; (3) pigment orientation relative to incoming light; (4) thylakoid folding and surface area; (5) the energy ladder along the carriers; (6) photolysis coupled to PSII; (7) carriers doubling as proton pumps; (8) surface area setting ATP synthase copy number; (9) cyclic flow topping up ATP; (10) compartmentalisation concentrating the Calvin cycle enzymes. Note the one marking point this question shares with Question 1(c), thylakoid folding for surface area; everything else on this list is new.

### Question 10

**(a)** *Explain how the concentration of intermediates of the Calvin cycle is affected by dinitrophenol.* **[3]**

**1.** **Fewer H+ diffuse through ATP synthase, that is the proton gradient is less steep, so less ATP is produced during the light-dependent reaction.**

**2.** **Less ATP is available for use in the Calvin cycle during carbon reduction and regeneration of RuBP.**

**3.** **Accumulation of 3PGA and reduced production of 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate / G3P / TP / RuBP.**

**4.** Note what dinitrophenol does **not** do. It does not block electron flow, so the chain keeps running; it makes the membrane **leaky to protons**, so the gradient collapses. That is the uncoupler case, and it is one of the three respiratory-poison outcomes worth holding.

**(b)** *Predict and explain the effect on the concentration of RuBP if DCPIP becomes reduced instead of the natural hydrogen acceptor.* **[1]**

**1.** **RuBP concentration will decrease as it is used up in carbon fixation but not regenerated.**

**(c)** *Suggest how paraquat results in the death of a plant.* **[2]**

**1.** **Prevents cyclic and non-cyclic photophosphorylation.**

**2.** **No ATP and NADPH for the Calvin cycle / conversion of GP to TP.**

**3.** **No glucose made for respiration.**

**Source:** IJC Prelim 2017 P3 (9744) Q2b, stem and key verbatim (a); EJC Prelim 2022 P2 Q7d(iii), stem and key verbatim (b), with the DCPIP definition folded into the stem so it stands without the earlier parts; JJC Prelim 2014 P2 Q6e, also filed as IJC Prelim 2017 P2 (9648) Q6d, stem and key verbatim (c).
**Archetype:** apply the mechanism to a novel inhibitor. The papers set 45 records on respiratory poisons and uncouplers; the compound names are decoration and the three consequences are pure chemiosmosis reasoning.
**Trap:** treating an uncoupler as a chain blocker, which predicts the wrong direction for electron flow; predicting that RuBP rises in (b) when the natural acceptor is bypassed, when the Calvin cycle still consumes it and the regeneration supply has been cut; in (c), stopping at "no photosynthesis" instead of following the chain through to no glucose for respiration.
**LOs:** 3(c) the light-dependent reactions; 3(d) the Calvin cycle; 3(l) chemiosmosis, all applied rather than recalled.
**Concepts fused:** (1) an uncoupler as a proton leak; (2) gradient steepness setting ATP output; (3) which Calvin intermediate accumulates when ATP runs short; (4) an artificial electron acceptor diverting electrons from NADP; (5) consumption without regeneration as the reason a pool empties; (6) blocking PSI killing both routes at once; (7) the plant dying of respiratory substrate shortage, not of no photosynthesis directly.

### Question 11

**(a)** *Describe what is occurring at point A.* **[2]**

**1.** **A is the compensation point**, which is the **light intensity at which respiration rate equals photosynthetic rate**.

**2.** The **products of photosynthesis, glucose and oxygen, are used up for cellular respiration**, while the **products of aerobic respiration, water and carbon dioxide, are used up for photosynthesis**.

**3.** There is **no net gain in dry mass**, and thus **no growth of the plant**.

**4.** Marking is **point 1 and either point 2 or point 3**.

**(b)** *Explain why the CO2 assimilation rate plateaus at high light intensity.* **[3]**

**1.** The **light saturation point has been reached**, and at this point the **maximum rate of chlorophyll a photoactivation is occurring at both photosystems**.

**2.** This means that the **maximum rate of production of ATP and NADPH is occurring**, and therefore the **maximum CO2 assimilation rate is attained in the Calvin cycle**.

**3.** At higher light intensities, **light intensity is no longer a limiting factor**, as a further increase does not increase the rate, and **other factors are now limiting, for example temperature**.

**(c)** *State the total volume of CO2 used for photosynthesis when light intensity is no longer limiting.* **[1]**

**1.** **14 plus 3 equals 17 arbitrary units.**

**2.** This is the point of the question. The plotted line is **net** assimilation, so the **gross** figure is the plateau height **plus the respiration rate read off the negative intercept at zero light**. Missing the intercept is the "graph does not start at the origin" failure that an examiner comment names by name.

**(d)** *Which statement can be concluded from the mustard-plant graph?* **[1]**

**1.** The answer is **C**.

**2.** **C is correct.** For the high-illumination plants the curve has **plateaued by 150 units**, and a plateau means the plotted variable has stopped limiting.

**3.** **A is wrong.** Below the compensation point the high-illumination plants have the **higher** respiration rate, so they give out **more** carbon dioxide, not less.

**4.** **B is wrong, and it is the reverse of the truth.** The **low**-illumination plants cross zero at the **lower** light intensity, because shade-adapted tissue respires more slowly and needs less photosynthesis to match it.

**5.** **D is wrong.** Over 20 to 50 units the two curves are clearly separated, so carbon fixation is not similar.

**(e)** *Suggest reasons for the negative percentage change in dissolved oxygen at 0.00 mol dm-3 NaHCO3.* **[2]**

**1.** **Low photosynthesis as low CO2.**

**2.** **Respiration uses up oxygen.**

**3.** So the plant is **below its compensation point**: with no added carbon dioxide the rate of photosynthesis falls below the rate of respiration, and the **net** change in dissolved oxygen is a loss.

**Source:** YIJC Prelim 2023 P2 Q1b(ii), stem, Fig. 1.2 description and key verbatim (a); YIJC Prelim 2023 P2 Q1b(i), stem and key verbatim (b); IJC Prelim 2018 P2 Q6c(i), stem and key verbatim (c), with Fig. 6.3's own numbers folded into the (a) stem so the three parts share one figure; MI Prelim 2017 P1 Q29, stem, graph description and all four options verbatim (d); HCI Prelim 2022 P2 Q8b(ii), stem and key verbatim (e), with the apparatus paragraph folded into the stem. No figure here could be recovered from a PDF, so each paper's own description is printed and every part is answerable from it.
**Archetype:** compensation point read off a net-exchange curve. The papers set 31 records on compensation point and 19 on sun and shade leaves, and the specimen built a 10-mark plan on both.
**Trap:** reading the plateau height as the gross rate when the axis is a net rate; getting the sun and shade compensation points the wrong way round, which is option B; explaining a negative dissolved-oxygen change as "the plant died" rather than as respiration outrunning photosynthesis.
**LOs:** 3(e) limiting factors, including compensation point and sun and shade plants; 3(k) the rate of respiration, since the negative region is respiration showing through.
**Concepts fused:** (1) compensation point as equal rates; (2) no net gain in dry mass; (3) saturation and photoactivation at maximum rate; (4) net against gross gas exchange; (5) reading a non-zero intercept; (6) shade tissue having the lower respiration rate and therefore the lower compensation point; (7) a negative net oxygen change as evidence of being below the compensation point.

### Question 12

**(a)** *Which statements about the expected results of the DCPIP experiment are correct?* **[1]**

**1.** The answer is **B, 1 and 2 only**. **Only 23.17% of the cohort got this right.**

**2.** **Statement 1 is true.** Different wavelengths are absorbed to different extents, so the rate of electron transfer to DCPIP, and therefore the rate of decolourisation, differs.

**3.** **Statement 2 is true.** Red light is absorbed strongly by chlorophyll a and green light is largely reflected, so decolourisation is faster in red.

**4.** **Statement 3 is false, and this is the trap.** Examiner comment: **"three quarters of the students selected the incorrect option D. There is a need to consider the colour of chloroplasts, which are green. The initial DCPIP-chloroplast mixture is blue-green. DCPIP turns from blue to colourless due to oxidation, when it accepts electrons from PSII. Chloroplasts are still present and the end point should have been a green suspension."**

**5.** So: **the dye goes blue to colourless; the suspension goes blue-green to green.** Read what is in the tube, not what is in the definition.

**(b)** *Explain the tube A colour change using your knowledge of the light-dependent reactions.* **[3]**

**1.** **DCPIP acts like a final hydrogen ion and electron acceptor, like NADP+ in non-cyclic photophosphorylation of the light-dependent reaction.**

**2.** **When light and chloroplast suspension are present, electrons from photolysis of water and photoexcited electrons from PSII are passed through the electron transport chain in the thylakoid membranes and finally to NADP+ or DCPIP to reduce them.**

**3.** **When the dye is reduced, the colour of the dye changes from blue to colourless.**

**4.** **DCPIP is thus an indicator of whether the light reaction, electron flow through the ETC between PSII and PSI, has taken place.**

**5.** This is **Hill's experiment**: isolated chloroplasts illuminated with no carbon dioxide evolved oxygen **only when an electron acceptor was added**.

**(c)** *Explain why the chloroplast suspensions were kept in the dark until required.* **[1]**

**1.** So that **light is not absorbed / to stop photosynthesis / to stop photoactivation / to stop the light-dependent stage** before timing begins.

**(d)** *Explain the differences between the results for red light and for green light.* **[3]**

Any three of the following, all written as red light against green light, or the reverse argument.

**1.** **More light and energy is absorbed by chlorophyll.**

**2.** **So there is more photoactivation and more electrons are emitted.**

**3.** **More electrons are transferred to DCPIP, at a faster rate.**

**4.** **There is more, and faster, decolourisation of DCPIP.**

**5.** The colorimeter reading is **absorbance of the suspension**, and it **falls** as DCPIP is reduced, so a **steeper fall means a faster light-dependent reaction**. Red falls from 1.50 to about 0.70 in four minutes; green falls only to about 1.44.

**Source:** School (HCI) Prelim 2024 P1 Q22, also set as HCI Prelim 2024 P1 Q22 and DHS Prelim 2023 P1 Q25, stem, all three statements, all four options, the percentage correct and the full examiner comment verbatim (a); JJC Prelim 2014 P2 Q6d, stem and key verbatim (b), with the tube A context named in the stem so the part stands alone; NYJC Prelim 2025 P2 Q7a(i), stem, Fig. 7.1 description and key verbatim (c); NYJC Prelim 2025 P2 Q7a(iii), stem and key verbatim (d). The colorimeter graph could not be recovered from a PDF, so the paper's own description is printed in the (c) stem and both parts are answerable from it.
**Archetype:** the redox-dye method, which isolates the light-dependent reaction from the Calvin cycle. This is one of two places in the whole Biology the papers where a real cohort failure rate is recorded, and it is three quarters.
**Trap:** the colourless endpoint; describing DCPIP as replacing water rather than replacing NADP; forgetting that keeping the suspension dark is about controlling the **start time**, not about protecting the chloroplasts; explaining the graph without saying which direction absorbance moves.
**LOs:** 3(c) the light-dependent reactions; 3(b) pigments and wavelength; 3(e) carrying out investigations on the rate of photosynthesis.
**Concepts fused:** (1) an artificial electron acceptor substituting for NADP; (2) where in the chain the dye intercepts; (3) reduction of the dye as the colour change; (4) the suspension colour against the dye colour; (5) wavelength setting the rate of photoactivation; (6) darkness as a way of fixing time zero; (7) absorbance falling as the dye is reduced.

### Question 13

**(a)** *Calculate the mean rate of decrease in the concentration of PGA between 200 s and 350 s, to two significant figures.* **[2]** [INFERRED: no official key for the 9477 specimen]

**1.** **Change in PGA concentration = 2.0 minus 0.2 = 1.8 arbitrary units**; **time interval = 350 minus 200 = 150 s**.

**2.** **Mean rate of decrease = 1.8 divided by 150 = 0.012 arbitrary units s-1**, to 2 significant figures.

**3.** This is the specimen's own calculation, on a paper whose the papers otherwise shows Calculate at only 2.4%. **Show the working, carry the unit through, and obey the significant-figures instruction**, all three of which are separately marked.

**(b)** *Describe the effects of heat stress and of spraying MeJA on net photosynthesis.* **[2]**

**1.** **Heat stress treatment results in a reduction of mean net photosynthesis rate from 12.8 to 8.2 micromol CO2 m-2 s-1.**

**2.** **The spraying of MeJA increases the mean net photosynthesis rate from 8.2 to 19.4 micromol CO2 m-2 s-1, reversing the effect of heat stress.**

**3.** Two marks, two figures each with its unit, and no mechanism asked for. The command word is **describe**.

**(c)** *Describe the differences in rates of carbon dioxide uptake by barley and sugar cane at 25 °C.* **[4]**

**Two trends plus corresponding data, maximum 4 marks.**

**1.** **Sugar cane reaches maximum rate of CO2 uptake at a lower CO2 concentration, or barley reaches maximum rate of CO2 uptake at a higher CO2 concentration.**

**2.** Quote data: **maximum rate for sugar cane is reached at 100 ppm against barley at 320 ppm at 25 °C.**

**3.** **Sugar cane reaches a higher maximum rate of carbon dioxide uptake than barley.**

**4.** Quote data: **34 micrograms m-2 h-1 for sugar cane against 20 micrograms m-2 h-1 for barley.**

**5.** **The rate of CO2 uptake by sugar cane increases faster than the rate of CO2 uptake by barley as CO2 concentration increases.**

**6.** Quote data: **the rate for sugar cane increases from 0 to 34 micrograms m-2 h-1 while barley increases from 0 to 5.5 micrograms m-2 h-1 as CO2 concentration increases from 0 ppm to 100 ppm.**

**7.** **Sugar cane has a lower CO2 compensation point than barley.**

**8.** Quote data: **the CO2 compensation point for sugar cane is 10 ppm against barley at 50 ppm at 25 °C.**
