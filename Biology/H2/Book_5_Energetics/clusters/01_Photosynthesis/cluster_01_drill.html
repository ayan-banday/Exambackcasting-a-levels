Cyanobacteria are photosynthetic prokaryotes that carry out both photosynthesis and respiration. Under favourable conditions they may undergo rapid population growth, forming harmful algal blooms in aquatic ecosystems, which can disrupt oxygen levels and produce toxins. To explore potential strategies for managing such blooms, researchers tested the effects of inhibitor Y, a compound known to disrupt the light-dependent reactions of photosynthesis. After inhibitor Y was administered, researchers observed a progressive decline in net oxygen evolution. Both PSI and PSII electron carriers remained in a reduced state for prolonged periods of time.

Figure not recovered. Described from the source paper: Fig. 7.1 shows the light-dependent stage in the thylakoid membrane. Photosystem II sits on the left, with a manganese-containing enzyme catalysing the photolysis of water beside it and oxygen leaving as a by-product. An electron transport chain runs from photosystem II across the membrane to photosystem I, pumping protons into the thylakoid space. From photosystem I the electron passes to ferredoxin and then to NADP reductase, which reduces NADP+ to NADPH on the stroma side.

(a) With reference to Fig. 7.1, state the enzyme which inhibitor Y acts on and explain why there is a progressive decline in net oxygen evolution. [3]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (a)

(a) State the enzyme which inhibitor Y acts on and explain the progressive decline in net oxygen evolution. [3]

Either of two routes is accepted.

1. Route one: NADP reductase.

2. Inhibition of NADP reductase prevents the transfer of electrons from the electron carrier to the final electron acceptor NADP+ / NADP.

3. Electron carriers remained reduced in PSII and PSI, photolysis of water is inhibited, resulting in a decrease in oxygen production.

4. Route two: manganese (Mn)-containing enzyme.

5. The Mn-containing enzyme catalyses the photolysis of water to produce electrons for the electron deficit in P680.

6. Inhibition of the Mn-containing enzyme inhibits photolysis of water, so oxygen will not be produced as a by-product, hence a decline in net oxygen evolution.

7. Examiner comment: some indicated cytochrome c, which is found in the mitochondria for aerobic respiration, not photosynthesis; some indicated that oxygen is the final electron acceptor, which is true for respiration, but oxygen is a by-product of the photolysis of water in photosynthesis.

(b) A student suggested that a small amount of oxygen is still produced at high concentrations of inhibitor Y due to cyclic photophosphorylation. Explain whether this statement is correct. [2]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (b)

(b) Explain whether the student's cyclic photophosphorylation suggestion is correct. [2]

1. The student is incorrect, because cyclic photophosphorylation only involves PSI and does not involve PSII.

2. Hence there will be no photolysis of water at PSII, and oxygen will not be produced as a by-product.

(c) In light-dependent reactions, photoactivation of chlorophyll results in the synthesis of ATP. Describe the photoactivation of chlorophyll in photosystem II. [3]

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (c)

(c) Describe the photoactivation of chlorophyll in photosystem II. [3]

1. A photon of light strikes a photosynthetic pigment in photosystem II (PS II).

2. Energy in the photon of light is passed on from one pigment molecule to the neighbouring pigment molecule via resonance energy transfer until it reaches the special chlorophyll a (P680) in the reaction centre of PS II.

3. An electron in special chlorophyll a / P680 is excited and boosted to a higher energy level, and this excited electron is accepted by a primary electron acceptor.

(d) Outline the fate of each product of the photolysis of water in the light-dependent reaction. [3]

---

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer - part (d)

(d) Outline the fate of each product of photolysis in the light-dependent reaction. [3]

1. Oxygen used by cells for aerobic respiration.

2. Excess oxygen is released out of the plant through stomata.

3. Protons diffuse through ATP synthase from the thylakoid space to the stroma to generate ATP.

4. Protons combine with electrons from PS-I and NADP to form NADPH.

5. Electrons are used to replace electrons lost from PS-II.

6. Electrons are transported along the electron transport chain by electron carriers of progressively lower energy levels.

7. In cyclic photophosphorylation, electrons from PS-I go back to PS-I.

8. In non-cyclic photophosphorylation, electrons from PS-I combine with protons and NADP to form NADPH.

9. The three products are electrons, protons and oxygen, and the proton fate is the one candidates omit.

Answer

Outline the process of photophosphorylation and its advantages. [10]

Section A, process, maximum 7 marks.

1. A1 Reference to the thylakoid membrane as the site of (non-cyclic / cyclic) photophosphorylation in chloroplasts.

2. A2 Reference to the presence of photosystems / electron transport chains (ETC) / series of electron carriers / ATP synthase found on the thylakoid membrane.

3. A3 Reference to the passing of photoexcited electrons from the primary electron acceptor of photosystem II to the first ETC / from the primary electron acceptor of photosystem I to the second ETC.

8. A8 Reference to ATP being synthesised by ATP synthase from ADP and phosphate.

Section B, advantages, minimum 2 marks.

9. B1 Reference to photophosphorylation allowing the conversion of solar / light energy into chemical energy.

10. B2 Reference to photophosphorylation producing ATP for the light-independent reactions / Calvin cycle of photosynthesis.

11. B3 Reference to photophosphorylation producing oxygen (gas) for cellular / aerobic respiration / to sustain life on Earth.

12. QWC, compulsory mark: at least one point from A and one point from B.

13. Note what the scheme does not reward: no total ATP yield, no proton-to-ATP ratio, and no named carriers beyond ferredoxin and the cytochrome complex, both of which the scheme itself uses.

Answer - part (d)

(d) Explain how rising sea temperatures affect photosynthesis in zooxanthellae. [4]

1. The increase in sea temperature causes damage to thylakoid membranes, with increased membrane fluidity, so the proton gradient across the thylakoid membrane cannot be maintained.

2. Less ATP is produced, so the rate of the Calvin cycle decreases or stops, as PGA cannot be reduced to G3P.

4. Electron flow in the electron transport chain is faster to release more energy for proton pumps to pump H+ into the thylakoid space to restore the proton gradient.

5. The stem rules denaturation out in writing, so an answer built on denatured rubisco earns nothing. The mark is for leakiness collapsing the gradient, which is Book 1 membrane fluidity applied here.
