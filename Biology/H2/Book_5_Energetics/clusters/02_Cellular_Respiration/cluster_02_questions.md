**(b)** *Name and outline two respiratory processes that depend on the proteins found in the mitochondrial matrix.* **[6]** [INFERRED: no official key for the 9477 specimen; the outlines below are built from LO 3(g)'s own wording and cross-checked against HCI Prelim 2021 P2 Q8b to Q8c and NYJC Prelim 2025 P3 Q2a]

**1. Name of process 1: the link reaction**, the oxidative decarboxylation of pyruvate.

**2. Outline:** **pyruvate is actively transported from the cytoplasm into the matrix**.

**3.** It is **decarboxylated, removing one carbon as carbon dioxide**, and **dehydrogenated by a dehydrogenase, so NAD is reduced to reduced NAD**.

**4.** The remaining **2-carbon acetyl group combines with coenzyme A to form acetyl coenzyme A**, which then enters the Krebs cycle. The enzymes of the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex are matrix proteins.

**5. Name of process 2: the Krebs cycle.**

**6. Outline:** the **2-carbon acetyl group from acetyl CoA combines with 4-carbon oxaloacetate to form 6-carbon citrate**.

**7.** Citrate is then **decarboxylated and dehydrogenated in a series of enzyme-controlled steps**, releasing **two molecules of carbon dioxide**, **reducing three NAD and one FAD**, and producing **one ATP by substrate-level phosphorylation per turn**.

**8.** **Oxaloacetate is regenerated so the cycle can repeat**, and the **reduced coenzymes pass their electrons to the electron transport chain** on the inner membrane. All of the cycle enzymes are matrix proteins.

**9. The carbon dioxide budget, because LO 3(g) asks for products formed.** Per glucose the link reaction runs **twice**, giving **2 CO2**, and the Krebs cycle runs **twice**, giving **4 CO2**. That is **6 CO2 per glucose**, with **6 H2O**. **Your chapter's Overview page on 5.2 page 5 prints 4 CO2 and it is wrong**: it has taken the Krebs figure and labelled it as the total. **(c)** *Outline how ATP is synthesised in the Krebs cycle.* **[2]**

**1.** **Substrate level phosphorylation.**

**2.** **Coupling of the (exergonic) dephosphorylation of a respiratory substrate to the phosphorylation of GDP to form GTP.** **Accept ATP.**

### Question 3

**(a)** *Outline briefly the process of glycolysis for one molecule of glucose.* **[3]**

**1.** **Glucose undergoes phosphorylation / is activated by ATP.**

**2.** **And is oxidised.**

**3.** **To give 2 molecules of NADH.**

**4.** **And substrate level phosphorylation.**

**5.** **To give 4 ATP / net of 2 ATP per glucose.**

**6.** **And two molecules of pyruvate.**

**7.** Note the depth. LO 3(f) asks for **location, raw materials used and products formed** and nothing else. The ten named intermediates, the enzyme names and the isomerisation are **excluded in writing**, which deletes four pages of your chapter and a whole Concept Check.

**(b)** *Suggest why the phosphorylated glucose molecules do not diffuse out of the cell.* **[3]**

**1.** **Phosphorylated glucose is too big to diffuse through (transient pores in) the cell surface membrane / the channel proteins that transport glucose.**

**2.** **Phosphorylated glucose is polar / hydrophilic, hence unable to diffuse across the hydrophobic cell surface membrane.**

**3.** **No specific transport / carrier / channel protein exists for phosphorylated glucose**, or **phosphorylated glucose is not complementary in shape, hence unable to bind to the carrier protein that transports glucose by facilitated diffusion**.

**4.** Any valid point, for example that **phosphorylated glucose is used up in glycolysis as soon as it is made**.

**5.** Any three.

**(c)** *Suggest why the lack of membrane-bound organelles in mature red blood cells makes them more susceptible.* **[2]**

**1.** **Mature red blood cells do not have mitochondria.** This point is **compulsory**.

**2.** **Pyruvate cannot be oxidised further to produce the bulk of the ATP yield in aerobic respiration**, or **they cannot carry out oxidative phosphorylation / chemiosmosis to synthesise large amounts of ATP**.

**3.** **Red blood cells depend on glycolysis for all the energy needs of cellular processes.**

### Question 5

**(a)** *Give the order of the electron carriers in this electron transport chain.* **[1]**

**1.** The order of the electron carriers in the chain is **Y, X, W, Z**.

**2.** The reasoning: an inhibitor leaves everything **upstream of the block reduced** because electrons pile up behind it, and everything **downstream oxidised** because nothing reaches them. Inhibitor B leaves only Y reduced, so Y is first. Inhibitor A leaves X and Y reduced, so X is second. Inhibitor C leaves W, X and Y reduced, so W is third. Z is oxidised in every row, so Z is last.

**(b)** *Explain why NAD cannot be regenerated from reduced NAD in mitochondria in the absence of oxygen.* **[3]** [INFERRED: no official key for the 9477 specimen; cross-checked against NJC Prelim 2022 P2 Q7c, which sets the identical question]

**1.** **Oxygen is the final (terminal) electron acceptor at the end of the electron transport chain**, where it combines with electrons and protons to form water.

**2.** Without oxygen the **electrons cannot be removed from the last carrier in the chain**, so **all the carriers remain reduced and the chain stops working**.

**3.** Reduced NAD can therefore **not be oxidised, because there is nowhere for it to pass its electrons to**, so **NAD is not regenerated**.

**4.** As a consequence no proton gradient is maintained, and the **link reaction and Krebs cycle also stop because they need a supply of oxidised NAD**.

**(c)** *Explain how the proton gradient that drives ATP synthesis is produced.* **[3]** [INFERRED: no official key for the 9477 specimen; cross-checked against TJC Prelim 2023 P2 Q6b, whose key gives the same three steps]

**1.** **Reduced NAD and reduced FAD are oxidised at the inner mitochondrial membrane and release electrons to the electron transport chain.**

**2.** The **electrons are passed along a series of carriers, each at a lower energy level, so energy is released at each transfer.**

**3.** This **energy is used by the carriers to actively pump protons (H+) from the matrix across the inner membrane into the intermembrane space.**

**4.** Because the **inner membrane is impermeable to protons**, they accumulate there, so the **intermembrane space develops a higher H+ concentration and lower pH than the matrix**, which is an **electrochemical (proton) gradient** across the membrane.

**(d)** *With reference to Figure 7.1, outline how the proton gradient drives ATP synthesis.* **[3]** [INFERRED: no official key for the 9477 specimen]

**1.** **Protons diffuse down the electrochemical gradient from the intermembrane space back into the matrix**, and because the rest of the membrane is impermeable to them they **can only pass through the channel in the ATP synthase**. This is **chemiosmosis**.

**2.** The **flow of protons through the channel causes the central stalk of the ATP synthase to rotate.**

**3.** The rotation **changes the shape of the catalytic sites in the head of the enzyme that projects into the matrix**, and this **conformational change catalyses the joining of ADP and inorganic phosphate to form ATP**. This is **oxidative phosphorylation**.

**4.** Note the ceiling this sets. LO 3(c) and LO 3(h) both exclude "detailed mechanism of action of ATP synthase", yet **the specimen put ATP synthase on a figure and asked about rotation and conformational change.** So: recognise the enzyme on a diagram, say the stalk rotates and the catalytic sites change shape, and stop there. Do not go further into the rotor.

**(e)** *Suggest why the inner membrane contains a very high proportion of cardiolipin.* **[1]**

**1.** **So H+ cannot move through / must move through ATP synthase.**

**2.** **To maintain the proton gradient.**

**3.** Any one.

**(f)** *Describe one difference between chemiosmosis in mitochondria and in chloroplasts.* **[1]**

Any one of the following paired differences earns the mark.

| feature | mitochondria | chloroplast |
|---|---|---|
| process | **oxidative phosphorylation** | **photophosphorylation** |
| location | **takes place at the inner mitochondrial membrane / cristae** | **takes place at the thylakoid membrane** |
| source of protons | **reduced NAD / reduced FAD give H+ and electrons** | **photolysis of water / PSI / special chlorophyll a give H+ and electrons** |
| electrochemical gradient of protons | **H+ accumulates in the intermembrane space** | **H+ accumulates in the thylakoid lumen / space** |
| final electron acceptor | **oxygen** | **NADP** |
| end product | **formation of water** | **reduced NADP** |
