## PART A: 8.1 AND 8.2, THE GREENHOUSE MECHANISM, HUMAN ACTIVITIES AND CARBON FOOTPRINTS

### 1. The greenhouse effect is a chain, and the gases re-emit rather than retain

- 1.1 **Shortwave solar** in → absorbed by the surface → **re-emitted as longwave infrared** → absorbed by **greenhouse gases** → **re-emitted downward** → temperature rises. "Retained" loses the mark. Outcome **limited to CO₂ and methane**.
- 1.2 Three properties decide how much a gas warms: **concentration**, **atmospheric lifetime**, **global warming potential**, which is why emissions are added as **CO₂ equivalent (CO₂e)**.

### 2. Three human activities drive it, and each must end in a named gas

- 2.1 **Burning fossil fuels**, linked to increasing energy usage → **CO₂**.
- 2.2 **Clearing forests** → **CO₂** twice, since a forest is both a **carbon store** released and a **carbon sink** removed.
- 2.3 **Food choices**, meaning more meat → **methane** from enteric fermentation, manure, rice paddies and landfill, all **oxygen-poor**, so one mechanism: **anaerobic decomposition** (Core Idea 3).
- 2.4 **Direct against indirect** contribution is the Book's highest-value distinction, asked in both directions.

### 3. Forests and mangroves are sinks because photosynthesis fixes carbon into biomolecules

- 3.1 A **carbon sink** absorbs more than it releases, a **carbon source** the reverse. The fixing step is **photosynthesis** (Core Idea 3), storing carbon as cellulose, lignin, starch, protein, lipid (Core Idea 1).
- 3.2 **Mangrove blue carbon**: biomass captures it above ground, roots trap carbon-rich sediment, litter accumulates, **waterlogged oxygen-poor soils slow decomposition**.
- 3.3 **Mangrove climate resilience** is the outcome's second half: roots cut wave height and stabilise shorelines. Keep **mitigating the cause** apart from **adapting to the consequence**.

### 4. Carbon footprints are ranked, not recalled

- 4.1 A **carbon footprint** totals emissions across a whole **life cycle**, indirect included, so wind and hydroelectric exceed zero.
- 4.2 The outcome asks only for **relative differences** across **fossil fuels, hydroelectric, nuclear, solar, wind, bioethanol**. Rank from the supplied table, quoting its units.
- 4.3 **Bioethanol** needs the argument, not a number: the crop fixed the CO₂ it later releases, so near **carbon neutral**, but growing and processing leave a **carbon debt**.
- 4.4 Food production splits **animal-based against plant-based**, ruminants highest through enteric fermentation plus land and feed demand.

## PART B: 8.3 AND 8.4, EFFECTS ON THE ENVIRONMENT, HABITATS AND ORGANISMS

### 1. The effects outcome is a checklist of eight, each examined as a chain

- 1.1 The eight: **polar ice caps melting**, **sea levels rising**, **extreme weather**, **fresh water stress**, **fish and insect migration**, **coral reef stress**, **seagrass and mangrove stress**, **greenhouse gases from frozen organic matter**.
- 1.2 Sea level rises mainly by **thermal expansion**, meltwater from **land ice** second. Melting **sea ice** raises nothing, since it already displaces its own mass.

### 2. Two positive feedback loops run through it, and a negative one must be spotted cold

- 2.1 **Ice-albedo**: melting exposes dark water → **albedo** falls → absorption rises → more warming → more melting.
- 2.2 **Permafrost**: thaw lets **microbes decompose** frozen organic matter, releasing **CO₂ and methane** (Core Idea 3), which warms further.
- 2.3 A **negative feedback** must be identified in an unseen diagram, such as more evaporation giving more summer cloud.

### 3. Coral is stressed by two separate mechanisms and the order matters

- 3.1 **Bleaching**: heat disrupts **photosynthesis** in the **zooxanthellae** (Core Idea 3) → they stop supplying food → coral **expels them alive** → starvation. Reversible if the water cools.
- 3.2 **Acidification**: dissolved CO₂ → carbonic acid → H⁺ → H⁺ removes **carbonate ions** → coral cannot **deposit** CaCO₃, which it deposits and never absorbs.

### 4. Warming is uneven, and endotherm against ectotherm decides the organism-level answer

- 4.1 Greatest at the **poles**, in **winter**, and in **minimum** rather than maximum temperatures.
- 4.2 **Stress** is defined by its link to evolution: any environmental change that **reduces fitness** (Core Idea 4).
- 4.3 An ectotherm's temperature follows ambient; endotherms show a measured **decrease in body size**, since larger size lowers surface area to volume.

### 5. Species track the climate, and where they cannot they mismatch or speciate

- 5.1 **Range shift**: poleward and upward, bounded by **minimum temperature**, only as far as **dispersal allows**.
- 5.2 **Phenological mismatch**: two interacting species read **different cues**, one tracking temperature and one not.
- 5.3 **Niche occupation** changes through shifted **competition** and **predator-prey** relationships, one case often carrying both.
- 5.4 Water stress acts by **osmosis** (Core Idea 1): drought and salinisation close stomata, so less CO₂ reaches the **Calvin cycle** (Core Idea 3).
- 5.5 Habitat bands contracting and re-expanding isolate populations, which is **allopatric speciation by geographical isolation** (Core Idea 4).

## PART C: 8.4 AND 8.6, INSECTS AND MOSQUITO-BORNE DISEASE

### 1. The insect outcome names two mechanisms and both are enzyme kinetics

- 1.1 **Increased metabolism**: insects are ectotherms, so within limits higher temperature raises **enzyme-catalysed reaction rate** (Core Idea 1).
- 1.2 **Narrow temperature tolerance**: past the optimum heat **damages the enzymes** regulating metabolism, and beyond the **upper and lower lethal limits** it dies. So the response is a **curve**, not a line.

### 2. Warming affects insects in five listed ways

- 2.1 Extended **geographical ranges**, bounded by the upper lethal limit and by dryness.
- 2.2 Increased **overwintering success**, since warming raises winter temperatures most.
- 2.3 Faster **population growth**, more generations a year, longer development seasons.
- 2.4 Changed **pest-host synchrony**, because heat-stressed hosts are more vulnerable.
- 2.5 Increased **poleward migration**.

### 3. *Aedes aegypti* is an example rather than an endpoint, and its life cycle is required

- 3.1 Four stages, **egg, larva, pupa, adult**, split **aquatic** (larva, pupa) and **terrestrial** (egg, adult).
- 3.2 Temperature acts on every stage, giving an **optimum around 25 °C** with survival falling on both sides. Near the ceiling eggs still hatch but larvae die, so the cycle never completes.

### 4. Warming spreads the disease beyond the tropics, through vector and pathogen

- 4.1 Vector effects: more **activity**, more frequent **biting**, better **egg and adult survival**, **expanded range**.
- 4.2 Pathogen effect, marked separately: warming **shortens the time the pathogen needs inside the vector** to become infective.
- 4.3 Range limits: a transmission floor for **dengue**, a higher one for **malaria**, a lethal ceiling, plus **latitude, winter temperature and altitude**. Warming pushes all outward, so answers finish **beyond the tropics**.
- 4.4 **Rainfall does not act in one direction**: torrential rain washes out eggs, low rainfall leaves river pools and forces water storage.
- 4.5 Not all spread is climate. **People, not mosquitoes, move the virus** between communities.

## PART D: 8.5 BIODIVERSITY, BIOMEDICINES AND FOOD SUPPLY

### 1. Biodiversity has three levels and climate change reaches each differently

- 1.1 **Genetic** diversity falls to directional selection and shrinking gene pools; **species** diversity to phenological mismatch and habitat loss; **ecosystem** diversity to biome shifts.
- 1.2 Species diversity splits into **species richness** and **species evenness**, and schemes police which you quoted.
- 1.3 Genetic diversity is the **raw material for adaptation** (Core Idea 4), so losing it is a natural selection problem, not just a conservation one.

### 2. The outcome is about the tropics specifically

- 2.1 Biodiversity concentrates near the equator, with tropical rainforests holding at least half of terrestrial species.
- 2.2 They are more vulnerable because they already live **close to their optimal temperature**, not because the tropics warm faster.

### 3. Two named losses follow, biomedicines and genetic diversity for food

- 3.1 **Biomedicines** need named organisms: the **rosy periwinkle**, the **marine sponge** behind **AZT**, the extinct **gastric brooding frogs**.
- 3.2 The food argument in fixed order: **monocultures** are pest-susceptible → **wild crop relatives** kept resistance genes that bred-for-yield varieties lost → **biological insurance** → most live in the **tropics**.

### 4. Food security has four dimensions, reached directly and indirectly

- 4.1 **Availability**, **accessibility**, **utilisation**, **stability**, and an extended answer is structured on those four.
- 4.2 **Direct** impacts are abiotic: temperature, drought, rainfall timing, storms. **Indirect** impacts run through **pests**, **weeds**, **pathogens** and **pollinators**.
- 4.3 Three production systems, **crops**, **livestock** and **fisheries**. In fisheries everything turns on **ectothermy** and **dissolved oxygen**, giving a **poleward redistribution** of catch away from the tropics.

## One-line mental map

**A:** Greenhouse gases re-emit outgoing longwave downward → fossil fuels and deforestation give CO₂, meat and anaerobic decay give methane → forests and mangroves are sinks because photosynthesis fixes carbon and waterlogged soil stops it decaying → footprints are life-cycle totals you rank.

**B:** Eight named effects, each a chain → thermal expansion not meltwater, land ice not sea ice → ice-albedo and permafrost are positive feedback loops → coral bleaches when zooxanthellae photosynthesis fails → species shift poleward, mismatch, or speciate.

**C:** Insects are ectotherms, so metabolism is enzyme kinetics with an optimum and lethal limits → warming extends ranges, overwintering, generations and migration → *Aedes aegypti*'s four stages share that optimum → vector activity, biting, survival and range rise, the pathogen matures faster, disease crosses beyond the tropics.

**D:** Genetic, species and ecosystem diversity each fall by a different route → the tropics hold most of it and sit closest to their optimum → losing them costs biomedicines and the wild crop relatives that insure agriculture → food security fails directly through abiotic stress and indirectly through pests, weeds, pathogens and pollinators.
