## 8.1 Climate Fundamentals: Biomes, Albedo and the Greenhouse Effect

1. This Book is **Extension Topic B**, not a Core Idea, and the syllabus's own word for what it wants is **assimilation of the Core Ideas** applied to real-world challenges.
2. The **greenhouse effect** is what **greenhouse gases** do to outgoing **longwave infrared radiation**, and it is the one mechanism in this chapter that every later outcome assumes.
3. The gases the syllabus cares about are exactly two, **carbon dioxide** and **methane**, and it says so in a bracket that quietly rules out **nitrous oxide**.
4. **Albedo** names how much **shortwave solar radiation** a surface reflects, and it matters here only because it drives a **feedback loop**, never as a definition.
5. **Shortwave radiation** comes in from the sun and **longwave infrared radiation** goes back out, and that asymmetry is the whole of the physics you need.
6. This chapter hands you four nouns to learn as vocabulary, and they are **habitat**, **organism**, **food chain** and **ecological niche**.
7. A **habitat** is a physical place and an **ecological niche** is a way of life, and the exam expects you to keep the two apart.
8. **Biodiversity** comes in three levels, **genetic**, **species** and **ecosystem**, and the whole of chapter 8.5 is built on that split.
9. The **tropics** are where the biodiversity is, which is why the outcome names them rather than naming biodiversity in general.
10. **Natural causes** of climate change exist, including **orbital change**, **El Niño**, **La Niña** and **volcanic eruptions**, and no outcome asks about a single one of them.

## 8.2 Anthropogenic Climate Change and Carbon Footprint

1. The outcome names exactly three **human activities**: **burning of fossil fuels** linked to increasing energy usage, **clearing of forests**, and **food choices** meaning increasing meat consumption.
2. Every activity has to be tied to a named gas, and the pairings are **fossil fuels and deforestation to CO2**, **livestock and rice to methane**.
3. **Deforestation** counts twice over, because a forest is both a **carbon store** and a **carbon sink**.
4. The distinction that earns the most marks per line in this Book is **direct** against **indirect** contribution, and the exam asks it in both directions.
5. A **carbon sink** absorbs more carbon than it releases and a **carbon source** releases more than it stores, and the same forest can switch from one to the other.
6. Three properties decide how much any gas warms the planet, its **concentration**, its **atmospheric lifetime** and its **global warming potential**.
7. **Global warming potential** is what lets different gases be added together, in the unit **carbon dioxide equivalent**, written **CO2e**, and the exam supplies the values rather than expecting them.
8. A **carbon footprint** is a total emissions figure for a person, an organisation, an event or a product, counted across its whole **life cycle**, and it covers **indirect** emissions as well as direct ones.
9. **Life-cycle assessment** is why **wind** and **hydroelectric** power have footprints above zero even though they emit nothing at the moment of generating.
10. Six energy sources have to be compared, **fossil fuels**, **hydroelectric**, **nuclear**, **solar**, **wind** and **bioethanol**, and the outcome asks only for **relative differences** between them.
11. **Bioethanol** needs an argument rather than a number, because it is close to **carbon neutral** but carries a **carbon debt**, and in food production the split that matters is **animal-based against plant-based**, driven by **enteric fermentation** in **ruminants**.

## 8.3 Effects of Climate Change on the Environment

1. This outcome is a checklist of **eight named effects**, and the examiner can ask for any one of them.
2. The eight are **melting polar ice caps**, **rising sea levels**, **extreme weather events**, **stress on fresh water supplies**, **migration of fishes and insects**, **stress to coral reefs**, **stress to seagrass and mangrove ecosystems**, and **release of greenhouse gases from frozen organic matter**.
3. Sea level rises for two reasons, and the larger one is **thermal expansion** of seawater rather than meltwater, which is the opposite of most people's guess.
4. Only **land ice** raises sea level, because **sea ice** already displaces its own mass, and that one sentence kills a recurring distractor.
5. Two **positive feedback loops** run through this chapter, the **ice-albedo** loop and the **permafrost** loop, and you also have to be able to spot a **negative feedback** in a diagram you have never seen.
6. **Coral bleaching** is what happens to the **symbiosis** between coral and its **zooxanthellae** when the water gets too warm.
7. **Ocean acidification** is the consequence of dissolved **CO2** for seawater **pH**, and the named casualty is **coral** and other **calcifying organisms**.
8. **Mangroves** and **seagrasses** are each stressed by a different short list of climate factors, and the two lists are not interchangeable.
9. **Fish** and **insects** move because they are **ectotherms**, and they move **poleward**, **upward in altitude**, or in fish also **deeper**.
10. **Permafrost** is the **frozen organic matter** the outcome names, and what it releases on thawing is **carbon dioxide and methane**.
11. A whole separate outcome covers one thing only, **how mangrove ecosystems mitigate climate change**, and it has two halves, **blue carbon storage** and **climate resilience**.

## 8.4 Effects on Habitats, Organisms and Insects

1. This outcome gives you four targets in its own words, **habitats**, **organisms**, **food chains** and **niche occupation**, and the chapter has one section per target.
2. **Stress** has a definition worth learning because of the word inside it that links this Book to evolution: anything that reduces an organism's **fitness**.
3. Observed warming is uneven in three ways that matter later, being greatest at the **poles**, greatest in **winter**, and greatest in **minimum** rather than maximum temperatures.
4. **Endotherms** and **ectotherms** differ in how they control body temperature, and nearly everything in this chapter follows from which one an organism is.
5. Warming has produced a documented **decrease in body size** in endothermic birds and mammals, and the syllabus's own reading list points at the paper that reports it.
6. **Phenology** is the timing of seasonal activity, and **phenological mismatch** is what happens when two interacting species read different cues.
7. Species track a warming climate by shifting **poleward in latitude and upward in altitude**, and the constraint that sets their boundary is **minimum temperature**.
8. Climate change alters **niche occupation** by shifting the balance of **competition** and of **predator-prey relationships**, and one named case can carry both at once.
9. The insect outcome names two mechanisms and you must keep them apart, **increased metabolism** and **narrow temperature tolerance**, and the first of the two is really **enzyme kinetics**.
10. The insect response to temperature is a curve with an **optimum**, not a straight line, and it is bounded by **upper and lower lethal limits**.
11. Warming affects insects in five listed ways: **extended geographical ranges**, **increased overwintering success**, **faster population growth and more generations**, **changed pest-host synchrony**, and **increased migration**.

## 8.5 Impact on Biodiversity, Biomedicines and Sustainable Food Supply

1. This outcome is not about biodiversity in general, it is about the **tropics**, and about two named losses, **biomedicines** and **genetic diversity for food**.
2. **Biodiversity** splits into **genetic**, **species** and **ecosystem** diversity, and climate change reaches each by a different route.
3. **Species diversity** has two components of its own, **species richness** and **species evenness**, and mark schemes police which one you have quoted.
4. **Genetic diversity** is what a population draws on to adapt, which makes losing it a **natural selection** problem and not only a conservation one.
5. Tropical species are more vulnerable than temperate ones because they already sit close to their **optimal temperature**, not because the tropics warm more than anywhere else.
6. The **biomedicines** argument needs named organisms, and the ones your notes supply are the **rosy periwinkle**, the **marine sponge** behind **AZT**, and the extinct **gastric brooding frogs**.
7. The food argument runs through three ideas in a fixed order, **monocultures**, then **wild crop relatives**, then **biological insurance**, and it ends in the **tropics**.
8. **Food security** has a four-part definition, **availability**, **accessibility**, **utilisation** and **stability**, and an extended answer is structured on those four.
9. Climate change reaches agriculture **directly** through abiotic factors and **indirectly** through other species, meaning **pests**, **weeds**, **pathogens** and **pollinators**.
10. The food outcome covers three production systems, **crops**, **livestock** and **fisheries**, and each carries its own short list of mechanisms.
11. In fisheries the whole argument turns on **ectothermy** and on **dissolved oxygen**, and the consequence has a direction, a **poleward redistribution** of catch away from the tropics.

## 8.6 Global Warming and the Spread of Mosquito-Borne Diseases

1. This outcome is not about dengue getting worse, it is about **mosquito-borne infectious diseases**, named as **malaria and dengue**, spreading **beyond the tropics**.
2. ***Aedes aegypti*** is named as an **example of a typical mosquito disease vector**, so its **life cycle** is required by name.
3. The life cycle has **four stages**, **egg**, **larva**, **pupa** and **adult**, split between an **aquatic phase** and a **terrestrial phase**.
4. Temperature acts on every stage, and the result is an **optimum** with survival falling away on both sides, exactly as it did for the insects in chapter 8.4.
5. Warming acts on the vector in four ways: **more activity**, **more frequent biting**, **better egg and adult survival**, and an **expanded geographic range**.
6. Warming also acts on the **pathogen inside the vector**, by shortening the time it needs to become infective, and that is a separate point from the vector effects.
7. Three temperature thresholds are worth knowing by name, the floor for **dengue transmission**, the floor for **malaria transmission**, and the ceiling above which mosquitoes die.
8. The mosquito's current range has three stated limits, in **latitude**, in **winter temperature** and in **altitude**, and warming pushes all three of them outward.
9. **Rainfall** does not act in one direction, because both **torrential rain** and **low rainfall** can raise transmission by different routes, which is this Book's clearest evaluate-level point.
10. Not all spread is climate, because **people** rather than mosquitoes move the virus between communities, and an answer that forgets this is one-sided.
11. **Arthropod vectors are ectotherms**, and that single fact is the reason this outcome sits inside a climate topic at all.

## Five errors in the notes themselves

1. **Chapter 8.3 says corals cannot absorb the calcium carbonate they need.** Corals deposit it, they do not absorb it, and the real mark schemes run the chain through **carbonate ions** instead. This sits on an examined mechanism and the scheme is better than your notes.
2. **Chapter 8.1 glosses what greenhouse gases do as absorb, meaning retain.** They absorb and then **re-emit**, some of it downward, and the same chapter says so correctly a page later. Retain is the word that loses the mark.
3. **Chapter 8.3 says the ocean's pH is about 30% more acidic.** pH is logarithmic, so what rose by 30% is the **hydrogen ion concentration** and the pH fell about 0.1 units.
4. **Your notes give methane a global warming potential of 25 and the specimen prints 28.** Both are published, from different IPCC reports. Learn the shape, roughly 25 to 30 times carbon dioxide, and **use the paper's number**.
5. **The current CO2 figure appears four times in this Book with four different values.** Memorise the **pre-industrial 280 ppm**, which is stable, and say over 400 ppm and rising for the present.

Two smaller ones: your notes call the coral's **tentacles** nematocysts when nematocysts are the stinging structures those tentacles carry, and they say malaria comes from **one of four** Plasmodium species when five infect humans.
