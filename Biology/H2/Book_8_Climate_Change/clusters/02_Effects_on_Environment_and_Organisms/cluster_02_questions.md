### Question 1

**(a)** *Which statement helps to explain the role of mangrove forests in mitigating the impact of climate change?* **[1]** [INFERRED: no official key]

**1.** **A**

**(b)** *Explain your answer, taking each of the four statements in turn.* **[4]** [INFERRED: no official key]

**1. Why A is right.** Mangroves mitigate climate change by acting as a **long-term carbon sink**. The **tangled prop roots slow the water flow so suspended organic matter settles and is buried in waterlogged, anaerobic sediment**, and the **lack of oxygen prevents aerobic decomposers from respiring the organic matter, so the carbon is locked away for centuries** instead of returning to the atmosphere as CO2. **The oxygen-poor step is the mark-bearing one.** Saying only that the roots trap sediment leaves out the mechanism.

**2. Why B is wrong.** **Trees fix carbon from atmospheric CO2 by photosynthesis, not by absorbing organic carbon through their roots.** The biomolecules named in the option are correct; the route into them is not.

**3. Why C is wrong.** The leaf litter is in fact **retained within the mangrove sediment**, not swept to the deep ocean.

**4. Why D is wrong.** D describes protection of the coast from the **consequences** of warming, which is adaptation rather than mitigation, and it is also factually wrong, because **mangroves cannot slow the rate of sea level rise**. They buffer waves and storm surge; the rate at which the sea rises is unaffected.

**(c)** *Calculate the annual expected benefit of mangroves in terms of people affected and property loss.* **[1]**

**1.** **People affected = 15 million.**

**2.** **Property loss = 65 $US billion.**

**3.** **Both must be correct for 1 mark.**

### Question 2

**(a)** *Describe how the relationship between zooxanthellae and corals is symbiotic.* **[2]**

**1.** It is **beneficial to the zooxanthellae as the corals provide nutrients and protection for the zooxanthellae.**

**2.** It is **beneficial to the coral as the zooxanthellae undergo photosynthesis to provide the corals with food.**

**3.** Fuller versions of both directions: algae to coral, photosynthesis produces **glucose, glycerol and amino acids**, which **provide most of the coral's food and nutrition**; coral to algae, **inorganic nitrogen and phosphorus from the polyp's metabolic waste**, plus **shelter and protection from excess ultraviolet radiation**. **Both directions must be stated for both marks.**

**(b)** *Explain how an increase in sea surface temperature may lead to coral bleaching.* **[2]**

**1.** **Photosynthetic algae, the zooxanthellae, are sensitive to heat stress, especially when the temperature rises above a critical threshold.**

**2.** The **zooxanthellae will then be expelled by the corals, and coral bleaching occurs as the coral appears white, this being the natural white colour of the calcium carbonate which forms the coral skeleton.**

**3. Examiner's comment, verbatim:** **many students think zooxanthellae first die or have their enzymes denatured due to heat and are then expelled. That is mainly not true, as they are expelled while still alive. Death of corals does not lead to bleaching; rather, prolonged coral bleaching can lead to corals dying.**

**(c)** *Suggest why zooxanthellae are expelled by coral polyp epithelial cells when sea temperatures are high.* **[2]**

**1.** **Zooxanthellae are no longer photosynthesising and producing food, therefore they are expelled.**

**2.** **H2O2 and reactive oxygen species produced by the zooxanthellae during photosynthesis diffuse into the cytoplasm of the coral inner epithelial cell and cause damage or death of the zooxanthellae or of the polyp epithelial cell.**

**(d)** *Explain two factors, resulting from climate change, that could contribute to the death of corals.* **[5]** [INFERRED: no official key]

**1. Factor 1, rising sea surface temperature causing bleaching.** **Increased atmospheric concentrations of carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases enhance the greenhouse effect and raise the mean sea surface temperature.**

**2.** **Above the coral's optimum temperature the symbiotic zooxanthellae living inside the coral's tissues become heat-stressed, their photosystems are damaged and they produce reactive oxygen species, so the coral expels them.**

**3.** The **coral loses its colour, which is the bleaching, and more importantly loses the products of photosynthesis supplied by the algae, which normally provide most of its organic carbon and energy.** **Without this supply the coral cannot respire, grow or repair itself, and if the high temperature persists the coral starves and dies.**

**4. Factor 2, ocean acidification, and this is the chain your notes get wrong.** **About a quarter of the additional atmospheric carbon dioxide dissolves in seawater and reacts with water to form carbonic acid, which dissociates and releases H⁺ ions, lowering the pH of the ocean.** **The extra H⁺ ions react with carbonate ions to form hydrogencarbonate ions, so the concentration of free carbonate ions in the water falls.**

**5.** **Corals need carbonate ions to deposit calcium carbonate for their exoskeleton, so the rate of calcification slows and existing skeletons begin to dissolve.** The **weakened skeleton is more easily broken by wave action and by boring organisms, so the coral colony breaks up and dies.**

**6.** Alternative acceptable factors: **rising sea level and increased storm intensity, which increase turbidity and sediment load so that less light reaches the zooxanthellae for photosynthesis**, and **changes in salinity caused by increased freshwater run-off from melting ice**.

**7. Two corrections to carry out of this part.** Your notes say corals **cannot absorb the calcium carbonate they need**. Corals do not absorb calcium carbonate, they **deposit** it, taking up **calcium ions and carbonate or hydrogencarbonate ions**. And your notes say the ocean is **about 30% more acidic** than in 1751; **pH is logarithmic, so it did not change by 30%**. What rose about 30% is the **hydrogen ion concentration**, which is a **pH fall of roughly 0.1 units**.

SAJC ships no key for that part, so its marking points are inferred; the acidification chain in points 4 and 5 is the chain the prelim schemes give and is the version the topic examiner's guide records as demonstrably better than the school notes.
**Archetype:** the full-cycle process question of this drill. Symbiosis both ways, then the bleaching sequence in order, then ocean acidification run end to end from dissolved CO2 to a dissolving skeleton. Mechanism explain-chain is 16% of this concept heading and the marks come one per link.
**Trap:** killing the zooxanthellae before expelling them, which the examiner comment names as the commonest error; stating only one direction of the symbiosis; writing the acidification chain as "corals cannot absorb calcium carbonate", which is chemically wrong and is what your own notes print; and saying the pH fell by 30%.

### Question 3

**(a)** *Which effect of warming in the polar regions could help to reduce global warming, according to this diagram?* **[1]** [INFERRED: no official key]

**1.** **A**

**(b)** *Explain your answer, taking each of the four options in turn and saying what the diagram shows it leads to.* **[4]** [INFERRED: no official key]

**1. Why A is right.** A is the only option that feeds **"more clouds in summer"**, which is the one node on the diagram reached by an arrow labelled **negative feedback**. **Clouds reflect incoming shortwave solar radiation back to space, so less is absorbed by the surface and the warming is reduced.** The moisture that makes those clouds arrives both as **increased evaporation over the warming polar ocean** and as **moisture transported from lower latitudes**, and the diagram draws both routes into the same node.

**2. Why B is wrong.** The diagram runs **increased growth of vegetation to decreased sun reflection**, because dark vegetation replaces pale snow and tundra, then to **increased absorption of heat by land**, then to **warming of land**, which is on the arm the diagram labels **positive feedback**. It amplifies warming.

**3. Why C is wrong.** **Melting of sea ice leads to decreased sun reflection**, because dark open water replaces bright ice, then to **increased absorption of heat by sea**, then to **warming of sea**, again labelled **positive feedback**. This is the ice-albedo loop.

**4. Why D is wrong.** **Earlier snow melt** takes the same route as C but on land: **decreased sun reflection, increased absorption of heat by land, warming of land**, labelled **positive feedback**.

**5. Note the wording tension and answer the diagram, not the phrasing.** The stem says *effect of warming in the polar regions*, and moisture transport in the diagram originates from the temperate and tropical box, while the polar box's own route to the clouds is increased evaporation. B, C and D are unambiguously effects of polar warming and all three are unambiguously positive feedbacks, so A is the only option left that reduces warming. **The examinable skill here is classifying a loop that is printed on the paper, not reciting the ice-albedo loop from memory.**

**(c)** *Suggest how melting of the permafrost containing frozen organic matter could further contribute to climate change.* **[2]** [INFERRED: no official key]

**1.** **Thawing exposes the trapped organic matter, which is the remains of dead plants and animals, to decomposers such as saprophytic bacteria and fungi that were previously inactive at sub-zero temperatures.**

**2.** **These decomposers respire the organic matter aerobically and release the carbon that had been locked away for thousands of years as carbon dioxide**, and **where the thawed ground is waterlogged and anaerobic, methanogenic archaea release methane instead.**

**3.** **Carbon dioxide and methane are greenhouse gases that absorb and re-radiate outgoing long-wave infra-red radiation back towards the Earth's surface, so the enhanced greenhouse effect raises the global mean temperature further.**

**4.** This is a **positive feedback loop, because the additional warming thaws still more permafrost, which releases still more greenhouse gas.**
