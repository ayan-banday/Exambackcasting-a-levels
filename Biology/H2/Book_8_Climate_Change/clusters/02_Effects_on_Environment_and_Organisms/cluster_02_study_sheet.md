## A. The warming signal is uneven, and the asymmetries drive everything downstream

**A1. Global warming has a technical definition.** It is an increase in **combined surface air and sea
surface temperatures**, averaged over the globe over a **30-year period** and expressed **relative to
1850 to 1900**. Observed warming is **about 1.1 °C**, rising at **0.2 °C per decade**, and **land is
warming faster than ocean**.

**A2. Four asymmetries, each generating a different biological consequence.** The rate has roughly
doubled over the century. Warming is spatially uneven, **greatest at the poles, with the Arctic warming
twice as fast**. It is seasonally uneven, **greatest in winter**. And the **diurnal temperature range is
narrowing, because minimum temperatures rise about twice as fast as maximum temperatures**.

**A3. The fourth asymmetry keeps paying out.** Warmer winters are why insects overwinter successfully in
§G and why vectors survive the cold season in Cluster 3, and rising night-time minima are what cut rice
yield in Cluster 3 while daytime maxima do almost nothing. Neither chapter makes that join.

## B. LO (b) names eight effects, and the examinable unit is the chain

**B1. All eight are short causal chains, and the examiner samples from the list.**

| Named effect | The chain |
|---|---|
| Melting of polar ice caps | higher air and sea temperature melts **ice sheets on land** → water added to the ocean → sea level rises |
| Rising sea levels | **thermal expansion of seawater**, the main cause, plus meltwater from **continental ice** → coastal flooding and **saltwater intrusion** |
| Extreme weather events | a warmer atmosphere **holds more water vapour** → precipitation more intense where it falls, evaporation dries where it does not → **wet areas wetter, dry areas drier** |
| Stress on fresh water supplies | glacier melt gives a **temporary increase then a permanent decrease**; heavy rain runs off faster than it can be stored and carries pollutants; sea level rise drives **saltwater intrusion** |
| Migration of fishes and insects | both are **ectotherms** → distribution set by ambient temperature → shift **poleward, to higher altitude, or deeper** |
| Stress to coral reefs | **thermal bleaching** and **ocean acidification**, both in §D |
| Stress to seagrass and mangroves | two different stressor lists, in §E5 |
| Release of GHGs from frozen organic matter | permafrost thaws → microbes **decompose the organic matter** → **CO2 and CH4** released → further warming |

**B2. Melting sea ice does not raise sea level.** When floating ice melts it **only replaces the volume
of water it originally displaced**, so **only land ice raises sea level**. An **ice sheet is glacier ice
greater than 50,000 km² covering land**; **sea ice is frozen ocean water that floats**.

**B3. Thermal expansion is the larger cause.** Water **becomes less dense as it warms**, and the notes
say plainly that **thermal expansion of seawater is the main cause of sea level rise**. An answer built
only on meltwater has missed the bigger half.

**B4. Fish and insects move because their tolerance is narrow.** Many fish have a **very narrow
preferred temperature, often as little as 3 °C**. The same ectotherm argument carries **crop pests and
disease vectors moving poleward**, which is the bridge into Cluster 3.

**B5. Two definitions the freshwater answer needs.** Water stress occurs when **demand for water exceeds
the available amount** during a certain period, or when **poor quality restricts its use**. **Saltwater
intrusion is the encroachment of saline water into fresh groundwater regions.**
`[Core Idea bridge → CI 1 LO l]` Salinisation and drought reach a plant **osmotically**: the external
solution becomes more concentrated, water uptake falls, and stressed plants **close their stomata**, so
**less CO2 reaches the Calvin cycle**.

## C. Feedback loops, and the skill is classifying one you have never seen

**C1. A positive feedback loop amplifies the original change**, and Book 8 has two. Ice-albedo:
**melting exposes dark open water → albedo falls → more solar radiation is absorbed rather than
reflected → warming rises → more ice melts.** Albedo earns its place here and nowhere else in the Book.

**C2. The permafrost loop adds a decomposition step.** Permafrost is ground in which **captured water
stays frozen for more than two consecutive years**, with an **active layer that thaws each summer**, and
it **stores massive amounts of carbon as frozen organic matter**. As the atmosphere warms, **permafrost
thaws, microbial decomposition releases CO2 and CH4**, warming rises, and more thaws. Whether **CO2 or
CH4** dominates depends on whether conditions are **aerobic or anaerobic**. Permafrost covers about
**25% of Northern Hemisphere land** and is projected to **decline 20% to 35% by mid-century**.

**C3. Negative feedbacks have to be identified too.** Specimen P1 Q28 supplies a feedback web with
**both positive and negative feedback already printed on the diagram** and asks which polar effect would
**reduce** warming. The answer is **increased evaporation giving more summer cloud**, because cloud
reflects incoming solar radiation. Memorising the ice-albedo loop is not enough when the loop is printed
on the paper; the mark is for classification.

## D. Coral: one symbiosis, two separate ways of breaking it

**D1. The organism, in one line.** Corals are **marine invertebrates living in colonies of soft-bodied
polyps**, each with a **mouth encircled by tentacles bearing stinging nematocysts**, and each colony
**secretes a calcium carbonate exoskeleton** beneath it, which over time builds the reef.

**D2. The symbiosis runs both ways, and both directions are marked.** **Zooxanthellae are single-celled
microscopic algae that photosynthesise**. Algae to coral: photosynthesis produces **glucose, glycerol
and amino acids**, which **provide most of the coral's food and nutrition**. Coral to algae: **inorganic
nitrogen and phosphorus from the polyp's metabolic waste**, plus **shelter and protection from excess
ultraviolet radiation**.

**D3. Bleaching runs in a fixed order, and the algae leave alive.** Higher than usual
temperature **disrupts photosynthesis in the zooxanthellae** → they **stop producing food** → the
**metabolism of the coral polyps is affected** → the polyps **expel their zooxanthellae** → the **white
exoskeleton shows through** → the coral starves and, if the stress persists, dies. An examiner comment
attacks the reverse: candidates think the algae **die or have their enzymes denatured and are then
expelled**, which **is mainly not true, as they are expelled while still alive**, and **prolonged
bleaching leads to corals dying rather than the reverse**. **Bleached coral can recover if the water
cools**, because zooxanthellae repopulate the host cells.

**D4. The thermal margin is tiny, which is why a small warming does so much.** Reefs generally occur
where **average water temperature is only 1 °C to 2 °C below the bleaching threshold**. That transfers
to every narrow-tolerance organism in the Book, and it is Cluster 3's tropical-ectotherm argument.

**D5. Ocean acidification runs through carbonate ions, and your notes do not.** Learn the mark scheme's
chain: **CO2 dissolves and reacts with water to form carbonic acid** → the acid **dissociates, releasing
H⁺ ions**, lowering pH → **H⁺ reacts with carbonate ions to form hydrogencarbonate ions** → **free
carbonate ion concentration falls** → corals **need carbonate ions to deposit calcium carbonate**, so
**calcification slows and existing skeletons dissolve**. See §I for the version your notes print.

**D6. Corals cannot migrate.** A scheme states it outright: **reject corals migrate to cooler waters
like marine fish, as corals are mainly sessile.**

## E. Mangroves are an outcome to themselves, and the specimen used it twice

**E1. What a mangrove is.** **Salt-tolerant trees or shrubs** growing in the **intertidal zone or
brackish water** of tropical and subtropical coasts, with **sprawling exposed roots**. The intertidal
zone lies **between the high and low tide marks**; brackish water is **where river freshwater mixes with
seawater**.

**E2. Blue carbon storage, in four steps, the fourth carrying the mark.**
`[Core Idea bridge → CI 3 photosynthesis and decomposition]`

1. **Canopy and woody biomass capture carbon above ground**, because **photosynthesis fixes atmospheric
   CO2 into organic molecules**.
2. **Roots slow tidal water and trap carbon-rich sediment.**
3. **Leaf litter and decaying roots accumulate** in those sediment layers.
4. **The soils are waterlogged and oxygen-poor, so decomposition is slow** and the carbon **stays locked
   away for centuries**. Mangroves store **up to four times as much carbon as other tropical forests**.

Specimen P1 Q27's correct option is step 4, phrased as **oxygen-poor sediments that limit further
decomposition**. Saying roots trap sediment and stopping leaves out the mechanism.

**E3. Its three distractors are each a real misconception.** Trees do
not **absorb organic carbon from sediments through their roots**; they **fix atmospheric CO2 by
photosynthesis**. Mangrove litter is **not swept to the deep ocean**. And mangroves **do not slow the
rate of sea level rise**; they buffer its consequences.

**E4. LO (c) covers mitigation and adaptation, which are different claims.** Blue carbon addresses the
cause. Climate resilience addresses the consequence: mangroves
**reduce wave height by more than 66%**, their **interlocking roots interrupt storm surge**, and they
**stabilise shorelines and trap sediment so shorelines rebuild** after storms, shielding **roughly 15
million people** and preventing **over $65 billion** in damage a year.

**E5. Mangroves and seagrasses have different stressor lists.** Mangroves are stressed by **flooding duration and salinity beyond a species-specific physiological
threshold**, by **uprooting and defoliation in extreme weather**, by **soil salinisation**, and by
**higher temperature at latitudinal limits**. Seagrasses are stressed by **thermal limits**, by
**acidification impairing photosynthesis**, and by **changing water depth, light availability and
substrate**. Seagrass beds cover **0.1% of the ocean floor** yet hold **more than 10% of the ocean's
carbon storage**, so they are blue carbon too.

## F. LO (f): stress, and the four targets it names

**F1. Stress has a definition, and the operative word is fitness.** Stress is **any environmental change
that acts to reduce the fitness of organisms**. Causes are **abiotic**, meaning changes in temperature,
climatic factors and chemical components, which the notes call the most important stress agent, and
**biotic**, meaning **competition, predation and parasitism**. `[Core Idea bridge → CI 4 LOs a, b]`
Fitness is what makes climate an agent of **natural selection** rather than a description of weather.

**F2. Organisms, part one: thermoregulation.** **Endotherms actively control core body temperature** and
can raise internal heat production in response to cold. **Ectotherms have no direct way to regulate core
temperature and depend on their environment**, using behaviour such as basking or shading.

**F3. Organisms, part two: the body-size argument.** Run it as a chain: **larger body size reduces
surface area relative to body mass** → **reduces heat loss** → **mass-specific metabolic rate falls as
body size rises** → **average body size increases with decreasing mean annual temperature and increasing
latitude** → warming has therefore brought a **documented decrease in average body size across bird and
mammal species**, smaller being more energetically efficient when it is warm. The **9477 syllabus's own
recommended reading names Sheridan and Bickford, 2011, on shrinking body size**, which is the syllabus
pointing straight at this section.

**F4. Food chains: phenology, mismatch, and the caribou numbers.** **Phenology is the timing of seasonal
activities of plants and animals.** A mismatch arises because **two interacting species use different
environmental cues**, one tracking temperature and one not, and that transfers to any predator-prey or
plant-pollinator pair an examiner invents. The worked case: **caribou reproduction is cued by day
length, which does not vary between years**, while plant growing season onset is cued by **mean spring
temperature, which rose 4.6 °C**. Result: **plant emergence advanced 14.8 days against caribou
reproduction's 1.28 days**, so **calf mortality rose and annual calf production varied fourfold**. A
fortnight is enough because **plant nutritional content and digestibility peak soon after emergence**.

**F5. Niche occupation: the Arctic fox is two mechanisms in one.** **Tundra is replaced
by boreal forest**, which is unsuitable habitat; **lemming and vole prey cycles weaken** as winters
shorten; and **the red fox, a superior hunter, encroaches northward**. That gives **competition** and
**a changed predator-prey relationship** together, exactly the pair LO (f) wants. Keep the vocabulary
apart: a **habitat is the physical place an organism lives**, while an **ecological niche is its total
way of life**, including every environmental factor it needs to grow, survive and reproduce.

**F6. Habitats and geographical distribution obey one rule.** **Temperature falls with increasing
latitude and increasing altitude**; a species' **upper altitudinal and poleward latitudinal boundaries
are set by minimum temperatures**; warming moves those isotherms; and species **track them to the extent
that dispersal allows**. Two named results: in the **Santa Rosa Mountains, 0.4 °C of warming over 30
years raised the average elevation of the ten dominant plant species by 65 m**; and in northern Alaska,
repeat photography from **1948 to 2001** shows **alder, willow and dwarf birch expanding northward into
tundra**. The trailing clause creates the extinction risk, because **a mountaintop species has nowhere
left to track to**.

This heading is tested off a figure more often than any other in Book 8. Reciting the caribou case is
not what gets tested; reading a new mismatch off a new graph is.

## G. LO (h), first half: insects are the enzyme-temperature outcome in a costume

**G1. The outcome names two mechanisms and both must be answered.** The syllabus words are **increased
temperature leading to increased metabolism** and **the narrow temperature tolerance of insects**. Answer
only the first and you produce the monotonic wrong answer that warming always means more insects.

**G2. Increased metabolism is enzyme kinetics.** `[Core Idea bridge → CI 1 LO q; Book 1 Cluster 2 §H4]`
**All insects are poikilotherms**, so body temperature varies with the surroundings, and **basic
metabolism is a function of ambient temperature: within certain limits, the higher the temperature the
faster metabolic reactions proceed**. The reason is Book 1's: higher temperature **raises the kinetic
energy of enzyme and substrate molecules**, so the **frequency of effective collisions rises**, **more
enzyme-substrate complexes form per unit time**, and the **rate of enzyme-catalysed reactions
increases**. A prelim scheme's first marking point is exactly that.

**G3. Narrow temperature tolerance is denaturation.** The notes state that **outside the optimal
temperature the rate of development decreases significantly, because of the deleterious effects of
extreme temperatures on the enzymes that regulate metabolism**, and that beyond the **upper and lower
lethal limits** death occurs. That is Book 1's curve with a different axis label, so give the same
chain: thermal agitation **disrupts the hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds and hydrophobic interactions**
holding the **3D conformation**, and the **active site is no longer complementary to the substrate**.

**G4. Metabolic rate is measured, not assumed.** Because respiration **requires oxygen and produces
carbon dioxide**, metabolic rate is estimated by **oxygen consumption or CO2 production**. The **lesser
grain-borer's CO2 production rises roughly linearly to 30 °C**.

**G5. Development is non-linear, and two named results prove it.** **Development time is the reciprocal
of development rate.** The **pea weevil's development time is minimum at about 30 °C and rises again
above it**, a pupa taking **5 days at 30 °C and almost 15 days at 40 °C**. **Pine false webworm eggs
fail to hatch below 5.8 °C and above 30.3 °C.** The curve peaks and falls away on both sides.

**G6. Five effects of warming on insects, each with a quantified case.**

| Effect | Mechanism, and the named result |
|---|---|
| **A. Extended geographical ranges** | expansion is bounded by the **upper lethal limit and by dryness**. A doubling of CO2 and **4 ± 2 °C** warming would push the **Colorado potato beetle** to northern England |
| **B. Increased overwintering success** | warming raises **winter temperatures more than summer ones** and **shortens winter by up to 3 weeks**. A **2.8 °C** elevation gave the **Svalbard aphid an 11-fold increase in overwintering eggs** |
| **C. Increased population growth rate** | **earlier emergence from dormancy** and **shorter life cycles** give **more annual generations**. Honeybee and small white both appeared earlier in spring as **mean February to April temperature** rose |
| **D. Changed synchrony in pest-host relations** | **heat-stressed host plants become more vulnerable to outbreaks**. Two models predict **southern pine beetle** outbreak-risk rises of **396% and 508%** |
| **E. Increased insect migration** | poleward movement to **stay within the temperature tolerance range**. Britain gained **14 extra migratory Lepidoptera species per 1 °C** rise in southwest Europe |

**G7. State a direction of change, never just an effect.** An examiner comment records that candidates
**merely stated the impacts** without explaining them, and that **using the term affect is vague, and
they should specify the direction of change**. Pair every effect with its consequence: **increased
metabolic rate gives increased activity, increased reproductive rate, shortened maturation and a larger
population.**

## I. Errors in your notes, and the corrected version to learn

**Two of these sit directly on examined mechanisms.**

1. **The ocean acidification mechanism on page 62 is chemically wrong.** The notes say **corals cannot
   absorb the calcium carbonate they need**. Corals do not absorb calcium carbonate, they **deposit**
   it, taking up **calcium ions and carbonate or hydrogencarbonate ions**. Learn §D5's chain instead.
   This is the one place in Book 8 where the mark scheme is demonstrably better than the notes, and it
   sits on a chain LO (b) requires.
2. **Page 62 says the pH of the ocean is about 30% more acidic than in 1751.** **pH is logarithmic, so
   it did not change by 30%.** What rose about 30% is the **hydrogen ion concentration**, which is a
   **pH fall of roughly 0.1 units**. Writing that the pH fell by 30% is writing something false.
3. **Two coral terms are wrong on page 60.** A nematocyst is the **stinging structure borne on the
   tentacle**, so write **tentacles bearing nematocysts**. And a colony **secretes** its exoskeleton, as
   the notes themselves say two pages later; **excretion is the removal of metabolic waste**.
4. **Page 50 says ice sheets hold more than 99% of Earth's freshwater**, while page 55 and chapter 8.1
   say almost **70%**. The 99% figure is true only of freshwater **ice**. **Use 70%**, and say roughly
   70% and roughly 30% rather than the remaining 30%.
5. **Permafrost is defined on page 70 as permanently frozen for at least two years**, which contradicts
   itself. Chapter 8.1's wording is usable: **frozen for more than two consecutive years**.
6. **Page 58 names 2024 as the hottest year on record in the body and 2025 in the caption.** Quote
   neither. Say **the last decade contains the ten warmest years on record**.
7. **The Santa Rosa caption says 1997 to 2007; the body says 1977 to 2006 or 2007.** The body's
   **30-year period** confirms **1977**.
8. **The ectotherm sentence on page 79 is garbled**, reading as though stable ocean temperatures let fish
   adapt to a small range as a benefit. Chapter 8.3 states it properly: **stable ocean temperatures mean
   fish have evolved narrow thermal tolerances**, which is why warming displaces them.
9. **Endotherm against ectotherm and homeotherm against poikilotherm are set up and never reconciled.**
   Use **ectotherm** for the mechanism claim; page 93's **insects are poikilothermic and ectothermic**
   is the safe phrasing.
