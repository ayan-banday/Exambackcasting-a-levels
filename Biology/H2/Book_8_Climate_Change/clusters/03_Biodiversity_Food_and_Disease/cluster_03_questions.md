**(a)** *With reference to Fig. 11.1, compare the effects of climate warming on the biodiversity in tropical and temperate regions.* **[3]**

**1. One route.** There are **greater reductions in biodiversity in tropical regions compared to temperate regions**. Accept **number of species**; **richness is rejected**.

**2.** There is a **poleward shift of species to keep within their tolerable range.** Reference to plants is ignored.

A handful of students mentioned that the temperature tolerance range of species is narrower in tropical regions (or wider in temperate regions). **1. Why B is the exception.** **Tuberculosis is transmitted person to person by airborne droplets and HIV by body fluids, so neither has an arthropod vector and neither transmission route depends on ambient temperature.** Warming acts on vector-borne disease because **arthropod vectors are ectotherms and therefore especially sensitive to climatic factors**, and TB and HIV have no vector to act on.

**2. ### Question 2

**(a)** *Complete Table 11.1 by calculating the change in suitable area for two of these species.* **[1]**

**(b)** *Using Fig. 11.1 and Table 11.1, predict and explain the effect of climate change on the distribution of the three medicinal plant species at the different altitudes.* **[5]**

**1. Quote the new temperatures at each altitude band.** **Altitudes between 3500 and 5000 m above sea level would be between 4 °C and 16 °C**, and **altitudes between 2000 and 2500 m above sea level would be between 14 °C and 24 °C.**

**2.** **In 2100, the temperatures at the current altitude would be too warm for all 3 species of medicinal herbs.** For example, ***Aconitum spicatum* grows at temperatures between 10 °C and 20 °C but the future climate would increase temperature to between 14 °C and 24 °C at altitudes between 1800 and 4200 m**; ***Aconitum ferox* grows at temperatures between 0 °C and 20 °C but the future climate would increase temperature to between 4 °C and 24 °C at altitudes between 2100 and 3800 m**; ***Delphinium himalayae* grows at temperatures between near 0 °C and 12 °C but the future climate would increase temperature to between 4 °C and 16 °C at altitudes between 3000 and 4500 m.**

**3.** **Hence they are expected to migrate or move upwards to higher elevations or altitudes**, **because warmer climates at their current altitude could result in some plants undergoing water stress, with stomata closing to reduce transpiration and hence less CO2 for the Calvin cycle.**

**5.** ***Delphinium himalayae* will have the largest decrease in suitable land area because it loses areas at low altitudes but is unable to gain more area as it migrates to higher altitudes, and faces competition from species such as *A. spicatum* and *A. ferox* which may have migrated into their original range.**

**6.** ***Aconitum spicatum* has the lowest decrease in suitable land area because it is able to survive at a wider temperature range of about above 0 °C to 20 °C.**

**(c)** *Discuss how climate change can affect the biodiversity of the Proteaceae species and its consequence to the production of biomedicines.* **[2]**

**1.** **Tropical species are often sensitive to hot weather as many species have evolved to become thermal specialists, or have a narrow temperature tolerance.**

**2.** **Hence they are very vulnerable and cannot adapt to climate warming.** **Reject "climate change", because it is lifted from the question stem.**

**3.** **With the loss of biodiversity due to climate changes, production of biomedicines decreases, or potential uses of Proteaceae species for biomedical usage may be lost forever.**

**Source:** ASRJC Prelim 2023 P2 Q11a, stem, figure, table and key verbatim (a); ASRJC Prelim 2023 P2 Q11c, stem and the full key verbatim including the error-carried-forward allowance (b). SAJC Prelim 2017 P2 Q8d, stem, key and the reject line verbatim (c).
**Archetype:** the hardest LO (g) question in the papers. It hands you a temperature-altitude cross-section and a table of areas, applies a 4 °C shift, and asks for a prediction that must be justified species by species. **(b)** *Suggest why there might not be a significant increase in growth of I. pes-caprae plants despite the increase in atmospheric CO2 concentrations.* **[2]**

Very few students linked the function of the stomata to gaseous exchange, which includes oxygen, and even fewer were able to articulate that plant growth is not a direct consequence of photosynthesis but an overall result: photosynthesis makes glucose or TP, which is used in cellular respiration where ATP is made for cell division to create more plant cells for growth, or the glucose is used to make cellulose cell walls or glycoproteins that are building blocks of cells.**

**(c)** *With reference to Fig. 3.2 and Table 3.1, discuss the likely impact of the predicted changes in carbon dioxide concentration, global temperatures and rainfall patterns on the global distribution of C3 and C4 plants.* **[3]**

**1.** **An increase in carbon dioxide concentration will likely increase temperature.**

**2.** **At a high carbon dioxide concentration of 500 parts per million and a high temperature of 25 °C, the rate of photosynthesis for C4 plants or sugar cane at 34 µg m⁻² h⁻¹ is higher than that of C3 plants or barley at 20 µg m⁻² h⁻¹.** Accept **maximum rate of photosynthesis**; **reject "peak"**. Accept any high value of carbon dioxide concentration above 400 ppm.

**3.** **Hence both plants will grow well, but C4 plants are better adapted than C3 plants in hotter areas and their population will likely increase.** Accept reference to latitude, tropical or temperate.

**4.** **Increased temperatures may result in lower rainfall in some places.** Accept higher rainfall.

**5.** **C4 plants absorb between 204 and 350 g of water, which is less than C3 plants at between 542 and 682 g.** **Reject average values.**

**6.** **Hence C4 plants are better adapted than C3 plants in drier areas and their population will likely increase.**

**7.** **However, the predicted change in temperature over the next century is only small, therefore it may not make a lot of difference.** That last point is the bounded counter-argument, and it is what turns a description into a discussion.
