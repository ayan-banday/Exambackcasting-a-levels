Answer - part (e)

(e) Explain your answer, taking each of the four options in turn. [2] [INFERRED: the published key highlights the letter only]

1. Why B is the exception. Tuberculosis is transmitted person to person by airborne droplets and HIV by body fluids, so neither has an arthropod vector and neither transmission route depends on ambient temperature. Warming acts on vector-borne disease because arthropod vectors are ectotherms and therefore especially sensitive to climatic factors, and TB and HIV have no vector to act on.

2. Why A, C and D are all real impacts. A is real, because heat stress cuts feed intake and therefore livestock productivity, with the comfort zone for temperate adult cattle at 5 °C to 15 °C. C is real, because warming expands pest ranges and reduces winter mortality, enabling earlier and larger dispersion. D is real, because most biodiversity is concentrated near the equator and about 25% of prescription drugs were originally derived from tropical plants, so losing tropical biodiversity shrinks the reservoir.

(b) Using Fig. 11.1 and Table 11.1, predict and explain the effect of climate change on the distribution of the three medicinal plant species at the different altitudes. [5]

1. Quote the new temperatures at each altitude band. Altitudes between 3500 and 5000 m above sea level would be between 4 °C and 16 °C, and altitudes between 2000 and 2500 m above sea level would be between 14 °C and 24 °C.

For example, Aconitum spicatum grows at temperatures between 10 °C and 20 °C but the future climate would increase temperature to between 14 °C and 24 °C at altitudes between 1800 and 4200 m; Aconitum ferox grows at temperatures between 0 °C and 20 °C but the future climate would increase temperature to between 4 °C and 24 °C at altitudes between 2100 and 3800 m; Delphinium himalayae grows at temperatures between near 0 °C and 12 °C but the future climate would increase temperature to between 4 °C and 16 °C at altitudes between 3000 and 4500 m.

4. 5. 6. Aconitum spicatum has the lowest decrease in suitable land area because it is able to survive at a wider temperature range of about above 0 °C to 20 °C.

(b) Explain your answer to (a), taking each of the four options in turn. [3]

Answer - part (b)

(b) Explain your answer, taking each of the four options in turn. [3]

1. Why D is correct. If a plant has a narrow flowering window and the pollinators are not active at the same time, fertilisation fails, especially in crops that depend on insects for pollination.

2. Why A is incorrect. Not all crops rely on insect pollinators, and some have broader flowering periods or use wind or self-pollination, so the impact will not be uniform. Note also that pollinator absence does not halt floral development; the flower still forms, it simply is not fertilised.

3. Why B is incorrect. Self-pollinating crops are less reliant on pollinators, so they are less vulnerable to phenological mismatches than insect-pollinated crops.

4. Why C is incorrect. The question is about timing mismatches, which is phenology, not CO2 levels or abiotic pollination. Wind-pollinated crops are the ones least exposed to a pollinator mismatch, not most.

(c) Although many crops rely on insect pollination, only about 25% of global production does, as major staples like cereals do not depend on insects. Answer - part (c)

(c) Explain why it is still important to protect the bees from effects of climate change. [2]

1. Bees are responsible for the pollination of 75% of food crops, hence their extinction would reduce crop diversity, which reduces the nutritional quality of our diets and affects our health when some nutrients are lacking.

2. Bees are producers of honey, which is a source of food for humans and some species of animals, has medicinal effects, and is a source of income for some.

Question 5 [10 marks]

Global food insecurity has been rising due in large part to anthropogenic climate change. Discuss the consequences to the global food supply of increased environmental stress resulting from climate change. [10]

---

Say your confidence out loud before revealing: high / medium / guessed.
Answer

Discuss the consequences to the global food supply of increased environmental stress resulting from climate change. [10]

1. General points, maximum 2. Anthropogenic climate change has led to increased temperature and more extreme weather conditions. A decrease in quality or quantity of food. Food insecurity, because demand exceeds supply for food.

2. Any seven of the following. Effects on plant crops. Heat stress may lead to a decrease in crop yield because the increase in temperature may be beyond the temperature tolerance levels of the plants, and result in death of plants.

3. Water stress, that is drought, may lead to a potential decrease in efficiency of photosynthesis as stomata close to prevent wilting, limiting CO2 concentration for carbon fixation.

4. Increase in temperature might also lead to expansion of range for some insect pests, which may decrease yield if they destroy crops.

5. Range shifts of weeds may lead to increased competition and affect crop yield.

6. Lack of pollinators. Any other valid point.

7. Effects on livestock and fisheries. Some crops are food for livestock, so will be indirectly impacted by the decrease in crop yields.

8. Death of livestock from drought, that is lack of water, or from overheating.

9. Increased susceptibility to diseases.

10. Changes to behaviour of the animals. There may be disruption to migratory or reproduction patterns that affect rearing of animals as livestock. Any other valid point.

11. Loss of genetic diversity for food. Wild crop relatives should be maintained as potential food sources or to outcross with existing farmed crops.

12. However, these wild crop relatives may go extinct if they are unable to adapt to the extreme weather conditions or increase in temperature.

Increased concentration of greenhouse gases leads to increased trapping of radiation or heat, causing more warming.
