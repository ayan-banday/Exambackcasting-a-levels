### Question 1

**(a)** *Figure 1.1, suggest how repeated changes in climate between the warm phase and cool phase may lead to the evolution of new species and greater species diversity.* **[5]** [INFERRED: the 9477 specimen ships without a mark scheme. The chain below is Core Idea 4 LO (j) allopatric speciation, cross-checked against the standard prelim keys for geographic-isolation speciation]

**1.** In the **warm phase the rain forest is restricted to a band near the tops of the two mountains, so the two patches of rain forest are separated by unsuitable lowland habitat**, and any rain forest population is split into **two geographically isolated populations**.

**2.** **Gene flow between the two isolated populations stops**, because individuals cannot cross the hot lowland between the peaks to interbreed.

**3.** The **two populations experience different selection pressures**, and **different random mutations arise in each population**, so **different alleles are selected for in each and allele frequencies diverge**.

**4.** Over many generations the accumulated genetic differences produce **reproductive isolation**, so that **when the cool phase reconnects the forest the two populations can no longer interbreed to produce fertile offspring**, and they are now **separate species**.

**5.** Because the **warm and cool phases repeat**, the cycle of **isolation, divergence and reconnection happens again and again**, each cycle **generating further new species**, so **species diversity increases** over time.

**(b)** *State one anthropogenic activity that indirectly contributes to increases in greenhouse gases in the atmosphere and explain how the activity results in these increases.* **[2]** [INFERRED: no official key]

**1. Activity: deforestation**, meaning the clearance of forest for agriculture, timber or building.

**2. Explanation:** removing the trees **removes a carbon sink**, so **less carbon dioxide is taken out of the atmosphere by photosynthesis**, and the concentration of CO2 in the atmosphere rises **even though the deforestation itself did not release it**. That last clause is what makes it indirect.

**3.** Accept alternatives with a matching explanation: **cattle farming or rice paddies**, because anaerobic bacteria in the rumen or in waterlogged soil **release methane**; **use of nitrogen fertilisers**, because **denitrifying bacteria convert the nitrate to nitrous oxide**; **landfill**, because anaerobic decomposition of buried organic waste **releases methane**.

**4.** A separate prelim scheme on the same distinction **rejects enlarging cities and choice of food where a direct cause was asked for, on the grounds that these cause deforestation and so are indirect causes**. Here the stem asks for indirect, so read which word the question used.

**(c)** *Suggest why the carbon footprint in Table 8.1 is measured in terms of an equivalent mass of carbon dioxide.* **[2]** [INFERRED: no official key]

**1.** **Several different greenhouse gases are released, and each one traps a different amount of heat per unit mass**, that is, each has a **different global warming potential**, so their masses cannot simply be added together or compared directly.

**2.** **Converting every gas into the mass of carbon dioxide that would have the same warming effect puts them all into one common unit**, so the **total effect of a mixture of gases can be added up** and a **valid comparison can be made between different energy sources**.

**(d)** *Explain why bioethanol has a much lower carbon footprint than oil, coal and gas.* **[2]** [INFERRED: no official key]

**1.** Bioethanol is made from **crop plants that photosynthesise as they grow, taking carbon dioxide out of the atmosphere**. The **carbon dioxide released when the bioethanol is burned is therefore carbon that was removed from the atmosphere only months or years earlier, and it is taken up again by the next crop**, so there is **little or no net addition of CO2 to the atmosphere** and the fuel is **close to carbon neutral**.

**2.** Oil, coal and gas are **fossil fuels containing carbon that was fixed millions of years ago and has been locked out of the carbon cycle ever since**, so burning them **adds carbon dioxide to the atmosphere that is not re-absorbed**, giving a large net increase.

**3.** Two prelim schemes protect this argument in both directions and **reject "burning of biofuel releases a large net amount of carbon dioxide into the atmosphere, since biofuel is supposed to be carbon neutral"** and **reject "biofuels make a large contribution to worsening global warming"**. The permitted criticism of bioethanol is the **carbon debt from harvesting, processing and transport**, never the combustion.

**(e)** *State two reasons why wind power and hydroelectric power have carbon footprints that are greater than zero.* **[2]** [INFERRED: no official key]

**1.** **Carbon dioxide is released during the manufacture of the turbines, generators and dam**, because **mining and processing the raw materials and making the steel and concrete use energy from fossil fuels**.

**2.** **Carbon dioxide is released during construction, transport, installation, maintenance and eventual decommissioning** of the equipment, which all use fossil-fuelled machinery and vehicles.

**3.** Also accept: **flooding land to make a hydroelectric reservoir drowns vegetation which then decomposes anaerobically, releasing methane**, and **destroys forest that had been acting as a carbon sink**.

**4.** The bar chart alone cannot answer this part. Every mark here comes from **upstream life-cycle stages, not from generation**.
