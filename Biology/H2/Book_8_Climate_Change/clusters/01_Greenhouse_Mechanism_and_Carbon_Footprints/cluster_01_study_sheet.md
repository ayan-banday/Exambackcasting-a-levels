## A. The greenhouse effect is absorb and re-emit, never trap and hold

**A1. The radiation asymmetry is the whole of the physics.** The sun delivers **shortwave radiation**;
about **30% is reflected back to space** and about **70% is absorbed** by ocean, land and atmosphere,
which is what an average planetary **albedo of 0.3** means. The warmed surface re-emits that energy as
**longwave infrared radiation**, and **net radiation is the difference between incoming shortwave and
outgoing longwave radiation**.

**A2. Greenhouse gases absorb the outgoing longwave radiation and re-emit it in all directions,
including downward.** That downward re-emission is the mechanism. A prelim scheme makes it a
**compulsory point** even in a question that looks like it is only about human activities, phrased as
**greenhouse gases absorb long-wave / infrared radiation, trapping heat within the atmosphere**. The
syllabus never names the greenhouse effect, yet neither LO (a) nor LO (b) is answerable without it.

**A3. The natural effect keeps the planet at about 15 °C; the enhanced effect is what humans changed.**
The **natural greenhouse effect is essential to keeping Earth's surface warm**, and without it the
average surface temperature would sit well below freezing. **As greenhouse gas concentrations rise, the
natural effect is enhanced, like a thicker blanket, so surface temperatures rise.** The gases named are
**carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide and water vapour**.

**A4. LO (a) is explicitly about anthropogenic activities.** Natural drivers of climate change, meaning
orbital cycles and El Niño, are not what this outcome asks for: **orbital change is not thought to play
any significant role on the timescales relevant to modern climate change**. Albedo matters here only as
the driver of a **feedback loop**, which Cluster 2 teaches.

## B. The carbon cycle is Core Idea 3 machinery wearing a climate hat

**B1. Three processes move all the carbon, and you already know all three.**
`[Core Idea bridge → CI 3 LOs c to k, photosynthesis and respiration]`
**Photosynthesis fixes atmospheric CO2 into organic molecules; respiration and combustion release it;
sinks store it.** Every LO (a) answer is one of these three being pushed out of balance by people. The
cycle is naturally balanced, and **human sources produce a net increase of CO2 in the atmosphere**.

**B2. Sink and source are defined by the net direction, not by the size of the store.** A **carbon sink
absorbs more carbon than it releases**; a **carbon source releases more carbon than it stores**. The
same forest can be either, which is why the **south-eastern Amazon is now a net carbon source**.

**B3. Sequestered carbon exists as named biomolecules, and the specimen asks for them.**
`[Core Idea bridge → CI 1 biomolecules]` Specimen P3 Q2 opens by defining **carbon sequestration** as
the long-term storage of carbon in biomass, then awards **3 marks for identifying three biomolecules in
which carbon may be sequestered in a tree**. The answer is **cellulose, lignin, starch, protein and
lipid**. Saying the carbon is stored in biomass earns nothing.

**B4. The Keeling curve's annual sawtooth is a photosynthesis answer inside a climate figure.** The
**Keeling curve plots atmospheric CO2 concentration since 1958**, and it carries a **5 to 7 ppm annual
oscillation**: **CO2 falls when Northern Hemisphere photosynthesis predominates from late spring to
early autumn, and rises when respiration predominates.** The long-run rise sits on top of that cycle.

**B5. Two baselines are stable and worth memorising; the current values are not.** **CO2 rose from a
pre-industrial 280 ppm to over 400 ppm, a rise of about 41%. Methane rose from 714 ppb to over 1850 ppb,
a rise of about 150%.** Quote the **280 ppm** and call the present figure over 400 ppm and rising.

## C. LO (a) names three activities, and every one must end in a named gas

**C1. Burning fossil fuels, linked to increasing energy usage, releases CO2.** **Fossil fuels are
hydrocarbons, namely coal, oil and natural gas, formed from the remains of living organisms** under
heat and pressure over millions of years. They are **non-renewable** because they cannot be replenished
within a human lifespan. Ranked by direct combustion, **for a given amount of energy released, coal
produces the most carbon dioxide and natural gas the least**.

**C2. Clearing of forests releases CO2 twice over, and the second half is the one that earns the mark.**
**Deforestation is the permanent clearing of forests to make the land available for other uses such as
agriculture.** The notes give the double effect: **the absorption of carbon dioxide ceases, and the
carbon stored in the trees is released as CO2 if the wood is burned or left to rot.** One activity
**removes a sink** and **releases a store** at once.

**C3. Food choices means increasing meat consumption, and the gas is methane.** **Livestock production
accounts for 70% of all agricultural land use and occupies 30% of the planet's land surface.**
**Ruminants generate methane by enteric fermentation**, the **digestive process in which carbohydrates
are broken down by microorganisms** in the gut, supported by **rumination**, the rechewing of the cud.
**Manure management adds more.**

**C4. Methane's four anthropogenic sources are one mechanism, not four facts.**
`[Core Idea bridge → CI 3, anaerobic respiration]` **Enteric fermentation, manure management, rice
paddies and landfill are all oxygen-poor conditions**, and **methanogenesis is the decomposition of
organic material by bacteria under those conditions to produce CH4**. Learn them as one anaerobic
mechanism and you can generate the list rather than recall it, which transfers straight to the
permafrost and mangrove-sediment arguments in Cluster 2.

**C5. The bracket in LO (a) is doing real work: limited to CO2 and methane.** **Nitrous oxide is
outside the accumulation outcome**, even though the school teaches it across four pages with a GWP of
298 and a fertiliser mechanism. Keep exactly one line, that **fertiliser use releases N2O**, because it
is a legitimate **indirect** activity for a question shaped like Specimen P2 Q8(b). Build nothing else
on it.

## D. Direct against indirect is the highest-value distinction in the Book

**D1. The carbon-footprint definition splits emissions in two.** Emissions are those **directly caused**,
such as burning fuel in a car, and those **indirectly caused**, such as the emissions associated with the
**production, transportation and disposal of food and goods**. Both belong inside the footprint.

**D2. Deforestation is the model indirect answer, and the reasoning is what scores.** Specimen P2 Q8(b)
gives 2 marks for **one anthropogenic activity that indirectly contributes to greenhouse gases, plus an
explanation of how**. Deforestation works because **removing the trees removes a carbon sink, so less
CO2 is taken out of the atmosphere by photosynthesis**, and the concentration rises **even though the
clearing itself released nothing**. That last clause is what makes it indirect.

**D3. Read which word the question used, because the schemes reject the wrong half explicitly.** A
prelim scheme rejects **enlarging cities and choice of food** when a **direct** cause was asked for, on
the grounds that **these cause deforestation and so are indirect causes**. The specimen asks for the
opposite, so the same items are right or wrong depending on one word in the stem. The same scheme also
requires **an example of a human activity, such as burning of fossil fuels, deforestation or food
choices, that releases CO2**, and states **reject human activities alone**.

**D4. The same distinction reappears in LO (e), so it is worth twice.** Chapter 8.5 splits climate
impacts on farming into **direct impacts, which modify abiotic factors**, and **indirect impacts, which
act through other species**. One framework, two outcomes.

## E. What makes one gas worse than another, and why CO2e exists

**E1. Three properties decide any gas's warming influence.** **Concentration or abundance** is the
amount of the gas in the air, in **parts per million (ppm) or parts per billion (ppb)**. **Atmospheric
lifetime** is how long it remains there, and all the major ones stay long enough to become **well
mixed**. **Global warming potential (GWP) is the total energy a gas absorbs over a set period, usually
100 years, compared to carbon dioxide**, so **the higher the GWP, the more warming it causes** per unit
mass.

**E2. CO2e exists because those three properties make raw masses incomparable.** **Mass released ×
100-year GWP = the equivalent mass of CO2 that would cause the same warming.** Specimen P2 Q8(c)(i)
gives 2 marks for **suggesting why a carbon footprint is measured as an equivalent mass of carbon
dioxide**: **different gases have different GWPs, so converting all of them to one unit puts every
source on a single comparable scale**. Specimen P1 Q26 runs that arithmetic across five gases.

**E3. Take the GWP off the paper, never out of your memory.** The school teaches **methane 25 and
nitrous oxide 298**, which are AR4 values. **The specimen prints methane 28 and nitrous oxide 265**,
which are AR5. Both are published and neither is wrong. **A candidate who substitutes a memorised 25
into a table that says 28 gets the arithmetic wrong.** Memorise only the shape, that **methane is
roughly 25 to 30 times as potent as CO2 over a century**.

**E4. Calculation marks are lost on presentation, not on arithmetic.** A sequestration scheme states
**max 1 mark if no headings at all**, and records the common errors as **no headings or formulas**,
**giving 2 s.f. where 3 s.f. was asked**, and using the wrong multiplier. Specimen P3 Q2(b) says **show
your working and give your answer in kilograms to three significant figures**. **Heading, formula,
working, unit, stated significant figures**, in that order, every time.

**E5. Contribution by gas.** **CO2 contributes over three-quarters, 76.6%, of human-caused climate
impacts**, and methane is second at roughly 15%.

## F. Carbon footprints are a ranking, and you rank from the table you are given

**F1. The definition carries three clauses, and each is examinable.** A carbon footprint is **the total
set of greenhouse gas emissions caused by an individual, an organisation, a single event, a service or a
product**, calculated **over a set period, generally a year or the lifetime of the product**, and
expressed **as CO2 emitted or as the equivalent value of other greenhouse gases, CO2e**. It counts
emissions **accumulated throughout the entire life cycle, from raw material extraction to disposal**.

**F2. Life-cycle assessment is why a zero-emission generator has a footprint above zero.** Assessment
covers **raw material extraction, manufacturing, transportation and operation**. Wind and hydroelectric
power emit nothing at generation, so Specimen P2 Q8(c)(iii)'s two marks come entirely from **upstream
construction, manufacturing, materials and transport**. The bar chart alone cannot answer it.

**F3. LO (d) names six energy sources, and two published rankings disagree about the top of the list.**

| | School, Fig. 2.12, **g CO2e per kWh** | 9477 Specimen P2 Q8, **g CO2e per MJ** |
|---|---|---|
| Highest | **coal 820** | **oil 1000** |
| Second | oil (text only, 733) | **coal 800** |
| Third | **natural gas 490** | gas 500 |
| Then | biomass 230, solar 48, geothermal 38, **hydro 24**, **nuclear 12**, **wind 11** | **hydroelectric 25**, **bioethanol 12**, **wind 12** |

**Both can be right.** The school's claim is about **direct combustion CO2 per unit of energy released**,
where coal genuinely is highest, while the specimen's table is a **life-cycle** figure per MJ, and
life-cycle accounting can reorder fuels. **LO (d) asks only for relative differences, so rank from the
supplied table and quote its units.** Reciting the school's ranking over the paper's is the runner-up
trap in this Book.

**F4. Bioethanol needs both halves of the argument or it scores nothing.** **Biofuels may be considered
carbon-neutral because the plants used to make them absorb CO2 as they grow, which offsets the CO2
released when the fuel is burned.** But **harvesting, processing and transport still emit, creating a
carbon debt that takes years to repay**, which is why the footprint is low rather than zero. Two schemes
reject the attack on combustion outright, so **the permitted criticism is the carbon debt, never the
burning**. The outcome and Specimen P2 Q8(c)(ii) both say **bioethanol**; the school's body text only
ever says biofuels and ethanol.

**F5. Nuclear power is named by the outcome and missing from the specimen's table.** Learn it anyway.
**Its life-cycle footprint is among the lowest per kWh**, and what emissions it has **arise upstream
during construction and fuel processing**, not at generation.

**F6. Food production is ranked animal-based against plant-based, and one figure carries the outcome.**
**Lamb has the greatest impact at 39.2 kg CO2e per kilogram eaten, more than twice pork, nearly four
times chicken, and more than 13 times vegetable proteins** such as beans, lentils and tofu, with beef
between lamb and pork. The mechanism is §C3's: **ruminants generate methane by enteric fermentation**,
and they need **more grazing land, more fertiliser and more energy-intensive feed** per kilogram of
protein. **Beef and dairy alone make up 65% of all livestock emissions.**

**F7. The cradle-to-grave split produces a counter-intuitive conclusion the exam likes.**

| Stage | Share of the food's footprint |
|---|---|
| **Production** | **dominates, about 90% for beef** |
| **Processing** | **5% for beef and lamb against 24% for chicken** |
| **Transportation** | **about 10%, and roughly constant between meats** |
| **Waste** | **at least 20% of food produced is wasted** |

Because transport is small and fairly constant, **buying locally does much less to cut the footprint of
meat than public debate suggests**. What you eat matters far more than how far it travelled.

**F8. Deforestation has a footprint of its own, and it is large.** Clearing forests accounts for roughly
**12% to 20% of global anthropogenic emissions**, around **5.6 Gt CO2e per year**. That figure and the
sink-removal argument in §D2 are two different marks.

## I. Errors in your notes, and the corrected version to learn

**One of these sits directly on an examined mechanism.**

1. **The greenhouse-effect gloss on page 23 says the gases absorb, meaning retain.** Absorption is not
   retention. A molecule **absorbs an infrared photon, is excited, and re-emits**, some of it downward,
   and the chapter's own page 24 says exactly that: **this absorbed radiation is emitted downward toward
   the surface as longwave radiation**. **Retain is the word that loses the mark**, because it drops the
   re-emission step, which is the mechanism.
2. **Four different current CO2 values appear in one Book**: **0.038%** on page 13, which is 380 ppm,
   **392,000 ppb** in Table 2.1, **above 394 ppm** on page 34 and **over 400 ppm** on page 35.
   **Memorise the pre-industrial 280 ppm instead, and call the present figure over 400 ppm and rising.**
3. **Methane's share of greenhouse gas output is 16% in Table 2.1 and 14% on page 37.** Say roughly 15%.
   CO2's 76% is consistent and safe.
4. **Page 32 says methane is emitted at much slower rates than CO2; page 37 says it is accumulating
   faster than CO2.** As printed these contradict. Methane's **proportional** rise since 1750 is larger,
   about 150% against about 41%, while its **absolute emission rate is far smaller**. Quoting page 37 in
   an answer about total warming contribution argues against page 32.
5. **Page 45 writes nitrous oxide as NO2**, which is nitrogen dioxide, a different molecule. **Nitrous
   oxide is N2O**, written correctly on four other pages.
6. **The text under Fig. 2.12 gives oil as 733 g CO2e per kWh, and that value appears nowhere in the
   figure.** The extracted bars are 820, 490, 230, 24, 48, 12, 11 and 38.
7. **Figure 2.1's caption double-counts industry**, so its sector shares do not partition.
8. **Singapore's net-zero date on page 1 is given as 2100.** The target was raised to **net zero by
   2050** in February 2022. The 65 Mt peak around 2030 and the roughly 50% cut by 2050 are correct.

**And one structural warning about the whole Book.** **Fourteen named examples are pointed to Google
Classroom and are simply not in the printed notes**, ten in chapter 8.4 and four in chapter 8.5, and the
concept maps list them as if they had been taught. Do not assume a chapter is complete because its
concept map looks complete. Clusters 2 and 3 name the two that are outcome-relevant.
