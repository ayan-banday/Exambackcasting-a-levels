# Book 8 DRILL: Climate Change, whole book

## Questions

### Question 1  [8 marks]

**(a)** Describe how greenhouse gases in the atmosphere cause the mean global temperature to rise. **[3]**

**(b)** State **two** human activities that increase the concentration of greenhouse gases, and for each
name the gas involved. **[2]**

**(c)** Clearing forests contributes to climate change in two distinct ways. Explain both. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why the carbon footprint of bioethanol is much lower than that of coal, even though
burning both releases carbon dioxide. **[1]**

### Question 2  [8 marks]

**(a)** Rising sea level has two separate physical causes. State both. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain how a rise in sea temperature causes coral bleaching, and why a bleached coral may then
die. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain how increased atmospheric carbon dioxide makes it harder for coral to build its
skeleton. **[3]**

### Question 3  [7 marks]

**(a)** Mangrove ecosystems store far more carbon per unit area than most terrestrial forest. Explain
why. **[3]**

**(b)** As permafrost thaws, greenhouse gases are released from frozen organic matter. Explain the
process responsible, and explain why this is described as positive feedback. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain what is meant by phenological mismatch, using a named example. **[2]**

### Question 4  [7 marks]

**(a)** Explain why tropical species are at particular risk from a small rise in temperature. **[2]**

**(b)** State **two** things that are lost to humans when tropical biodiversity is lost, other than the
species themselves. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how global warming increases the spread of mosquito-borne disease into regions that
previously had none. **[3]**

# Mark Schemes

### Question 1

**(a)** All three.

**Shortwave radiation from the Sun passes through the atmosphere and warms the Earth's surface.**

**The warmed surface re-emits energy as longwave infrared radiation.**

**Greenhouse gases absorb this longwave radiation and re-emit it in all directions, including back towards the surface, so heat is retained and mean global temperature rises.**

Reject **"trap"** and reject **"reflect"**. The words are **absorb and re-emit**, and it is the **longwave** radiation that is absorbed, not the incoming shortwave.

**(b)** Any two, activity plus gas.

**Burning fossil fuels, driven by increasing energy usage, releasing carbon dioxide.**

**Increasing consumption of meat, since cattle produce methane by enteric fermentation in the rumen.**

**Clearing of forests, releasing carbon dioxide.**

**(c)** Both, and both are needed.

**The carbon stored in the trees is released as carbon dioxide, particularly when they are burned.**

**A carbon sink is removed, so less carbon dioxide is subsequently taken out of the atmosphere by photosynthesis.**

**(d)** One.

**The carbon released when bioethanol burns was recently fixed from the atmosphere by the crop, so it is close to carbon neutral, whereas coal releases carbon that has been locked away for millions of years and so adds to the active carbon cycle.**

Gap check on the footprint ranking, since a stem will give you a table: **coal and oil are highest, gas roughly half, then a large gap down to hydroelectric, nuclear, solar and wind, which are low but not zero because of construction and manufacture.** **Rank from the table you are given and quote its units**, because per kilowatt hour and per megajoule give different orders.

**Source:** LOs (a) and (d), marking points from School (HCI) Prelim 2018 P3 Q5(b), 9477 Specimen P1 Q26 and RI Prelim 2024 P3 Q2(c).

### Question 2

**(a)** Both.

**Land ice melts and the water added flows into the oceans.**

**Water expands as it warms, that is thermal expansion of the existing ocean water.**

**(b)** All three.

**Raised sea temperature causes the coral to expel its zooxanthellae, the symbiotic algae living in its tissues.**

**The algae are expelled alive; the coral loses its colour, which is what bleaching means.**

**The coral depends on the products of photosynthesis supplied by the zooxanthellae, so without them it starves and may die.**

**(c)** All three.

**Carbon dioxide dissolves in the sea water and forms carbonic acid, which dissociates and raises the H⁺ concentration, so pH falls.**

**The H⁺ reacts with carbonate ions, so the concentration of free carbonate available falls.**

**Coral cannot deposit calcium carbonate for its skeleton, so growth slows or stops, and existing skeleton may begin to dissolve.**

Say **"pH has fallen"** or **"H⁺ concentration has risen"**. The ocean is becoming **less alkaline**; do not reproduce the "30% more acidic" phrasing from the notes.

**Source:** LO (b), marking points from MI Prelim 2023 P3 Q3(c), NJC Prelim 2023 P3 Q3(a) and SAJC Prelim 2025 P3 Q3(c).

### Question 3

**(a)** All three.

**Mangroves photosynthesise at a high rate and fix atmospheric carbon dioxide into organic compounds, some of which become long-lived biomass in wood and roots.**

**Much of the organic matter is buried in waterlogged sediment, which is anaerobic.**

**Decomposers cannot respire aerobically there, so decomposition is very slow and the carbon remains locked in the sediment for centuries rather than being returned to the atmosphere.**

**(b)** Both.

**Thawing makes the previously frozen organic matter available to decomposers, whose respiration releases carbon dioxide, and methane where conditions are anaerobic and waterlogged.**

**It is positive feedback because the warming causes a release of greenhouse gases, which causes further warming, which thaws more permafrost.**

**(c)** Both.

**Phenology is the timing of seasonal biological events, and warming shifts these events earlier but not by the same amount in every species, so two interdependent species fall out of synchrony.**

**For example, a bird's chicks hatch at the time that used to coincide with peak caterpillar abundance, but the caterpillars now peak earlier, so the chicks hatch after the food supply has passed and fewer survive.**

The mechanism is the mark. **Naming an example without the mismatch mechanism earns almost nothing.**

**Source:** LOs (c), (b) and (f), marking points from ASRJC Prelim 2022 P3 Q3(c), CJC Prelim 2025 P3 Q3 and VJC Prelim 2023 P3 Q3.

### Question 4

**(a)** Both.

**Many tropical species already live close to their upper thermal tolerance limit, so a small rise pushes them beyond it, at which point enzymes denature and function fails.**

**Tropical species tend to have narrower ranges of tolerance because their environment has historically been stable, and a species already at the equator or at the top of a mountain has nowhere cooler to move to.**

**(b)** Both.

**The reservoir for biomedicines: many medicines are derived from or modelled on compounds found in wild organisms, and species lost before they are studied take potential drugs with them irreversibly.**

**Genetic diversity for food: crop wild relatives and landraces carry alleles for disease, drought and heat resistance that uniform modern crops lack, and that diversity is the raw material for breeding crops able to cope with the new conditions.**

**(c)** All three.

**Warming brings higher latitudes and higher altitudes within the mosquito's range of tolerance, so the geographical range of the vector expands.**

**Higher temperature raises the ectothermic mosquito's metabolic rate, shortening development time and increasing the number of generations per year, so the vector is more abundant; the optimum is around 35 °C and development ceases by about 40 °C.**

**The extrinsic incubation period shortens, so the pathogen develops faster inside the mosquito and a greater proportion of mosquitoes survive long enough to become infective, and transmission of malaria and dengue occurs in populations with no prior exposure and no established control programme.**

Gap check. Marking point 3 is the one candidates miss. **Range and abundance alone do not complete the argument** — the pathogen developing faster inside the vector is what turns more mosquitoes into infective ones.

**Source:** LOs (g), (h) and (i), marking points from RI Prelim 2023 P3 Q4(a), TJC Prelim 2017 P3 Q3(e) and CJC Prelim 2021 P3 Q3(b).
