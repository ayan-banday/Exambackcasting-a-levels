# Mark Schemes

### Question 1

**(a)** Any three, and each needs its mechanism, not just the name.

**Mucus in the respiratory tract traps pathogens and cilia sweep the mucus up and out of the airway to be swallowed.**

**Lysozyme in tears, saliva and mucus hydrolyses the peptidoglycan cell wall of bacteria, causing them to lyse.**

**Stomach acid at about pH 2 kills most ingested pathogens.**

**Commensal microbiota occupy the surface and compete with pathogens for space and nutrients, so pathogens cannot establish.**

**(b)** All four.

**The neutrophil recognises surface molecules on the pathogen through its receptors and binds to it.**

**It extends arm-like pseudopodia around the pathogen and engulfs it into a vesicle called a phagosome.**

**A lysosome fuses with the phagosome.**

**Hydrolytic enzymes from the lysosome digest the pathogen, and the products are absorbed or expelled.**

**(c)** One.

**Antibody coats the pathogen, and Fc receptors on the phagocyte bind the antibody, so engulfment is faster and more efficient. This is opsonisation.**

**Source:** LO (a), innate half. Marking points from ASRJC Prelim 2021 P3 Q3(a), VJC Prelim 2025 P2 Q4(c) and the specimen's opsonisation sequence.

### Question 2

**(a)** All three.

**Damaged tissue and mast cells release histamine.**

**Histamine causes vasodilation and increased permeability of capillaries at the site.**

**More blood reaches the area, bringing more phagocytes and plasma proteins to destroy the pathogen; the visible signs are redness, heat, swelling and pain.**

**(b)** All three.

**Interferon diffuses to neighbouring uninfected cells and binds to receptors on their surface.**

**This induces an antiviral state: the induced genes degrade viral RNA and inhibit viral protein synthesis in those cells.**

**So if the virus reaches them it cannot replicate, which limits its spread through the tissue.**

**(c)** Both.

**A virus-infected cell displays reduced MHC class I on its surface.**

**The natural killer cell detects this absence of normal MHC class I display and kills the cell, releasing perforin and granzymes to trigger apoptosis.**

**Source:** LO (a). Marking points from EJC Prelim 2023 P2 Q9(b), NJC Prelim 2022 P2 Q8(c) and TMJC Prelim 2025 P2 Q11(b).

### Question 3

**(a)** All four, and the ORDER is the point.

**Each lymphocyte already carries a receptor of one specific shape, generated before it ever meets an antigen.**

**The antigen binds only to the lymphocyte whose receptor is complementary to it, so that lymphocyte alone is selected and activated.**

**The selected lymphocyte divides repeatedly by mitosis to produce a clone of genetically identical cells.**

**The clone differentiates into effector cells, plasma cells secreting antibody in the case of a B cell, and into memory cells.**

Gap check. **The antigen selects a pre-existing cell and then that cell proliferates.** Saying the antigen causes the cell to be made, or that clonal selection *is* the mitosis, loses the marking point.

**(b)** All four.

**The primary response has a lag phase because the specific lymphocyte must first be selected from a very small number of cells and then proliferate before enough antibody can be made.**

**The secondary response is faster because memory cells specific to that antigen are already present in large numbers from the first exposure.**

**The secondary response reaches a much higher peak because far more cells are available to differentiate into plasma cells.**

**It remains elevated for longer, and the antibody produced is mainly IgG rather than the IgM of the primary response.**

Do not write **"rapid"** alone; the scheme rejects it for **lacking the comparison with the primary response**.

**(c)** Both.

**Helper T cells secrete cytokines that activate B cells, cytotoxic T cells and macrophages, so they coordinate the whole adaptive response.**

**Losing them disables all three arms at once, which is why HIV, which infects helper T cells, leaves the individual vulnerable to infections a healthy immune system would clear.**

**Source:** LOs (a), (b) and (c). Marking points from RVHS Prelim 2017 P2 Q9(a), TJC Prelim 2023 P2 Q10(a) and School (HCI) Prelim 2025 P3 Q1(f).

### Question 4

**(a)** All three.

**Four polypeptide chains: two identical heavy chains and two identical light chains, held together by disulfide bonds in a Y shape.**

**Each arm has a variable region whose amino acid sequence differs between antibodies, forming two antigen binding sites with a shape complementary to one specific antigen.**

**The rest is the constant region, which is the same within a class and determines how the antibody is handled, for example binding to Fc receptors on phagocytes.**

**(b)** Both.

Any two of: **neutralisation, blocking the pathogen's binding sites so it cannot attach to host cells; agglutination, clumping pathogens together so they are more easily engulfed; opsonisation, coating the pathogen so phagocytes bind it more readily.**

**Antibodies do not destroy the pathogen themselves; they mark or immobilise it so that phagocytes or complement destroy it.**

**(c)** All three.

**The germline DNA contains many V, D and J segments, and DNA rearrangement joins one V, one D and one J segment together, deleting the intervening segments.**

**This rearrangement occurs differently in each developing B cell, so an enormous variety of variable regions is produced from a limited amount of DNA.**

**Further diversity arises from the random pairing of different heavy and light chains, and from somatic hypermutation in activated B lymphocytes.**

Careful: **hypermutation occurs in ACTIVATED B lymphocytes, not in plasma cells**, which are terminally differentiated and no longer divide.

**Source:** LOs (c) and (d). Marking points from SAJC Prelim 2017 P3 Q2(c), RI Prelim 2023 P2 Q10(b) and the 9477 Specimen P3 Q3(c).

### Question 5

**(a)** Four, one per box.

**Naturally acquired active: recovering from an infection, so you make your own response and memory cells.**

**Artificially acquired active: vaccination, which supplies antigen in a harmless form so you make your own response.**

**Naturally acquired passive: antibodies crossing the placenta, or received in breast milk.**

**Artificially acquired passive: injection of antiserum containing ready-made antibodies.**

**(b)** Both.

**The antibodies are already made and are supplied directly, so they act as soon as they are given without any lag.**

**The individual's own lymphocytes are never selected or activated, so no memory cells are produced, and once the supplied antibodies are broken down the protection is gone.**

**(c)** Both.

**The vaccine introduces antigen in a harmless form, live attenuated, inactivated or as a toxoid, which triggers a primary response and the production of memory cells.**

**On later exposure to the real pathogen, the memory cells produce a rapid, high secondary response that clears the pathogen before symptoms develop.**

**Source:** LOs (b) and (f). The two-by-two grid and marking points from ASRJC Prelim 2021 P2 Q10(b) and NYJC Prelim 2025 P2 Q10(c).

### Question 6

**(a)** All four.

**Penicillin inhibits the enzymes that cross-link peptidoglycan in the bacterial cell wall.**

**As the bacterium grows, new wall cannot be cross-linked, so the wall weakens.**

**Water enters by osmosis and the cell bursts, so penicillin is bactericidal and acts only on growing bacteria.**

**A virus has no peptidoglycan cell wall, no ribosomes and no metabolism of its own, since it uses the host cell's machinery, so there is no bacterial structure for the antibiotic to act on.**

**(b)** All three.

**A resistance allele arises by random mutation, or is acquired from another bacterium by horizontal gene transfer through conjugation, transformation or transduction.**

**The antibiotic acts as a selection pressure and kills the non-resistant bacteria, while the resistant ones survive.**

**The survivors reproduce by binary fission and pass the allele on, so the frequency of the resistance allele in the population rises.**

**(c)** All three.

**Its waxy cell wall of mycolic acids resists the hydrolytic enzymes of the lysosome, so the bacterium survives inside the macrophage and multiplies there.**

**Immune cells are recruited and cluster around the infected macrophages to form a tubercle with a necrotic centre.**

**In active disease the tubercle enlarges and ruptures into a bronchiole, so bacteria spread through the lungs and are expelled in aerosol droplets when the person coughs.**

The boundary examiners police: **vaccines ≠ antibodies ≠ antibiotics.**

**Source:** LOs (e), (g) and (i). Marking points from RI Prelim 2025 P2 Q1(d), the 9477 Specimen P2 Q9, and CJC Prelim 2023 P3 Q2(a).

### Question 7

**(a)** One.

**R₀ is the average number of secondary cases produced by one infected individual introduced into a fully susceptible population.**

**(b)** Both.

**R₀ greater than 1 means each case produces more than one further case, so the number of cases grows and the outbreak spreads.**

**R₀ equal to 1 means the disease is stable and endemic; R₀ less than 1 means each case produces fewer than one further case, so the outbreak dies out.**

**(c)** All three.

**Herd immunity threshold = 1 − 1/R₀.**

**Measles: 1 − 1/15 = 0.933, so about 93.3% of the population must be immune. Smallpox: 1 − 1/5 = 0.80, so about 80%.**

**The smallpox threshold was achievable with the vaccination coverage available, so transmission could be broken worldwide, whereas measles requires over 93% coverage sustained everywhere, and any region falling below that allows transmission to resume.**

**(d)** Both. **These are defined by geographic extent, not by severity.**

**An outbreak is a sudden rise in the number of cases confined to a limited area; an epidemic is a rise spreading through a population or region.**

**A pandemic is an epidemic that has spread across multiple countries or continents.**

**Source:** LOs (j) and (k). **No real prelim question in the archive tests R₀** — the 9477 Specimen P1 Q30 is the only record of it anywhere, and the syllabus lists R₀ in its Mathematical Requirements, so this is examinable but your school papers will not have prepared you for it.
